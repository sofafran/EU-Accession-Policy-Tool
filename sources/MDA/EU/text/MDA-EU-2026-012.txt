1. INTRODUCTION1 
1.1 CONTEXT 
The Republic of Moldova (‘Moldova’) has continued to progress on the path to EU accession 
since its application in 2022 and the formal opening of accession negotiations in June 2024. 
After concluding bilateral screening of the EU acquis  in September 2025, the Commission has 
assessed all negotiating clusters as ready to be opened. Since then, cluster 1 (fundamentals) 
was formally opened at the Intergovernmental Conference on 15  June, followed by cluster 6 
(external relations) on 14  July 2026 as well as cluster 2 (internal market) and cluster 3 
(competitiveness and inclusive growth) on 13 October 2026. 
EU accession is a strategic objective for Moldova, as anchored in its Constitution. The 
September 2025 parliamentary election reaffirmed the growing pro -EU sentiment. Nevertheless, 
a notable degree of political polarisation remains. 
Moldova continues to face sustained and multi- faceted pressure by Russia and its proxies to 
destabilise the country, as well as significant challenges posed by Russia’s war of aggression 
against Ukraine. By autumn 2026, the number of airspace violations by Russia had more than 
doubled compared with 2025, underscoring the growing spillover of Russia’s war and increasing 
risks to Moldova’s security. Despite these challenges, Moldova has continued to prioritise EU -
related reforms and alignment with EU standard s, economic development and building up its 
democratic resilience. Moldova has set itself the goal of concluding accession negotiations by 
2028 and joining the EU in 2030.  The second EU -Moldova Summit was held in Brussels on 
22 June 2026, showing the ever -closer ties between Moldova and the EU.  Moldova has been 
very responsive in the context of the joint task force set up by the European Commission 
supporting Moldova to meet the interim benchmarks.  
The Growth Plan for Moldova, worth EUR  1.9 billion, continues being implemented to support 
economic transformation and EU integration, and is underpinned by the Reform and Growth 
Facility. Moldova continues to implement the EU -Moldova Association Agreement (AA), 
including the Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area (DCFTA). 
The EU remains fully committed to supporting Moldova’s sovereignty and territorial integrity as 
well as the peaceful resolution of the Transnistrian conflict, based on the sovereignty and 
territorial integrity of Moldova within its internationally recognised borders. The government has 
stepped up its efforts to advance the reintegration of the Transnistrian region. The DCFTA 
applies to the entire territory of Moldova.

1.2 MAIN FINDINGS OF THE REPORT 
CLUSTER 1: THE FUNDAMENTALS OF THE ACCESSION PROCESS 
2.1 FUNCTIONING OF DEMOCRATIC INSTITUTIONS AND PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION REFORM 
2.1.1 Democracy 
The existing framework provides an adequate foundation for holding democratic elections , in 
line with international standards. In August 2026, the Central Electoral Commission adopted a 
new set of amendments to the Electoral Code, mainly aiming to transpose into law the 
Constitutional Court decision of 9 July 2026 pertaining to the Gagauzian regional electoral 
commission. Other amendments included provisions aimed at facilitating alternative electoral 
methods, including postal voting, and at strengthening campaign-finance oversight and the fight 
against new forms of illicit financing and misuse of public resources. Moldova should address 
all outstanding recommendations on these amendments, including by the Venice Commission.  
Moldova continued to face unprecedented levels of foreign interference and has pursued 
efforts to address this issue, including by strengthening its legal framework and the capacities 
of the Centre for Strategic Communication and Countering Disinformation. As recommended by 
the Organisation for Security and Cooperation in Europe (OSCE) / Office for Democratic 
Institutions and Human Rights (ODIHR), Moldova could further strengthen transparency in its 
fight against disinformation and manipulative content. This may include measures like public 
reporting of the ‘take down requests’ sent to platforms or providing access to timely information 
to assist voters in independently assessing disinformation.  
Parliament is able to exercise its powers in a generally effective way. The newly established 
standing committee on European integration and the adoption of the annual legislative plan aim 
to enhance legislative output. The adoption of the Code on the Organisation and Functioning of 
Parliament in April 2026 has raised  concerns among the opposition and civil society with 
regards to the prohibition  on individual MPs switching parliamentary groups and the lack of 
safeguards against the excessive use of emergency procedures. Moldova has requested an 
opinion from the Venice Commission to assess the Code’s compliance with European 
standards. While progress was made in enhancing the transparency of parliamentary activities,  
further efforts are needed to increase parliamentary accountability and integrity. This includes 
improving the use of oversight tools, less resource to accelerated procedures, and adop ting a 
code of conduct and ethics for members of parliament, as recommended by GRECO.  
The institutions in charge of EU  integration process are mostly in place. The 2025- 2029 
National Programme for the Accession of Moldova to the EU was updated in  December 2025 
with the addition of new reforms that need to be implemented on the accession path, 
distribution of institutional responsibilities and new mechanisms for monitoring progress.  
The government has given the  Bureau for European integration (BEI) the resources to 
coordinate EU accession policy. Additionally,  steps were taken to improve coordination 
mechanisms for policy planning, including with the launch of the e -monitoring tool. Legislative 
provisions on the transparency of decision making should be implemented more efficiently. The 
capacity of ministries to systematically oversee policy implementation and address 
recommendations of independent institutions should be further strengthened. The dialogue 
between the central authorities and the Autonomous Territorial Unit of Gagauzia should 
continue.

Civil society , including human rights defenders, operate in an overall enabling environment. 
There is a regulatory framework governing the establishment and functioning of civil society 
organisations (CSOs) standards. While there has been an increase in the number of CSOs 
gaining access to funds through the percentage designation mechanism, the lack of financial 
sustainability remains the sector’s most important vulnerability. Further efforts by the 
government are needed to improve existing funding mechanisms and tax incentives to ensure 
long-term organis ational development across the sector. While some gaps persisted in the 
consistent enabling of CSO’s participation in the legislative process, Moldova adopted in 
September 2026 a law on public participation in decision -making, that aims to facilitate the 
involvement of citizens and civil society in public consultations and guarantee their right to 
make recommendations. 
Public administration reform 
Moldova is moderately prepared in the area of public administration reform. Good progress has 
been made with the adoption in July 2026 of a new law on public institutions, the subsequent 
methodology for the evaluation of certain categories of public institutions in October 2026 (and 
of the concept for the salary reform. The local administration reform was relaunched with the 
aim of completing the process of amalgamating municipalities, significantly reducing their 
number, before the local elections in 2027. The effective implementation of this reform remains 
to be demonstrated. Other steps take n include adopting the new law on public participation in 
the decision-making process and improving fiscal sustainability by amending the law on public 
finance and fiscal responsibility. The first meeting of the newly established EU -Moldova PAR 
Subcommittee took place in June 2026. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were largely implemented and remain 
mostly valid. In the coming year, Moldova should, in particular: 
→ finalise the transparent reorganization of the central public institutions, with the aim of  
ensuring equal payment and status for all staff carrying out civil service tasks; 
→ continue the reform of local self- governance by enhancing local administrative capacity, 
including by ensuring an appropriate level of fiscal autonomy, with the aim of delivering 
better public services and opportunities to foster local economic and social development; 
→ ensure adherence to fiscal rules and improve budget transparency and accountability by 
substantially reducing the general actions budget line and revising the administrative budget 
classification. 
Chapter 23: Judiciary and fundamental rights 
Moldova is between having some level of and a moderate level of preparation  and has made 
good progress in the area of the judiciary, the fight against corruption and fundamental rights. 
Functioning of the judiciary 
Moldova is moderately prepared in the area of the judiciary. The country made good progress in 
reforming the justice sector. The vetting of all sitting judges with leadership positions has been 
completed, and the vetting of candidates and prosecutors continued. Under the Superior 
Council of Magistrates and the Supreme Council of Prosecutors respectively, the selection and 
evaluation boards for judges and prosecutors are in full composition with vetted members, 
while appointments to the disciplinary boards for judges and prosecutors are nearing 
completion. Sufficient resources for these self -governing bodies must be guaranteed to enable 
them to operate effectively. With vetting of appeal judges moving forward, Moldova drafted a

new legislative proposal to extend vetting to judges working on administrative and insolvency 
cases. Despite new appointments, the Supreme Court of Justice is still not operating at full 
capacity, which has been delaying the implementation of its new regulatory framework. The 
National Institute of Justice adopted a new Strategic Development Plan, which integrates the 
recommendations of the 2022 TAIEX review. In order to increase the practical effectiveness of 
the justice system, more efforts are needed to reduce the significant number of vacancies in the 
judiciary and further improve clearance rates, which will also ensure that the excessive length of 
proceedings is tackled across all areas. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and some of 
them remain valid. In the coming year, Moldova should in particular: 
→ finalise the vetting and appointment of judges and candidates, especially for the Supreme 
Court of Justice and anticorruption panels, and make substantial progress with vetting 
prosecutors from prosecutorial institutions, in particular specialised prosecutors’ offices; 
→ implement a significant part of the Strategic Development Plan of the National Institute of 
Justice, develop and implement human resource strategies for the judiciary and prosecution 
services, further implement the judicial and prosecutorial maps and significantly improve 
clearance rates and disposition time in courts; 
→ strengthen the autonomy and integrity of the prosecution service, including by finalising the 
implementation of a manual random case allocation system for prosecution and transition 
towards an electronic system of randomised allocation of cases for prosecutors based on 
strict and objective criteria, by removing the ex officio membership of the Minister of Justice 
in the Superior Council of Prosecutors at the end of 2027 .  
Fight against corruption 
Moldova is  between having some and a moderate level of preparation  in the fight against 
corruption and has made good progress. The track record has improved, including an increase 
in the overall number of prosecutions. More efforts are needed to proactively investigate 
corruption, and translate investigations into convictions, including at high level. A unified 
statistical system is needed to improve the accuracy of reported data. In December 2025, an 
amendment introduced the mandatory vetting of all judges of the specialised anticorruption 
panels, in line with last year’s recommendation. Moldova adopted amendments to the Crimina l 
Procedural Code in June 2026, transferring some of APO’s competences to the National 
Anticorruption Centre (NAC), while retaining APO’s prosecutorial oversight. Merit- based, 
objective and transparent selection of the NAC leadership must be ensured in practice for future 
appointments. The powers of the Prosecutor General have been revised with the aim to 
safeguard the independence of APO.  On the prevention of corruption, while the implementation 
of the National Programme on Integrity and Anticorruption (NIAP) 2024-2028 registered general 
progress, several indicators, in particular on governance and justice aspects, show a slight 
deterioration compared to 2024. Targeted risk assessments in vulnerable sectors are being 
developed, but Moldova should address corruption risks and improve integrity especially in 
SOEs and public institutions. Moldova aligned the legislation on whistleblower protection with 
the EU acquis, in line with 2025 recommendations. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and some 
remain valid. In the coming year, Moldova should in particular: 
→ deliver increasingly proactive and efficient anticorruption investigations to ensure an 
improved track record on investigations, prosecutions and final convictions at all levels with

Fundamental rights 
The legislative and institutional framework is largely in place. Implementation of the national 
human rights action programme and programmes on gender equality and gender- based 
violence was enhanced following last year’s recommendations. Moldova has established an 
interinstitutional dialogue to increase the implementation rate of the People’s Advocate Office 
and the Equality Council recommendations. Detention and pre- trial police custody conditions 
remain a concern, as does the treatment of persons with disabilities in specialised institutions.  
The number of judgments issued by the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) concerning 
Moldova remains high, including the cases under enhanced supervision, and their execution rate 
should be improved. Moldova has developed a National Programme for the social inclusion of 
persons with disabilities. Efforts to strengthen procedural rights, the rights of victims of crime, 
the rights of persons belonging to national minorities, including Roma 2, and combat gender-
based violence need to continue. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and therefore 
some remain valid. In the coming year, Moldova should in particular: 
→ ensure that adequate resources are available to strengthen the independence and 
effectiveness of the Equality Council and the People’s Advocate Office; 
→ step up safe deinstitutionalisation of persons with disabilities through community -based 
solutions;  
→ increase the implementation rate of judgments issued by the ECHR, notably by improving 
detention and prison conditions by addressing the outstanding European Committee for the 
Prevention of Torture recommendations of 2023 and 2025; including by transferring the 
responsibilities of the penitentiary medical system from the Ministry of Justice and the 
medical services in pre -trial police custody from the Ministry of Internal Affairs, both to the 
Ministry of Health.

Chapter 24: Justice, freedom and security 
Moldova is moderately prepared in the area of justice, freedom and security. Good progress  
was made during the reporting period by further aligning with the EU acquis , notably on 
preventing and combating trafficking in human beings, protecting victims, and in the field of 
migration and asylum.  In parallel, the legal framework for investigation and prosecution of 
money laundering and other financial crimes was further enhanced and a roadmap of 
complementary measures to strengthen the entire financial crimes investigation system was 
adopted. Additionally, Moldova continued to implement its general home affairs strategy and 
updated all six sectoral development strategies for 2026 -2030 covering organised crime, public 
order, safety, civil protection, migration and education/ethics/digitalisation . The legal 
framework for fighting cybercrime was strengthened by adopting several legal acts. 
Moldova also enhanced operational capacities, especially on asset recovery. Consequently, 
Moldova has presented an improving track record across the different categories of crime, 
specifically on organised crime, money laundering, cybercrime, illicit drugs, and firearms 
trafficking. Capacity constraints across the board remain however a challenge for operational 
delivery. The continuation of strengthening of operational capacities remains essential to 
translate reforms into further tangible results. Moldova improved its  interinstitutional 
cooperation and coordination and further intensified cooperation with EU agencies in the field. 
In the field of migration, beyond progress on legal alignment, Moldova adopted an Integrated 
State Border Management Programme for 2025 -2030 as well as a National Programme on 
Migration and Asylum for 2026- 2030 in line with last year’s recommendations. Moldova should 
intensify efforts to address the continued occurrence of unfounded asylum applications by 
Moldovan nationals in EU Member States. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were largely implemented. In the coming 
year, Moldova should in particular:

Fight against serious and organised crime 
Moldova is  moderately prepared  in the fight against serious and organised crime. Good 
progress was made in this area, including through continued efforts to tackle firearms and 
drugs trafficking, demonstrating an improved track record in these areas. Moldova also 
increased its operational capacity to fight serious international crime, including through 
continuous participation in EMPACT and more qualitative data exchange with EU Member 
States. Additionally, it further aligned its drug -related legal framework with the EU acquis, 
covering the National Drug Observatory, the national early warning system, and new 
psychoactive substance classifications. Further work is needed to strengthen these structures 
and ensure adequate capacity to fully implement and enforce the improved legal framework. 
Moldova also made good progress in the areas of financial crimes investigation , asset recovery 
and confiscation by strengthening the legal and institutional framework. This included adopting 
a revised law on preventing and combating money laundering and the financing of terrorism in 
June 2026, which addresses recommendations of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) and 
the standards of the Egmont Group. Mechanisms for the fight against corruption, financial 
crime and organized crime were also strengthened, NAC was given the competence for serious 
financial crime, including stand -alone money laundering. Additionally, a national mechanism for 
parallel financial investigations was set up and Moldova operationalised a national register for 
seized assets and strengthened the mandate of the Criminal Assets Recovery Agency (CARA), 
which also saw a considerable increase in parallel financial investigations and seized assets. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and therefore 
remain partly valid. In the coming year, Moldova should in particular: 
→ further improve the track record of investigations, prosecutions and final judgments in all 
fields of serious and organised crime, including by strengthening the intelligence- led 
policing framework the interagency cooperation across all organised and serious crime 
categories; 
→ further align with the EU acquis  on money-laundering investigation and criminalisation and 
on asset recovery and confiscation, in particular Directive (EU) 2024/1260; Operationalise 
the national parallel- financial-investigation mechanism, strengthen FIU –NAC–Prosecution–
CARA cooperation, and demonstrate measurable results in stand -alone money -laundering 
investigations, confiscation, enforcement and actual recovery of criminal assets;

The existence of a functioning market economy 
Moldova has some level of preparation and has made good progress in creating a functioning 
market economy. Authorities remain committed to macroeconomic stability and show good 
progress on economic governance, including through advanc ing the Reform Agenda 
implementation with a strong track record and submitting the third Economic Reform 
Programme in January 2026. Discussions are ongoing for a three- year IMF non-financing Policy 
Coordination Instrument (PCI). A close engagement with the IMF serves as an important anchor 
for macroeconomic and fiscal policies and provides a signal of reform commitment to 
international partners and investors. The economic rebound in 2025 slowed down during the 
first half of 2026, driven largely by the impact of the Middle East conflict and renewed upward 
pressure on energy prices and inflation, the current account deficit continues to remain wide, 
reflecting structural vulnerabilities and the cessation of electricity supply from the Transnistrian 
region. Moldova’s fiscal position weakened with a persistently high deficit and upward debt 
trajectory, while public investment execution remains weak. Fiscal structural reforms are 
progressing, particularly on public investment management and spending efficiency, th ough 
results have yet to materialise. The banking sector remains stable and well capitalised. Credit 
growth has been robust but financial intermediation remains low despite ample market liquidity. 
Efforts to improve the business environment are ongoing, notably  through cutting red tape 
and streamlining business support programmes. The state -owned enterprise ( SOE) reform is 
advancing gradually, however weak SOE governance remains a persistent challenge. The 
spillovers from Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine, a large informal economy, low 
labour force participation, and weak competition enforcement continue to weigh on domestic 
and foreign investment. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and remain 
valid. In the coming year, Moldova should, in particular: 
→ pursue fiscal policy aimed at achieving prudent medium- term fiscal positions and ensuring 
debt sustainability, notably by further broadening the tax base, including by systematically 
streamlining tax expenditures and improving spending efficiency through the new spending 
reviews while implementing the medium- term debt management strategy by deepening the 
domestic government securities market and broadening the institutional investor base; 
→ reduce the economy’s vulnerability to external shocks, including through diversifying its 
export base and reducing energy import dependence while accelerating progress on the 
implementation of the SOE reform, notably the triage implementation, accelerating steps to 
strengthen SOE governance, notably through the selection of independent SOE board 
members and reforming the Public Property Agency, and completing the full digitalisation of 
the public property register; 
→ continue efforts to address low labour market participation and informal employment, 
including through increased active labour market measures and a reform of income 
taxation. 
The capacity to cope with competitive pressure and market forces within the EU 
Moldova has some level of preparation and has made good progress to cope with competitive

the quality of education and coverage of dual vocational training; however, ongoing labour

production and deeper integration into the EU electricity grid. However investment gaps,

The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and remain

and increase efforts to promote innovation, including through technology transfers and 
stronger linkages with foreign investors. 
Chapter 5 - Public procurement 
Moldova is between having  some and a moderate level of preparation  in the area of public 
procurement. Good progress has been made with the adoption of the new Law on public 
procurement and the Law on remedies and review procedures, as well as important 
amendments to the laws on utilities and the law on defence and security procurement, ensuring 
further alignment with the EU acquis. The implementation of the National Programme for the 
Development of the Public Procurement System for 2023-2027 has continued. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented. In the coming 
year, Moldova should, in particular: 
→ finalise the implementing legislation necessary for the Law on public procurement;  
→ implement the National Programme for the Development of the Public Procurement System, 
with a focus on professionalisation and centralisation; 
→ finalise the development of a new electronic procurement system and make it operational. 
Chapter 18 - Statistics 
Moldova has some level of preparation  in the area of  statistics. The country  made some 
progress in strengthening the legal and institutional framework for official statistics, and in 
modernising statistical production across several domains, while alignment in the 
macroeconomic statistics domain continued to lag. Staffing remained a constraint. Data 
transmission to Eurostat showed an upward trend. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and therefore 
remain valid. In the coming year, Moldova should, in particular:

domain; 
→ expand the use of administrative and privately held data in the production of official

Chapter 32 - Financial control 
Moldova is between having some and a moderate  level of preparation in the area of financial 
control. Good progress was achieved, notably with the adoption of legal amendments 
strengthening the financial independence of the Court of Accounts as well as strengthening the 
anti-fraud coordination service (AFCOS) and the AFCOS network. 
The Commission’s recommendations from last year were partially implemented and therefore 
remain valid. In the coming year, Moldova should, in particular: 
→ further strengthen managerial accountability in public administration by increasing the 
delegation of authority in financial management and internal control, notably by adopting 
and implementing the Guide for the application of delegation standards within the public 
sector; 
→ build capacity for auditing the use of EU funds, notably through filling vacant posts and 
implementing the training and certification programmes for internal auditors; 
→ improve the implementation rate of the Court of Accounts’ recommendations across central 
administrative authorities and other relevant organisational structures. 
 
CLUSTER 2: INTERNAL MARKET 
Chapter 1 - Free movement of goods 
Moldova is moderately prepared in the area of the free movement of goods. Good progress 
was made during the reporting period  on both the horizontal framework and alignment with EU 
product acquis, however further efforts are required to ensure full and consistent 
implementation of the acquis in this chapter.

authorities work actively and in a coordinated manner to detect and counter foreign

Policy development and coordination

Public service and human resources management 
The public service and human resources management system needs to be improved. A new law

accountability and transparency, will be carried out in 2027, accordingly to a new transparent

decentralisation. New legal amendments were adopted, aiming at strengthening and clarifying 
responsibilities of the role of Secretaries General in ministries by allowing greater flexibility in 
internal structures and reinforcing their coordination of key administrative functions, including 
public policy, external assistance, legal affairs, HR, finance, ICT, and document management. 
Efforts are ongoing to implement a competency framework for senior management and 
introduce a centralised recruitment system for central public administration. 
The current salary system is fragmented and complex and fails to ensure competitiveness and 
transparency of remuneration. A civil service remuneration reform has been developed in the 
framework of a broad process of public consultations; a new law is planned to come into force 
in January 2027. The reform should include job evaluation, pay analysis, the design of a revised 
salary scale and a budget impact assessment framework.  
A new human resource management information system  (HRMIS) is still in the development 
phase. Currently the system remains mostly decentralised, with limited monitoring and 
reporting.

The accountability of the public administration still needs to be improved. The legislative

Legal framework 
The legal framework on the fight against corruption is broadly adequate regarding preventive 
measures and was further strengthened in 2025, while major gaps remain regarding repressive 
measures, resources and training for specialised bodies, lobbying regulation and comparable 
statistical reporting.  
GRECO’s Fifth Evaluation Round Compliance Report published in April 2026 concluded that 
seven out of 25 recommendations had been implemented satisfactorily, while 12 were partly

In July 2026, Moldova amended its national legislation with a view to aligning whistleblower

disclosures, and the measures of support and protection against retaliation.

Institutional framework 
The institutional framework on the fight against corruption is in place and is adequate regarding 
preventive measures and satisfactory regarding law enforcement. 
Through amendments to the Criminal Procedural Code in June 2026, Moldova re- distributed the 
anticorruption investigation and prosecution functions of APO and NAC. The new institutional 
framework is expected to strengthen the financial crime investigation system and places APO in 
the lead of investigations. For the other high- level corruption cases, NAC is led by APO in the 
investigative process. The 2026- 2027 modernisation programme complements this model 
through NAC capacity -building, specialised functions  for criminal -asset recovery and virtual

Moldova has an overall well -established and operational specialised institutional setup for 
corruption prevention and anticorruption policy coordination, centred on the NAC for the 
prevention, coordination and investigation of corruption and corruption -related offences within 
its competence, and on the NIA for asset and interest declarations, conflicts of interest, 
incompatibility of office and related integrity restrictions. Capacity constraints remain, in 
particular on the NIA’s effective verification cap acity and the full practical functioning of 
automated and interoperable verification tools. In August 2026, NIA launched public 
consultations on a new concept for the e- Integritate system, to replace the current one, and 
started implementing its internal guidelines, expanded the powers of its president for more 
coherent control practice and tightened the declaration and verification of assets system.  
Attention should be paid to ensuring a more effective framework for the proactive detection of 
latent high-level corruption cases. In this regard, cooperation, operational coordination and the 
overall institutional setup for high-level corruption detection should be further strengthened. 
Asset recovery and management reforms advanced with the introduction in February 2026 of a 
Law on Social and public -interest use of confiscated assets. Moldova should ensure that 
financial investigations lead to timely freezing, final confiscation and measurable recovery.  
Following the adoption in 2025 of the law to set up specialised anticorruption panels, Moldova 
faced a low participation rate in the competitions for these panels. Moreover, legislative 
amendments are needed to ensure that these panels retain jurisdiction over all high -level 
corruption cases.

strategic vulnerabilities. Since taking office in July 2026, the new Government has placed 
continued emphasis on resilience- building, making it a cross -cutting objective, underpinning 
economic and energy security, crisis preparedness and resilience to hybrid threats.

Chapter 16 - Taxation 
EU rules on taxation cover value added tax, excise duties and aspects of corporate taxation. They 
also cover cooperation between tax administrations, including the exchange of information to 
prevent tax evasion. 
As regards  indirect taxation , Moldova has made some progress in further aligning national 
legislation with the EU acquis. However, structural misalignments regarding VAT refunds and 
exemptions remain. Moldova applies a standard VAT rate of 20% and a reduced rate of 8%. In 
January 2026, the reduced VAT rate was extended to registered medical devices, a reverse-
charge mechanism was introduced for supplies of electricity and natural gas between 
registered traders, and imports of natural gas, electricity, thermal energy and cooling energy 
delivered through networks became exempt with the right of deduction. Further alignment is 
needed, notably on the structure and scope of exemptions, deductions, reduced rat es and 
special schemes, and on VAT refund procedures. Moldova also needs to advance the technical 
interoperability of its systems with  the VAT Information Exchange System (VIES) and with the 
solutions under the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package.

building is needed in this area. Excise duty rates on energy and tobacco products have not yet 
been aligned with the EU acquis.  
Regarding direct taxation , Moldova still needs to align with the EU directives on taxation

Regarding administrative cooperation and mutual assistance , there have been no

Chapter 17 - Economic and monetary policy 
EU rules require the independence of central banks and prohibit them from directly financing the 
public sector. Member States coordinate their economic policies and are subject to fiscal,

On monetary policy, Moldova made substantial progress on the alignment with the EU acquis . 
Amendments to the National Bank of Moldova Law to strengthen the appointment and 
dismissal procedures of members of the decision -making bodies and improving governance,

On economic policy , Moldova made progress in strengthening fiscal governance by 
institutionalising spending reviews and linking the implementation of their results with the
