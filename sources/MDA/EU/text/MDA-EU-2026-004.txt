Commission’s assessment of the preconditions for Union support 
 
Effective democratic and rule of law mechanisms 
Moldova continues to im plement justice sector reforms,  in particular through the vetting 
process, and by strengthening the integrity and independence of the judiciary.  
 
The functioning of democratic institutions is satisfactory. Moldova shows a continued 
commitment to implement reforms, despite hybrid attack s from Russia and its proxies on 
democratic institutions and their attempts to undermine social cohesion in the country.  
Moldova has taken significant steps to structure the work of the government and make tangible 
progress towards EU integration. 
 
The Parliament has an oversight role over the executive.  Accountability lines between the 
Parliament and other relevant institutions are established, with the Parliament appointing state 
officials at the head of central administrative institu tions and independent institutions. Further 
efforts are needed to increase Parliament’s transparency, accountability, integrity, and overall 
effectiveness. This includes enhancing the track record of parliamentary oversight of 
independent institutions and ensuring a timely and comprehensive publication of parliamentary 
activities and decisions, as well as meaningful public consultations on draft laws and 
accompanying documents. Going forward, Moldova should continue to enhance its use of 
parliamentary oversight tools.  
 
Parliamentary elections were held on 28 September 2025 . The recently revised legal 
electoral framework provided a sound basis for conducting democratic elections, although 
legislative changes shortly before the elections impacted legal certainty. The national 
authorities worked actively and in a coordinated manner to prevent, detect and counter foreign 
interference attempts, including illicit financing and vote -buying schemes, wi th the aim of 
safeguarding electoral integrity and democratic process. Nonetheless, some OSCE/ODIHR and 
Venice Commission recommendations remain unaddressed. Moldova should ensure its 
legislative framework pertaining to elections, including the Law on combating electoral 
corruption, is brought fully in line with European standards.  
 
While freedom of expression is generally ensured, the legislative environment still needs 
to be further aligned with the EU acquis and European standards. Moldova has made good 
progress in  2025, notably by : i)  adopting new legislation on access to information ; ii) 
implementing the law on the Media Subsidy Fund; iii) amending the audiovisual media services 
code and on advertising ; and iv ) on the protection of journalists . Moldova has made good 
progress in  2025, notably by : i)  adopting new legislation on access to information ; ii)  
implementing the law on the Media Subsidy Fund ; and iii) amending the audiovisual media 
services code and the Law 62/2022 on state  advertising to bring it in line with the European 
Media Freedom Act ( EMFA). Rules for selecting members of the public service broadcaster 
and the Audiovi sual Council have also been reviewed. Nevertheless, media ownership 
concentration and political influence persist. The intimidation of journalists by private actors 
has increased, and no notable progress has been made on the protection of journalists.

Respect for all human rights obligations 
 
The legislative and institutional framework on fundamental rights is largely in place and 
Moldova continues to meet its international obligations on human rights. Implementation 
of the national human rights action programme is ongoing. Institutional capacity and 
implementation need to be strengthened to guarantee the full enjoyment of human rights. 
Mechanisms for preventing  and countering gender-based violence are in place. Detention 
conditions, progress on deinstitutionalisation and access to specialised support services  for 
victims of gender -based violence , including shelters and psychological support, should be 
improved. The legislative framework safeguards the rights of persons belonging to minorities. 
Moldova should continue support for intercultural dialogue and efforts to include ethnic 
minorities in public administration.  
 
Based on the above assessment, Moldova continues to fulfil the preconditions for Union support 
under Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2025/535. 
 
General conditions 
 
Macroeconomic and financial stability 
 
Following a stagnation in 2024 due to declining agricultural output, real GDP rebounded, 
but recovery remains fragile, driven by a narrow range of sectors making the economy 
vulnerable to external shocks . The 2025 outturn exceeded expectations, with GDP growth 
projected at 3%  by the authorities , driven primarily by a recovery in agriculture, stron g 
construction activity, and an uptick in manufacturing in the fourth quarter, particularly in food 
processing. Strong domestic demand, supported by real wage growth and favourable 
agricultural outcomes, underpinned this expansion. Private investment accel erated through 
supportive policies including zero-tax rates for reinvested SME profits and improved financing 
conditions. Broad-based energy support mitigated the impact of winter energy price increases 
on households and businesses.  However, risks remain elevated considering the economy’s high 
vulnerability to external shocks. 
 
The external sector presents persistent structural challenges . The current account deficit 
expanded from 11.1% of GDP in 2023 to 16.6% in 2024, with forecasts suggesting a further 
widening to approximately  20% in 2025. This deterioration reflects structural dependence on 
volatile agricultural exports and vulnerability to imported energy price fluctuations. The trade 
deficit remains wide due to a weak export base and energy i mport reliance. While exports 
showed weakness in early 2025, gradual recovery emerged mid-year, primarily supported by a 
good harvest.  Services exports continue to expand, and remittances  continue to play an 
important role but declined slightly as share of  GDP to 7.6% in the third quarter of 2025 . 
Foreign currency reserves declined in early 2026 by 2% to EUR 5.1 billion, equivalent to over 
five months of import coverage.  
 
Monetary and Fiscal Policies 
Inflation peaked at 9.1% in  the first quarter of 2025 following sharp electricity price 
increases exacerbated after Russian gas supplies to the Transnistrian region were cut 
off. However, inflation moderated to 4.8% by January 2026  due to declining energy and food 
prices supported by improved domestic agricultural production. This puts inflation rates within 
the bandwidth targeted by the National Bank of Moldova.

The predominantly foreign-owned banking sector demonstrated continued stability with 
strong capital adequacy, liquidity, and profitabilit y. The non -performing loan ratio 
decreased slightly from 4.2% to 4.1% y-o-y in December 2025in the remaining relatively low.  
 
The 2025 fiscal deficit is projected to reach 4% of GDP, lower than the 5% of GDP target 
and broadly unchanged to previous year’s outturn of 3.9% of GDP.  The Parliament 
approved a 2025 supplementary budget targeting a 5% deficit to 
accommodate additional expenditures financed by the EU's Reform and Growth 
Facility.  Preliminary data suggest the 2025 deficit remained a round 4%, reflecting capital 
expenditure under -execution and slightly higher than expected revenues, supported by the 
strong economic recovery. The 2026 deficit is projected at 5.7% of GDP.  
 
The authorities project public debt to remain manageable at 38.3% of GDP by end-2025, 
though projections expect an increase to 46% by 2028,  still below the Maastricht reference 
value. The IMF's latest public debt sustainability analysis confirmed a low risk of external debt 
distress and moderate risk of overall public debt distress. Most external state debt is owed to 
multilateral lenders, with the EU share accounting for 15. 8% of total. Sovereign credit ratings 
from Fitch (B+), S&P Global (BB-), and Moody's (B3) all carry stable outlooks.  
 
Vulnerability to External Shocks and Resilience Efforts 
Moldova faces substantial external vulnerabilities from structural current account 
imbalances, mainly through heavy reliance on volatile agricultural exports, and energy import 
dependence, and low lev els of preparedness against extreme weather events and climate 
change.  
 
Existing resilience-building efforts by the government include maintaining foreign reserves 
above five months of import coverage, implementing targeted energy support programs, and 
support measures for broadening the export base. Services export expansion provides some 
diversification while stable remittance flows continue supporting the economy. The banking 
sector's strong fundamentals provide financial stability.  
 
The international support framework  remains robust. The IMF ECF/EFF arrangement 
expired in October 2025,  with two final tranches withheld.  The government has requested a 
new non-disbursing program to be agreed by mid-2026. Moldova participated in the economic 
and financial dialogue with the EU in May 2025,  and submitted its third Economic Reform 
Programme (ERP) in January 2026, with the Commission assessment to be published on 1April 
2026.  
 
Policy Mix Assessment and Medium-term Stability 
Moldova remains committed to macroeconomic stability and economic reforms despite a 
difficult external environment, as confirmed in the October 2025 Enlargement Package. The 
macroeconomic policy mix has contributed to sustaining macroeconomic stability amidst the 
several recent crises, including the latest energy price increase, but public investment remains 
very low, and the country is vulnerable to external shocks. The May 2025 Economic  Policy 
Dialogue joint conclusions provided concrete reco mmendations for macroeconomic and 
financial stability, fiscal sustainability, and sound monetary policies. 
   
The fiscal policy contribution to medium -term stability presents mixed signals.  With 
structural fiscal deficits expected to remain elevated over the medium term, the public debt-to-
GDP ratio is projected to be on a rising trajectory. Although the public debt ratio is set to remain 
relatively low, it is subject to several risks , including  foreign exchange developments,

contingent liabilities stemming from government guarantees and potential reclassification of 
several indebted state-owned enterprises into the general government sector .  In addition, the 
consistent under-execution of capital expenditure suggests implement ation 
challenges. However, increasingly targeted support measures improve efficiency.  
 
Monetary policy has  generally supported stability despite external shocks . While energy 
price shocks tested inflation management, inflation returned towards the  target. Although 
inflation has returned to its target range of 5 ± 1.5%, inflationary risks remain elevated, driven 
by uncertainties related to the agricultural recover y, regulated prices, and the fiscal stimulus 
provided by the Growth Plan.  Banking sector stability persists with strong fundamentals. The 
slight NPL ratio increase for mortgage loans warrants monitoring but remains manageable. 
  
The policy mix appears broa dly coordinated  and generally conducive  to 
stability. Engagement with the IMF and EU provides external discipline and support, 
reinforcing domestic policy efforts and sending positive signals to the market. The key 
challenge is balancing growth support with deficit reduction while addressing structural 
vulnerabilities, particularly the large current account deficit.  
 
The policy mix is  generally conducive  to stability , but its effectiveness will  ultimately 
depend on consistent and high-quality implementation of structural reforms, diversification of 
the economic base, and strengthening institutional capacity, particularly in managing state -
owned enterprises and improving public expenditure  efficiency and increasing public 
investment implementation. 
 
Based on the above assessment, Moldova continues to fulfil  the general condition related to 
macro-financial stability.  
 
Public financial management (PFM) 
 
Moldova is implementing a comprehensive PFM Reform Strategy 2023 -2030, developed 
based on lessons learned from the previous strategy covering 2013-2022 and findings from the 
2022 PEFA assessment. The strategy addresses identified weaknesses in budgetary discipline, 
public investment management, capacity constraints, and external audit independe nce. 
Authorities remain committed to PFM reforms and implementation of the Public 
Administration Reform roadmap. However, sustained implementation require s strengthened 
human resources to ensure continued progress. 
 
Credibility and Relevance of PFM Reforms 
The reform strategy demonstrates strong credibility and relevance through its evidence -
based approach, drawing from multiple diagnostic assessments. The reforms appropriately 
target the identified weaknesses. Legislative proposals have been introduced to institutionalise 
spending reviews into the budget process, enhancing systematic evaluation of public 
expenditure. In November 2025, a comprehensive legislative package was introduced to amend 
the Law on Public Finance and Fiscal Responsibility, establishing mandatory budget reporting 
for extra-budgetary entities from 2027 onwards. This change will enhance budget transparency 
by incorporating additional units partially funded by the budget but not currently included in 
national public budget reporting. 
 
Main Achievements Since Previous Assessment

Moldova has demonstrated tangible progress through several key achievements . The 
Single Project pipeline approach to capital investments was consistently implemented in 2025. 
The spending reviews have been institutionalised through legal amendments introducing a legal 
requirement for systematic expenditure reviews. Budgetary transparency has improved through 
legal amendments reclassifying public institutions under self-management establishing a more 
rigorous accounting and reporting requirements. The q ualifications of public procurement 
specialists improved significantly through the introduction of a certification requirement. The 
establishment of public investment management focal points and subdivisions in line ministries 
strengthened the public investment management implementation, ensuring proper review and 
approval mechanisms for new public capital investment projects. Moldova has also taken 
important steps toward green and gender-responsive budgeting, demonstrating alignment with 
up-to-date PFM practices. 
 
The Court of Accounts reformed its internal governance . To address overlapping 
responsibilities, it  clarified the roles of the President, Members, and Head of Office Each 
member was assigned responsibility for one element of the Quality Management System, 
ensuring quality management became a collective leadership responsibility , rather than a 
separate technical function. 
 
Progress with budgetary reforms has been mixed. The draft Budget Law for 2026 achieved 
greater budget transparency by reducing the Genera l Actions  section of the state budget to 
below 30%, reflecting transfers to the Mandatory Medical Insurance Funds and State Social 
Insurance Budget under their respective ministries. The 2024 Report on the Execution of the 
State Budget was prepared in line  with the Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 
methodology, improving international comparability and transparency.  On the other hand, 
while prepared and introduced for approval at the ministerial level, the medium-term budgetary 
framework has not been adopted. The spending reviews for health and social sectors have not 
been endorsed by the government despite efforts taken to increase spending efficiency in both 
sectors. 
 
The digitalisation of the exchange of information for taxe s and customs declaration 
processes represent ed a major achievement , strengthening data protection and reducing 
fraud and corruption. Moldova joined the Inclusive Framework on Base erosion and profit 
shifting (BEPS) in May 2024 and participated in the agreement to address tax challenges from 
economic digitalisation, committing to implement the BEPS package to tackle tax avoidance 
and ensure a more transparent tax environment. 
 
Anti-Fraud Measures and Response to Corruption Cases 
Moldova has established a robust anti-fraud system. Fourteen cooperation agreements were 
signed between the anti -fraud unit (State Inspectorate of Financial Control) and network 
members, establishing clear regulatory frameworks and functional institutional mechanisms. 
These agreements ens ure clear allocation of responsibilities, standardi sation of reporting and 
intervention processes, and coherent information flow among relevant authorities. The network 
encompasses the State Chancellery, the Ministry of Finance, the Court of Accounts, the 
National Anti -Corruption Centr e, the central bank , prosecution authorities, and other key 
institutions. 
 
Capacity to Manage and Monitor Reforms 
The institutional capacity to manage and monitor reforms presents mixed results.  While 
the key institutions remain committed to reform implementation, significant capacity 
constraints persist. The shortage of qualified staff in internal audit units represents a critical

Transparency and oversight of the budget 
Between 2023 and 2025 the Government continued making publicly available budgetary 
documents. The latest available Open Budget Survey (OBS) for 2023 was published in 2024. 
In this publication, Budget Transparency in Moldova scored 81/100, ranking significantly better 
than its peers and improving the score on the 2019 results.  The budget related information is 
accessible online with all relevant laws and regulations. The Ministry of Finance has prepared 
and published annual Citizens’ Budgets based on the enacted budgets since 2015 . Budget 
related information, including the Medium -Term Budget Framework for 2025 -2027 is made 
publicly available in full, and it is easily accessible. The 2026 State Budget Law was approved 
on 29 December 2025and it is seen as complete and comprehensive. The budgets of the State 
Social Insurance Budget and the Mandatory Health Insurance Fund are also published and 
available.  
 
Budget oversight  continued to improve . The  Supreme Audit Institution - the Court of 
Accounts (CoA), extended its Development Strategy in October 2025 by two years until 2027. 
Additionally, a 202 5-2027 Audit Strategy was approve d in December 2024  with the 
involvement of the EU and SIGMA . The first international Peer Review was successfully 
organised in 2022, with a follow up performance report published in December 2025 to assess 
progress since the peer review. The report notes that t he CoA operates under well-developed 
legal and institutional arrangements. Functional independence of the CoA has improved, 
notably with regards to self-organisation without undue influence and with increased freedom 
on organisation structure. Furthermore, draft amendments to strengthen CoA’s constitutional 
and financial indepen dence have been prepared and are expected to be adopted in 2026. 
However, the lack of capacity hinders the work of the institution . The dedicated Public 
Financial Control Committee in the parliament is functioning  properly; however, it could 
benefit from f urther support and empowerment. Ethical and integrity safeguards were 
reinforced through adoption of the Code of Ethics and establishment of an Ethics Committee, 
aligning with ISSAI 130 and INTOSAI-P 20.

administrative procedures for issuing, validating, and updating permits,

without interfering with the technical standards or material requirements 
regulated by EU legislation in the respective fields. European directives do 
not set validity periods, renewal frequencies, lists of administrative 
documents required, or the duration of procedures, as these are left to the 
discretion of Member States, provided that the principles of proportionality 
and good re gulation are respected. The elimination of authorisations that

requirements. Consequently, the revisions strengthen the compliance of the 
national framework with EU principles on freedom of establishment, 
freedom to provide services, transparency, and predictability of regulation, 
without affecting harmonised technical requirements at European level.

a secure online environment for the interaction between the prosecution 
body; the Asset Recovery Agency and other state bodies linked to  the 
administration and recovery of criminal assets. Following the adoption of 
Government Order No. 68 of 31  December 2025, the Automated 
Information System “Register of Seized Criminal Assets” has had its 
management and user’s guide approved, an administrator appointed and a 
subdivision responsible for ensuring the functionality of the system 
designated.  
 
Access to the registry is granted to all law enforcement entities, namely the 
courts, prosecutor’s offices, bailiffs, State Tax Service , and the Asset 
Recovery Agency within the National Anticorruption Centre.  In addition, 
the system is interoperable with Population Register, the Register of Legal 
Entities, the Vehicle Register, and the Cadastral Register. 
The register generates and disseminates relevant statistics and reports on 
assets such as:  
• the estimated value of seized assets; 
• the number of requests formulated/ executed in other countries;  
• the responsible institutions tasked with entering the data; 
• history of the seized assets and supporting documents; 
• measures applied to the assets; 
• court rulings; 
• user activity and records of accesses to personal data 
• the value of goods reused for social purposes.
