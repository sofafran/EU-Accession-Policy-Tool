Assessment of the Conditions for Payments – Republic of Moldova 
 
 
1. EC assessment of the preconditions for Union support 
Effective democratic and rule of law mechanisms 
Moldova continues to implement justice sector reforms, in particular through the vetting 
process, to strengthen the integrity and independence of the judiciary.  
The functioning of democratic institutions is mostly satisfactory. Moldova shows a continued 
commitment to implement reforms, despite hybrid attacks from Russia and its proxies on 
democratic institutions and their attempts to undermine social cohesion in the country.  
Moldova has taken significant steps to structure the work of the government to make tangible 
progress on EU integration. As part of the EU accession process, Moldova has adopted the PAR 
and Rule of law Roadmaps that set out tangible reform plans. 
The Parliament has an oversight role over the executive. Accountability lines between the 
Parliament and other institutions are established, with the Parliament appointing state officials 
at the head of central administrative institutions and independent institutions . Further efforts 
are needed to increase its transparency, accountability, integrity, and overall effectiveness. This 
includes enhancing the track record of parliamentary oversight of independent institutions and 
ensuring the timely and comprehensive publication of parliamentary activities and decisions, 
as well as meanin gful public consultations on draft laws and accompanying documents. 
Moldova should continue to enhance its use of parliamentary oversight tools.  
 
Presidential elections and a referendum on EU accession were held in autumn 2024. Moldova's 
legal and institutional framework is conducive to the organisation of a democratic electoral 
process. Further efforts are needed to address challenges identified by OSCE/ODIHR and the 
Venice Commission and to align with EU legislation and standa rds. Efforts to strengthen the 
resilience of the electoral process against illicit financing and other forms of interference should 
adhere to European standards and best practices.   
 
Freedom of expression is generally ensured. Moldova has a pluralistic media environment and 
has progressed in the area of freedom of expression. The Audiovisual Media Service Code was 
amended in July 2023 to reduce the dominant audience share in order to reduce market 
concentration and promote a more diverse and balanced media  landscape. Amendments to the 
Code were adopted by the Parliament in July 2025 to further align with the EU acquis, ensuring 
the independence of the Audiovisual Council and public broadcaster, regulating online content, 
combating disinformation, and closing advertising market loopholes. Financial sustainability 
remains a significant challenge for independent media organisations. 
 
Respect for all human rights obligations 
 
The legislative and institutional framework on fundamental rights is largely in place and 
Moldova continues to meet its international obligations on human rights. Institutional capacity 
and implementation need to be strengthened to guarantee the full enjoyment of human rights in 
practice. Mechanisms to prevent and counter sexual violence are in place. Concerning

General conditions 
Macro-financial stability 
Macroeconomic and fiscal developments 
The economic recovery, which started in 2023, came to a halt in 2024, with real GDP growth 
of 0.1%, primarily due to a sharp drop in agricultural output following a summer drought. The 
contraction remained contained thanks to a rebound in private consumption underpinned by 
rising real wages as well as strong investment growth. According to the Commission Spring 
2025 Forecast, growth is set to gradually regain traction in 2025. 
Moldova’s current account balance continues to be structurally affected by its heavy reliance 
on volatile agricultural exports and vulnerability to fluctuations in prices of imported energy 
sources. By the end of 2024, the current account deficit  expanded to 16% of GDP, up from 
11.5% in 2023, reflecting falling exports to Ukraine (mostly minerals) and a sharp drop of 
agricultural exports. Remittances continued to play a significant role but fell in 2024 compared 
to 2023. Similarly, the inflows of Foreign Direct Investment as share of GDP declined in 2024.  
Moldova’s foreign currency reserves have stabilized, following significant volatility during 
2022, and increased further in 2023 -2024, reaching EUR 5.6 billion by end -2024. Thus, 
reserves account ed for 6.7 months of import of goods and services and 200% of the IMF  
Assessing Reserve Adequacy (ARA) metric end of 2023. 
Following a disinflationary period, inflation rebounded again in the second half of 2024 due to 
higher food prices, partly attributed  to the drought in summer 2024 and an increase in energy 
prices. Inflation peaked at 9.1% in January 2025 due to a sharp increase in electricity prices 
following the cut-off of Russian gas to the Transnistrian region. The National Bank of Moldova 
(NBM) shifted to monetary tightening, with two subsequent increases of the base rate up to 
6.5% by February 2025. The government responded swiftly to rising energy prices and 
increased energy support spending, supported by EU grants, to mitigate the impact on 
businesses and households. 
Moldova’s public debt-to-GDP ratio remains relatively low but increased by 3.5 pps. to 38.2% 
by end-2024. The fiscal deficit declined to 4% of GDP in 2024 supported by stronger revenues, 
under-execution of investments, and better -targeted energy support. The revised 2025 budget 
projects deficit to increase to 5.1% of GDP due to higher spending supported by the Reform 
and Growth Facility. The most recent Debt Sustainability Analysis by the IMF (December 
2024) confirms that Moldova is at  a low risk of external debt distress and at moderate risk of 
overall public debt distress and Moldova’s public debt is considered sustainable with current 
debt carrying capacity assessed as strong.  
The mainly foreign -owned banking sector remains stable a s capital adequacy, liquidity, and 
profitability remained strong in 2024.

Relations with international partners and macroeconomic policy 
The authorities are taking some steps in implementing reforms supported by their international 
partners. Moldova completed a macro-financial assistance operation of EUR 295 million with 
the final disbursement in December 2024, following the successful implementation of all policy 
conditions. On 17 December 2024, the IMF completed the sixth review under the ECF/EFF, 
and the second review under Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF) with Moldova. In 
response to concerns about the independence of the central bank following the abrupt dismissal 
of the Governor of the National Bank of Moldova, additional amendments to the central bank 
law have been drafted to strengthen the institutional set -up of the National Bank of Moldova, 
their adoption by Parliament remains pending. 
Moldova has partially implemented the recommendations from the 2024 joint conclusions and 
submitted its second Economic Reform Programme in January 2025 2. Following the 
Commission’s and ECB’s assessment3 and discussions at the Economic Policy Committee and 
the Economic and Financial Committee in April 2025, the 2025 joint conclusions were endorsed 
at the Economic and Financial Dialogue on 13 May 2025. They include concrete 
recommendations for Moldova to e nsure macroeconomic and financial stability, fiscal 
sustainability and to follow sound monetary policies4. 
The macroeconomic policy mix has contributed to sustaining macroeconomic stability amidst 
the several recent crises, including the latest energy pri ce increase, but public investment 
remains very low, and the country is vulnerable to external shocks. The key risks for 
macroeconomic stability include the impact of Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine, 
further energy price hikes given the country large energy import dependency, Moldova’s weak

Public financial management (PFM) 
The Government’s Public Financial Management (PFM) reform agenda is set out in the 2023 -
2030 Public Finance Management Reform Strategy which was adopted in spring 2023. The 
strategy was adopted following the 2021 Public Expenditure and Financial Accountabi lity 
(PEFA) assessment, published in July 2022, and in addition to PEFA was based on already 
available Public Investment Management Assessment (PIMA) and independent evaluations in 
Debt Management, Taxation, Procurement and Public Investment Management. The EU-MD 
PFM Policy Dialogue took place in Chisinau on 18 June 2025 ; operational conclusions are 
being finalised before being published on Ministry of Finance website. 
Summary Appraisal PFM progress and reforms 
The Ministry of Finance , its con stituent departments and other key institutions remain 
committed to PFM reforms. The country has demonstrated progress in PFM and Domestic 
Revenue Mobilization (DRM) through the following key reforms: 
1. Steps taken to enable future reforms in Public Investment Management through the 
development and establishment of a national procedure for the appraisal of public 
investment projects. The automated procedure through a web -based application, 
increasing effi ciency and transparency in allocating government resources to public 
investment, is operational and is being applied.  
2. Enhancement of debt management and development of domestic securities market, in 
particular with the issuance of the first ever 10-year bond in the domestic market. 
3. Improvements in public financial control and audit including enhancement of technical 
capacity in the Central Harmonisation Unit (CHU) and selected line ministries, 
including in the Ministry of Energy. 
4. Revision of the strategic framework for the Supreme Audit Institution and enhancement 
of capacities in financial and compliance audits; 
5. Enhancement of the parliamentary oversight of the budget through the establishment of 
a standing Public Accounts Committee (Committee for control of public finances) and 
public hearings of the reports of the Court of Accounts;  
6. Improved scoring in latest available Open Budget Index; 
7. Budget circular mandating institutionalisation of Gender responsive budgeting from 
2025. 
8. Anti-fraud system is established. 
 
Some of the important improvements in PFM include the regulation regarding public capital 
investment projects, as approved by Government Decision No. 684/2022. In addition, the 
Instruction on the evaluation of public capital investment projects was a pproved by Order of 
the Minister of Finance no. 69/2023, and the Working Group for public capital investments was

As reported in the EU Enlargement package country report 2024, Moldova has taken steps in 
amending legislation for procurement in the energy, water, transport and postal services ; 
National Programme for Development of the Public Procurement System was adopted, and the 
mandate of the Public Procurement Agency was extended. Moldova needs to further align its 
legislation with the EU acquis, especially by aligning the laws on concessions and public -
private partnerships. Moldova also needs to monitor low-value public procurement procedures 
more effectively. The efficiency and transparency of the public procurement system needs to 
be improved, including by designing and developing the new e-procurement system.  
Moldova has also taken steps in establishing its anti-fraud system. On 7 May 2025 a government 
decision was adopted setting up a national anti -fraud system. The system will comprise three 
main components: the National Anti -Fraud Network, the whistle -blowing system and the 
responsible anti-fraud authority (the State Inspect orate of Financial Control, which will also 
become a contact point for cooperation with the European Anti -Fraud Office - OLAF). The 
network will include institutions , such as the State Chancellery, the Ministry of Finance, the 
Court of Accounts, the Nation al Anti -Corruption Center, the National Bank, prosecution 
authorities and others. 
Moldova has taken steps to align legislation with the EU acquis on VAT and excise duties. 
Legislation on direct taxation has yet to be aligned with the EU acquis and international best 
practice. Moldova is to step up procedures to limit tax avoidance and i ncrease tax revenue, in 
particular by aligning legislation with the Anti -Tax Avoidance Directive (CFC rules). 
Significant efforts are needed to strengthen the administrative and operational capacity of the 
tax administration, including with the IT strategy on risk management and taxpayer compliance.  
In conclusion, the public finance management reform strategy is sufficiently relevant and 
credible, including on domestic revenue mobilisation, and the eligibility criterion is met. 
Domestic Revenue Mobilisation 
There is continuous commitment of the authorities to improve domestic revenue mobilisation. 
The general budget revenue of Moldova as percentage of GDP (30%) remains lower than in its 
peers, indicating further revenue mobilisation potential. The IMF there fore expects the tax 
revenue to increase by more than 150% in nominal terms between 2022 and 2027. This growth 
is expected to be mainly due to an increase in domestic revenue collection, as in coming years 
official transfers (grants) are planned to decrease. The share of indirect taxes in total tax revenue 
is however expected to remain very high at 66-69%.  
The new Customs Code was enacted by the Parliament on 24th of August 2021 5 and entered 
into force from 1 January 2024. It was developed with considerable EU support.  
The new Code will substantially improve the quality of services provided by the Customs 
Service to business entities. More specifically:

Reforms to the tax administration are ongoing. International practices are in place in some areas, 
such as taxpayers assistance, electronic services. In other areas, such as the risk -based 
compliance management, Moldova made explicit reform commitments under the recently 
adopted Reform Agenda. Identified weaknesses include unreliable taxpayers register, limited 
risk based-focus for the audit, value -added tax (VAT) refund programs, outdated IT systems 
etc. Frequent changes in tax policy and tax rates are still common.  
The VAT approximation road map and related recommendations providing for gradual 
alignment of the VAT regulation with the EU VAT Directive is a priority for the M inistry of 
Finance in 2025. While smuggling or  non-declaration of goods and undervaluation schemes 
still exist and affect state revenue as well as competition and fair trade the authorities are taking 
steps to curb these activities.  
In May 2024, Moldova joined the Inclusive Framework on BEPS and part icipated in the 
agreement to address the tax challenges arising from the digitalisation of the economy. Moldova 
will participate in the implementation of the  BEPS package to tackle tax avoidance, improve 
the coherence of international tax rules and ensure a more transparent tax environment6. 
Based on the analysis, it is concluded that there is satisfactory progress in the 
implementation of reforms to improve the public finance management, including 
domestic revenue mobilisation and continued relevance and credibility of the reform 
programme.

Baseline  Existing draft law non-harmonised with EU VAT law.

=== PAGE 18 ===

Description
  
Partial harmonization of Moldovan VAT legislation with those of the EU is necessary 
to eliminate, to the maximum extent possible, factors which may distort conditions of 
competition in the market, and which may lead to legal uncertainty. In particular, 
integration into SDAC, SIDC and EU Balancing platforms requires a high level of 
harmonization of applicable regimes of VAT in order to prevent non taxation, double 
taxation, tax evasion and tax fraud. This harmonized VAT reverse taxation regime is 
also envisaged by the EU-Moldova Association Agreement as one of the preconditions 
to create a level playing field between all mark et participants in the Moldovan 
electricity and gas market. 
 
Deadline of 
the step  
December 2025 
Actual 
results  
On 13 June 2025, the Parliament of Moldova adopted in the second reading draft law 
amending certain normative acts (Fiscal Code no. 1163/1997 and Law no. 1417/1997 
for the implementation of Title III of the Fiscal Code). The l aw includes the 
amendment to Title IV of the Fiscal Code, no. 1163/1997.    
It transposes Articles 38, 39, 44, Article 59 letter (h), Article 143 paragraph (1) letter 
(b), Article 183, 195, Article 199a paragraph (1) letter (e), Article 207 and Article 226 
paragraph (11a) of Council Directive 2006/112/EC on the common system of value 
added tax, published in the Official Journal of the European Union L 347/1 of 11 
December 2006. 
This implies amendments of Articles 93, 96, 101, 103, 104, 110, 111, 112, 115 and 
117 of the Fiscal Code No. 1163/1997.  As well as amendment of Article 4 of Law 
No. 1417/1997 for the implementation of Title III of the Fiscal Code. 
The amendment achieves the reform’s goal of bringing Moldovan VAT rules partly 
into line (given that Moldova has incorporated the EU VAT framework solely for the 
energy sector, even though it re mains committed to transposing the Directive in full) 
with the EU framework. By aligning this segment, the reform removes the risk of 
double taxation, non-taxation or inconsistent treatment across borders and paves the 
way for Moldova’s seamless participation in the Single Day-Ahead Coupling (SDAC), 
Single Intraday Coupling (SIDC), EU balancing platforms, as well as country’s 
integration into the regional gas markets. 
Thelaw establishes new place -of-supply rules that distinguish between wholesale 
transactions and final consumption. For gas and electricity traded before the final stage 
of consumption, the place of supply is deemed to be the location of the customer’s 
establishment; for deliveries to end-users, it is the place where the goods are actually 
used and consumed. This change mirrors EU practice and ensures that cross -border 
business-to-business trades are taxed once, in the customer’s jurisdiction, while retail 
customers are taxed where they take delivery.

Equally important is the introduction of a r everse-charge mechanism for VAT on 
business-to-business supplies. Under this mechanism, the obligation to account for 
VAT shifts from the supplier to the purchaser, eliminating cash -flow hurdles for 
traders and closing loopholes exploited in “carousel” fra ud schemes —an essential 
safeguard when markets are coupled and volumes increase. The draft law also 
incorporates the EU concepts of “collected VAT” and “deductible VAT”, providing 
operators with clear rules for output tax and input tax recovery and creatin g the 
predictable fiscal environment required for integrated energy trading. 
By transposing these elements of EU law, Moldova removes a key barrier to cross -
border transactions, fosters legal certainty for market participants and fulfils the 
Association Ag reement’s and Reform Agenda’s objective of establishing a level 
playing field in the electricity and gas sectors. The amendments will help prevent non-
taxation and double taxation, facilitate competitive trading across borders and 
underpin the country’s cr edibility as it deepens its participation in regional energy 
markets.

Actual results  The National Agency for Energy Regulation, based on the Decision No. 248 of 23-

The transparent and non -discriminative selection procedure was launched on 17 
April 2025.  Out of 33 license holders for the activity of supplying natural gas, only 
two suppliers (LLC "Transautogaz" and JSC "Energocom") expressed interest in

On 14 May 2025, the Commission for the selection of the public service provider(s)

ratio between borrowed capital and equity does not constitute at least 50% of own 
sources.  The Selection Committee decided to return the applica tion and not admit 
the respective supplier to the evaluation stage. 
In this context, due to the insufficient number of applications to guarantee a 
minimum competition, the procedure for selecting the natural gas supplier/suppliers 
to ensure the execution of the public service obligations was terminated.  
In these circumstances, according to Article 89 para. (21) and Article 90 para. (21) 
of Law no. 108/2016, the Agency shall designate a supplier that will supply natural 
gas to certain categories of final consumers at the established quality parameters, at 
regulated, transparent, non-discriminatory and easily comparable prices by decision.   
Gas supplier should meet at least the following conditions: 
a) actively operates on the natural gas market in the Republic of Moldova; 
b) has one of the highest cumulative turnovers of natural gas supply within 
the natural gas market of the Republic of Moldova in the last calendar year 
preceding that of a designation procedure;
