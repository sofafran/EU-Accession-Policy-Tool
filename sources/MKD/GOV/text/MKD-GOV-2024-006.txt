1. FOREWORD
This document represents the third strategic framework for the protection of personal data in 
the Republic of North Macedonia. It has been created to address the needs and perspective of 
stakeholders, align with the governing legislation, evaluate the current state of personal data 
protection, and anticipate future challenges in this domain. The strategy adopts a comprehensive 
and modern approach to privacy and personal data protection, covering the period of 2025-2030. 
In an era where the value and volume of data are increasing exponentially, privacy and personal 
data protection have become critical topics and potential sources of competitive advantage. 
The Personal Data Protection Agency (PDPA) must operate within the context of continuous 
digitalization, identifying emerging trends in data processing and addressing the risks they pose 
to data privacy.
The Strategy for the Implementation of the Right to Personal Data Protection 2025-2030 was 
developed with expert support from the European Commission. Consultations were conducted 
with representatives from the public and private sectors, as well as citizens› associations. This 
collaboration significantly benefited the PDPA, given that privacy and personal data protection 
transcend all areas of public and private life and extend beyond the borders of the Republic 
of North Macedonia. To gather additional insights, a workshop organized with stakeholders, 
including representatives from municipalities, banks, telecommunication operators, law firms, 
state institutions, trading companies, etc. These discussions focused on global trends and local 
challenges affecting personal data protection in the Republic of North Macedonia. The information 
collected during these consultations and workshops has been integrated into the development of 
the PDPA’s new strategic goals, mission, vision, and values.
In this context, it is important to emphasize that the awareness of controllers and citizens 
regarding personal data protection has significantly increased through the implementation of 
the Law on Personal Data Protection (LPDP). This law introduced comprehensive and preventive 
implementation mechanisms that align with the standards of the General Data Protection 
Regulation (GDPR). To this end, the contribution of stakeholders has been invaluable in shaping 
the Strategy, which aims to reflect the impact of the law on the lives of citizens and controllers 
in the Republic of North Macedonia. It seeks to promote improved outcomes in both personal and 
professional domains, without compromising the interests of either party.
It should also be noted that the Strategy is a dynamic document. If necessary, it can be revised 
or adapted to address emerging challenges arising from technological advancements, changes in 
legislation, or other factors that may influence its goals, assumptions, resources, and stakeholders.

3. STRATEGIC GOALS
In order to fulfil the Mission and Vision, the following four strategic goals have been identified as 
crucial for implementation. These goals focus on priority areas of action where key challenges in 
personal data protection will be addressed:
• Strategic goal 1:  Ensure the overall autonomy and independence of the PDPA.
• Strategic goal 2:  Harmonize legislation.
• Strategic goal 3:  Foster a continuous increase in personal data protection culture.
• Strategic goal 4:  Implement accountability tools.
Below, we provide a more detailed explanation of these goals and the corresponding activities. 
The Annex will outline the actions required to achieve these goals, including an action plan with 
indicators to measure results.
3.1. Strategic goal 1 – Ensure the overall autonomy and independence of the PDPA 
Desired outcome: Our primary objective is to ensure that PDPA has all the necessary resources 
to perform its functions and powers efficiently and effectively, emphasizing its independent, 
autonomous, and impartial functioning as outlined in the GDPR.
Adoption of the Law Amending and Supplementing the Law on Personal Data Protection in order to 
ensure the autonomy and independence of the PDPA in managing finances and human resources
The PDPA has prepared a Draft Law for Amending and Supplementing the Law on Personal Data 
Protection because the Law. This law has not fully implemented the provisions necessary for the PDPA 
to have the resources needed to perform its functions and powers effectively. These amendments 
will guarantee the PDPA’s complete autonomy and independence, particularly in managing both 
human and financial resources without influence (opinions and approvals) from other institutions.
Budget constraints and severely limited staff may hinder the PDPA›s ability to carry out its tasks 
and powers. The adoption of the prepared Draft Law will enable creation of conditions to provide 
sufficient funds for the efficient and effective performance of PDPA’s tasks and powers, ensuring its 
independence and autonomy in managing these allocated funds.

Strengthening the capacities of the PDPA
The PDPA must continuously organize training programs to enhance the skills of its employees. 
Given the increasing demands in areas requiring specialized knowledge, PDPA staff should be 
equipped to understand and oversee new technologies (e.g. implementation of AI-based systems). 
The PDPA should be recognized as an institution that fosters self-development and education of its 
employees – an environment that challenges staff to improve their skills and where they are highly 
valued and respected.
A key element in achieving PDPA’s complete independence is the autonomous recruitment of new 
employees with the necessary qualifications, particularly those with IT expertise. Additionally, 
activating the promotion process of existing employees based in planned work positions is also 
essential.
Amendment of Sectoral Legislation on Misdemeanour Sanctions
According to the GDPR, misdemeanour sanctions should be dissuasive, effective, and proportionate. 
Therefore, it is necessary to amend the sectoral legislation related to misdemeanours in order to 
increase fines for natural persons (both responsible and official persons), and to adjust the provisions 
concerning the statute of limitations for misdemeanour prosecution.
To achieve this goal, the PDPA will undertake the following actions:
• Initiating and promoting the need for adoption of the Law for Amending and Supplementing the Law 
on Personal Data Protection, along with its full implementation
• Striving for independent and autonomous management of the PDPA’s allocated budget funds.
• Providing education to PDPA employees to ensure they have up-to-date knowledge and skills.
• Enhancing the PDPA›s technological foresight to ensure the Agency is equipped and prepared to answer 
rapidly evolving technologies.
• Ensuring the recruitment of new staff at the PDPA.
• Continuously promoting the existing PDPA staff. 
• Initiating and advocating for amendments and additions to the sectoral legislation on misdemeanours, 
and ensuring their full implementation. 
• Applying dissuasive, effective, and proportionate misdemeanour sanctions to encourage behaviour 
change and foster an improved culture of compliance with personal data protection by controllers and 
processors.

3.2.  Strategic goal 2 – Harmonize legislation
Desired outcome: Achieving compliance with EU legislation and the necessary level of 
harmonization of sectoral legislation with the LPDP .
The PDPA has adopted the Methodology for Harmonization of Sectoral Legislation with the LPDP, 
which provides guidelines regulating the actions of ministries in harmonizing departmental legislation. 
This includes revising existing laws and assessing the impact of new laws on personal data protection. 
However, the obligation established by the LPDP to align other laws relating to the processing of 
personal data with its provisions has not yet been fully met. Further efforts are needed to ensure 
complete harmonization with EU legislation, including full transposition of the GDPR and Directive (EU) 
2016/680 of the European Parliament and the Council of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural 
persons regarding processing of personal data by competent authorities for purposes related to the 
prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of criminal offences or the execution of criminal 
penalties, and the free movement of such data. This directive also repeals Council Framework Decision 
2008/977/JHA (the EU Law Enforcement Directive).
Adoption of a Law Transposing the EU Law Enforcement Directive 
The lack of national legislation to transpose the EU Law Enforcement Directive presents a significant issue 
that must be addressed promptly. In the coming period, it is essential to pass the law that will transpose 
the EU Law Enforcement Directive to complete the normative alignment with EU law in this area.
Harmonization of National Laws with EU Legislation in the Area of Personal Data Protection
Laws and regulations governing the processing of personal data must comply with the provisions of 
the LPDP and EU legislation. In that context, the PDPA provides guidelines to ministries on how to 
conduct impact assessments of laws related to data protection, ensuring they address and elaborate 
on issues concerning the processing of personal data. 
As part of aligning with EU legislation, it is essential 00to harmonize the laws regarding the transfer 
of personal data. This includes simplifying processes for transferring personal data by proposing new 
measures to facilitate the transfer of personal data to third countries and international organizations, 
thereby simplifying the processes for controllers and processors.
Harmonization of Legislation in the Context of Digitization Processes
As part of the EU›s broader digital strategy, several acts have been adopted or are in the process 
of adoption, including the EU e-Privacy Directive, AI Act, Data Governance Act, Data Act, European 
Health Data Space Regulation, Digital Services Act, Digital Markets Act, Network and Information 
Security Directive. These acts must be reflected in national legislation to fully implement the new 
standards and values, particularly those related to personal data. Within these frameworks, activities 
must be undertaken to implement the Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human 
Rights, Democracy, and the Rule of Law of the Council of Europe, once ratified by the Republic of 
North Macedonia.

3.3.  Strategic goal 3 – 
         Foster a continuous increase in personal data protection culture
Desired outcome: Controllers from both the public and private sector are well-informed and 
accountable for their activities related to the protection of personal data, while citizens have a 
better understanding of their rights regarding personal data protection, know-how to exercise 
those rights, and are aware of when to turn to the PDPA for assistance.
The PDPA is recognized as a promoter and protector of fundamental rights and freedoms in the 
processing of personal data. However, despite the ongoing efforts of the PDPA and its partners to 
organize and implement successful awareness programs and actions, there remains is significant 
need to raise and educate citizens’ awareness about the importance of personal data protection. At 
the same time, there is a need to improve the general culture around the processing of personal data 
by controllers, processors and users. The PDPA also continues to foster international cooperation 
with data protection authorities, contributing to the improvement of data protection culture in line 
with European values and standards.

Data Protection Trainings

their implementation, the PDPA aims to educate all stakeholders on the measures they can take to 
better protect personal data in their daily operations.

3.4.  Strategic goal 4 – Implement Accountability Tools
Desired outcome: Controllers should design systems for processing personal data in accordance 
with the principle of data protection by design and by default, demonstrating and proving 
compliance with personal data protection regulations.
As technology evolves, the volume of processed data grows rapidly, increasing the risk associated 
with personal data processing. The PDPA will support controllers and processors in adhering to the 
fundamental principles and requirements of the LPDP in their personal data processing activities and 
business practices. By offering a wide range of techniques and tools to effectively implement personal 
data protection principles and protect  individual rights, controllers and processors will be encouraged 
to implement a system that is compliant with LPDP provisions.

Improving Accountability Tools to Promote Compliance Among Controllers 
Accountability tools for data protection are essential for controllers, as they ensure compliance 
with LPDP. Tools such as data protection by design and default, data protection impact assessment, 
the appointment of a DPO, and binding corporate rules help controllers actively manage the risks 
associated with personal data processing. This encourages trust among citizens, reduces risks and 
promotes a culture of personal data protection with the controllers. While accountability tools 
help controllers comply with data protection regulations, they also enhance their personal data 
management practices.
Certification and Codes of Conduct
Certification of personal data processing systems is one of the most effective tools for ensuring 
compliance of controllers and processors with personal data protection regulations. This proactive 
approach minimizes number of risks for controllers and processors while supporting the efficient use 
of PDPA’s resources. In many countries, key sectors covering a number of controllers and processors 
have developed codes of conduct to set standards in the field of personal data protection and 
privacy. This is a form of self-regulation, overseen and approved by the PDPA, to ensure compliance 
of controllers and processors, and provide greater protection for data subjects.
Networking of DPOs
DPOs currently lack sufficient influence over personal data protection issues, and their indepen-
dence is not adequately guaranteed. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen their position and 
role among the controllers through their continuous training, alongside efforts to raise awareness 
among management about the importance of compliance with personal data protection regulations. 
For the purposes of cooperation and exchange of experiences between DPOs, they should utilize the 
opportunities of the DPO network. Given the importance of mutual interaction and especially the 
importance of sharing best practices and advice, the network is a useful tool for enhancing DPO’s 
access to information and improving their efficiency. It is also advisable to establish sector-special 
groups within the network for giving advice and exchanging views on shared issues and challenges. 
DPOs should follow personal data protection training and use self-improvement tools.
Activities to Improve the Position of DPOs
The fact is that DPOs often operate in isolation within controllers and their role is regularly 
misunderstood. In order to strengthen their independence in the workplace, the PDPA should carry 
out supervisory activities to assess their position and the effectiveness in fulfilling their legally 
established duties. These supervisory activities should evaluate whether the DPO at the controller is 
appropriately and timely involved in all issues related to personal data protection, whether support 
and resources are provided by the controller for DPOs to perform their duties, whether the controller 
works to maintain his/her her professional knowledge etc.

5. MONITORING AND ASSESSING 
   STRATEGY PROGRESS
One of the success factors of strategy management is the regular measurement/monitoring of 
progress towards the implementation of goals and objectives. 
To track this progress, a set of indicators has been defined, linked to the hierarchy of strategic goals 
and activities:
• Key performance indicators at the level of strategic goals and objectives
• Performance or operational indicators and success criteria at the level of actions
The table summarizing the strategic goals also defines the tasks and activities linked to these 
indicators/success factors. The initial and target value for the indicators will be determined once 
the definition and data sources of the indicators are finalized. Indicator values will be measured 
annually, and target values will be updated as needed based on the results.
Additionally, the PDPA will establish mechanisms for cooperation with key institutions in monitoring 
strategy progress, especially the Ministry of Justice, the Ministry of Public Administration, the 
Ministry of Finance, and the Ministry of Digital Transformation. This cooperation is crucial, bearing 
in mind that the achievement of strategic goals depends not only on the PDPA, but also on the 
strategic priorities of these institutions.
5.1.  Proposed monitoring approach for Strategy implementation 
The development of the Strategy for implementing the right to personal data protection in the 
Republic of North Macedonia (2025-2030) is a systematic work, based on the definition of the 
Strategy’s Mission and culminating in the creation of an Action Plan to achieve the vision. By imple-
menting the elements of the Action Plan, the activities will be fulfilled, and the cumulative outcome 
of these activities will lead to the achievement of the strategic goals. In turn, this will ensure that 
the vision is realized and the mission of the strategy is fulfilled.
To efficiently monitor the implementation of the Strategy, the PDPA must assess whether the activ-
ities have been carried out and whether the strategic goals have been achieved. For the action level, 
simple, easily measurable KPIs should be defined to track the actual progress towards each goal.
The next chapter outlines the KPI system and proposes a method for evaluating the results.

5.2. Proposed system of KPIs
When developing the Strategy, a set of KPIs was defined to measure the level of implementation 
of strategic goals and activities. The table below provides a definition, description, and method of 
measurement for each KPI.
• Activities are considered “fulfilled” if the associated KPI meets the target value, demonstrating the required 
development through a percentage %, number or Yes/No indicator.
• Strategic goals are considered “fulfilled” if all tasks associated with them are completed.
There are two categories of KPI:
• Quantitative KPIs: These reflect an absolute figure or percentage of completion relative to the total number of 
relevant activities. It is crucial that these KPIs are based on objective calculations of factual data. Therefore, 
these KPIs are linked to databases/registers already available at the PDPA.
• Event/happening based KPIs (Yes/No type): These measure the success of a set of actions aimed at a 
particular issue (such as the decision to adopt the EU Law Enforcement Directive).
