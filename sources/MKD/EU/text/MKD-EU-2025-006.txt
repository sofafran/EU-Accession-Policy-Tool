PREAMBLE  
After the first Intergovernmental Conference on accession negotiations with North Macedonia in July 
2022, the Commission immediately launched the screening process.  
The screening is based on the enhanced enlargement methodology, which was adopte d in 2020 and 
organises the negotiating chapters in thematic clusters in order to inject dynamism into the negotiating 
process and to foster cross -fertilisation of efforts beyond individual chapters. These thematic clusters 
bring together the chapters / areas according to broader themes and will allow a stronger focus on core 
sectors. Within each individual cluster, screening is carried out through meetings on a chapter -by-
chapter basis. The substance of those clusters and chapters, as set out in Annex II o f the Negotiating 
Framework, mirror the requirements for membership stemming from the Copenhagen criteria. 
This report provides an assessment of where North Macedonia stands in terms of alignment and capacity 
to apply the EU acquis in Cluster 5. Since the negotiating Framework also refers to the possibility of 
accelerated integration, thus where appropriate, North Macedonia has requested participation in certain 
areas already before accession. This report provides the Commission’s opinion and answer to thes e 
requests. 
Regional cooperation and good neighbourly relations remain essential elements of the Stabilisation and 
Association process.  
It is important to continue the dialogue with civil society and other stakeholders, with the aim of 
ensuring the support of citizens for the EU accession process.  North Macedonia is expected to 
strategically communicate the benefits and obligations of the accession process to its public.

A) INTRODUCTION 
The enhanced enlargement methodology 1, as reflected in the Negotiating Framework with North 
Macedonia, puts the negotiating chapters of the EU acquis into clusters. The Resources, agriculture 
and cohesion cluster encompasses the legislation on agriculture and rural development, fisheries and 
aquaculture, food safety, veterinary and phytosanitary policy, as well as regional policy and 
coordination of structural instruments, and financial and budgetary provisions . This cluster includes 
five chapters of the EU acquis:  
⎯ Under Chapter 11 , agriculture and rural development aiming to  ensure food security , 
safeguard farmers’ incomes, maintain vibrant rural areas, while helping to tackle climate 
change and environmental challenges.  
⎯ Under Chapter 12, food safety, veterinary, and phytosanitary policies aiming to ensure food 
and feed safety as well as an high level of consumer’s interests protection. The chapter also 
covers animal health, welfare, and the safety of food of animal origin.  
⎯ Under Chapter 13, fisheries and aquaculture manages the Common Fisheries Policy aiming 
to ensure long-term sustainability for fisheries and aquaculture, the availability of food supplies 
and a fair standard of living for fisheries and aquaculture communities. 
⎯ Under Chapter 22, regional policy and coordination of structural instrument s is the EU’s 
main tool for reducing regional disparities and investing in sustainable and inclusive socio -
economic growth. This requires appropriate administrative capacity on programme and project 
levels as well as the fulfilment of other EU acquis elements such as environmental or public 
procurement legislation. 
⎯ Under Chapter 33, financial and budgetary provisions govern the funding of the EU budget, 
requiring appropriate administrative capacity to adequately coordinate and manage its own 
resources.  
The chapters under the Resources, Agriculture and Cohesion cluster should not be seen in isolation, 
as several have commonalities and so  interact with each other  to be mutually reinforcing. For 
example, regional policy and coordination of structural instrument s is closely related to Green 
agenda and sustainable connectivity policies. 
This screening report is structured according to the respective areas and chapters  of the cluster. 
Each area/chapter starts with a short overview of the main content of the EU acquis / European 
standards. Each thematic area within a chapter then first summarises the country’s presentations 
followed by the outcome of the expert discussions of the bilateral screening meeting, ended with the 
Commission’s assessment.  
In the last section, based on its findings the Commission draws recommendations for the next steps 
in the accession negotiations process related to the present Cluster. 
North Macedonia stated that it accepts the EU acquis in the chapters of Cluster 5. It reserved 
however the right to request for transitional arrangements in certain limited areas of the acquis 
in this Cluster at a later stage in the negotiations.

B) CHAPTER 11 – AGRICULTURE AND RURAL 
DEVELOPMENT 
The acquis on agriculture and rural development covers a large number of binding rules, many of which 
are directly applicable. Their proper application and their effective enforcement by an efficient public 
administration are essential for the functioning of the common agricultural policy (CAP). This requires 
the setting up of a paying agency and management and control systems such as the integrat ed 
administration and control system (IACS), and the capacity to implement rural development actions. 
Member States must be able to apply EU legislation on direct support schemes and to implement the 
common market organisation for various agricultural prod ucts. The introduction of the CAP Strategic 
Plans for the implementation period 2023-2027 gives more flexibility to the Member States in adapting 
measures to local conditions while delivering on overall CAP objectives. 
The implementation, management and co ntrol of the CAP require the creation, modification and/or 
reinforcement of appropriate administrative structures. In some cases, the acquis sets out detailed 
specifications for the required administrative structures. In others the acquis simply uses terms such as 
the “competent authority” to refer to the administrative structure that is needed. This means that it is 
left to each Member State to decide which institution is responsible for the effective implementation of 
the acquis. However, the functions that EU Member States must carry out - through the administrative 
structures they establish - are clearly specified in the acquis.  
North Macedonia intends to align and implement the acquis by the date of accession and reserves the 
right to request any transitional periods. North Macedonia stated that it needs to further strengthen the 
administrative capacity to implement the acquis on agriculture and rural development. North 
Macedonia demonstrated clear knowledge of the acquis covered by this chapter, is fully aware of the 
challenges ahead to ensure full implementation of the acquis upon accession and reported that its new 
Law on agriculture and rural development under preparation aims for a lignment with the current 
common agricultural policy. Its adoption is planned for 2025.  
I. HORIZONTAL ISSUES 
I.A. GENERAL ISSUES  
a. Country presentation  
In 2022, North Macedonia had 1.256 million hectares (ha) of agricultural land (arable land, vineyards, 
orchard, grassland) representing 49% of the total territory , with forestry making up a further 42%.  
Agriculture accounted for 13.7% of employment in 2019 and represented 7.2% of the country’s GDP 
in 2022. There were 178 125 agricultural holdings in 2016. The average size of a holding was 1.8 ha. 
North Macedonia is characterised by small subsistence and family farming w ith low productivity 
farming occupying 85% of the agricultural land while larger agricultural companies average farm size 
of 197 ha, in total 15% of the agricultural land. North Macedonia is a net importer of food products. 
Crop production prevails with 75% (2018), comprising cereals, industrial crops, forage crops, 
vegetables, potatoes, fruit, and wine. The high share results from the value of vegetable and production 
of perennial crops (fruits and wine grape). The larger agricultural companies specialise mainly in 
livestock breeding, cereals, and industrial crops, and therefore are concentrated in the grain producing 
regions Ovce Pole and Pelagonija. North Macedonia’s production about matches consumption for sheep 
and pigmeat. The main export products are tobacco, wine and vegetables.  
North Macedonia stated that its agricultural policy is based on the 2010 Law on Agriculture and Rural 
Development. This law sets out the objectives of agriculture policy and provides the general framework 
for the development of and support to agriculture and rural areas. The National Strategy for Agriculture 
and Rural Development 2021 -2027 (NSARD) defines t he national policy for improving agricultural 
competitiveness, economic sustainability and increased farms incomes, climate resilience, and 
development of rural areas . Its strategic goals are aligned with the strategic objectives of the EU

I.B. FINANCING OF THE AGRICULTURAL EXPENDITURE  
a. Country presentation  
The agricultural policy of North Macedonia is financed from the annual state budget with the Agency 
for Financial Support in Agriculture and Rural Development, submitting an annual budget plan to the 
Ministry of Finance. For 2023 the agricultural budget amounted to approximately EUR 130 million, of 
which EUR 104 mi llion was for direct support and some EUR 20 million for rural development. In 
2022 and 2023 an intervention fund of around EUR 10 million (2022) and EUR 5 million (2023) was 
also set up to support farmers cope with the hardships due to COVID-19.  
b. Commission assessment 
Financing of North Macedonia’s agricultural expenditure is not aligned with the EU acquis. The 
financing of agricultural expenditure in North Macedonia will have to be fully aligned to the EU acquis 
and the delivery model of the CAP.  
I.C. AGRICULTURE SUPPORT SCHEMES 
a. Country presentation  
According to North Macedonia, direct support is based on the Law on agriculture and rural development 
of 2010, the National programme for the development of agriculture and rural development 2023-2027, 
the Annual programme for financial support in agriculture  and the yearly Decree on criteria for direct 
payments. All direct payments are coupled to production. They are paid per hectare of farmed arable 
land and additional payments are granted e.g. for us e of certified seeds, for selling field crops to 
processing facilities, for vegetable production and for fruit crop production. Coupled payments are paid 
for cows, sheep, goats, pigs, chicken and bees.  Recipients of direct support need to respect some 
conditionality partially aligned with the acquis and include animal welfare, good agricultural practice 
and environmental protection. Additional payments are granted to young farmers, and organic 
production as a percentage increase to the basic direct payments . Payments coupled to production are 
paid for tobacco, milk, meat, and fruit and vegetables. P ayments are also made linked to specific 
agricultural practices, such as using certified seeds or livestock insemination techniques and for honey 
laboratory analysis. The Agency for Financial Support in Agriculture and Rural Development 
determines whether all eligibility criteria for direct payments are met through an integrated 
administration and control system. North Macedonia indicated as future plans the alignment with the 
EU acquis and a new Law on agriculture and rural development. 
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia has a low level of alignment with the acquis on agriculture support schemes . The 
support measures, which are numerous, are coupled to production and subject to limited conditionality. 
The support measures need to be fully aligned with the EU acquis, decoupling them from production 
and fully introducing the system for area based payments. Under the CAP, voluntary coupled support 
is limited to certain sectors and types of production.

I.D. RURAL DEVELOPMENT SUPPORT  
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia legislative framework on rural development consist s of the Law on Agriculture and 
Rural Development (2010), the National Strategy for Agriculture and Rural Development 2021 -2027, 
the National Program me for Agriculture and Rural Development 2021 -2027 and the Program me for 
financial support of Rural Development. The main aims of the Strateg y for Agriculture and Rural 
Development 2021-2027 are the improvement of the agri -food sector competitiveness and income, 
environmental protection and climate change mitigation and to ensure the sustainable development of 
rural areas. The actions are funded from the national budget, EU IPA and IPARD programmes and other 
donors’ funds.  
North Macedonia has been implementing programmes under the Instrument for Pre -Accession 
Assistance for Rural Development (IPARD)  since 2009. The following measures are implemented - 
Investments in physical assets  of agricultural holdings , Investments in physical assets concerning 
processing and marketing of agriculture and fishery products, F arm diversification and business 
development, and technical assistance. 
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia’s policy on rural development is  partially aligned with the EU acquis. It will need 
to be further harmonised in particular as regards the selection of support measures, programming, 
monitoring, controlling and evaluati on capacities. The environmental and climate orientation of the 
policy should be strengthened in line with the EU objectives . The rural development expenditure will 
have to be adapted to the new delivery model of the CAP and the EU acquis. 
I.E. MANAGEMENT AND MONITORING OF AGRICULTURE EXPENDITURE 
a. Country presentation  
The Agency for Financial Support in A griculture and Rural Development (AFSARD) is the 
implementing body for national support schemes in agriculture and rural development . It is also 
entrusted with budget implementation tasks under the IPARD programme s. It is responsible for all 
elements of the Integrated Administrative and Control System ( IACS), except the Land Parcel 
Identification System (LPIS) and the farmers’ register . The IACS is used to implement payments to 
farmers. Procedures are certified according to the quality management system ISO 9001/2015. The 
AFSARD adopts an annual plan for the prevention of corruption. North Macedonia explained t hat it 
plans the full harmonisation of the AFSARD systems and procedures with the EU acquis. 
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia is partially aligned with the EU acquis in the area of management and monitoring 
of agriculture expenditure  and in establishing the necessary administrative structures. The AFSARD 
has elements in place to ensure the quality of its work and prevent corruption. It will need to develop 
further to demonstrate its capacity to sustainably deliver on the future implementation of the CAP. 
Resources need to be ensured to improve and develop capacity building to respond to further 
harmonisation efforts of North Macedonia. 
I.F. INTEGRATED ADMINISTRATION AND CONTROL SYSTEM (IACS) 
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia has an Integrated Administration and Control System (IACS) in line with the acquis. 
The system has the following elements: Computerised database for the applications for direct payments, 
a Land Parcel Identification System (LPIS), a system for identification and  registration of the right to 
direct payments, an animal identification and registration system, a system for applications for direct 
payment, a control and sanctions system and a system for checking agricultural holdings’ identity as 
well as a register of purchasers of agricultural products. Area-based payments are determined based on

I.H. STATE AIDS  
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia indicated that State aid in agriculture is granted as additional support to the 
agricultural funding programmes - direct payments and rural development. North Macedonia indicated 
that it applies a number of aid schemes through the agricultural budget including for insurance premia, 
fuel purchase, investments in modernisation, compensation for natural disasters and for knowledge and 
advisory services. The Law on agricultur e and rural development provides conditions for aid 
programmes. The registry mechanism for State aid has not been established yet. North Macedonia 
indicated these priorities for development: alignment with the EU acquis, setting up a notification of 
State aid system and a granted State aid register as well as administrative capacity strengthening. 
b. Commission assessment  
North Macedonia’s State aid to agriculture and rural development is partially aligned with the EU 
acquis on State aid. The establishment of a registry mechanism is crucial to avoid over-compensation. 
State aid in agriculture and in rural areas should be aligned with and not duplicate the general State aid 
provisions. North Macedonia will need to bring all its aid measures  in line with the acquis upon 
accession. (See Cluster 2 Chapter 8 – Competition Policy for more detailed information) 
I.I. SUMMARY OF FINDINGS – HORIZONTAL MEASURES  
North Macedonia is partially aligned with the horizontal elements of the EU acquis on agriculture and 
rural development . There is already partial alignment on the integrated administration and control 
system (IACS) , farm sustainability data network (FSDN)  and with the EU acquis in the area of 
management and monitoring of agriculture expenditure and rural development support. Work should 
continue to establish the necessary admini strative structures. Further progress should be made on 
agriculture support schemes, financing of agricultural expenditure, State aid.

II. AGRICULTURAL MARKETS / COMMON MARKET ORGANISATION 
II.A. TRADE MECHANISMS 
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia is a member of the World Trade Organisation since April 2003. There are no export 
subsidies or special safeguard measures. It applies the Combined Nomenclature for the classification of 
products. Legislation under the Customs Law prescribes the method for the allocation of tariff quotas. 
North Macedonia considers it to be partially aligned with the acquis. The use of tariff quotas is based 
on th e "first come first served” principle , managed by the Customs Administration. All customs 
formalities are administered through an electronic system. North Macedonia does not apply any system 
of licencing for imports or exports although entities importing a nd exporting of certain agriculture 
products need to fulfil conditions and be registered in traders registries in MAFWE. Importers of wheat 
and wheat flour intended for the national market must be registered in a register of importers of 
agricultural products. Importers of raw tobacco intended for the national market must be registered in a 
register of tobacco purchasers and legal entities registered for the production of tobacco products and 
related products..  
The Stabilisation and Association Agreement with the EU provides North Macedonia with practically 
duty-free access to the EU market for most agricultural products ( for baby beef sugar and wine tariff 
quotas or reduced duties are applied). North Macedonia’s import tariffs for agricultural products from 
the EU have been liberalised, except for certain live animals, meats, fruits, and vegetables. Certain 
vegetable oils, potatoes and dairy products are subject to tariff quotas.  North Macedonia is a member 
of the Central European Free Tra de Agreement (CEFTA) where trade in agricultural goods is fully 
liberalised. North Macedonia has concluded preferential trade agreements with EFTA (Iceland, 
Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland), Türkiye, Ukraine and the United Kingdom. 
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia has a low level  of alignment with the EU acquis on trade mechanisms.  North 
Macedonia’s tariffs and trade arrangements for agricultural products must be fully aligned  with the 
acquis upon accession. 
II.B. MARKET INTERVENTIONS  
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia does not to have a public intervention system . The Commodity Reserves Agency 
purchases and sells agricultural products (primarily, mercantile wheat and barley for fodder) in line with 
the Law on Commodity Reserves, exclusively for the purpose of renewal in order to constantly have a 
sufficient quantity of goods of appropriate quality and shelf life in the commodity reserves. North 
Macedonia does not perform market intervention in order to regulate market prices. Aid for private 
storage is provided in this context to agricultural holdings storing domest ically produced wheat and 
barley, and which are registered in the Farm Register and have applied for area-based support payments. 
North Macedonia has legal provisions for exceptional support measures in case of market disruption 
but has no system in place to implement such measures.  
b. Commission assessment  
North Macedonia’s policy on storage has a low level of alignment with the acquis on the common 
markets organisation. North Macedonia will need to be able to apply all market interventions of the 
acquis, including on storage and exceptional support measures and communication requirements upon 
accession.

II.C. SCHOOL SCHEMES 
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia implemented a programme to encourage the consumption of fresh fruit in educational 
establishments for the first time in 2022 with a budget of about EUR 325 000. In all 350 schools on the

II. D. PLANT PRODUCTS 
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia’s main arable crops produc tion is cereals: wheat (45% of sown area, 70  000 ha in 
2022), barley (30%, 45 000ha in 2022) and maize (19%, 30 000 ha in 2022). Oats, rye and rice are also 
produced to a limited extent. Production of oilseed has increased by 26% since 2019 (sunflower, 6000 
ha in 2022). In 2022 overall, 548 000 tonnes of cereals and oilseeds were produced on 161 000 ha (about 
13% of th e agricultural area). Import exceeds export, as production does not cover the national 
consumption (2022: 135  469 tonnes import, 47 401 tonnes export). North Macedonia has marketing 
standards for cereals, which it considers already well aligned with the EU acquis. 
There is some production of protein crops (around 11  000 ha). Dried fodder was produced on 36  000 
ha in 2022. North Macedonia indicated that in the production year 2022/2023, 30 suppliers had been 
registered for the production and certification of  seed material from cereal plants, however seed 
production is marginal. North Macedonia indicated that it has no production or processing of fibre crops 
(flax and hemp) and hops. There is no production of potato starch, cotton and silkworms. There is no 
sugar beet or sugar production.  
b. Commission assessment  
North Macedonia has a low level of alignment with the common market organisation on arable crops. 
North Macedonia currently has no production of hops but would need to consider if it must align with 
EU acquis including on marketing standards and on certification and related controls.  
II.E. ANIMAL PRODUCTS 
a. Country presentation  
In the area of milk and milk products, there were some 97 000 dairy cows, 465 000 sheep and 54 000 
dairy goats in 2022, with the numbers steadily decreasing in recent years. The sector is fragmented with 
more than 60% of the holdings involved in cow milk production having up to 6 dairy cows. Support 
payments per head are paid for cows, sheep and goats. Milk productivity is low. Total milk production 
in 2021 is recorded at 378 million litres, 86.5% of which was cow milk, 9% sheep milk and 4.5% goat 
milk. North Macedonia had a negative trade balance of around EUR 14 mi llion in dairy products in 
2021. Yoghurt and cheese are the main products of North Macedonia’s processing industry. Marketing 
standards for milk and milk products are in place. North Macedonia considers them closely aligned with 
the EU acquis. Public intervention, private storage aid, contractual relations and producer organisations 
are not implemented in North Macedonia. In 2022 an exceptional market support measure was applied, 
with payment per litre of milk delivered to a registered buyer. An agricultural market information 
system (AMIS) is in place as a weekly price monitoring system. 
There were 165 000 head of beef cattle in 2022, the number steadily decreasing in recent years. There 
is no division between dairy and beef production and bovine  meat is mainly a side product from milk 
production. Support measures are paid  per head of cattle. Domestic production of bovine meat

had in total 220 000 bee hives. Direct support is provided per colony (EUR 10 per colony, minimum 10 
registered colonies) and for quality analysis (50% of laboratory costs). North Macedonia considers its 
legislation to be aligned with the acquis on apiculture and honey. There are 17 facilities approved by

partial. Alignment with EU acquis should continue.

II.F. WINE, AROMATISED WINE AND SPIRIT DRINKS 
a. Country presentation  
Wine production in North Macedonia reached 83.7 million litres in 2022. The total vineyard area was 
23 425 ha with 65% of the vineyard area planted mainly with Vranec and Smederevka varieties. Around 
85% of the area harvested (265 556 tonnes of grapes) was for wine with 15% of table grapes. There are 
163 wineries registered  with varying degrees of capacity; 4 wineries account for around 52% of the 
total wine production with 123 having capacity below 50 000 litres . North Macedonia has a vineyard 
register. The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Water Economy keeps records of the individual 
producers’ harvests, production and stocks.  
A National strategy for the development of viticulture and wine (2023 -2033) is in place . Framework 
legislation on vineyards and wines aligning with the EU acquis was last updated in 2019 . including 
alignment with most EU definitions on oenological practices, and labelling and presentation of wine. A 
new wine law to fully align with the acquis has been drafted, adopted and published. North Macedonia 
also intends to align with the EU acquis on aromatised wine and spirit drinks in 2025. North Macedonia 
is a member of the OIV since 2004.  
North Macedonia´s spirit drinks legislation is partially harmonised with the acquis. No records are 
kept o f distilleries or the production of spirit drinks. North Macedonia informed of its intention to 
improve its administrative capacity in this sector, develop the sector with regard to production, labelling 
and controls and fully align with the EU acquis. 
b. Commission assessment  
In the wine sector, North Ma cedonia has partially aligned its legislation with the EU acquis, with 
ongoing efforts with a view to further aligning and updating . A vineyard register is established . The 
Commission takes note of the alignment planned until 2026, including on aromatised wine. As regards 
spirit drinks, North Macedonia needs to fully align with the EU acquis with regard to the production 
and labelling and the protection of geographical indications. 
II.G. FRUIT AND VEGETABLES  
a. Country presentation  
The fruit and vegetables sector is an important part of North Macedonia’s agriculture. It accounts for 
around 65% of the agricultural production value , including potatoes. The area sown is around 49 000 
ha of vegetables (including around 12  000 ha potatoes) and 16  500 ha of orchards. In 2019, 90 4 000 
tonnes of vegetables and 16 9 000 tonnes of fruits were harvested. Potato (21%), tomato (17%) and 
pepper (20.5%) are the most important vegetables while the most important fruit is apples (53% of fruit 
production volume). North Macedonia is a net exporter of fruits and vegetables . There are over 150 
processing enterprises, partly EU-compliant. 
Producer organisations, producer groups or interbranch organisations as defined by EU legislation do 
not exist in North Macedonia, although legislative provisions for producer organisations are in place. 
However, agricultural cooperatives under national legislation have so me elements of producer

C) CHAPTER 12 – FOOD SAFETY, VETERINARY AND 
PHYTOSANITARY POLICY 
The EU acquis in the field of food safety, veterinary and phytosanitary policy aims to ensure safe and 
wholesome food for European citizens. The high level of safety, authenticity and integrity of food (and 
feed) are safeguarded thanks to the application of specific la ws, together with rules on animal health, 
animal welfare, plant protection products, and plant health. 
The principal pre -requisites for a candidate country in this domain are the alignment with EU 
legislation, as well as its correct implementation by a pro perly structured and trained administration. 
The acquis in this chapter consists of a very large number of Regulations, Directives and Decisions. 
For the implementation of the EU acquis on food safety, appropriate administrative structures are 
needed to be able to ensure that agri-food chain legislation to protect human health, animal health and 
welfare, and plant health, is correctly applied and enforced including an appropriate laboratory capacity.  
The General Food Law Regulation (Regulation (EC) 178/2002) is the foundation of food and feed law. 
It sets out an overarching and coherent framework for the development of food and feed legislation both 
at Union and national levels. To this end, it lays down general principles, requirements and procedures 
that underpin decision making in matters of food and feed safety, covering all stages of food and feed 
production and distribution.  
Amongst others, it lays down the risk analysis principle, including the precautionary principle. As 
regards requirements, it sets out the primary responsibility of all food and feed business operators and 
it requires food and feed placed on the EU market to be safe. It also sets out traceability requirements 
for food and feed for safety purposes, throughout the food chain.  
Traceability is particularly important for the protection of consumers, in light of food and feed found to 
be unsae/non-compliant.  
The General Food Law Regulation also sets up an independent agency responsible for scientific advice 
and support, the Europe an Food Safety Authority (EFSA). Moreover, it creates the main procedures 
and tools for the management of emergencies and crises as well as the Rapid Alert System for Food and 
Feed (RASFF). 
Furthermore, food -related legislation covers packaging, labelling,  marketing, additives, extraction 
solvents, flavourings, food contact materials, food supplements, mineral waters and ionising radiations, 
contaminants, novel foods, quick frozen food, quality of seeds and propagating material, plant 
protection products, harmful organisms, plant variety rights, as well as genetically modified organisms.  
The animal health law (Regulation (EU) 2016/429) lays down rules for the prevention and control of 
animal diseases which are transmissible to animals or to humans. In addition, concerning plant health, 
Regulation (EU) 2016/2031 establishes rules to determine the phytosanitary risks posed by any species, 
strain or biotype of pathogenic agents or parasitic plants injurious to plants or plant products (‘pests’) 
and measures to reduce those risks to an acceptable level. 
EU Member States  are responsible for the implementation and enforcement of agri -food chain 
legislation. Competent authorities organise official controls systems on their territory to verify that 
operators' activities and animals and goods placed on the EU market (either EU produced or imported 
from non-EU countries) comply with relevant standards and requirements. Regulation (EU) 2017/625 
on official controls and other official activities (‘Official Controls Regulation’ – OCR) is a horizontal 
framework of basic principles and rules that apply falling in its scope and ensures that official control 
systems of Union Member States operate in the most effective, impartial and transparent manner. OCR 
has also introduced a coherent, uniform regime for the official controls of animals and goods entering 
the Union.

D) CHAPTER 13 – FISHERIES AND AQUACULTURE 
The acquis in this chapter consists of a number of regulations, some of which do not need transposition 
into national legislation, but which require the introduction of measures to allow the administration and 
the operators to correctly implement the Common Fi sheries Policy (CFP) rules. The scope, the 
objectives and the principles of the CFP are laid down in the Basic Regulation on the Common Fisheries 
Policy (Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (EU) No 1380/2013).  
The CFP provides rules f or the conservation of marine biological resources, the limitation of the 
environmental impact of fisheries, the conditions of access to waters and resources, and the promotion 
of sustainable aquaculture. It also comprises the common market organisation fo r fishery and 
aquaculture products, a structural policy with a dedicated fund (European Maritime, Fisheries and 
Aquaculture Fund), specific rules for the management of the fleet capacity, the control of fishing activity 
and on enforcement.  The CFP promotes  measures based on sound scientific advice and provides a 
framework for the collection, management and use of fisheries data, further developed in Regulation 
(EU) 2017/1004 (the data collection framework – DCF). Technical conservation measures are further 
developed by Regulation (EU) 2019/1241, the technical measures regulation.  Specific management 
measures for the sustainable exploitation of fisheries in the Mediterranean Sea are laid down in 
Regulation (EC) No 1967/2006 (Mediterranean Regulation), while specific measures are laid down for 
the stock and fisheries under the purview of the relevant Regional Fisheries Management Organisations. 
Specific rules also apply for the management of some stocks, such as the European eel (Regulation 
1100/2007).  
With re gard to resource and  fleet management , the EU acquis contains specific rules for the 
adjustment of fishing capacity in order to seek a balance between fleets and stocks as well as for the 
measurement of engine power and vessel tonnage. Member States are re quested to collect and update 
data related to the fishing capacity in a specific fishing fleet register according to Regulation (EU) 
2017/218 of 6 February 2017.  
Implementation of inspection and control is mainly the responsibility of Member States who need to 
ensure the administrative capacity for effective implementation, in line with the EU legal framework 
established by Council Regulation (EC) 1224/2009 which establishes inter alia detailed rules for the 
registration and reporting of catches and the op eration of a satellite -based vessel monitoring system. 
The recent revision of the fisheries control system (through Regulation (EU) 2023/2842, amending the 
fisheries control regulation) expands digitalisation and monitoring of fishing activities, and inclu des 
new reinforced rules on traceability for fisheries products. In addition, the Council Regulation (EC) No 
1005/2008 establishes a Community system to prevent, deter and eliminate illegal, unreported and 
unregulated fishing which includes a catch certification scheme for the importation and exportation of 
fishery products. This means that only marine fishery products accompanied by catch certificates 
validated by the competent flag state authorities can be imported into the EU.  
On structural support, EMFAF Regulation (EU) No 2021/1139 lays down provisions for the European 
Maritime Fisheries and Aquaculture Fund (EMFAF 2021 -2027) on financial support for the 
implementation of the Common Fisheries Policy, relevant measures relating to the Law of the Sea, the 
sustainable development of fisheries and aquaculture areas and inland fishing and sustainable 
aquaculture as well as for measures under the Integrated Maritime Policy. In order to benefit from the 
EMFAF, EU Member States are required to adopt a strategic  document and Programmes. Member 
States should also have the necessary administrative management and control capacity in place in order 
to ensure a sound and efficient implementation of the Programmes.  
The market policy acquis is defined by Regulation (EU ) 1379/2013 of the European Parliament and 
of the Council and provides rules on professional organisations, common marketing standards, 
consumer information, competition rules and market intelligence.  
In addition to general state aid rules, the EU acquis contains specific state aid rules applicable to the 
fisheries and aquaculture sector.

E) CHAPTER 22 - REGIONAL POLICY AND 
COORDINATION OF STRUCTURAL INSTRUMENTS 
Regional policy is the EU’s main tool for reducing regional disparities and investing in sustainable and 
inclusive socio -economic growth. The acquis under this chapter consists mostly of framework and 
implementing regulations, which do not require transposition but are directly applicable to Member 
States. They define the rules for drawing up, approving and implementing the Fund s (the European 
Regional Development Fund, the European Social Fund Plus, the Cohesion Fund and the Just Transition 
Fund) and setting up programmes reflecting each country's territorial organisation. These programmes 
operate through shared management betwe en the Commission and EU Member States. The effective 
and efficient implementation of cohesion policy requires appropriate administrative capacity on 
programme and project level, the establishment of systems of sound financial management and control 
and the fulfilment of other EU acquis elements such as environmental or public procurement legislation, 
as well as the fulfilment of enabling conditions, for example on effective application and 
implementation of the Charter of Fundamental Rights. These programm es are negotiated and agreed 
with the Commission, but implementation is the responsibility of the Member States. Thus, part of 
Cluster 5, Chapter 22 is closely interconnected to policy clusters including Cluster 3 Competitiveness 
and inclusive growth and Cluster 4 Green agenda and sustainable connectivity , as well as public 
administration reform under Cluster 1 Fundamentals. 
A legislative framework must be put in place allowing for multi-annual programming at national and 
sub-national levels and budget fle xibility, enabling co-financing capacity at national and sub -national 
level and ensuring sound and efficient financial control and audit of interventions. Member States must 
also put in place procedures to ensure the respect of Union legislation, in areas relevant for cohesion 
policy implementation (eg State aid, public procurement, environmental protection, transport, gender 
equality, non-discrimination, sustainable development, accessibility) when selecting and implementing 
projects. In addition, national authorities will have to assess whether the thematic and general enabling 
conditions linked to selected specific objectives of the programmes are fulfilled. 
Member States must set up an institutional framework establishing all structures at national and 
regional level required by the regulations as well as setting up a specific framework for financial 
management and control  including audit with a clear definition of tasks and responsibilities of the 
bodies involved, in particular with regard to requirement s of Title VI of the Common Provisions 
Regulation. The institutional framework also requires establishing an efficient mechanism for inter -
ministerial coordination as well as the involvement and consultation of a wide partnership of 
organisations in the pr eparation and implementation of programmes. The definition of conflict of 
interest from the EU’s Financial Regulation should be carefully analysed and applied during the 
programmes’ implementation. Appropriate administrative capacity must be ensured in all  relevant 
structures. Performance of public administration is built on three blocs: architecture/structures, human 
resources, and systems and tools. These include recruiting and training qualified and experienced staff 
and establishing measures to retain s uch staff. In this context, Member States need to make the 
necessary organisational arrangements, adapt procedures and organisation charts and prepare 
accompanying documents. 
The programming process covers the preparation of a partnership agreement and a s eries of 
programmes, duly supported inter alia by Strategic Environmental Assessments. There is a need to 
organise broad partnerships for the preparation of programming documents. A sufficient pipeline of 
mature projects must be established, enabling the full financial implementation of programmes. Specific 
information, publicity and transparency measures will also have to be carried out. Communication is an 
integral part of the whole programme and project cycle. 
Establishing a monitoring and evaluation  system includes the set -up of monitoring and evaluation 
structures and processes in different relevant bodies, as well the installation of a comprehensive 
management information system that is accessible and usable for all concerned bodies.

under each Multi-annual Financial Framework of the EU. The chapter is linked to a number of acquis 
from other chapters related to enabling conditions for the implementation of cohesions policy (e.g. State 
aid, public procurement, etc). By the date of North Macedonia's accession to the EU, the application 
and enforcement of these acquis will need to be fully ensured, and a framework for establishing, 
approving, implementing and coordinating all instruments under cohesion policy will need to be in 
place. 
The strategic framework is strengthened with the adoption of new strategies on public procurement and 
gender equality. However, other strategies and plans such as the National Development Strategy and 
the National Energy and Climate Plan need an update. 
North Macedonia's budget planning system allows for annual workplans. In this context, the country 
will start implementing the Law on Organic Budget, which will allow the country to define conditions 
for cohesion policy such as strengthening medium-term budget planning. North Macedonia will adopt 
a new law on the Financing of Local Self-government units. 
The co-financing of projects at national level is currently only provided for by the state budget. Co -
financing from sources other than the state remains rather limited. Local authorities can draw th eir 
revenues from multiple sources, and they are autonomous in executing their own budgets. However, 
they have limited borrowing capacity and depend upon prior consent of the central government, which 
makes co-financing capacity at local level challenging. Finding new sources of funding for local self -
government units is thus pertinent. A mechanism needs to be established to ensure a financially sound 
basis for investments and sufficient authority for project delivery, potentially across municipality 
boundaries (e.g., public-private partnership framework).

III. ADMINISTRATIVE CAPACITY 
a. Country presentation 
North Macedonia bases recruitment and job mobility (rotation, promotion) on merit, equal conditions,

and competent staff. North Macedonia plans a new Law on the Civil Service which will also address

IV.  PROGRAMMING 
a. Country presentation 
In North Macedonia, state and local institutions conduct their strategic planning in accordance with 
the Law on Budgets (Article 13) and several methodological and guiding documents for strategic 
planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, reporting and evaluation of the Annual Work 
Programme (AWP) of the Government and sector and cross -sector strategies. The AWPs are aligned 
with the National Programme for Adoption of the Acquis Communautaire . The GS plays a central role 
in defining priorities and ensuring consistency. Several digital solutions are used for the integrated 
planning system. 
A new National Development Strategy 2024-2044 was adopted in October  2024. It will enshrine key 
principles relevant to regional policy such as multi-level governance, complementarity, comprehensive 
financing, and social and demographic balance. Key priorities are territorial development, green and 
digital society, demographic and socially balanced development, innovative and competitive economy, 
just to name a few. The strategy was built on a consensus among different social stakeholders. The 
Strategy for Regional Development (2021-2031) is a shorter ten -year planning document, which is in 
line with the Law on Balanced Regional Development and according to North Macedonia, in line with 
basic strategic goals of the EU's cohesion policy. North Macedonia aims at allocating at least 1% of 
GDP for balanced regional development, 55% of which for the least developed regions, and 15% each 
for the development of villages, urban areas and areas with specific development needs.  
North Macedonia is transitioning from project -based (IPA I), through programme -based (IPA II) to 
currently sector-based assistance approach (IPA III). North Macedonia requires sectoral strategies (e.g. 
environment, transport, energy, employment) , institutional capacity, consultative and coordination 
framework as well as a sustainable, multi -annual sector budget. North Macedonia applie s in its 
programming process, the Performance Assessment Framework as an instrument to monitor and

As for consultations with stakeholders, including business community , donor community  and Civil

North Macedonia's communication framework relies, as presented, on three pillars: national

North Macedonia shared its experience in monitoring and evaluating IPA assistance components

VI. FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT AND CONTROL 
a. Country presentation 
All payments are processed by the CFCD, or by the paying agency in the case of IPARD. All costs are 
certified by the NAO. Discussions between North Macedonia and the Commission about transition from 
ex-ante to ex-post control are ongoing.  
The NAO can execute financial adjustments, whereas the Commission may apply a procedure for the 
settlement and acceptance of accounts or mechanisms for financial corrections. North Macedonia has 
procedures for bridge financing, which constitute a transfer from the state budget as a temporary 
measure to cover the EU contribution befor e payment by the Commission. The NAO has full 
responsibility for refunding the union contribution towards the Commission and reclaiming national co-
financing from the one committing irregularities, fraud or corruption or had financial gains from that.  
North Macedonia uses a system for monitoring the N+3 / N+5 rule and of financial reporting to the 
EU. There have been several decommitments under IPA I/IPARD I (30%), but no decommitments 
registered under IPARD II for the past four years and three cases of decommitment under IPA II (in 
2022, in 2023 and 2024).  
The accounting system  on an accrual basis is in accordance with the International Accounting 
Standards for the Public Sector. North Macedonia uses four parallel systems for monitoring and 
reporting, two of which serve reporting to the Commission.  
The Central Harmonisation Unit conducts monitoring of the implementation of the internal financial 
control system  on behalf of the Minister of Finance , and issues annual reports. According to the 
country’s statistics, more than 97% of central and local authorities have established a Financial Affairs 
Units. However, the number of these units, which adopted annual plans for financial management and 
control, risk management strategy and risk registers,  and internal act/procedure/regulation on the 
implementation of control was rather low (under 60%), notably in the case of local authorities. The 
implementation of recommendations of internal audit also remained largely under 60% in 2022. As far

G) CHAPTER 33 – FINANCIAL AND BUDGETARY 
PROVISIONS 
This chapter covers the rules concerning the financial resources necessary for the funding of t he EU 
budget (‘own resources’). These resources are made up from so -called traditional own resources from 
customs duties and sugar levies, which are levied by the Member States on behalf of the EU; a resource 
based on value -added tax; a resource based on n on-recycled plastic packaging waste, and finally, a 
resource based on each Member State's gross national income. Member States must have appropriate 
administrative capacity to adequately co -ordinate and ensure the correct calculation, collection, 
payment and control of their own resources’ contributions. The acquis in this area is directly binding 
and does not require transposition into national law. 
I. OWN RESSOURCES AND ADMINISTRATIVE INFRASTRUCTURE 
I.A. TRADITIONAL OWN RESOURCES  
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia set out its state of play concerning traditional resources. North Macedonia’s alignment 
with EU customs  legislation, including the operational set -up of the customs administration and its 
implementing capacity; exist ing IT infrastructure as well as current customs rules and procedures in 
place. (See cluster 3 chapter 29 - Customs Union for additional customs issues ). 
North Macedonia focused specifically on the course of the customs procedure and the collection of 
customs debt. The presentation also set out the main areas of future work, and the timeline for aligning 
with the acquis and with EU practice and procedures.  
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia is partially aligned with the EU acquis on traditional own resources and in terms of 
process and practice. Further work is required to ensure full alignment and to strengthen the capacity of 
the administration in the areas of in the areas of traditional own resources, including customs. 
I.B. VAT OWN RESOURCE 
a. Country presentation  
North Macedonia set out its legal framework related to value added tax which is partially aligned with 
the acquis. Areas of non-alignment concern in particular: new means of transport; territorial scope; intra 
- Community acquisition of goods; distance sales; keeping records of e -commerce cross -border 
payments; and small businesses. North Macedonia set out the structure of the VAT administration, the 
resources available and its implementing procedures. A number of are as for future work have been 
identified, in particular related to the Integrated Tax Information System and managing risk. 
b. Commission assessment 
North Macedonia has a VAT system. North Macedonia understands that the VAT system is different 
from the VAT based resource itself. The distinction is important as the resource may take into account 
elements that are not VAT. For example, interest payments or collection f ees in circumstances where 
collection is contracted out. North Macedonia has a low level of alignment with the acquis in this area. 
Overall further work is required to align with the acquis and to strengthen the administrative capacity 
to manage the VAT based resource.
