## In brief

By accession, EU workers and their family members must no longer face work permits or similar conditions, yet in 2023 the Commission found that EU citizens needed an employer-requested temporary residence permit for work and numerous documents, and that their family members were treated as other foreign workers (MDA-EU-2023-002-0016; MDA-EU-2023-002-0017). Moldova amended the law on the regime of foreigners, and from January 2024 EU citizens could work without a work permit (MDA-EU-2023-001-0048; MDA-EU-2024-001-0052). In July 2025 it adopted Law No. 200/2025, under which EU citizens need no work authorisation and, for stays over 90 days, a residence card not tied to a purpose of stay; the Commission reports that the law entered into force in March 2026 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0010; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0011; MDA-EU-2026-012-0078). These are adopted laws, not shown results. Practice lags: after the General Inspectorate for Migration approved an operating procedure in March 2026, 28 applications for the new residence documents had been submitted, while 422 EU citizens were still to be documented under the old stay-for-work regime in 2025 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0083; MDA-EU-2026-012-0084). Full separation of the two regimes depends on a draft law on foreigners that would enter into force on 1 June 2027, and the Ministry of Internal Affairs still needs to revise its rules on registering EU citizens (MDA-GOV-2026-006-0039; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0032; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0036). Most Government sources are machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, mainly in law. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The earliest Commission material on this issue in the evidence is the February 2023 Analytical Report, which described the permit and document requirements for EU citizens and the treatment of their family members as other foreigners (MDA-EU-2023-002-0016; MDA-EU-2023-002-0017). The 2023 report found limited progress and recorded amendments easing temporary residence and a further amendment passed in first reading in July 2023 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0042; MDA-EU-2023-001-0049; MDA-EU-2023-001-0048; MDA-EU-2023-001-0050). The 2024 report moved from describing the barrier to recording its removal in law, with amendments in force from January 2024 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0052). The 2025 report recorded the separate regime for EU citizens adopted in July 2025, and its key recommendations on access for EU citizens to the labour market, repeated in 2026, can be read as covering the issue, though they do not name it, and no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses it (MDA-EU-2025-001-0065; MDA-EU-2025-001-0020; MDA-EU-2026-012-0012). By 2026 the Commission's attention had turned to practice: the law's entry into force and the slow take-up of the new residence documents (MDA-EU-2026-012-0078; MDA-EU-2026-012-0083).

## Open questions

- How quickly EU citizens and their family members move from the old stay-for-work regime to the new residence cards could be settled by Migration Inspectorate figures on applications and cards issued after March 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0084).
- Whether the Ministry of Internal Affairs' revised rules on registering EU citizens, and the draft amendment to the identity documents decision, have been approved would be shown by the adopted acts (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0036; MDA-GOV-2024-006-0020).
- Whether the draft law on foreigners is adopted with the regimes separated and enters into force as planned in June 2027 would be shown by its adopted text (MDA-GOV-2026-006-0032; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0039).
- Whether the July 2023 first-reading amendment became the 2023 law on 90 days' work without a permit could be settled by the parliamentary record (MDA-EU-2023-001-0050; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0008).
