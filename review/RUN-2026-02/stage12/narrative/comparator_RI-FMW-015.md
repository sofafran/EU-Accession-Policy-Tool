## Reading this brief
This brief compares Moldova's missing domestic preconditions for the European Health Insurance Card with three countries: Croatia, which issued the card from its accession in 2013; Montenegro, which recognises the card in law, has a feasibility study and an action plan, and is now asked to adapt its existing health information system; and Serbia, a contrast case in which the Commission recorded no developments for three years. Montenegro was rated of High relevance, Croatia of Medium relevance, because it shows the end state rather than Moldova's problem, and Serbia of Low relevance. No case shows how to build a national health insurance card or an integrated health information system where none exists, which is the core of Moldova's gap (MDA-EU-2025-001-0076). Croatia already had both before accession (HRV-GOV-2014-001-0005), and Montenegro already has a system to adapt (MNE-EU-2026-003-0005). The Croatian evidence dates from 2011 to 2014 and is used by logged exception, and its results rest on the issuing body's own reports. The cases therefore show the route and the end point, not how to lay the foundations. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses; the transferability of the Serbian case could not be rated.

## Lessons across the cases
Three points recur. First, once a feasibility study and a plan exist, the Commission's request becomes concrete. After Montenegro's updated study and its February 2026 action plan, the Commission asked it to adapt its health information system for the card, equip its Health Insurance Fund and budget for reimbursements, all by accession (MNE-EU-2026-003-0011; MNE-EU-2026-003-0006; MNE-EU-2026-003-0005). Moldova reached the same point in August 2026, when it finalised its feasibility study and approved an EHIC Action Plan (MDA-EU-2026-012-0097; MDA-EU-2026-012-0098), so the same three tasks are likely to follow, with the added task of building the integrated system that Montenegro already has (MDA-EU-2024-001-0071). Second, legal recognition is not delivery. Montenegro recognises the card in its health insurance law but will introduce it only after accession (MNE-EU-2026-003-0007), and the Commission still asks for its preconditions after the chapter was provisionally closed; whether the EU would accept such a deferral for Moldova cannot be judged from the evidence. Third, a recommendation alone did not move the position in Serbia: the Commission recorded no developments on the card in 2024, 2025 and 2026, including in the year after a key recommendation worded almost as Moldova's (SRB-EU-2024-001-0014; SRB-EU-2025-001-0013; SRB-EU-2026-001-0002; MDA-EU-2025-001-0021). Croatia adds that a secondary act fixing the card's validity, form and issuing was adopted about 18 months before accession (HRV-EU-2012-001-0006), and that its insurer saw preparing contracted providers to treat visitors as the main test at accession (HRV-GOV-2014-001-0008; HRV-GOV-2014-001-0009).

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (open) and on comparator cases validated at G6b; each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional for Croatia and Montenegro. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and no case shows that any of these steps made the card work, since only Croatia issued it and its results come from its own insurer.

**Option 1: Set the national health insurance card and the integrated health information system as dated, budgeted steps that come before the card itself.**
- *Gap addressed:* the missing national health insurance card and integrated health information system, without which Moldova cannot issue or accept the European Health Insurance Card (MDA-EU-2025-001-0076; MDA-EU-2024-001-0071; MDA-EU-2026-012-0014).
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* Croatia (relevance Medium) shows the end state, with its insurer issuing the national card and the European card together by 2013 from a central health information system (HRV-GOV-2014-001-0005; HRV-GOV-2013-002-0001). Montenegro (relevance High) shows that, after the study and the plan, the Commission's next request is to adapt the information system, equip the insurer and budget for reimbursements (MNE-EU-2026-003-0005). Transferability conditional: both countries had the foundations Moldova lacks, so neither case shows how to build them, only that they come first.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the content of Moldova's EHIC Action Plan, which the evidence describes only as a roadmap for operational implementation (MDA-EU-2026-012-0098); a decision on how the card module under development in the health insurance information system relates to the wider integrated system (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0117).
- *Main risks:* building a card module before the national system it depends on; a plan that sets steps without a budget, given that the work depends on external and national funds.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government, which the Commission has asked since 2024 to develop an integrated e-health system (MDA-EU-2024-001-0021), with the National Health Insurance Company (CNAM), which coordinates the card (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0068).

**Option 2: Adopt the legal basis and the secondary act early, with provision for particular groups.**
- *Gap addressed:* the legal basis for the card, since the EU acts on it are not transposed and the amendments to the health care and compulsory health insurance laws are planned for 2026 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0063; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0072).
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); the mechanisms recorded for this issue do not include a legal gap, although the missing transposition is part of what remains.
- *Comparator evidence:* Croatia's 2011 regulation set the card's validity, its form as a separate card and the details needed to issue it, with longer validity for posted workers (HRV-EU-2012-001-0006), and its insurer agreed a procedure for issuing the card to officials (HRV-GOV-2014-001-0004). Moldova's draft CNAM order on the card's technical specifications and the provisional replacement certificate is the nearest counterpart (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0098). Montenegro recognised the card in its health insurance law ahead of its introduction (MNE-EU-2026-003-0007). Transferability conditional: a secondary act could work in Moldova, but only on top of a national card and information system that Croatia already had.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the law amendments adopted before the CNAM order; decisions on groups such as posted workers and officials.
- *Main risks:* treating adoption as progress: Montenegro shows a card recognised in law but not issued, so adopted acts would need to be followed by evidence that the card is issued and accepted.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and Parliament for the law amendments, and CNAM for the order on the card.

**Option 3: Staff and fund the insurer for issuing the card and reimbursing care, and prepare providers to treat visitors.**
- *Gap addressed:* the capacity and resources to run the scheme, which the 2026 key recommendation names (MDA-EU-2026-012-0014).
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* the Commission asks Montenegro to equip its Health Insurance Fund with technical and administrative capacity and to budget for future reimbursements by accession (MNE-EU-2026-003-0005). Croatia's insurer presented the treatment of visitors at the height of the tourist season as its test at accession (HRV-GOV-2014-001-0008; HRV-GOV-2014-001-0009), a self-report with no figures. CNAM's IT directorate had 3 of 8 planned specialists working on the card's IT components, according to a machine-translated government report (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0069). Transferability conditional, as for Montenegro.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the financial impact estimated in Moldova's finalised feasibility study (MDA-EU-2026-012-0097), whose findings are not in the evidence.
- *Main risks:* posts planned but not filled; reimbursements not budgeted until after accession.
- *Who would need to act:* CNAM, with the Government for its budget.

**A warning rather than an option: a recommendation and a plan on paper will not by themselves move the position.**
Serbia (relevance Low) received in 2025 a key recommendation on IT infrastructure for the card worded almost as Moldova's, and the Commission recorded no developments in that year or the next (SRB-EU-2025-001-0013; SRB-EU-2026-001-0002). The Serbian case gives no reason for the stall and its transferability cannot be rated, so it supports only a caution: Moldova recorded no progress on the card in 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0075), and its 2026 study and action plan are planning steps that do not yet show a change in the card or the health information system.
