## In brief

The Commission has found that no authority was responsible for open data policies and that Moldova was not yet aligned with the Open Data Directive, and the 2024 Growth Plan Communication asks Moldova to strengthen implementation of the European data strategy rules, including the Open Data Directive (MDA-EU-2024-001-0078; MDA-EU-2025-001-0084; MDA-EU-2024-002-0016). Parliament adopted Law No. 109/2025 on open data and the reuse of public sector information in May 2025, partially transposing the Directive, followed by methodological rules in Government Decision No. 563/2025, and the ministry created an open data policy service with two staff (MDA-CSO-2026-001-0097; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0191; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0192; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0193). The law and rules are adopted, but no source shows their effect on data publication. Civil society states that adoption of the law and continued efforts to implement it have strengthened proactive access to information, but it gives no measure of datasets published or re-used, so success cannot be shown (MDA-CSO-2026-001-0113). The only count is the Government's figure of 1,268 datasets on the portal against a target of 1,500 by 2027, and the World Bank found in 2024 that the portal is still lacking in use (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0152; MDA-IO-2024-003-0012). The Government itself says the portal needs updating and completing with data, and the draft law on data and data governance has been suspended until 2027 (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0092; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0093). What remains is full alignment with the Open Data Directive and related EU data rules, and regular publication and updating of datasets by institutions. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legally and institutionally only. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2022 Opinion contains no item on open data in the evidence. The Commission's analytical report of February 2023 and its 2023 and 2024 reports state in almost identical words that no authority has responsibility for open data policies (MDA-EU-2023-002-0051; MDA-EU-2023-001-0081; MDA-EU-2024-001-0078). The 2024 Growth Plan Communication asks for stronger implementation of the Data Governance Act, the Data Act, the Open Data Directive and the rules on high-value datasets (MDA-EU-2024-002-0016). The 2025 report moves the finding from institutions to law, stating that Moldova is not yet aligned with the Open Data Directive (MDA-EU-2025-001-0084). The 2026 report records that in July 2026 Moldova adopted a law partially harmonising with the Directive (MDA-EU-2026-012-0118). No key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses open data.

## Open questions

- Whether institutions are publishing and updating datasets more regularly is not measured, and a time series of datasets on the portal, ideally checked independently, would settle it (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0152; MDA-IO-2024-003-0012).
- The Commission dates the adoption of a partially harmonising law to July 2026 while Government and civil society sources date Law No. 109/2025 to May 2025, and the Commission's reference would need to be identified to know whether a later act exists (MDA-EU-2026-012-0118; MDA-CSO-2026-001-0097).
- Whether the list of high-value datasets and the technical norms on publication have been approved is not reported, and their adoption would show whether the remaining transposition is advancing (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0514).
- Whether the draft law on data and data governance resumes after its suspension until 2027 is not known, and its adoption or withdrawal would settle alignment with the related EU data rules (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0093; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0252).
