## In brief

The Commission's key recommendations of 2024, 2025 and 2026 ask Moldova to align its legislation with EU cybersecurity standards, and the Reform Agenda commits it to a law transposing the NIS 2 Directive, with cybersecurity responsibilities clarified (MDA-EU-2024-001-0027; MDA-EU-2025-001-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0022; MDA-EU-2025-002-0009; MDA-EU-2025-002-0073). Moldova adopted Law No. 48/2023 on cybersecurity, in force since January 2025, and the Commission records Government decisions implementing it with the aim of NIS 2 alignment, further secondary legislation on cyber incidents and 5G security in 2026, and the National Cybersecurity Programme 2026-2030, adopted in December 2025 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0071; MDA-EU-2025-001-0092; MDA-EU-2025-001-0091; MDA-EU-2026-012-0125; MDA-EU-2026-012-0106). The Government itself describes Law No. 48/2023 as only partially harmonising national law with NIS 2, and the amending law for NIS 2, the laws on the Cyber Resilience Act and on cybersecurity certification, and the designation of a market surveillance authority were still planned for 2026 and 2027 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0205; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0275; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0283; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0284; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0304). Most of the new acts are adopted but not yet shown in application, and no outcome has been assessed. The Cyber Security Agency reports that some of its 2025 work was only partly done because of too few staff and incomplete rules, and the ministry's cybersecurity policy unit has four posts, one vacant (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0036; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0165). These Government documents are machine translated. What remains is full transposition of NIS 2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the certification framework, with the authorities designated to apply them. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, mainly legal and strategic. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

In 2021 the Commission's concern was operational: a national computer emergency response team foreseen by the 2019-2024 strategy was still pending approval of its mandate (MDA-EU-2021-001-0002). The February 2023 analytical report said Moldova would need to align with the NIS framework (MDA-EU-2023-002-0058). After the Law on cybersecurity was adopted, the November 2023 report said its implementation had to start (MDA-EU-2023-001-0071; MDA-EU-2023-001-0072). From 2024 the requirement became a standing key recommendation to align legislation with EU cybersecurity standards, including the 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, repeated in 2025 and, as "further aligning", in 2026 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0027; MDA-EU-2025-001-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0022). The 2025 Reform Agenda sharpened it: it found no holistic cybersecurity framework and no plans for large-scale incidents, noted that the 2019-2024 strategy had lapsed, and set out a NIS 2 law, clearer institutional roles and mandatory requirements for products in view of the Cyber Resilience Act (MDA-EU-2025-002-0026; MDA-EU-2025-002-0068; MDA-EU-2025-002-0009; MDA-EU-2025-002-0073; MDA-EU-2025-002-0010). It also warned that operators may resist compliance costs (MDA-EU-2025-002-0028).

## Open questions

- Whether the law amending Law No. 48/2023 to transpose NIS 2 has been adopted, and how far it aligns, would be settled by the adopted text and a Commission assessment of it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0275; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0205).
- How the supervision rules of 2025 are applied is not shown; numbers of audits received and inspections and sanctions by the Agency would settle it (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0035; MDA-EU-2026-004-0019).
- The Agency's account that it designated 265 essential and important service providers is not corroborated by an independent source; a Commission or audit finding would confirm it (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0055).
- Whether staffing limits continue to hold back the Agency and the ministry would be shown by current staffing figures against approved posts (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0010; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0165).
