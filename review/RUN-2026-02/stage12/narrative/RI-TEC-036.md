## In brief

The EU expects Moldova to offer a full range of public services online through its national portal, used by all groups. The Reform Agenda set two conditions: an e-government ecosystem for business services by December 2025, and the EVO Sign digital wallet integrating at least 20 digital public services by December 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0015; MDA-EU-2025-002-0006). Moldova launched the EVO application in June 2024 and EVO 2.0 in June 2026, brought services together on the integrated EVO portal, and set up 118 Unified Public Service Centres to give rural residents access to electronic services (MDA-EU-2026-012-0122; MDA-EU-2026-012-0038; MDA-EU-2026-012-0040). The Commission reports that 447 of the 636 services on the EVO portal, 70.3 percent, are available online, and that by June 2026 82 percent of business services and 61 percent of citizen services were digitalised (MDA-EU-2026-012-0038; MDA-EU-2026-012-0117). It verified that 310 of 414 business services are digitalised, meeting the 75 percent objective, although only 133 run fully end to end (MDA-EU-2026-004-0002). User uptake increased significantly, including in rural areas, but the Commission gives no rural figure and low demand persists there (MDA-EU-2026-012-0039; MDA-EU-2025-001-0045). The portal lacks integration with EU systems, and its link to the Single Digital Gateway is still planned (MDA-EU-2025-002-0064; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0190). What remains is a fuller range of citizen services delivered end to end online, integration with EU systems, and use by people with weaker digital access or skills. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2022 Opinion recorded only that the Government's Action Plan for 2021-2022 set objectives on extending electronic services for citizens and entrepreneurs (MDA-EU-2022-001-0001). In 2023 the Commission found digitalisation lagging behind, with about 40 percent of government services for businesses available online, and set this against the weak results of the Digital Moldova 2020 strategy, which reached 12 percent of services online against a 100 percent objective (MDA-EU-2023-001-0002; MDA-EU-2023-001-0086). In 2024 it noted notable progress on digitalising public services but still put about 40 percent of mapped services online, with varying delivery conditions (MDA-EU-2024-001-0023; MDA-EU-2024-001-0037). The 2025 Reform Agenda turned the requirement into dated payment conditions and noted the portal's lack of integration with EU systems, and the 2025 report found over half of mapped services available digitally (MDA-EU-2025-002-0015; MDA-EU-2025-002-0006; MDA-EU-2025-002-0064; MDA-EU-2025-001-0039). By 2026 the Commission's language had moved from availability to uptake: it reported a significant increase in use while noting persistent low demand in rural areas (MDA-EU-2026-012-0039). The Commission's measures differ from report to report, so the percentages are not strictly comparable.

## Open questions

- Whether the EVO Sign wallet will integrate at least 20 digital public services by December 2026 is open; the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step would settle it (MDA-EU-2025-002-0006).
- How many people use the Unified Public Service Centres, and whether rural use is rising, is not measured by any independent source; service counts by centre would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0040; MDA-EU-2026-012-0039).
- Whether the portal will be integrated with the EU Single Digital Gateway, and when, is unclear; the adopted amendment to Law No. 234/2021 and a working connection would show it (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0190; MDA-EU-2025-002-0064).
- Government counts of business service requests in 2023 differ between its 2024 and 2025 documents, possibly because they use different measures; a single published series would settle it (MDA-GOV-2024-004-0013; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0297).
