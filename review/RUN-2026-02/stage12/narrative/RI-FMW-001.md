## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to align its legislation with the EU acquis on access for EU citizens to the labour market and, from 2026, on equal treatment of EU workers (MDA-EU-2025-001-0020; MDA-EU-2026-012-0012). Amendments to the law on the regime of foreigners in force since January 2024 let EU citizens work in Moldova without a work permit, and Law No. 200/2025 on the free movement and residence of EU citizens, adopted in July 2025, entered into force in March 2026 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0052; MDA-EU-2025-001-0065; MDA-EU-2026-012-0078). The Government states, in machine-translated documents, that the law gives EU citizens and their family members equal treatment with Moldovan citizens (MDA-GOV-2026-007-0034). No source yet describes how the law is applied, and no outcome has been assessed. The secondary and sectoral legislation needed to give EU workers equal access to employment services, active measures, service housing and private placement is still at draft or planning stage, and private employment agencies could until now be licensed only if their staff held Moldovan citizenship (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0031; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0032; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0034; MDA-GOV-2026-007-0022; MDA-GOV-2026-007-0023). The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is Medium and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

No requirement item from the 2022 Opinion is held for this issue. In February 2023 the Commission's analytical report found that the legal framework on access to the labour market did not differentiate between EU workers and other third-country nationals, and that family members of EU workers followed the same rules as those of other foreign workers (MDA-EU-2023-002-0014; MDA-EU-2023-002-0017). Access for EU citizens was not among the key recommendations of the 2023 and 2024 reports, although the 2023 report contained a general call to align legislation with the acquis (MDA-EU-2023-001-0044). The 2025 report made it a key recommendation to align national legislation with the acquis on access for EU citizens to the labour market (MDA-EU-2025-001-0020). In 2026, after Law No. 200/2025 entered into force, the wording became "continue alignment" and was widened to include equal treatment of EU workers (MDA-EU-2026-012-0012; MDA-EU-2026-012-0078). Over the same years the Commission's rating of the whole chapter moved from early stage in 2023 and 2024 to some level of preparation in 2025 and between some and moderate in 2026 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0041; MDA-EU-2024-001-0014; MDA-EU-2025-001-0016; MDA-EU-2026-012-0008). No benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on the issue was found.

## Open questions

- Whether Law No. 200/2025 works in practice is not shown; figures on EU citizens registering, working and using the National Employment Agency's services since March 2026 would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0078; MDA-GOV-2026-007-0018).
- Whether the drafts amending Law No. 105/2018 and Government Decision No. 1276/2018 are adopted, and in what form, is not known; the published acts would settle it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0032; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0100).
- Foreigners with temporary residence for work or study are excluded from employment services, and the Government describes EU citizens as still needing a residence permit for work under Law No. 200/2010; the adopted employment law and revised registration rules would show whether any such barrier remains for EU workers (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0005; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0006).
- The planned training of employment agency and public authority staff on equal treatment of EU citizens is described only as a plan; training records would show whether it has taken place (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0103; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0104).
