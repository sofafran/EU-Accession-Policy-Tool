## In brief

This issue is not raised as a Chapter 2 requirement in EU material. The Commission records the residence rules and counts residence permits for work, such as 2,991 issued in 2023, and its 2023 call to register foreigners and give them access to services sits under Chapter 24 and does not concern employment (MDA-EU-2024-001-0055; MDA-EU-2026-012-0082; MDA-EU-2023-001-0023). Moldova grants displaced persons from Ukraine temporary protection with the right to work, its employment agency signed cooperation agreements with partner organisations on refugee employment, and a draft law on the admission, stay and supervision of foreigners would replace Law No. 200/2010 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0368; MDA-GOV-2025-004-0015; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0033). The agency reports that 450 displaced jobseekers registered and 164 found work from January to September 2025, figures not corroborated by another source (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0369). UNHCR finds that only 29 percent of working-age refugees are in paid work, with language gaps, non-recognition of diplomas and scarce childcare as barriers, and that formal employment services remain underused (MDA-IO-2026-002-0002; MDA-IO-2026-002-0003; MDA-IO-2026-002-0019). Research describes the procedure for employing foreigners as particularly bureaucratic, and foreigners residing for work or study cannot use employment services or register as unemployed (MDA-RES-2025-001-0027; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0056). Consultation comments on the draft law note that work permits are tied to one employer and that labour inspection is not separated from immigration control (MDA-GOV-2026-006-0112; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0109). The draft law would enter into force only on 1 June 2027 (MDA-GOV-2026-006-0032). The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The issue is not raised as a Chapter 2 finding or requirement in the Commission material in the evidence, from the 2022 Opinion to the 2026 report. Across its reports the Commission records the temporary residence rule and residence permit numbers, noting in 2024 that most permits for work in 2023 went to citizens of Ukraine, Türkiye, Russia and Nepal, and in 2026 that most permits went to citizens of Nepal, Türkiye, Bangladesh and Uzbekistan (MDA-EU-2024-001-0055; MDA-EU-2026-012-0082). Its only related call, in 2023, to register foreigners and give them access to services, falls under Chapter 24 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0023; MDA-EU-2023-001-0051). There is no Commission recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on third-country workers' access to the labour market, so the analysis rests on national, international organisation and research sources.

## Open questions

- Whether the draft law on admission of foreigners will be adopted, and whether it will allow a change of employer and separate labour inspection from immigration control, is open; the adopted text would settle it (MDA-GOV-2026-006-0032; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0112; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0109).
- Whether foreigners residing for work or study will gain access to employment services depends on the draft amendment to the employment law; its adoption and the agency's registration data would show it (MDA-GOV-2026-007-0017; MDA-GOV-2026-006-0056).
- The effect of the agency's refugee support is shown only by its own figures; independent data on refugee registration and employment through the agency would settle it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0369; MDA-IO-2026-002-0019).
- Whether the referral platform built with Helvetas became functional in the second half of 2025 is not reported; agency activity reports for 2025 would show it (MDA-GOV-2025-004-0018).
