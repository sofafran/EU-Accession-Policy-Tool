## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to strengthen interoperability between its automated asset verification systems and state registries, and the Reform Agenda requires all declarations to be filed by December 2026 in a new e-Integrity system that is interoperable through the MConnect platform (MDA-EU-2025-001-0013; MDA-EU-2025-002-0050). More broadly, the issue is whether institutions use the national interoperability platform regularly. Moldova has had a law since 2018 requiring agencies to exchange data only through the interoperability solution, with an express duty to connect and sanctions in force since January 2021 (MDA-IO-2023-001-0043; MDA-GOV-2021-002-0008; MDA-GOV-2021-002-0009; MDA-GOV-2021-002-0011). SIGMA found MConnect well functioning in 2023, with base registers connected and 336 institutions exchanging data, and the Government reports 144 state and 252 private systems integrated, a different measure (MDA-IO-2023-001-0060; MDA-IO-2023-001-0007; MDA-IO-2023-001-0044; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0093). One result is shown: the Commission reports that data exchange through MConnect let the electoral commission check party financing more effectively, though it gives no measure of this (MDA-EU-2023-001-0007). Underuse persists. The World Bank finds that many ministries work in silos, the Government finds its systems fragmented, and the data protection authority reports that 13 entities still access state registers through COI technology outside the platform, with no progress despite repeated requests (MDA-IO-2024-003-0011; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0094; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0025; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0028). In 2026 the Commission found the integrity authority's interoperable verification tools still constrained (MDA-EU-2026-012-0049). The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission has raised interoperability mainly in the context of asset declaration checks under the rule of law chapter, not as a requirement on use of the platform as a whole (MDA-EU-2025-001-0013). In 2023 its only relevant item was positive, recording the benefit of MConnect for checks on party financing (MDA-EU-2023-001-0007). The Reform Agenda made interoperability through MConnect part of a step on the new e-Integrity system, due by December 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0050). The 2025 report then said that interoperability between automated verification systems and state registries must be strengthened and the services adequately staffed (MDA-EU-2025-001-0013). The 2026 report repeated the concern as a finding, that the automated and interoperable verification tools remain constrained, while noting that the integrity authority had consulted on a new e-Integrity concept in August 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0049; MDA-EU-2026-012-0050). No benchmark or key recommendation addresses use of the platform generally (MDA-EU-2026-012-0049).

## Open questions

- The Government states that all data exchange runs exclusively through MConnect, while the data protection authority finds that state bodies still access personal data through COI technology outside it; data from the e-Governance Agency on remaining COI connections would settle this confirmed conflict (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0073; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0029).
- SIGMA gives 665.7 million MConnect transactions in 2022 alone, while the e-Governance Agency gives 600 million exchanges in total, possibly different measures, and a definition of what each counts would settle it (MDA-IO-2023-001-0008; MDA-GOV-2024-003-0016).
- Whether the new e-Integrity system is in place by December 2026, with working interoperability for asset checks, would be shown by the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step (MDA-EU-2025-002-0050; MDA-EU-2026-012-0050).
- Whether the fines for breaching the interoperability law have ever been imposed would be shown by enforcement records (MDA-GOV-2021-002-0009).
