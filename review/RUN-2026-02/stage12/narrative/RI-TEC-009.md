## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to develop an integrated e-health system with interoperability between all health information systems, and the Reform Agenda requires electronic health records for 40% of the insured population by December 2027 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0021; MDA-EU-2025-002-0048). Moldova has adopted the National Programme for Digitalisation and Innovation in Health 2025-2030 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0102). An electronic prescription system has operated since April 2024 for reimbursed medicines and devices, and by 2026 it had been modernised and a fully digital system for medical certificates introduced (MDA-EU-2024-001-0072; MDA-EU-2026-012-0103). The Government reports over 270,000 reimbursed prescriptions issued a month at the end of 2024, but this figure is its own (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0028). The rest is still planned: a concept, architecture and regulatory framework for the electronic health record by the end of 2026, a draft Government decision on the record still in preparation in 2026, and a decision creating a national e-health entity (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0423; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0060; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0432). The Government itself names the lack of a functional electronic health record, limited interoperability and the absence of a dedicated institutional framework as major challenges (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0029), and describes the legal framework as only partially aligned with EU law (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0418). What remains is an eHealth legal framework aligned with the EU acquis, an interoperable integrated health information system and electronic health records in use. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2023 report found limited progress on an e-health legal framework and no e-health strategy in place (MDA-EU-2023-001-0114). In 2024 the Commission found that an integrated health information system was not yet in place, while noting the new electronic prescription system, and made an integrated e-health system and interoperability of all health information systems part of a key recommendation on the health strategy (MDA-EU-2024-001-0071; MDA-EU-2024-001-0021). The 2025 key recommendation shifted the wording to the digitalisation of health services and the protection of personal data (MDA-EU-2025-001-0024). The Reform Agenda of 2025 stated that Moldova lacked an approved eHealth strategy and regulatory framework, and set conditions for a legal framework followed by electronic health records, a National Contact Point for eHealth by December 2025 and records for 40% of the insured by December 2027 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0046; MDA-EU-2025-002-0047; MDA-EU-2025-002-0049; MDA-EU-2025-002-0048). The 2026 report records the new health digitalisation programme and the modernised prescription system rather than a new requirement (MDA-EU-2026-012-0102; MDA-EU-2026-012-0103).

## Open questions

- It is not known whether the National Programme for Digitalisation and Innovation in Health 2025-2030 is the eHealth strategy the Reform Agenda asked for; a statement by the Commission or the Ministry of Health linking the two would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0102; MDA-EU-2025-002-0049).
- Whether the electronic health record will reach 40% of the insured population by December 2027 cannot yet be judged; the adopted Government decision on the record and later coverage figures would show it (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0060; MDA-EU-2025-002-0048).
- No source shows whether the National Contact Point for eHealth or the national e-health entity has been set up; the founding decisions or a Commission statement would confirm it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0425; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0432).
- Use of the electronic prescription system rests on Government figures alone; data from the health insurance fund or an independent review would corroborate it (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0028).
