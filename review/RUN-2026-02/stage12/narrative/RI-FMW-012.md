## In brief

The EU requirement is that workers who move between Member States can acquire and preserve their supplementary pension rights (MDA-EU-2023-002-0019). The Commission finds that these EU rules are not incorporated into Moldovan law, and that Moldova has no occupational pension scheme, with voluntary private schemes supplementing public pensions (MDA-EU-2024-001-0060; MDA-EU-2024-001-0059). Moldova adopted Law No. 198/2020 on voluntary pension funds, but the Government states that effective implementation of the occupational pension system remains at an early stage (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0080). The Government undertakes to transpose Directive 98/49/EC fully and plans an amendment to Law No. 198/2020 for both directives (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0081; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0099). Neither is shown to have been adopted, and nothing is shown to have happened in practice. Moldova currently has no occupational supplementary pension system linked to employment, so the rules have limited practical reach (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0079). Sources disagree on how far the law is already aligned: the Government says, on the basis of the financial market regulator's conformity tables, that Moldovan law is aligned with Directive 2014/50/EU and partly transposes Directive 98/49/EC, while the Commission says the EU rules are not incorporated (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0078; MDA-EU-2024-001-0060). The Government sources are machine translated. What remains is incorporating the EU rules on acquiring and preserving supplementary pension rights into Moldovan law in a form the Commission accepts. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's wording has not changed. The February 2023 Analytical Report found that the EU rules on supplementary pension rights of mobile workers were not incorporated in Moldovan law, and noted that Moldova had no occupational pension scheme (MDA-EU-2023-002-0019; MDA-EU-2023-002-0018). The 2023 report and the 2024 report repeated both findings in almost the same words (MDA-EU-2023-001-0055; MDA-EU-2023-001-0056; MDA-EU-2024-001-0059; MDA-EU-2024-001-0060). The finding is stated as a gap, not as a recommendation, and no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses supplementary pension rights (MDA-EU-2023-001-0056). No Commission item in the evidence after 2024 returns to the issue, so the Commission has not yet responded to the Government's 2025 statement that the law is aligned with one directive and partly transposes the other (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0078).

## Open questions

- The Commission says the EU rules on supplementary pension rights are not in Moldovan law, while the Government says the law is aligned with Directive 2014/50/EU and partly transposes Directive 98/49/EC; the regulator's conformity tables, or a later Commission assessment of them, would settle this confirmed conflict (MDA-EU-2024-001-0060; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0078).
- Whether the amendment to Law No. 198/2020 is adopted, and whether it completes transposition of both directives, would be shown by the adopted text (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0099; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0081).
- Whether an occupational pension scheme develops, which would give the rules practical reach, would be shown by data on schemes and members under Law No. 198/2020 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0080; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0079).
