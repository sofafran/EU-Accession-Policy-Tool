## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to improve the accessibility of public sector websites, mobile applications and audiovisual services for persons with physical, sensory and intellectual disabilities (MDA-EU-2026-012-0024). Moldova has guidelines for government websites, and its digital strategy implementation programme includes an action to make ICT products and services accessible, including rules based on the WCAG 2.2 guidelines (MDA-IO-2023-001-0063; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0195). That action had not started by April 2026, because the e-Governance Agency order setting the WCAG 2.2 rules awaits a prior Government Decision on accessibility requirements (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0046). In August 2026 Moldova adopted a law on accessibility requirements for certain products and services, including digital and e-commerce services, which the Commission describes as in line with the acquis (MDA-EU-2026-012-0041; MDA-EU-2026-012-0160). This is an adopted law, not a shown result, and it concerns products and services rather than public sector websites. No improvement in practice is shown. In 2023 SIGMA found poor compliance with the WCAG 2.0 standard, with an average of 45 errors per page and only one of 26 sampled government websites faring well (MDA-IO-2023-001-0065; MDA-IO-2023-001-0076). In 2026 the Commission found public sector websites and mobile applications still insufficiently accessible, and accessibility insufficiently enforced and funded (MDA-EU-2026-012-0135; MDA-EU-2026-012-0058). What remains is binding accessibility rules for public sector websites and applications, with enforcement and funding. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The requirement is new as an EU ask. The Commission did not raise the accessibility of public sector websites and applications in any report in the evidence before 2026, although SIGMA, an international partner, had documented poor compliance in 2023 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0024; MDA-IO-2023-001-0063). The 2026 report raised it at once as a key recommendation, asking Moldova to improve the accessibility of public sector websites, mobile applications and audiovisual services (MDA-EU-2026-012-0024). In the same report the Commission found that public sector websites and mobile applications remain insufficiently accessible, that accessibility is insufficiently enforced and funded across buildings, transport, websites and digital services, and that public and private job portals are largely inaccessible (MDA-EU-2026-012-0135; MDA-EU-2026-012-0058; MDA-EU-2026-012-0144). It also welcomed the August 2026 law on accessibility requirements for products and services (MDA-EU-2026-012-0041). No Reform Agenda payment condition names the issue, so there is no sequence of Commission wording to compare (MDA-EU-2026-012-0024).

## Open questions

- Whether the Government Decision on accessibility requirements is approved, and the e-Governance Agency then issues the WCAG 2.2 rules, would be shown by the adopted decision and order (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0046).
- Whether public sector websites have become more accessible since 2023 could be settled by a repeat of the SIGMA test or a similar independent audit (MDA-IO-2023-001-0065).
- Whether the August 2026 law reaches digital public services, and who enforces it, would be shown by its text and implementing rules (MDA-EU-2026-012-0041; MDA-EU-2026-012-0160).
- Whether funding and enforcement for accessibility increase would be shown by budget allocations and enforcement records after 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0058).
