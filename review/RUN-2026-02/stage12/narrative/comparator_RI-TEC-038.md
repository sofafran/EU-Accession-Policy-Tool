## Reading this brief
This brief compares Moldova's outdated electronic procurement system, MTender, which it is replacing with a new system, with two countries: Montenegro, where an independent assessment found within about two years that a system introduced in 2021 rested on old technology that limited upgrading, and North Macedonia, whose integrated system the Commission has described as functioning efficiently in every report from 2021 to 2026. Montenegro was rated of Medium relevance and North Macedonia of Low relevance. Neither case shows an outdated system being replaced: Montenegro upgraded a recent system and no source shows whether the technology limit was removed (MNE-IO-2023-001-0007; MNE-IO-2023-001-0008), and North Macedonia upgraded a working one. The cases therefore offer nothing on how to replace a system, which is what Moldova is doing (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0016). What they do offer is a caution about building and a method for checking before building, and that is why this brief gives a warning first and options about diagnosis rather than about the replacement itself. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses.

## Lessons across the cases
Two points recur. First, a new system can itself become the problem. Montenegro's system was under implementation from January 2021 (MNE-IO-2023-001-0001), and an independent World Bank assessment, requested by the national procurement policy body, found in 2023 that it was a good start but needed substantial upgrading and that old technology limited further upgrading (MNE-IO-2023-001-0002; MNE-IO-2023-001-0006; MNE-IO-2023-001-0007). The assessment's findings were then used to secure EU funds for an upgrade (MNE-IO-2023-001-0008), but the only stated effect since is the Commission's unmeasured sentence on improved monitoring functions (MNE-EU-2024-001-0019). Moldova received the technical specifications for its new system in September 2025 and published the call for bids in December 2025 (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0013; MDA-GOV-2026-011-0016), so its technology choice is being made now. Second, a working system follows the law and grows in stages. North Macedonia's system was adapted to its new procurement law and modules were added over several years, under one responsible body, and its use is obligatory for all contracting authorities (MKD-EU-2020-001-0095; MKD-EU-2023-003-0112; MKD-EU-2026-007-0062); this is the state Moldova is aiming for, not the route to it. Moldova's own problem is that MTender does not allow all the tools in its law and lacks annual planning and contract management (MDA-EU-2025-001-0064; MDA-EU-2025-002-0075; MDA-EU-2025-002-0076), and contract management is not among the stages listed for North Macedonia either, so neither case gives evidence on it.

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (open) and on comparator cases validated at G6b; each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and no case shows that any of these steps produced a working system.

**A warning first: a new system can rest on technology that limits it within a few years.**
Montenegro (relevance Medium) shows a system introduced in 2021 and judged within about two years to rest on old technology that limits further upgrading (MNE-IO-2023-001-0001; MNE-IO-2023-001-0007). Moldova is replacing MTender because the Commission finds it outdated (MDA-EU-2025-001-0064), and the Commission asks it to finalise the new system and make it operational (MDA-EU-2026-012-0006). The comparator evidence advises against treating the award of a development contract, or a pilot of essential modules by December 2027 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0043), as evidence that the new system will avoid MTender's problems. It gives no support for any particular technology, design or supplier.

**Option 1: Commission an independent review of the new system's design and technology choice, early enough to change it.**
- *Gap addressed:* designing, building and putting into operation a new system that covers every procedure and stage of the procurement cycle (MDA-EU-2025-002-0043; MDA-EU-2025-002-0075; MDA-EU-2026-012-0006).
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03), in the technical judgement needed to specify and check a large system.
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro's procurement policy body asked the World Bank for an assessment under a recognised method with a dedicated e-procurement module and a three-day technical review of the system (MNE-IO-2023-001-0002; MNE-IO-2023-001-0004), and the findings directed EU funds (MNE-IO-2023-001-0008). Moldova already has World Bank review of its tender documents for the new system, according to a machine-translated government report (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0014), which is a review of the procurement documents, not of the system's technology. Transferability conditional: the review would need to reach the technology choice before the system is built, whereas Montenegro's came after; and the case does not show that its upgrade worked.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a review whose findings can still change the contract, and a method recognised by the funders.
- *Main risks:* a review that arrives after the design is fixed; a review used only to unlock funds, with no check that its findings were acted on.
- *Who would need to act:* the body procuring the new system, named in the Government's report as OGPAE (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0014), with the Centre for Information Technologies in Finance, which owns and operates MTender (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0379), and the international partners funding the system (MDA-EU-2025-001-0063).

**Option 2: Specify and accept the new system against the procurement law and the whole cycle, and plan how it will follow later changes to the law.**
- *Gap addressed:* the procedures, award instruments, annual planning and contract management that MTender lacks, and the monitoring the Public Procurement Agency cannot carry out because delivery, invoices, payments, amendments and performance are not captured digitally (MDA-EU-2025-002-0075; MDA-EU-2025-002-0076; MDA-EU-2026-012-0072).
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03) and insufficient resources (MEC-09), since the system depends on funding from international partners (MDA-EU-2025-001-0063).
- *Comparator evidence:* North Macedonia (relevance Low) shows a working system adapted to a new procurement law, extended module by module under one responsible body and obligatory for all contracting authorities, which the Commission describes as functioning efficiently year after year (MKD-EU-2020-001-0095; MKD-EU-2023-003-0112; MKD-EU-2021-001-0047; MKD-EU-2026-007-0062). Transferability conditional: it applies only once Moldova has a working new core system, it shows nothing about replacing an outdated one, and contract management is not among its listed stages.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a map of every procedure and stage in Moldova's law against the modules planned, including those beyond the essential modules in the Reform Agenda pilot (MDA-EU-2025-002-0043); a budget for adapting the system after donor funding ends, which neither case shows.
- *Main risks:* a pilot of essential modules that leaves planning and contract management for later, keeping the alternative solutions that the Commission says may cause delays (MDA-EU-2025-002-0075).
- *Who would need to act:* the Centre for Information Technologies in Finance as owner and operator, with the Public Procurement Agency, whose monitoring depends on the data captured.

No option is offered on how to migrate from MTender to the new system, because neither case gives evidence on replacing a system.
