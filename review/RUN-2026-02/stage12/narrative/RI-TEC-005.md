## In brief

The Commission's 2026 report states that it is essential for Moldova to further increase the digitalisation of tax services, and the Reform Agenda sets a target of 18 of 19 taxpayer services, or 90%, digitalised for businesses and citizens by December 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0141; MDA-EU-2025-002-0044). The digitalisation of State Tax Service services is planned, with at least two services to be digitalised by 2025 and two more in 2026, but no source reports which services have so far been digitalised, so the measure remains proposed (MDA-EU-2025-002-0082; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0454). The e-Invoice service on the Electronic Fiscal Services platform is in place and is assessed as implemented (MDA-IO-2024-002-0083). On use, the Government's own machine-translated indicator gives 64% of companies using electronic invoices against a target above 70%, a figure not independently corroborated, and SIGMA found that for fiscal year 2022 81% of businesses declared corporate income tax digitally but only 10% of citizens filed personal income tax returns electronically (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0138; MDA-IO-2023-001-0033). These figures describe uptake, not whether the services meet the EU requirement. The Reform Agenda names limited staff and time, and many taxpayers' preference for in-person service, as barriers (MDA-EU-2025-002-0079; MDA-EU-2025-002-0080). What remains is completing the digitalisation of taxpayer services and raising their use, especially by citizens. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2022 Opinion contains no item on taxpayer e-services in the evidence. From 2023 the Commission's wording is a finding of improvement rather than a demand: the 2023 report states that the tax administration improved its services to taxpayers, including electronic tax returns, and the 2024 report that it improved its electronic services and promoted voluntary compliance (MDA-EU-2023-001-0100; MDA-EU-2024-001-0087). The 2025 report and the 2026 report record good progress in Chapter 16 that includes the digitalisation of tax administration services, though these statements cover the whole chapter (MDA-EU-2025-001-0032; MDA-EU-2026-012-0025). The Reform Agenda turned the matter into a payment condition with a numerical target for December 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0044). The 2026 report then returns to a requirement, stating that further digitalisation of tax services is essential alongside an automated compliance risk management system (MDA-EU-2026-012-0141). No key recommendation names taxpayer e-services.

## Open questions

- Which of the 19 taxpayer services have been digitalised is not reported, and a State Tax Service or Reform Agenda payment request report listing them would show whether the December 2026 target was met (MDA-EU-2025-002-0044; MDA-EU-2025-002-0082).
- The share of companies using electronic invoices comes only from the Government's own machine-translated indicator, and independent data, for example from the Commission or SIGMA, would be needed to confirm it (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0138).
- Whether electronic filing by citizens has risen from the 10% recorded for fiscal year 2022 is not known, and more recent filing statistics would settle it (MDA-IO-2023-001-0033).
- Whether the staffing constraint named in the Reform Agenda has been eased is not recorded, and evidence on State Tax Service resources for digitalisation would be needed (MDA-EU-2025-002-0079).
