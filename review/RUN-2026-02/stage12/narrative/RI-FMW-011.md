## In brief

The European Health Insurance Card is part of the Chapter 2 acquis on social security coordination, and the Commission's 2025 and 2026 key recommendations ask Moldova to develop the IT infrastructure and capacity for the Card scheme alongside the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information (MDA-EU-2025-001-0021; MDA-EU-2026-012-0014). The Government stated in 2025, in machine-translated documents, that the EU acts on the Card are not transposed in Moldova, that Moldova is to apply the Card, and that it plans a law on cross-border medical care in EU Member States, the European Economic Area and Switzerland by December 2026 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0063; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0058; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0092). The Commission reports that Moldova conducted a feasibility study on introducing the Card, finalised in August 2026, and that an action plan on the Card was approved the same month as a roadmap for operational implementation (MDA-EU-2026-012-0010; MDA-EU-2026-012-0097; MDA-EU-2026-012-0098). No source shows the law adopted or the Card issued, and no item states whether EU citizens can obtain healthcare in Moldova with the Card, so no outcome has been assessed. The international cooperation section of the health insurance fund that deals with Member State institutions has three specialists (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0493). What remains is a decision on introducing the Card and the legal and financial arrangements that would give EU citizens access to healthcare through it. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

No requirement item on the Card from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 and 2024 reports was found. The Card first appears in the 2025 report, whose key recommendation names IT infrastructure and capacity for the Card scheme to be developed together with the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information, and whose sub-area wording records no progress on the Card (MDA-EU-2025-001-0021; MDA-EU-2025-001-0075). The 2026 report repeats the key recommendation and records the feasibility study and the action plan, so the Commission's attention moves from the absence of progress to the first preparatory steps (MDA-EU-2026-012-0014; MDA-EU-2026-012-0010; MDA-EU-2026-012-0097; MDA-EU-2026-012-0098). Both reports address the domestic preconditions for the Card rather than whether EU citizens can use it in Moldova. No Reform Agenda payment condition on the Card was found in the evidence.

## Open questions

- Whether EU citizens can obtain healthcare in Moldova with the Card, or only under bilateral arrangements, is not stated in any source; a statement by the health insurance fund or the Commission on current access would settle it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0063).
- The findings and cost estimate of the feasibility study are not known; the published study would show whether cost is the main constraint (MDA-EU-2026-012-0010; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0116).
- Whether the law on cross-border medical care has been adopted, and whether it transposes the EU acts on the Card, would be settled by the adopted text (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0092).
- Whether the three-person international cooperation section is enough to run the scheme is not assessed in any source; staffing plans in the action plan would show it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0493; MDA-EU-2026-012-0098).
