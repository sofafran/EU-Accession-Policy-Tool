## In brief

The Commission has asked Moldova to prioritise infrastructure investment to overcome the rural-urban digital divide, and it reports that 5G coverage remains well below the EU average (MDA-EU-2024-001-0013; MDA-EU-2026-012-0069). Moldova's regulator set licence conditions for the 700 MHz and 800 MHz bands in August 2024, with coverage obligations from October 2027, and awarded spectrum to three operators in February 2025 and to Moldtelecom in a further 2025 competition (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0074; MDA-GOV-2026-014-0033; MDA-GOV-2026-014-0037). A new law on electronic communications entered into force in January 2026, and it requires the regulator to survey broadband coverage (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0182). Coverage and take-up are shown to have risen: home internet access went from 67.5% of households in 2023 to nearly 95% in 2026, fixed-broadband connections rose by 6.9% in the year to early 2026, and median download speeds rose by about a third (MDA-EU-2023-001-0033; MDA-EU-2026-012-0067; MDA-EU-2026-006-0023; MDA-EU-2026-006-0026). The Commission estimates 5G population coverage at 25 to 35%, and the success of the spectrum awards is assessed as too early to judge (MDA-EU-2026-012-0128; MDA-GOV-2026-014-0089). What remains is high-capacity fixed and 5G coverage in rural and small localities: the Government counts 431 localities without fixed broadband, most of them small and isolated, and the plan to build networks there waits for the regulator's survey (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0022; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0023; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0055). Much of the Government and regulator evidence is machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's concern has narrowed from a general one to a specific one. In 2023 it found that digitalisation in Moldova was lagging behind and that connectivity gaps remained, contrasting near-universal 4G coverage with home internet access of 67.5% of households, and 58% in rural areas (MDA-EU-2023-001-0029; MDA-EU-2023-001-0078; MDA-EU-2023-001-0033; MDA-EU-2023-001-0034). In 2024 an economic key recommendation asked Moldova to prioritise infrastructure investment, including to overcome the rural-urban digital divide (MDA-EU-2024-001-0013). In 2025 the Commission found digitalisation steadily advancing while regional disparities persisted, and the Reform Agenda described a significant urban-rural divide (MDA-EU-2025-001-0059; MDA-EU-2025-002-0056). By 2026 the focus had moved to 5G, with some progress recorded but coverage well below the EU average (MDA-EU-2026-012-0128; MDA-EU-2026-012-0069). No Chapter 10 key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses coverage; the related Reform Agenda step on electronic communications, assessed as satisfactorily fulfilled, concerns legal alignment rather than coverage (MDA-EU-2026-006-0028).

## Open questions

- Whether operators meet the coverage obligations in the licence conditions by October 2027 would be shown by the regulator's compliance reports (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0074).
- Whether 5G coverage rises after the 2025 awards could be settled by independent coverage data, such as later European 5G Observatory figures, since the Moldtelecom 4G gains rest only on the regulator's report (MDA-EU-2026-012-0069; MDA-GOV-2026-014-0047).
- Whether the geographic survey is carried out and networks are then built in the white areas would be shown by the survey and the list of localities connected (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0182; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0055).
- Whether the rural-urban gap in home internet access has closed is not shown, as the 2026 household figure is national, and rural and urban data would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0067; MDA-EU-2023-001-0034).
