## In brief

The EU requirement is a body that supports EU workers in their rights and informs them, and in 2023 the Commission found that Moldova had no specific body responsible for enforcing the right to free movement of workers (MDA-EU-2023-002-0013). Moldova's Law No. 200/2025 gives the Equality Council the competence to promote, monitor and support equal treatment of EU citizens and their family members, and makes it the national contact point for cooperation with similar bodies in EU Member States (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0023; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0024). The Commission confirms that the law entered into force in March 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0078). This is a designation in law, not a shown result. The steps needed to make it work are still planned: an amendment to the Equality Council law, a restructuring with more staff, training of Council and public authority staff, and information and referral measures by the General Inspectorate for Migration (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0085; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0039; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0104; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0105; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0037). The Council had filled 19 of 33 planned posts, and its anti-discrimination directorate, which handles complaints, 4 of 7 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0025; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0026). No source shows the Council at work for EU workers. The Government sources are machine translated. What remains is a body that in practice supports EU workers and informs them of their rights, with the mandate, structure and staff to do so. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is Low and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission has raised this issue only once, in its February 2023 Analytical Report, which found that no specific body was yet responsible for enforcing the right to free movement of workers (MDA-EU-2023-002-0013; MDA-EU-2023-002-0022). No later Commission item in the evidence returns to the Equality Council or to an enforcement body, and no benchmark, Reform Agenda payment condition or key recommendation names it (MDA-EU-2023-002-0022). The 2026 report records that the law on free movement and residence of EU citizens entered into force in March 2026, aiming to align with the acquis on the rights of workers in the context of free movement, but it does not assess the body the law designates (MDA-EU-2026-012-0078). The 2026 key recommendations address alignment and equal treatment of EU workers in general terms (MDA-EU-2026-012-0011; MDA-EU-2026-012-0012). The EU requirement has therefore not changed in wording since 2023; what has changed is that Moldova has answered the 2023 finding in law.

## Open questions

- Whether the amendment to the Equality Council law is adopted, giving the Council the powers to carry out its new tasks, would be shown by the adopted law (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0085).
- Whether the Council is restructured and recruits enough staff would be shown by its approved structure and staffing figures after 2025 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0039; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0025).
- Whether the Council handles discrimination complaints from EU citizens and acts as national contact point would be shown by its activity reports or case data (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0024; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0035).
- Whether EU citizens arriving in Moldova are informed of their rights and referred would be shown by evidence of the Migration Inspectorate's information measures in operation (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0037).
