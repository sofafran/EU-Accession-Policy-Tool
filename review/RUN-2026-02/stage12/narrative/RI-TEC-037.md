## In brief

The Commission has asked Moldova in four successive reports to digitalise processes for businesses: to simplify and digitalise processes to cut administrative burden in 2023 and 2024, and to digitalise the processes of the Entrepreneurship Development Organisation in 2025 and 2026 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0103; MDA-EU-2024-001-0031; MDA-EU-2025-001-0033; MDA-EU-2026-012-0028). The Reform Agenda required an e-government ecosystem for business services, a fully operational State Register of Legal Units and a life events methodology by December 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0015). The register portal was launched on 29 December 2025 and the methodology approved on 18 December 2025, and the Commission verified that 310 of 414 business services are digitalised, meeting the 75% objective, and judged the step satisfactorily fulfilled (MDA-EU-2026-004-0003; MDA-EU-2026-004-0004; MDA-EU-2026-004-0002; MDA-EU-2026-004-0008). That outcome is assessed as having worked, but only 133 of the 414 services are fully digital end to end (MDA-EU-2026-004-0001; MDA-EU-2026-004-0002). By June 2026, 82% of business services and 61% of citizen services were digitalised, and the Commission reports that use of e-services increased significantly, including in rural areas, though it gives no rural figure and does not attribute the rise to the public service centres set up there (MDA-EU-2026-012-0117; MDA-EU-2026-012-0039). An audit of the permits system found improved accessibility but only partial use and some reluctance by authorities, a mixed result (MDA-GOV-2024-001-0061). The systems of the entrepreneurship and employment agencies are still being procured or built (MDA-EU-2026-012-0145; MDA-EU-2026-012-0142). What remains is end-to-end online delivery and wider use of sector services, especially for citizens and in agencies such as the entrepreneurship and employment agencies. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2022 Opinion contains no item on the issue in the evidence. The 2023 and 2024 key recommendations framed it as reducing administrative burden by simplifying and digitalising processes (MDA-EU-2023-001-0103; MDA-EU-2024-001-0031). Alongside, the Commission's measures of availability rose from about 40% of mapped services available digitally in 2024 to over 50% in 2025, with 64% for businesses and 54% for individuals in 2024, while noting each time that delivery conditions vary (MDA-EU-2024-001-0038; MDA-EU-2025-001-0040; MDA-EU-2025-001-0061). In 2025 the Commission also flagged low demand, especially in rural areas, and narrowed the key recommendation to the entrepreneurship agency's processes, which it repeats in 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0045; MDA-EU-2025-001-0033; MDA-EU-2026-012-0028). The Reform Agenda business services step was met in March 2026, and the 2026 report records 82% of business and 61% of citizen services digitalised (MDA-EU-2026-004-0001; MDA-EU-2026-012-0117).

## Open questions

- Whether the State Register of Legal Units is used at scale is not shown, and registration data from the register or the Commission would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-004-0003).
- How far use of e-services has grown in rural areas is not measured, and figures on services delivered through the public service centres, checked independently, would be needed (MDA-EU-2026-012-0039).
- The Government gives two different counts of business service requests in 2023, 3.7 million with 2.48 million online in one report and 4,524,731 with 3,058,794 online in another, possibly on different measures, and a single defined series would settle the level of online use (MDA-GOV-2024-004-0013; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0297).
- Whether the entrepreneurship agency's integrated information system, procured in June 2026, and the employment agency's new system are in operation is not known, and evidence of their use would show whether the sector gap is closing (MDA-EU-2026-012-0145; MDA-EU-2026-012-0142).
