## Reading this brief
This brief compares Moldova's national e-services portal with three countries: North Macedonia, whose portal usage rate doubled in the year its most-requested citizen services went online; Albania, which placed most services on one portal under a law obliging institutions to use it and reports high use; and Montenegro, which launched a new portal in December 2024 and is adding services with Reform Agenda support. North Macedonia was rated of High relevance, and Albania and Montenegro of Medium relevance. Moldova's position differs from all three: its EVO portal brings together 636 services, 447 of them online (MDA-EU-2026-012-0038), and the Commission reports that uptake rose significantly in 2026, including in rural areas, where demand had been low (MDA-EU-2026-012-0039; MDA-EU-2025-001-0045). What remains is a fuller range of citizen services delivered end to end, integration with EU systems, and use by people with weaker digital access or skills. No case shows what caused a rise in use: North Macedonia's Commission links its rise to new services only by saying that one accompanied the other (MKD-EU-2026-007-0022), Albania's sources do not show that its law produced its range or use, and Montenegro's results are too early. None of the cases says anything about assisted access of the kind Moldova already provides through 118 service centres (MDA-EU-2026-012-0040), so this brief offers no option on it. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses.

## Lessons across the cases
Three points recur. First, which services go online may matter more than how many. In North Macedonia use stayed low while a growing count of rarely requested services went online, and rose in the year that birth, marriage and citizenship certificates were added (MKD-EU-2026-007-0021; MKD-EU-2026-007-0022). Moldova's remaining gap sits among citizen services, of which 61% were digitalised by June 2026 against 82% of business services (MDA-EU-2026-012-0117). Second, a count of services delivered does not show that they work end to end or are used. Montenegro developed 57 e-services in 2025 with Reform Agenda support, yet the Commission says their operability still needs to be improved (MNE-EU-2026-003-0017; MNE-EU-2026-003-0015; MNE-EU-2026-003-0016). Moldova's own figures show the same gap: of 414 business services, 310 are counted as digitalised but 133 run fully end to end (MDA-EU-2026-004-0002). Third, consolidation on one portal does not by itself close a digital divide or pay for upkeep. Albania offers 95% of applications for 1,257 services online, yet in 2026 the Commission asks it to address an emerging digital divide, resolve accessibility problems and plan and budget for maintaining its platforms (ALB-EU-2026-001-0006; ALB-EU-2026-001-0007; ALB-EU-2026-001-0004; ALB-EU-2026-001-0005). Moldova has decided, in a government decision known from machine translation, to strengthen access to e-services through EVO (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0027), and its Government says the cost of digitalisation exceeds its own resources (MDA-GOV-2024-004-0044).

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (partly addressed) and on comparator cases validated at G6b; each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and no case shows that any of these steps caused a rise in use.

**Option 1: Rank the remaining citizen services by how often people request them, put them online end to end in that order, and follow use after each addition.**
- *Gap addressed:* the fuller range of citizen services delivered end to end, with 61% of citizen services digitalised (MDA-EU-2026-012-0117), and low demand among some users (MDA-EU-2025-001-0045).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11).
- *Comparator evidence:* North Macedonia (relevance High), where the usage rate doubled as the most-demanded certificates went online (MKD-EU-2026-007-0022; MKD-EU-2025-001-0136). Transferability conditional: the rate has no stated definition, it rose alongside Single Sign-On integration (MKD-EU-2026-007-0098), and North Macedonia's range is narrower than Moldova's, so each added service may matter less in Moldova.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* data on how often each service is requested, on every channel, and a measure of use per service. Moldova's current measures, such as the share of public service users who accessed services electronically and active users of the EVO application, are defined differently and would need to be followed over time; both figures come from machine-translated government documents (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0173; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0042).
- *Main risks:* the most-requested services may depend on registers and data exchange that are not ready; added services may not reach older and rural users, since people aged 18 to 29 use e-services three times as often as those aged 60 to 74, according to a machine-translated government programme (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0011).
- *Who would need to act:* the e-Governance Agency, which runs the shared platforms, with the institutions that own the services.

**Option 2: Test whether lower fees for digital services and promotion of the portal raise use.**
- *Gap addressed:* use of the portal by people who still apply in person, and the persistent low demand the Commission records (MDA-EU-2026-012-0039).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11).
- *Comparator evidence:* North Macedonia's State Audit Office tied earlier low use to weak promotion and equal prices for electronic and paper documents, in a finding known from machine translation (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0006). Moldova's own strategy already proposes lower fees or shorter delivery times for digital services (MDA-GOV-2023-001-0059). Transferability conditional: the account of causes rests on one machine-translated audit, and no case shows the effect of a price change.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a test designed so that its effect on use can be measured, for example on a few high-volume services.
- *Main risks:* lost fee income for a Government that says digitalisation already costs more than its resources (MDA-GOV-2024-004-0044); lower fees that do not reach people without digital access or skills.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government, with the institutions that set fees for their services.

**Option 3: Report Reform Agenda progress on services that work end to end and are used, not only on services counted.**
- *Gap addressed:* the open condition to integrate at least 20 e-services in the EVO Sign wallet by December 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0035; MDA-EU-2025-002-0006), and the gap between services counted as digitalised and those delivered end to end (MDA-EU-2026-004-0002).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11) and weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro (relevance Medium), where services were delivered under the Reform Agenda while their operability still needs to be improved (MNE-EU-2026-003-0017; MNE-EU-2026-003-0016). Transferability conditional and too early: no source measures use of Montenegro's portal itself, and Moldova's portal already offers several times as many services.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* an agreed definition of a service delivered end to end, which Moldova already applies to business services (MDA-EU-2026-004-0002).
- *Main risks:* a condition met on paper by integrating services that are little used.
- *Who would need to act:* the e-Governance Agency, reporting with the Government on the Reform Agenda.

**A warning rather than an option: consolidating services on EVO will not by itself raise use among rural and older users, and the platforms need their own maintenance budget.**
Albania (relevance Medium) combines near-complete range on one portal with high reported use, yet the Commission still asks it to close a digital divide and to plan and budget for maintaining its platforms (ALB-EU-2026-001-0007; ALB-EU-2026-001-0005), and no source shows that its 2023 law produced its range or use. Transferability conditional: for Moldova, which has decided to strengthen access through EVO (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0027), the comparator evidence supports budgeting for maintenance alongside consolidation, not expecting consolidation to drive use on its own.
