## Summary
This overview lists the 53 reform issues identified for Moldova in RUN-2026-02: 16 under Chapter 2 (Freedom of movement for workers) and 37 under the cross-cutting technology and AI theme. All values shown are judgements validated by the analyst at G6. Seventeen issues are rated High priority (3 in Chapter 2 and 14 in the technology theme), 24 Medium and 12 Low. The three High priority Chapter 2 issues are the framework for joining EURES, gaps in bilateral social security and healthcare agreements, and the missing domestic preconditions for the European Health Insurance Card. The dominant pattern is improvement: 28 of 53 issues have an improving direction of travel, 15 are persistent, one is new (host-language tuition for EU workers and their children, which is not raised in EU material) and 9 cannot be judged on the evidence. Five High priority issues are persistent: the European Health Insurance Card preconditions, digital communication with the courts, the independence and resources of the data protection authority, the security of 5G networks and the outdated electronic procurement system. Where an improvement is in law only, the issue briefs say so. No issue's gap is closed: 16 gaps are open, 33 partly addressed and 4 cannot be judged. Most issues are of Medium tractability (35), 13 are High, 2 are Low and 3 cannot be judged. The comparator step covered three High priority issues: the European Health Insurance Card preconditions (RI-FMW-015), the outdated electronic procurement system (RI-TEC-038) and the national e-services portal (RI-TEC-036).

## Notes on evidence coverage
Coverage is uneven between the two strands. The technology theme draws on far more evidence than Chapter 2, because the theme makes the whole of each annual report, the Reform Agenda and the national digital strategies relevant. Within Chapter 2, the access and rights of third-country workers, social security coordination and work permits are well covered, while corruption prevention in worker mobility, alignment with the European Labour Authority regulation and access to healthcare through the card rest on very few items, and no monitoring of the rights of foreign or migrant workers from 2020 onwards was found. Moldova entered the annual enlargement package only in 2023, so the Commission's Opinion of 2022, its Analytical Report of 2023 and the earlier Association Implementation Reports stand in for annual reports before then. The 2026 Moldova report, published on 4 November 2026, is included and adds 146 in-scope items; the run's evidence stops there. Most national government sources are in Romanian only: 1,712 of the 3,534 Moldova items extracted are machine-translated Romanian, including 1,535 of the 3,173 in-scope items, and all are capped at Medium confidence. The share is highest in Chapter 2, where much of the country response comes from Romanian-language government documents; the share for each issue is shown in the tables. Moldova's core laws are not held as texts, so legal alignment is judged from EU assessments, the National Accession Programme and government drafts (LIM-0047). Evidence on the Transnistrian region is recorded as sources state it, but implementation is not judged there (LIM-0044). The evidence count shows how much material exists, not how important the issue is: priority did not depend on it.
