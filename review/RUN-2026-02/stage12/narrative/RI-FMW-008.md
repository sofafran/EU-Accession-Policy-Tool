## In brief

Until accession, workers moving between Moldova and the EU rely on bilateral social security agreements, and the Commission asks Moldova to conclude more of them, in particular with EU Member States (MDA-EU-2023-001-0045; MDA-EU-2026-012-0013). By 2023 Moldova had agreements with 13 Member States and Türkiye (MDA-EU-2023-002-0025). Since then the agreement with Greece entered into force in January 2023 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0062), those with Spain and Latvia in June and July 2024 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0061), agreements with Italy and France were signed in October 2024 and March 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0072), the Italian one entered into force in September 2025 and one with Slovakia was signed in March 2026 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0091). The Government states that it has signed agreements with 17 of the 27 Member States (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0495; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0059). The National Social Insurance House negotiates the agreements and applies them (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0065). Signature and entry into force do not show that workers are better protected: the Government's claim of better social protection for Moldovans working abroad is not backed by data on coverage or benefits paid, so success cannot be assessed (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0062). The Government also says it cannot commit to a number of agreements because this depends on the political will of other states (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0496). What remains is agreements with the Member States not yet covered, entry into force of those signed, and gap analyses on the coordination regulations (MDA-EU-2026-012-0013; MDA-EU-2026-012-0093). The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2023 analytical report described the existing agreements, noting that they apply the principles of equal treatment and aggregation (MDA-EU-2023-002-0027). The 2023 report asked Moldova to work on concluding additional coordination agreements, notably with Member States, and to step up signing them (MDA-EU-2023-001-0045; MDA-EU-2023-001-0063), and judged progress limited (MDA-EU-2023-001-0042). The 2024 report repeated the recommendation and still found limited progress (MDA-EU-2024-001-0015; MDA-EU-2024-001-0017). In 2025 the Commission found good progress through new agreements, and widened the recommendation to cover IT infrastructure for the European Health Insurance Card and the electronic exchange of social security information (MDA-EU-2025-001-0017; MDA-EU-2025-001-0021). The 2026 report again found good progress, citing the Slovakia agreement, kept the recommendation to conclude more agreements and added a request for gap analyses on the coordination regulations (MDA-EU-2026-012-0009; MDA-EU-2026-012-0013). Each report from 2024 to 2026 says that negotiations with other Member States have been initiated and should continue (MDA-EU-2024-001-0063; MDA-EU-2025-001-0074; MDA-EU-2026-012-0093).

## Open questions

- It is not known how many workers and pensioners benefit from the agreements; data from the National Social Insurance House on claims handled and benefits paid under each agreement would show whether they work in practice (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0062).
- The dates of entry into force of several listed agreements, and of the agreements with France and Slovakia, are not given; the treaty register or official gazette entries would settle which agreements are in force (MDA-EU-2026-012-0091).
- Which Member States are still without an agreement, and how far negotiations with them have gone, is not set out; a list from the Government of agreements under negotiation and the planned presidential decrees would answer it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0093; MDA-EU-2026-012-0092).
- Whether the gap analyses on the coordination regulations have been started is not recorded; the analyses themselves, or a Government plan for them, would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0013).
