## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to have a digital transformation strategy and the institutional capacity to implement it (MDA-EU-2023-001-0076; MDA-EU-2026-012-0022). The Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030 was adopted in September 2023 (MDA-EU-2023-001-0088), a National Council for Digital Transformation was set up in November 2023 to coordinate it (MDA-EU-2024-001-0073), and a programme implementing the strategy for 2025 to 2027 was adopted in May 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0081). The Commission reports tangible progress on the strategy's key performance indicators in 2025 and 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0080; MDA-EU-2026-012-0104). Earlier experience shows why delivery matters: the expired Digital Moldova 2020 strategy met only 6 of its 12 targets (MDA-EU-2023-001-0085). Capacity remains the weak point. The Government estimates the programme at 613 million lei, of which 225 million lei are uncovered (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0172), and states that implementation requires building capacity in the ministry and the e-Governance Agency (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0169). It names the lack of centralised coordination of digitalisation initiatives as the main barrier (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0103), and a public body consulted on the programme warned that the two bodies are responsible for most of its 68 actions (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0281). Government sources also cite low pay for IT staff, scarce resources and staff turnover after reorganisations (MDA-GOV-2023-001-0020; MDA-GOV-2024-004-0006; MDA-GOV-2024-001-0018), and the Court of Accounts found a recommendation on documenting information systems only partly implemented, citing insufficient institutional capacity (MDA-GOV-2024-001-0035; MDA-GOV-2024-001-0106). What remains is the staff, funding and coordination capacity to deliver and monitor the strategy. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 2023 report asked Moldova to adopt the Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030 and ensure the institutional capacity to implement it, and to set up a National Digital Transformation Council (MDA-EU-2023-001-0076; MDA-EU-2023-001-0089). Once the strategy was adopted, the 2024 key recommendation shifted to ensuring capacity to implement its priority areas, in particular alignment on electronic identification, data protection and cybersecurity, including the 5G Toolbox (MDA-EU-2024-001-0027). The 2025 and 2026 reports keep this wording, asking for capacity to "continue" implementation, with the focus on data protection and cybersecurity standards (MDA-EU-2025-001-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0022). Over the same period the Commission's findings moved from some progress in 2024 to good progress in 2025 and 2026, on the strength of progress against the strategy's indicators (MDA-EU-2024-001-0023; MDA-EU-2025-001-0026; MDA-EU-2026-012-0017).

## Open questions

- How the uncovered 225 million lei of the implementation programme will be financed is not known; budget allocations or donor commitments for the programme would show whether it can be delivered (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0172).
- It is not shown how well the National Council for Digital Transformation coordinates implementation; minutes of its meetings or an evaluation of its work would settle it (MDA-EU-2024-001-0073).
- The sources disagree on whether all state information systems have been registered in the state register: SIGMA says they have, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed; an updated audit or register extract would resolve this (MDA-GOV-2024-001-0088; MDA-IO-2023-001-0050).
- The sources also give different targets for the share of digitised public services, 75% by the end of 2024 in a civil society account against 80% and 100% in the strategy's indicator table; the Government's annual implementation report against the agreed indicators would clarify which target applies (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0041; MDA-GOV-2023-001-0118).
