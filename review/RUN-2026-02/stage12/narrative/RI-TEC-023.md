## In brief

The Commission has asked Moldova to make its new national cybersecurity authority operational, and the Reform Agenda set the step of making the National Agency for Cybersecurity fully operational with a national CERT by December 2025 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0085; MDA-EU-2025-002-0036). Moldova established the Agency in December 2023, launched it in February 2024, and brought the law on cybersecurity into force in January 2025 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0084; MDA-EU-2024-001-0094; MDA-EU-2026-004-0017). With EU funding, seconded experts and training, the Agency had 32 staff by December 2025, and the Commission reports that it became operational as the single point of contact and national CERT, with an incident management platform in use (MDA-EU-2026-004-0012; MDA-EU-2026-004-0027; MDA-EU-2026-004-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0124; MDA-EU-2026-004-0015). The Commission records 38 incidents handled and 52 vulnerability notifications in 2025, and judged the Reform Agenda step satisfactorily fulfilled (MDA-EU-2026-004-0016; MDA-EU-2026-004-0033). Gaps remain in the Agency's own account: supervision was only partly carried out for lack of staff and a complete framework, its security monitoring system was still being configured, its incident playbooks were 70% complete, and it lacks qualified IT specialists (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0036; MDA-GOV-2026-010-0018; MDA-GOV-2026-010-0024; MDA-GOV-2026-010-0010). The Government reports that cyber incidents rose by over 30% between 2021 and 2023, and the Commission notes security vulnerabilities in the border police information system (MDA-GOV-2025-003-0238; MDA-EU-2026-012-0061). The Agency's reports are machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's wording has moved from a missing institution to its operational capacity and then to fulfilment. In 2021 it found that a national CERT foreseen by the 2019 to 2024 information security strategy was pending approval of its mandate (MDA-EU-2021-001-0003). In 2023 it described the Information Technology and Cyber Security Service as the government's incident response team (MDA-EU-2023-001-0097). In 2024, after the Agency was set up, it called for further effort to ensure its operational capacity, and in 2025 it found progress but said further efforts were needed (MDA-EU-2024-001-0085; MDA-EU-2025-001-0093; MDA-EU-2025-001-0094). The Reform Agenda then made the Agency's full operation and a national CERT a step, noting that the Agency lacked staff, IT resources and legal authority (MDA-EU-2025-002-0036; MDA-EU-2025-002-0031). In 2026 the Commission judged the step satisfactorily fulfilled and described the Agency as further strengthened, without repeating a recommendation on its capacity, while pointing to vulnerabilities in a specific state system (MDA-EU-2026-004-0033; MDA-EU-2026-012-0159; MDA-EU-2026-012-0061).

## Open questions

- Whether the Agency reaches its planned staffing and recruits qualified IT specialists would be shown by staffing figures after 2025 (MDA-EU-2026-004-0012; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0141).
- Whether the Agency fully supervises essential and important service providers, which it reports doing only in part, would be shown by audit and supervision records corroborated by an independent source (MDA-GOV-2026-010-0036).
- Whether the national incident and crisis response plan is drawn up and the State Register of Cyber Incidents built would be shown by the approved plan and the register in operation (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0021; MDA-GOV-2026-010-0026).
- The Government's claim of substantial progress towards a resilient cyber ecosystem is not confirmed independently, and incident trends after 2023 from an independent source would settle it (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0017; MDA-GOV-2025-003-0238).
