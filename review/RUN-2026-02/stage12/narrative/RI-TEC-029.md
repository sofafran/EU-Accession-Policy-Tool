## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to build customs IT systems that enable a fully digital customs environment interoperable with the EU, to adopt Customs Code amendments that give these systems a legal basis, and to advance its customs IT programme, prioritising core interconnectivity systems and a single window (MDA-EU-2026-012-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0031). Moldova migrated nationwide to Phase 5 of the New Computerised Transit System in October 2024 and completed the Phase 6 rollout in September 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0103; MDA-EU-2026-012-0146). The Customs Decisions System was given its legal framework by Government Decision No. 222/2026 and was fully operational by June 2026, with all 17 types of applications and authorisations handled electronically (MDA-EU-2026-006-0001; MDA-EU-2026-006-0002). In October 2025 the Government approved a programme for harmonising customs information systems for 2025-2030 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0151). Beyond adoption, there is shown effect: the Commission reports that the transit system made possible Moldova's full accession to the Common Transit Convention in November 2025 and significantly enhanced the interoperability of its customs operations, and the evidence attributes this result firmly to the transit system (MDA-EU-2026-012-0147). The Commission also judged the related Reform Agenda steps satisfactorily fulfilled (MDA-EU-2026-004-0011; MDA-EU-2026-006-0004). Only three of the programme's 25 systems are operational (MDA-EU-2026-012-0152), implementation of the Union Customs Code remains incomplete because of transitional provisions and a lack of IT tools (MDA-EU-2026-012-0148), and the Government states that the programme has no budget coverage (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0535). What remains is the Customs Code amendments and most of the programme's systems, including the core interconnectivity systems and a single window. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

In 2021 the Commission described Moldova's accession to the Common Transit Convention and the transit system as uncertain (MDA-EU-2021-001-0008). The 2023 analytical report said customs IT needed to be brought in line with EU standards, and the 2023 report asked Moldova to prepare an IT strategy for a paperless environment interoperable with EU systems and to finalise the IT system needed to join the Convention (MDA-EU-2023-002-0068; MDA-EU-2023-001-0106; MDA-EU-2023-001-0107). In 2024 the request became approval and implementation of the Customs Service IT strategy and full operation of the transit system (MDA-EU-2024-001-0032; MDA-EU-2024-001-0033). The 2025 report found good progress on the transit system and asked for a multi-annual IT programme and a fully operational Customs Decisions System (MDA-EU-2025-001-0034; MDA-EU-2025-001-0035), matching Reform Agenda steps for December 2025 and June 2026 (MDA-EU-2025-002-0018; MDA-EU-2025-002-0019). With those delivered, the 2026 report moves on to the Customs Code amendments, the IT programme's core interconnectivity systems and a single window (MDA-EU-2026-012-0030; MDA-EU-2026-012-0031).

## Open questions

- How the 2025-2030 customs IT programme will be funded is not settled; a budget allocation or donor commitment covering the programme would show whether the pace can be kept (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0535).
- The draft law amending the Customs Code is not shown adopted; the adopted law would provide the legal basis for full automation that the Commission asks for (MDA-EU-2026-012-0149).
- The technical arrangements for the Import Control System 2 were pending in 2025; a later monitoring mission or Commission finding would show whether they are in place (MDA-EU-2025-001-0104).
- No source reports how far traders use the Customs Decisions System; data from the Customs Service on applications and authorisations processed would show its use in practice (MDA-EU-2026-006-0002).
