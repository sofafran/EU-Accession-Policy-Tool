## In brief

Under the Chapter 2 acquis, national law has to be aligned with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 establishing the European Labour Authority, including the arrangements for cooperating with it, but no Commission requirement, key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on the Authority was found in the evidence. The Commission records that Moldova signed a cooperation agreement with the Authority in May 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0018; MDA-EU-2025-001-0071). The Government reports, in machine-translated documents, that the agreement was signed in Bratislava on 2 May 2025 and that Moldovan authorities then started a dialogue with the Authority on priority areas, including the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0051; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0052). The Government also states that a draft law to harmonise national legislation with the regulation is being prepared, planned for October 2026 and covering the Authority's role as mediator, and that it is strengthening the capacity of the labour ministry, the National Employment Agency and the State Labour Inspectorate to cooperate with the Authority (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0053; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0091; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0054). These are the Government's own account, no source shows the draft law adopted, and the capacity work names no specific measure. No outcome has been assessed. What remains is adopting and applying the law that aligns national legislation with the regulation, and building the capacity to cooperate with the Authority. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission has not set a requirement on this issue. No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 and 2024 reports mentions the European Labour Authority. The Commission's only references, in the 2025 report, record that Moldova signed a cooperation agreement with the Authority in May 2025 (MDA-EU-2025-001-0018; MDA-EU-2025-001-0071). Neither is a finding or recommendation on alignment with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149, and no Reform Agenda condition covers the issue. The only Chapter 2 sub-area wording found concerns legal alignment in general in 2023 and the European Health Insurance Card in 2025, so there is no sequence of Commission wording on the Authority from which to read a change. The issue therefore rests on the acquis itself and on the Government's 2025 accession programme, which plans the aligning law (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0053; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0091).

## Open questions

- Whether the draft law aligning national legislation with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 has been adopted, and whether it covers the Authority's role as mediator, would be settled by the adopted text or the Government's legislative record (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0053; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0091).
- The capacity building of the labour ministry, the employment agency and the Labour Inspectorate is stated only in general terms; a list of training, staffing or procedures put in place would show what has been done (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0054).
- Whether the dialogue started after the cooperation agreement has led to joint activity with the Authority rests on the Government's account alone; an Authority record of joint inspections, exchanges or meetings with Moldova would confirm it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0052).
- Whether the Commission regards alignment with the regulation as a requirement for Moldova is not known; a future report or screening finding on the Authority would settle it (MDA-EU-2025-001-0071).
