## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to align its personal data protection legislation with the General Data Protection Regulation and the Law Enforcement Directive, to ratify the amending protocol to Council of Europe Convention 108 and to adopt rules on standard contractual clauses between controllers and processors (MDA-EU-2025-001-0053; MDA-EU-2026-012-0022). Parliament adopted Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection on 25 July 2024 to align with the Regulation, and it entered into force in 2026 (MDA-EU-2024-001-0044; MDA-EU-2026-012-0052). In May 2026 Moldova ratified the second protocol to Convention 108, and in August 2026 it adopted a law on personal data processed for crime prevention, aligning with the Law Enforcement Directive (MDA-EU-2026-012-0055; MDA-EU-2026-012-0054). No source yet shows either law being applied, and no outcome has been assessed; in 2025 the data protection authority was still preparing guidelines on applying the new law (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0016). The order on standard contractual clauses exists only as a draft, and the Commission states in 2026 that Moldova should still implement these standards (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0385; MDA-EU-2026-012-0056). The review of related national laws, described in 2023 as pending since 2020 and recommended by the World Bank in 2024, is not reported as completed (MDA-EU-2023-001-0017; MDA-IO-2024-003-0038). What remains is the rules on standard contractual clauses and the alignment of related national laws with the new data protection laws. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is High and tractability is High.

## How the EU requirement has changed

In 2021 the Commission noted that a draft data protection law had been awaiting its second reading in Parliament since November 2018 (MDA-EU-2021-001-0005). The February 2023 analytical report found some laws aligned with the Regulation, eID and ePrivacy rules and called for secondary legislation, including on personal data protection, to be amended (MDA-EU-2023-002-0052; MDA-EU-2023-002-0061). The November 2023 report found the law only to some extent aligned with the Regulation and the Law Enforcement Directive and said implementing legislation needed amending (MDA-EU-2023-001-0016; MDA-EU-2023-001-0074). From 2024 the requirement entered the Chapter 10 key recommendations, as aligning legislation with data protection standards within the capacity to implement the digital strategy, and the 2024 report found the framework not yet aligned with the Law Enforcement Directive (MDA-EU-2024-001-0027; MDA-EU-2024-001-0045). The 2025 report made the requirement specific: alignment with the Law Enforcement Directive, ratification of the Convention 108 protocol and rules on standard contractual clauses, and it also called for better capacity at the data protection authority (MDA-EU-2025-001-0053; MDA-EU-2025-001-0051). By 2026, with two of these met, the key recommendation asks for further alignment and the remaining specific requirement is the contractual clauses (MDA-EU-2026-012-0022; MDA-EU-2026-012-0056). No Reform Agenda payment condition on the issue was found.

## Open questions

- Whether the order on standard contractual clauses has been adopted would be settled by its publication or a statement from the data protection authority (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0385; MDA-EU-2026-012-0056).
- Which related national laws still conflict with the new data protection laws is not known; a list of laws revised since 2020, or an updated gap analysis, would settle it (MDA-EU-2023-001-0017; MDA-IO-2024-003-0038).
- How Law No. 195/2024 and the law enforcement data protection law are applied is not shown; the data protection authority's first activity report under the new laws, with complaints and sanctions, would show it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0052; MDA-EU-2026-012-0054).
- Whether the Convention 108 protocol ratified in May 2026 is the amending protocol signed in February 2023 is not clear from the sources; the ratification law would settle it (MDA-EU-2023-001-0018; MDA-EU-2026-012-0055).
