## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to finalise the development of a new electronic procurement system and make it operational, and the Reform Agenda sets as a payment condition a pilot of the new system, with eTendering, eAwarding, eInvoicing, eComplaint and e-catalogues, by December 2027 (MDA-EU-2026-012-0006; MDA-EU-2025-002-0043). The current MTender system, launched in 2018, has enabled electronic procurement and public access to documents, but it lacks annual planning and contract management modules and does not offer all the procedures and award instruments set out in law (MDA-EU-2025-002-0074; MDA-EU-2025-002-0075; MDA-EU-2025-002-0076). Its re-engineering is part of the Government's public procurement development programme 2023-2026, and the Commission reports that development of a new system advanced with funding from international partners (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0378; MDA-EU-2025-001-0063). Government monitoring, in machine translation, records that technical specifications were received in September 2025, tender documents went to the World Bank in October 2025, and the tender to develop the new system was published in December 2025 with bids due on 10 February 2026 (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0013; MDA-GOV-2026-011-0014; MDA-GOV-2026-011-0016; MDA-GOV-2026-011-0019). The new system has not yet been built, and no outcome has been assessed. In 2026 the Commission still found the outdated MTender limiting the procurement agency's monitoring, since delivery, invoices, payments, amendments and performance are not captured digitally (MDA-EU-2026-012-0072). What remains is designing, building and putting into operation a new system that covers every procedure and stage of the procurement cycle. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

In 2021 the Commission found centralised procurement held back by inadequate e-procurement systems, called for improvements in e-procurement, and recorded that the macro-financial assistance memorandum required a re-design of the e-procurement system (MDA-EU-2021-001-0012; MDA-EU-2021-001-0001; MDA-EU-2021-001-0013). No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2024 report on e-procurement was found. The February 2023 analytical report found legal and technical problems limiting the system and called for it to be re-engineered, and the November 2023 report said it must be overhauled, as it did not cover all procedures or the whole procurement cycle (MDA-EU-2023-002-0003; MDA-EU-2023-002-0005; MDA-EU-2023-001-0110; MDA-EU-2023-001-0113). From 2025 the wording assumes a new system: the Reform Agenda set the December 2027 pilot, the 2025 release decision called for designing and developing the new system, and the 2025 key recommendation asked Moldova to speed up its design and development, describing MTender as outdated and a main bottleneck (MDA-EU-2025-002-0043; MDA-EU-2025-004-0001; MDA-EU-2025-001-0015; MDA-EU-2025-001-0064). In 2026 the key recommendation moved to finalising the system and making it operational, while the Commission recorded limited progress on digitalising procurement processes (MDA-EU-2026-012-0006; MDA-EU-2026-012-0070).

## Open questions

- Whether a developer was contracted after the February 2026 bid deadline, and on what timetable, would be settled by the contract award notice (MDA-GOV-2026-011-0019).
- Whether the concept of the new e-procurement information system has been approved by Government decision is not clear; the published decision would settle it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0384; MDA-GOV-2026-011-0012).
- Whether the pilot will meet the December 2027 Reform Agenda condition, with all the required modules, would be shown by the Commission's assessment of that payment request (MDA-EU-2025-002-0043).
- Whether the operator has enough staff to run the new system is not assessed; only 18 of its staff work on electronic procurement, and a staffing plan for the new system would show it (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0380).
