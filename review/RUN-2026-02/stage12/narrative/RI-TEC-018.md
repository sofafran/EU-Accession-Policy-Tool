## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to increase the capacity of its data protection supervisory authority, the National Centre for Personal Data Protection, especially its staff (MDA-EU-2024-001-0047; MDA-EU-2025-001-0055). Moldova adopted Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, which entered into force in August 2026, widens the authority's mandate and creates a data protection inspector function (MDA-EU-2026-012-0053; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0350). Strengthening the authority's administrative capacity, especially its human resources, is set as an objective for 2026 but has not yet been carried out (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0079). No increase in capacity is shown. The Commission found 39 staff for 45 posts in 2023, and the authority reports 36 staff at the end of 2025, nine short of its limit, with a staff turnover rate of 18% (MDA-EU-2023-001-0019; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0060; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0062). In 2026 the Commission found that the authority's human, technical and financial capacities had not grown with its expanded mandate (MDA-EU-2026-012-0053). In its own account, the authority reports pay below comparable supervisory bodies, too few staff in its surveillance and legal departments, and pressure, intimidation and abusive challenges against its staff without adequate safeguards (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0068; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0069; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0071). What remains is to give the authority the staff, pay, budget and safeguards it needs to supervise independently under the new law. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's wording has moved from description to a repeated recommendation and then to a finding tied to the new law. In 2021 it recorded that civil society and the media had criticised the draft data protection law for limiting access to information and widening the authority's powers (MDA-EU-2021-001-0006). The 2023 report described the authority's position: 39 staff for 45 authorised posts, the lowest since 2018, high turnover and difficulty attracting and retaining staff because of low salaries and the expertise required (MDA-EU-2023-001-0019). The 2024 report recommended that the authority's capacity be increased, especially its staffing, and the 2025 report repeated this, noting that the authority was examining a growing number of complaints (MDA-EU-2024-001-0047; MDA-EU-2025-001-0054; MDA-EU-2025-001-0055). The 2025 Enlargement Communication asked for additional and sustained resources and stronger independence for such bodies in all enlargement countries (MKD-EU-2025-002-0020). In 2026 the Commission linked the shortfall to Law No. 195/2024, finding that the law had significantly expanded the mandate while capacities remained limited (MDA-EU-2026-012-0053). The finding has been recorded in four successive reports with no increase in capacity, but it is not a benchmark or a Reform Agenda payment condition in the evidence (MDA-EU-2026-012-0053).

## Open questions

- Whether the authority's staff numbers and pay rise to match its wider mandate would be shown by staffing and remuneration figures for 2026 and later (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0060; MDA-GOV-2026-009-0068).
- Whether data protection inspectors are appointed under Law No. 195/2024 and the law is applied in practice would be shown by appointment records and the authority's later activity reports (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0350).
- The authority's account of pressure and intimidation against its staff rests on its own report, and independent evidence, such as civil society monitoring or Commission findings on independence, would settle how serious it is (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0071).
- Whether the authority's budget grows with its mandate would be shown by the approved budgets after 2025 (MDA-GOV-2026-009-0063).
