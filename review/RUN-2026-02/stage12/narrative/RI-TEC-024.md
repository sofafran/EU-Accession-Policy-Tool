## In brief

The Commission asks Moldova to implement the EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, including assessing the risk profile of suppliers and putting in place restrictions or exclusions for high-risk suppliers (MDA-EU-2026-012-0130). The Reform Agenda sets full alignment by December 2027 as a step: a legal framework, an assessment of assets and suppliers, and restrictions on high-risk suppliers for critical and highly sensitive assets (MDA-EU-2025-002-0034). Moldova has started preparatory work. A grant agreement with Swedfund, approved in March 2025, funds a study of the impact and risks of the Toolbox (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0212), and in October 2025 the ministry contracted KPMG AB for a supplier risk assessment, recommendations on restricting high-risk suppliers, cost estimates, an operator compensation mechanism and a draft law (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0213). In April 2026 the Government reported the study still under way and a draft act on 5G network security still being finalised (MDA-GOV-2026-002-0113; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0114). The Commission's 2026 report records secondary legislation on the security of public 5G networks among Moldova's steps to align with the NIS 2 Directive (MDA-EU-2026-012-0125). A law on 5G network cybersecurity is planned by December 2027 (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0211). No source shows a supplier assessment completed or any restriction applied. The Government notes that compliance requires major investment in 5G networks (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0152). What remains is the 5G security law, the completed supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers applied to critical assets. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The 5G Toolbox entered the Commission's key recommendations in 2024, as part of building capacity to implement the Digital Transformation Strategy, and the same report found no progress on alignment (MDA-EU-2024-001-0027; MDA-EU-2024-001-0086). The 2025 report kept the Toolbox in the key recommendation and spelled out what implementation means: assessing the risk profile of suppliers and restricting or excluding high-risk suppliers (MDA-EU-2025-001-0030; MDA-EU-2025-001-0095). The 2025 Reform Agenda turned this into a step with a December 2027 deadline and a national risk assessment of 5G suppliers carried out in consultation with the EU (MDA-EU-2025-002-0034; MDA-EU-2025-002-0040). In 2026 the key recommendation again names the Toolbox, and the body of the report shifts from "continue" to asking Moldova to "start" implementing the measures, including restrictions or exclusions for high-risk suppliers (MDA-EU-2026-012-0022; MDA-EU-2026-012-0130).

## Open questions

- It is not clear whether the secondary legislation on security of public 5G networks that the Commission reports in 2026 is the Government decision that was still being finalised in April 2026; the adopted act and its date would settle it (MDA-EU-2026-012-0125; MDA-GOV-2026-002-0114).
- The results of the supplier risk assessment by KPMG AB are not in the evidence; the completed study would show which suppliers and assets are judged high risk (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0213).
- How the cost of replacing high-risk equipment will be met is not settled; the proposed operator compensation mechanism, and its funding, would show whether operators can comply (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0152; MDA-GOV-2025-002-0213).
- Whether the 5G security law will be adopted by December 2027 cannot yet be judged; a draft submitted to Parliament would show progress against the Reform Agenda deadline (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0211; MDA-EU-2025-002-0034).
