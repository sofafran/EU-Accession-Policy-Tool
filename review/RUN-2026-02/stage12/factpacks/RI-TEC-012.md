# Fact pack RI-TEC-012: Electronic communications regulator independence and market oversight weak

The electronic communications regulator lacks guaranteed independence in appointments and funding and sufficient capacity, and competitive safeguards in a concentrated telecom market are not applied.

## Validated issue judgements

- direction_of_travel: improving (validated). The Chapter 10 scale is context only, since this is part of the chapter: some level of preparation from 2023 to 2025 and moderately prepared in 2026. The 2026 report finds that the new legal framework reinforced the regulator's administrative and financial independence, and the Commission's 2026 release decision records access tariffs for the incumbent's infrastructure cut by 16% to 49% from 1 March 2026, against minimal network sharing (0.95%) in the 2025 baseline. The gain on independence is legal only, and an ITU finding reported in the Government's 2025 programme still describes independence and accountability as insufficient (machine translated). Items: MDA-EU-2023-001-0066;MDA-EU-2024-001-0022;MDA-EU-2025-001-0025;MDA-EU-2026-012-0016;MDA-EU-2026-012-0111;MDA-EU-2026-006-0013;MDA-EU-2025-002-0023;MDA-GOV-2025-003-0071
- unresolved_gap: partly_addressed (validated). The Reform Agenda requires at least 3% of the incumbent's infrastructure to be shared and network-sharing agreements by the incumbent; access tariffs have been cut and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act transposed in June 2026, but the agreements are still planned and no item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files reports the sharing rate after the 0.95% baseline. The law reinforcing the regulator's independence is adopted, and the regulator had filled 105 of 114 posts by March 2026. What remains is independence shown in practice and competitive safeguards that actually raise infrastructure sharing in a market where the incumbent holds more than half of broadband. Items: MDA-EU-2025-002-0033;MDA-EU-2025-002-0038;MDA-EU-2026-006-0013;MDA-EU-2026-006-0014;MDA-EU-2025-002-0024;MDA-EU-2026-012-0111;MDA-EU-2026-012-0110;MDA-CSO-2024-001-0035
- priority: Medium (validated). The Reform Agenda conditions on infrastructure sharing touch the competition side of this issue, but they are framed as implementing the Electronic Communications Code and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act, whose alignment belongs to the legal alignment issue. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found regulator independence in no benchmark, key recommendation or Reform Agenda payment condition; the Commission mentions it only in its 2026 finding that the new law reinforced it. Items: MDA-EU-2025-002-0033;MDA-EU-2025-002-0038;MDA-EU-2025-002-0024;MDA-EU-2026-012-0111
- tractability: High (validated). The levers are legal and regulatory and within national control, and recent delivery is strong: a new law in force from January 2026, updated access tariffs in force within the Reform Agenda timetable, working arrangements with BEREC from 2025 and a regulator close to full staffing. The main constraint is market concentration, with the incumbent holding more than half of broadband. ## Interventions and validated implementation status

- INT-MDA-0091 ARCOM Board Decision No. 22 of 20.06.2025 amending Board Decision No. 42/2017 on ex-ante measures for the wholesale local access market: adopted. The regulator reports that Board Decision No. 22 of 20 June 2025 amended its ex ante measures for the wholesale local access market.
- INT-MDA-0093 ARCOM Board decisions on access to property under Law No. 28/2016: implemented. The Commission reports that the updated infrastructure access tariffs entered into force on 1 March 2026 and that the reference offer was published on the incumbent operator's website. The regulator also reports issuing Board decisions where access disputes could not be settled by dialogue.
- INT-MDA-0095 ARCOM inspections: implemented. The regulator reports 36 inspections of electronic communications and postal services in 2025 and the main violations found. The figures are the regulator's own and machine-translated.
- INT-MDA-0210 Draft ANRC Board decisions implementing the European Electronic Communications Code: unclear. The Government listed these regulator board decisions as drafts in 2025. The regulator reports that it adopted the regulatory acts needed to implement the new electronic communications framework and continues drafting others in 2026, and the Commission reports approximated secondary legislation, but no source names these specific decisions as adopted.
- INT-MDA-0453 Law No. 72/2025 on electronic communications: adopted. Parliament adopted the law on 10 April 2025 and the Commission confirms it entered into force on 1 January 2026. The regulator was renamed under it, but in 2026 the secondary acts needed to implement it were still being drafted, and a civil society source notes only partial compatibility with Directive (EU) 2018/1972.
- INT-MDA-0457 Law on Electronic Communications (in force since 2017): reversed. The new Electronic Communications Law No. 72/2025 entered into force on 1 January 2026, as the Commission confirms, and the regulator was renamed under it. None of the linked items states the repeal of the earlier law in terms, but the new law has replaced it as the sector framework, which is a replacement rather than a policy reversal.
- INT-MDA-0465 Legal framework on ARCOM's independence: adopted. The Commission reports that the new legal framework reinforced ARCOM's administrative and financial independence. No source reports how it has been applied.
- INT-MDA-0488 Methodology for measuring and evaluating mobile service quality (ARCOM Board Decision No. 27 of 23.08.2024): implemented. The regulator reports that it repeatedly measured mobile coverage and quality under the methodology approved by Board Decision No. 27 of 23 August 2024. This rests on the regulator's own report.
- INT-MDA-0499 Mobile service quality measurements: implemented. The regulator reports that it has measured mobile service quality since 2019, covered 45 localities and 35 national roads, and added 5G networks to the 2025 measurements. The evidence comes only from the regulator's own report.
- INT-MDA-0502 Modernisation of the National Spectrum Monitoring System (SNMS): implemented. The regulator reports investing about MDL 3.26 million in 2025 to improve the National Spectrum Monitoring System. The linked item is the regulator's own machine-translated report.
- INT-MDA-0560 Network-sharing agreements (Gigabit Infrastructure Act): proposed. The Reform Agenda commits the incumbent operator to implement network-sharing agreements in line with the Gigabit Infrastructure Act. The linked item records the commitment only.
- INT-MDA-0599 Public register of electronic communications network and service providers: implemented. The regulator reports that 315 providers were registered in the public register at the end of 2025, 20 fewer than a year earlier. The figures are the regulator's own and machine-translated.
- INT-MDA-0605 Radio frequency licence competitions (2024 and 2025): implemented. The regulator held the 2024 competition, announced the results in January 2025 and issued the licences to three operators in February 2025, and held a further competition in 2025 that awarded spectrum to Moldtelecom. All items come from the regulator's and the Government's own machine-translated reports, so the status rests on Medium confidence evidence and reported coverage gains are not independently corroborated.
- INT-MDA-0625 Renaming of ANRCETI as ARCOM: implemented. The regulator reports that ANRCETI was renamed the National Agency for Regulation in Communications from 1 January 2026, when Electronic Communications Law No. 72/2025 entered into force, which the Commission confirms.
- INT-MDA-0639 Secondary legislation on electronic communications aligned with the European Electronic Communications Code and the Roaming Regulation: adopted. The Commission reports that Moldova harmonised its primary legislation with the European Electronic Communications Code and parts of the Roaming Regulation, and that secondary legislation including measures under the Code was also approximated. No source yet describes how the secondary rules are applied.
- INT-MDA-0680 Transfer of civil radio frequency management to ANRCETI: implemented. The Commission reports that the communications regulator has absorbed the technical management of civil radio frequencies from the ministry's service, with powers to set fees and provide conformity assessment.
- INT-MDA-0694 Working Arrangements between ARCOM and BEREC: adopted. The regulator reports that it signed Working Arrangements with BEREC in March 2025, giving it participant status without voting rights. Its participation in three BEREC working groups is planned from 2026, and all items are the regulator's own machine-translated report.

## Outcomes

- INT-MDA-0694 leads to MDA-GOV-2026-014-0086: success insufficient_evidence, attribution Low. The only evidence is the regulator's own annual report, machine translated: none of the 1,451 Moldova items of this run from EU, international, civil society or research sources assesses the effect of the BEREC working arrangements, so success is not judged.
