# Fact pack RI-TEC-018: Data protection authority lacks independence and resources

The data protection supervisory authority depends on government for staffing and funding, has capacity below its tasks, and can be left without a director.

## Validated issue judgements

- direction_of_travel: persistent (validated). The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is only part of the chapter. The 2023 report finds 39 staff for 45 posts with high turnover, the 2024 and 2025 reports call for more capacity, especially staff, and the 2026 report says the authority's human, technical and financial capacities have not grown with its expanded mandate. The authority's own 2025 report gives 36 staff at the end of 2025 and a turnover rate of 18%, which confirms the finding but is its own account. Items: MDA-EU-2023-001-0019;MDA-EU-2024-001-0047;MDA-EU-2025-001-0054;MDA-EU-2026-012-0053;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0060;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0062
- unresolved_gap: open (validated). The Commission's latest finding is that capacities remain limited while Law No. 195/2024 has widened the mandate, and the response aimed at capacity, strengthening the authority's administrative and human resources, has not yet been carried out. The authority itself reports pay below comparable supervisory bodies, staff overload and pressure on staff, and gives this as its own account. What remains is giving the National Centre for Personal Data Protection the staff, pay, budget and safeguards it needs to supervise independently under the new law. Items: MDA-EU-2026-012-0053;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0079;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0068;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0069;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0071;MDA-GOV-2026-009-0064
- priority: High (validated). An adequately resourced and independent supervisory authority is a core requirement of the EU data protection acquis, and the Commission records the shortfall in the 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports with no increase in capacity recorded, which meets the test for High without a benchmark. The 2025 Enlargement Communication's call for more resources and independence for data protection authorities applies to all enlargement countries and only supports this. Items: MDA-EU-2023-001-0019;MDA-EU-2024-001-0047;MDA-EU-2025-001-0055;MDA-EU-2026-012-0053;MKD-EU-2025-002-0020
- tractability: Medium (validated). The remedy is within the government's control, through the staffing limit, pay and budget, and Law No. 195/2024 was adopted. Against that, staff numbers fell from 39 in the 2023 report to 36 at the end of 2025 despite repeated recommendations, and the authority links turnover to pay below comparable bodies, which needs wider pay decisions. ## Interventions and validated implementation status

- INT-MDA-0004 Code of Contraventions: adopted. The Code of Contraventions sets liability for processing personal data in breach of data protection law and for failing to comply with the data protection authority's requirements, and remains part of the framework until Law No. 195/2024 applies. No source describes sanctions imposed under these articles.
- INT-MDA-0175 Data protection inspector function (Law No. 195/2024): adopted. Law No. 195/2024, which entered into force in 2026, institutes the function of data protection inspector, and the Government notes that the data protection authority's capacity needs strengthening for it. No source shows inspectors appointed.
- INT-MDA-0270 Draft law on personal data protection: insufficient_evidence. Moldova later adopted Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, which is recorded as a separate intervention. No source says whether the 2018 draft became that law or was withdrawn, so its status at the freeze date cannot be established.
- INT-MDA-0429 Law No. 182/2008 on the National Centre for Personal Data Protection: unclear. The data protection authority operates under this Law and had 36 staff at the end of 2025, nine short of its staffing limit. The Government lists the Law among the acts making up the framework until the new data protection law entered into force in August 2026, and the linked items do not say whether it remains in force since then.
- INT-MDA-0430 Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection: adopted. Parliament adopted Law No. 195/2024 on 25 July 2024 to align with the GDPR, and it entered into force on 23 August 2026, as the Commission confirms. The sources describe preparation for its application, such as guidelines from the data protection authority, but none yet shows it being applied.
- INT-MDA-0459 Law on personal data protection (number not stated): implemented. A civil society report states that personal data is protected under a dedicated law and that the National Centre for Personal Data Protection monitors compliance. The source does not give the law's number, so it may refer to Law No. 133/2011, which remains in force until Law No. 195/2024 takes effect.
- INT-MDA-0649 State Budget Law No. 310/2024: adopted. The data protection authority reports its final approved budget for 2025 under the State Budget Law as MDL 12,705.6 thousand.
- INT-MDA-0665 Strengthening the administrative capacity of the National Centre for Personal Data Protection: proposed. Strengthening the Centre's capacity is set out as a planned measure. At the end of 2025 it had 36 staff against a limit of 45, and no source reports the strengthening carried out.
- INT-MDA-0666 Strengthening the preliminary complaint assessment procedure: proposed. The data protection authority states that strengthening its preliminary complaint assessment procedure remains a priority. No source shows the change made.
