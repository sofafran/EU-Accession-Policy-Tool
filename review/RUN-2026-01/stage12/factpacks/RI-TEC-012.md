# Fact pack RI-TEC-012: Electronic communications regulator independence and market oversight weak

The electronic communications regulator lacks guaranteed independence in appointments and funding and sufficient capacity, and competitive safeguards in a concentrated telecom market are not applied.

## Validated issue judgements

- direction_of_travel: persistent (validated). Commission concern about the regulator's independence recurs in nearly unchanged terms in each report from 2020 to 2025, with no recorded improvement, so the default applies; in September 2024 parliament dismissed all five commission members, which the source reports without saying what it meant for independence. The chapter scale was not decisive. The competition part has moved a little, from no relevant developments in 2020 and 2021 to a market still shared by two operators in 2023 and a new operator taking part in a frequency tender in 2025, so the two parts of this issue do not move together. - tractability: Medium (validated). The two parts differ. Independence looks hard, because it depends on appointments by parliament and exposure to political interests, and six years of reports record no progress, while competition looks more realistic because a tender has reached a compliant bid. That tender rests on one bidder and was held up by elections and a missing opinion from the anti-corruption commission, as machine-translated regulator reporting states, so Medium is a blend that would not hold if the issue were split. Items: MKD-EU-2021-001-0096;MKD-EU-2024-001-0087;MKD-EU-2025-001-0133;MKD-EU-2025-001-0144;MKD-GOV-2026-003-0012;MKD-GOV-2026-003-0013;MKD-GOV-2025-004-0112
- unresolved_gap: open (validated). The 2026 report again asks the Agency for Electronic Communications to increase its institutional independence, and the 2025 law's statement that the Agency is independent is a legal provision, not evidence of practice. The competition part has moved, as a new operator was licensed in January 2026 as the third mobile network operator after a tender with a sole bidder, although no item yet shows it operating or its effect on a market held mainly by two operators. What remains is independence shown in appointments and practice, the third operator's actual entry, and resolving the financing provisions for the Operational and Technical Agency, on which the Commission and the country disagree and which concern that agency and not the regulator itself; the gap stays open because the requirement repeated in 2026 concerns independence, on which nothing has changed. Items: MKD-EU-2026-007-0112;MKD-EU-2026-007-0118;MKD-EU-2025-001-0023;MKD-EU-2025-001-0133;MKD-EU-2025-014-0006;MKD-GOV-2025-008-0005;MKD-GOV-2026-003-0012;MKD-GOV-2026-003-0006;MKD-EU-2026-002-0009;MKD-EU-2026-002-0010;MKD-EU-2026-002-0012
- priority: High (validated). Strengthening the independence and capacity of this regulator was a key Commission recommendation in every report from 2021 to 2025, and an independent regulator is central to the digital infrastructure theme. The 2026 key recommendation names only the media regulator and the public service broadcaster, and the call for the Agency for Electronic Communications to increase its independence moves to body text, so High rests on five earlier reports and a requirement still stated in 2026; it is not a Reform Agenda step, and if the 2027 report also leaves it out of the key recommendations, Medium would fit. The competition part is supported by repeated but less prominent findings and did not drive the rating. ## Interventions and validated implementation status

- INT-MKD-0023 Law on Electronic Communications 2025: adopted. The Commission reports that the new law became fully applicable in June 2026, which settles the date of application. No item reports by-laws or measures taken under it, and the Commission's statement that it strengthens consumer protection and competition is a stated outcome, not evidence of application in practice.
- INT-MKD-0189 5G spectrum assignment: in_practice. The Commission reports that approvals for 5G frequencies were granted and that 5G coverage keeps expanding, and the State Audit Office reports 96.99% population coverage. One frequency remained to be tendered in 2024, and that tender is rated separately as INT-MKD-0512.
- INT-MKD-0512 Public tender for radio frequency bands 700 MHz, 1800 MHz and 3.x GHz: proposed. The regulator published the tender in 2025 and received one compliant bid, but the decision was postponed and the deadline moved to 12 January 2026. No source gives the outcome, so the assignment is treated as work under way and not concluded.
- INT-MKD-0529 Third mobile network operator: proposed. The strategy says an obligation to introduce a third operator will be set, and no item shows it done.

## Outcomes

- INT-MKD-0512 leads to MKD-GOV-2026-003-0012: success insufficient_evidence, attribution insufficient_evidence. The sole bid and the extended deadline of 12 January 2026 come only from machine-translated regulator reporting, and the Commission confirms only that a new operator took part in a tender. No item shows an award or any effect on competition, so no result can be judged from the evidence held.
