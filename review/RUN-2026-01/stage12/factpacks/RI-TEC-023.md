# Fact pack RI-TEC-023: Operational cybersecurity capacity of public institutions weak

The national incident response centre and state institutions lack staff, expertise and secure infrastructure, so government services and digital infrastructure remain exposed to attacks.

## Validated issue judgements

- direction_of_travel: persistent (validated). The Commission's wording is essentially unchanged across three reports, that state institutions need stronger capacity and infrastructure and the national centre more staff (2023, 2024, 2025), and its reform programme assessments repeat large or major deficiencies in 2025 and 2026, so the default of persistent applies; the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. Incident counts cannot show a trend in capacity, because reporting was not mandatory and the figures are not comparable (1,441 or 1,443 for 2020, 2,804 for 2022, about 170 in 2023 on an unstated definition). The World Bank's advanced rating and the audit's finding of poor protection are a noted difference and not a conflict, since an index of national commitments and a test of practice measure different things, and the 2024 index itself lists technical and capacity measures as areas for growth. - unresolved_gap: open (validated). The latest EU items, of November 2025 and April 2026, still call for significant strengthening and report major deficiencies, while responses are proposed as adopted on paper (the law, strategy and action plan, the national and government response teams) or only planned (government data centre, secure network, security operations centre), and the disaster recovery centre is used by 3 of about 140 bodies; these statuses are themselves proposals for review. What remains is staffed and trained response teams working to the Reform Agenda standard, security officers and tested plans in institutions, and secure government infrastructure in actual use. The Reform Agenda's 2024 baseline of CERTs not operational sits against statements that MKD-CIRT is in place, possibly because the baseline means NIS2 readiness, and the audit evidence behind this finding is machine translated, capped at Medium confidence, with percentages from a survey of 18 institutions. Items: MKD-EU-2025-001-0141;MKD-EU-2025-001-0142;MKD-EU-2026-003-0009;MKD-EU-2024-007-0003;MKD-EU-2024-007-0007;MKD-GOV-2025-007-0025;MKD-GOV-2025-007-0057;MKD-GOV-2025-004-0043;MKD-GOV-2024-001-0046;MKD-GOV-2024-002-0004;MKD-EU-2025-004-0093;MKD-GOV-2025-005-0044;MKD-GOV-2024-008-0043;MKD-GOV-2024-008-0054;MKD-GOV-2024-008-0068
- priority: High (validated). Operational national and government CERTs, a staffed competent authority and a secure government network are Reform Agenda payment steps, and the Commission has repeated the need for capacity in its 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports. The Commission's reform programme assessments also name these deficiencies, and without operating capacity the legal framework has little effect, so the issue is central to the theme. The rating does not rest on the volume of audit and action plan items. Items: MKD-EU-2024-007-0003;MKD-EU-2024-007-0004;MKD-EU-2024-007-0007;MKD-GOV-2024-002-0004;MKD-EU-2023-001-0147;MKD-EU-2024-001-0097;MKD-EU-2025-001-0142;MKD-EU-2025-012-0010;MKD-EU-2026-003-0009
- tractability: Low (validated). Progress needs people the state has struggled to hire and keep (IT posts about half filled, 3 of 11 MKD-CIRT posts filled, a shortage the strategy says extends across the region), action across about 140 bodies, and budgets the strategy itself calls at risk, while the main infrastructure is at pre-feasibility stage and looks to outside funding. Past progress is weak, as most surveyed institutions had no response team five years after the previous strategy called for them, and the new 40% salary supplement is adopted with no item yet showing its effect. Strengthening MKD-CIRT alone would be more achievable than system-wide capacity, and the audit and strategy items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence. ## Interventions and validated implementation status

- INT-MKD-0023 Law on Electronic Communications 2025: adopted. The law was adopted on 27 June 2025 and amended on 29 December 2025; it entered into force on the eighth day after publication on 4 July 2025 and was due to apply from 1 June 2026, but no later item confirms application or reports measures under it, and the security supervision reported for 2025 rests on the earlier law. By-laws were due within 15 months (October 2026) and operators have 18 months to align (January 2027), so that deadline has not yet passed. The Commission's May 2026 finding that the step is not achieved, with a grace period to 30 June 2026 whose outcome is not in the evidence, concerns alignment, and 'draft' or 'proposal' wording in GOV-2025-004-0172 and GOV-2026-002-0100 is historic rather than a conflict.
- INT-MKD-0035 Law on Electronic Management and Electronic Services: implemented. The law was adopted in 2019 and some rulebooks and the interoperability platform are in place, but the State Audit Office found only 2.93% of obliged institutions connected and parent laws not aligned. I rely on the audit over the government's description of the framework, so this is not in_practice. The 2025 strategy still calls for full application.
- INT-MKD-0042 Law on Security of Network and Information Systems 2025: adopted. The law was adopted on 27 June 2025, published on 4 July 2025, entered into force on the eighth day and applies from 1 January 2026, except that entities in Art. 4(2) points 8 to 16 are covered only from EU accession. No item shows the measures it requires, such as the by-laws (due within six months), the entity lists, or the government team and network (due within twelve months, so by July 2026), and MKD-CIRT predates the law; the regulator's 'applies' statement gives the legal status only. 'Proposal' in GOV-2026-002-0097 comes from an annex of 2025 opinions, so it is historic rather than a conflict, and the Commission's finding that alignment is incomplete concerns content, not status.
- INT-MKD-0079 ICT Development Strategy SMART/MK: 2030: adopted. The Commission reports in November 2025 that the country adopted a long-term ICT strategy and action plan, and the strategy text says some activities began before formal adoption. The goals in the text are targets, so they do not show delivery. The link between the Commission's sentence and SMART/MK is inferred from timing and is for the analyst to confirm.
- INT-MKD-0085 Cyber Security Strategy 2025-2028: adopted. The Government adopted the strategy on 21 January 2025, which the Commission confirms; a February 2025 Commission item still planning adoption is out of date. The only item saying more is the Agency for Electronic Communications' one-line status that the strategy 'is applied', which is government reporting without specifics, comes from a body other than the lead ministry, and which I have not accepted, so the reviewer should decide. The new cyber security law is one delivered measure, but no item reports wider delivery or the half-yearly reports the strategy promises. Sources conflict: tier 3 for the reviewers.
- INT-MKD-0086 Action Plan 2025-2027 for implementing the Cyber Security Strategy 2025-2028: adopted. The plan's own rows are planned activities, but the Commission states that the Government adopted the accompanying action plan on 21 January 2025, so the plan as a whole is adopted (the Commission labels it 2025-2028, the document 2025-2027). Only one row is shown delivered, the cyber security law, adopted in June 2025. No item reports on other rows, including those due by end 2025, so nothing supports implemented.
- INT-MKD-0099 Reform 2.2.1.1 Setting up a comprehensive framework for cyber resilience: adopted. The items in the input are plans, but later Commission evidence shows the Law on Security of Networks and Information Systems adopted in June 2025 and the cybersecurity strategy adopted in January 2025, and the step accepted as achieved although alignment is not complete. The 15 by-laws and the operational authority fall under later steps, and no item shows them delivered. The law itself is a separate entity.
- INT-MKD-0100 Reform 2.2.2.1 Secure Digital infrastructures roll-out: proposed. The reform as a whole is a Reform Agenda plan for infrastructure, 5G and related law. A new Law on Electronic Communications was adopted in June 2025 as one part, but no item shows the secure infrastructure rolled out.
- INT-MKD-0114 NIS2 competent authority: adopted. The 2025 law designates the Ministry and the regulator as competent authorities, and the cyber strategy names the Ministry. No item shows the authority staffed or performing supervisory checks, the June 2026 Reform Agenda test.
- INT-MKD-0115 National and governmental CERTs: adopted. The 2025 law establishes the national and governmental teams, with the governmental team to start within 12 months of entry into force, but no item shows either operating to NIS2 and SIM3 standard. The Reform Agenda's baseline says not operational (2024), while the regulator reports MKD-CIRT handling incidents and North Macedonia says it is in place; the baseline may mean NIS2 readiness only. Sources conflict: tier 3 for the reviewers.
- INT-MKD-0118 National ICT council: insufficient_evidence. The only evidence is two passages in one ITU profile of February 2022, describing a council monitoring projects. Sources do not say whether it is the National Cyber Security Council, and no later item mentions it. The difference with the audit's absent body was noted at G5 as different objects, so it is not treated as a conflict here.
- INT-MKD-0120 Operational Team for Cybersecurity and Hybrid Threats: implemented. Commission documents consistently report the team set up in 2022 and placed within the Ministry of Interior. No item shows what it has done in practice.
- INT-MKD-0128 Interoperability platform (MIM): in_practice. The State Audit Office found the platform operational but used by only a small share of institutions, and the Commission confirms use by customs and few institutions overall. The 2025 strategy gives 63 connected institutions, of which 37 actively exchange data. I rely on the audit and Commission over the screening statements that it simply functions.
- INT-MKD-0172 Government data centre: proposed. Every source describes the data centre as a future step, the latest being the October 2025 digital strategy, and the Reform Agenda step begins with a feasibility study. No source shows it built.
- INT-MKD-0173 Secure Government Network: proposed. The items are Reform Agenda and project descriptions with a December 2027 target. No item shows the network rolled out.
- INT-MKD-0175 Disaster recovery centre: implemented. The centre was built, but the government's own documents say only three institutions out of about 140 state bodies use it. The wider back-up of other institutions' systems is a step due by December 2027.
- INT-MKD-0184 Accelerated integration: engagement with ENISA: proposed. North Macedonia requested further engagement and the Commission said it would explore the possibilities. No item shows engagement agreed.
- INT-MKD-0218 Rulebook on entry in the list of legal persons (Level 1 certificates): adopted. The State Audit Office item refers to a duty on connected institutions to renew certificates by 29 August 2022, which shows the rule in force. It does not show compliance.
- INT-MKD-0253 Government Conclusion of the 131st session on mandatory online cyber security training: implemented. The Conclusion was adopted on 21 February 2023, and the State Audit Office cites over 19,500 attendees against 129,374 employees. The audit also found no monitoring of delivery, so application is shown to be partial and in_practice is not supported.
- INT-MKD-0254 Information with proposed measures to improve information system security 2023: in_practice. The Government adopted the information in February 2023, and the State Audit Office found 13% of institutions had not taken the measures, which implies most had. This is audit evidence and not government reporting.
- INT-MKD-0257 Government conclusions of June and September 2021 on connecting to the interoperability platform: adopted. The State Audit Office records the conclusions and the obligations they set. It also records only six requests for new connections despite them, so there is no evidence that the conclusions were carried out as intended.
- INT-MKD-0290 Interior Ministry disaster recovery and business continuity centre: implemented. The 2025 strategy says the Interior Ministry built the centre and that only 3 institutions use it. That is the government's own reporting, so it does not meet the test for in practice.
- INT-MKD-0358 EU for Secure Common Government Infrastructure: proposed. The source sets out the aim of the project. It reports no delivery.
- INT-MKD-0368 PESCO project 'EU Cyber Academy and Innovation Hub': proposed. The screening report says only that the country is interested in joining this project. No source reports that it has joined.
- INT-MKD-0379 Cyber Security and Digital Forensics Institute: proposed. The screening report refers to ongoing national work to establish the institute. No source shows that it exists.
- INT-MKD-0544 Security operations centre for the Government network (A 1.2.1.4): proposed. This is an action plan row in the SMART/MK action plan, so it is a plan however worded. No item shows delivery.

## Outcomes

- INT-MKD-0253 leads to MKD-GOV-2024-008-0047: success mixed, attribution Medium. Training was mandatory for state administration bodies, but the State Audit Office reports just over 19,500 attendees by December 2023 against 129,374 public administration employees, about 15%, and calls the 73% from its survey of 18 institutions not a real picture, with no monitoring set up. The denominator is wider than the bodies obliged and the snapshot is ten months after the Conclusion, so the evidence shows real but well short uptake and not outright failure. This is a single independent audit and not government self-reporting, machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, and no later figure exists.
- INT-MKD-0254 leads to MKD-GOV-2024-008-0064: success mixed, attribution Low. The State Audit Office found 13% of surveyed institutions had not taken the proposed measures, implying most had, but the same audit found 67% without two-factor authentication, which it calls a minimum measure, and no way of monitoring delivery; these two findings pull in different directions and are not resolved here. The percentages come from answers to a survey of 18 institutions, and the audit is independent and not government self-reporting, but it is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence.
