# Fact pack RI-TEC-006: Electronic public procurement tools underused

The electronic public procurement system works, but some of its modules, such as the e-marketplace for low-value contracts and e-catalogues, are incomplete or little used by contracting authorities, and its data are not yet used for red-flag analysis.

## Validated issue judgements

- direction_of_travel: improving (validated). The e-market moved from development in 2021 to launch in 2022, and awards rose from 70 in 2022 to 258 in 2023 (259 in the Reform Agenda, a minor difference of count), with 350 small-value transactions in 2024, though that item does not name the e-market. The Commission's finding of poor use in 2023 is not repeated later, and no later item shows change on e-catalogues or red-flag analysis. The Chapter 5 'Limited progress' wording of 2022 and 2025 is context only and was not decisive. Items: MKD-EU-2021-001-0048;MKD-EU-2022-001-0056;MKD-EU-2023-001-0074;MKD-EU-2023-001-0075;MKD-EU-2023-003-0137;MKD-EU-2024-001-0056;MKD-GOV-2024-001-0041;MKD-EU-2025-001-0091;MKD-EU-2025-001-0009
- priority: Medium (validated). E-market use is a Reform Agenda payment condition, which meets one limb of the High test, but the Commission finds the core system functions efficiently and the shortfall concerns low-value contracts and add-on modules. It is therefore not central to Chapter 5 or the theme, and the proposal rests on that reading of centrality. Items: MKD-EU-2024-007-0001;MKD-GOV-2024-002-0002;MKD-EU-2023-003-0138;MKD-EU-2025-001-0092
- tractability: High (validated). One bureau runs a platform that is already obligatory for all contracting authorities, awards rose about fivefold in two years, and a payment is attached to the target. The main limit is the confidence of contracting authorities, and the target is still more than double the 2024 count. Items: MKD-EU-2023-003-0112;MKD-EU-2023-001-0075;MKD-EU-2025-001-0091;MKD-EU-2023-003-0137;MKD-GOV-2024-002-0002
- unresolved_gap: partly_addressed (validated). The electronic procurement system operates efficiently and covers all transactions, and e-market use rose to 647 contracts in 2025 from 325 in 2024; the 2025 report gave 350 small-value transactions for 2024, a different measure, so the two 2024 figures are not compared. Against the Reform and Growth Facility, e-auctions fell to 22% of contract notices against a 40% target, and the e-market count remains below the 800 awards required in 2026. What remains is reaching the e-market target, raising e-auction use to the agreed share, wider take-up of e-catalogues and the data analysis the Commission asked for in 2023. Items: MKD-EU-2026-007-0062;MKD-EU-2026-007-0060;MKD-EU-2026-007-0061;MKD-EU-2025-001-0091;MKD-EU-2025-001-0092;MKD-EU-2024-007-0001;MKD-EU-2023-003-0114;MKD-EU-2023-003-0123

## Interventions and validated implementation status

- INT-MKD-0101 Reform 2.2.2.3 Widen the offer of public e-services: proposed. This is a Reform Agenda target to raise e-services by 50% by June 2027, and the Commission still described it as a measure in April 2026. No item reports the target met. The stated 2024 baseline differs between sources (392 and 264), which does not affect the status.
- INT-MKD-0132 Electronic System for Public Procurement (ESPP): in_practice. The Commission itself states the system is working efficiently in 2023 and 2024 and that its use is obligatory. This goes beyond the country's own description in the screening report.
- INT-MKD-0133 Upgrade of the electronic public procurement system: implemented. The Commission reports that the Bureau launched an updated system on 1 April 2025, following earlier upgrades. It also records limited progress, and no item gives evidence of use of the updated version.
- INT-MKD-0134 E-market system for low value procurement: in_practice. The Commission gives specific use: the e-market system awarded 258 small value procurements in 2023. The target of 800 awards by December 2026 remains a plan, and no item reports whether it was met.
- INT-MKD-0135 ESPP electronic archive and e-complaints modules: in_practice. Three Commission reports from 2021 to 2023 state what the modules do in current procedures, and the Commission also finds the wider e-procurement and e-appeals system working. Evidence after 2023 is absent and no usage figures are given.
- INT-MKD-0136 e-appeals function of the ESPP: in_practice. North Macedonia reports the function fully operational from 1 April 2019, and the Commission's own assessment in the 2023 screening report says the system provides for efficient and transparent appeals. This is the latest evidence, and nothing later contradicts it.
- INT-MKD-0137 Red flag system in the ESPP: in_practice. The Commission reported in 2021 that use of the red flags contributed to enhanced administrative control by the Bureau, and in 2023 that the portal includes the system. It still asks for further development, and there is no evidence after 2023.
- INT-MKD-0138 ESPP COVID-19 markers and data feed: implemented. The Commission says the Public Procurement Bureau developed this function in the e-procurement system. There is no evidence of use after 2021.
- INT-MKD-0139 SAC processing system: insufficient_evidence. The Commission described further improvement of the system as ongoing in 2021 and 2022, and no later item says whether it was completed. Four years without evidence is too thin to give a status at the freeze date.
- INT-MKD-0144 Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS): proposed. North Macedonia reported that documentation had been prepared in 2023, and the Reform Agenda calls the IFMIS reform ongoing in 2024. No item shows the system in operation.
- INT-MKD-0145 Integrated tax information system (ITIS): proposed. The 2022-2024 Economic Reform Programme foresaw the system and the Reform Agenda calls the work ongoing, while the Commission in 2025 still asks for a new integrated system. The World Bank wording suggests an ITIS already exists to be expanded; I follow the Commission and flag the difference. Sources conflict: tier 3 for the reviewers.
- INT-MKD-0211 Bylaw on low-value e-marketplace: insufficient_evidence. The Commission said in 2021 that adoption was pending, and no later source mentions the bylaw. The e-marketplace itself existed by 2023, but that does not show the bylaw was adopted.

## Outcomes

- INT-MKD-0137 leads to MKD-EU-2021-001-0051: success worked, attribution Medium. The Commission states that red flags contributed to enhanced administrative control by the Bureau. It is one qualitative statement with no measure, and the Commission still asked in 2023 for the system to be developed further.
- INT-MKD-0136 leads to MKD-EU-2023-003-0117: success worked, attribution Medium. The Commission assessment states that the system significantly benefited from e-appeals, and the country's description of the function fits, though it alone would not show success. The conflict on timing (still being developed in 2020; reported as fully operational from April 2019) does not affect the 2023 assessment but is unresolved.
- INT-MKD-0136 leads to MKD-EU-2023-003-0119: success worked, attribution High. The Commission assessment states that e-appeals provided an efficient and transparent system for submitting and conducting appeals. This comes from the same passage as LNK-000437 and is not independent of it, and no figures are given.
- INT-MKD-0136 leads to MKD-EU-2023-003-0121: success worked, attribution Medium. The Commission assessment states that a high level of transparency was achieved through the electronic procurement and e-appeals system, which is a finding on transparency only. It does not say that corruption fell.
- INT-MKD-0132 leads to MKD-EU-2023-003-0121: success worked, attribution Medium. The same Commission sentence as LNK-000439 credits the electronic system with a high level of transparency, and it says nothing on whether corruption fell. Its obligatory coverage is reported by the country and supports the claim only as description.

## Confirmed conflicts

- MKD-EU-2020-001-0096 vs MKD-EU-2023-003-0115: The Commission 2020 report says the e-appeals section was still being developed, while North Macedonia reports that e-appeals became fully operational on 1 April 2019.
