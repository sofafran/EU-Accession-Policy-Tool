## In brief

Under the Reform Agenda, the EU asks North Macedonia to join the EU Third Countries trusted list, make qualified trust services available, implement a Digital Identity Wallet and sign the Western Balkans mutual recognition agreement on trust services, and the Commission repeatedly finds the existing e-identification and e-signature tools not user friendly or free for citizens (MKD-EU-2024-007-0010; MKD-EU-2024-007-0014; MKD-EU-2025-001-0050). The 2019 law is in practice, two providers accredited in 2021 offer advanced and qualified signatures, and the Ministry of Digital Transformation has supervised them (MKD-EU-2026-002-0021; MKD-EU-2026-002-0022; MKD-EU-2026-002-0025). This provider outcome is assessed as a mixed success with Medium attribution. Electronic identity is in practice but little used, and the machine-translated audit found that high-level eID did not raise use (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0049; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0102; MKD-EU-2023-003-0042). The country requested trusted list inclusion on 30 October 2025, but the Commission considers the step not achieved, and the wallet and cross-border recognition remain proposed (MKD-EU-2026-002-0026; MKD-EU-2026-002-0029; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0074). The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The requirement has grown from usage to recognition. In 2021 the Commission noted only that electronic signature use had increased modestly (MKD-EU-2021-001-0013). In 2024 the Reform Agenda set payment conditions to join the trusted list, issue qualified certificates and implement a wallet and the regional agreement, and the annual report asked for alignment with the revised eIDAS Regulation and described the tools as cumbersome, not free and not user friendly (MKD-EU-2024-007-0010; MKD-EU-2024-007-0014; MKD-EU-2024-001-0030; MKD-EU-2024-001-0031). The 2025 report says the tools are "still" not user friendly or free (MKD-EU-2025-001-0050; MKD-EU-2025-001-0147). In May 2026 the Commission specified the outstanding trusted list tasks and set a grace period to 31 December 2026 (MKD-EU-2026-002-0028; MKD-EU-2026-002-0030).

## Open questions

- North Macedonia reports all trusted list preparations complete and considers the step achieved, while the Commission says the self-assessment and published list are outstanding and the step not achieved, a confirmed conflict that the Commission's decision by the end of the grace period would settle (MKD-EU-2026-002-0018; MKD-EU-2026-002-0024; MKD-EU-2026-002-0028; MKD-EU-2026-002-0029).
- The audit could not confirm use of the 150,000 digital identities announced through the Mastercard arrangement or who received 12,000 qualified signatures, in machine-translated text capped at Medium confidence, and certificate issuance and usage data would settle it (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0100; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0101).
- There are no current figures on how many citizens and businesses use eID and qualified signatures, and the latest benchmark score of 23% dates from 2022, so a newer benchmark or portal usage data would show whether use has changed (MKD-EU-2023-003-0042).
- It is not shown whether the failed procurement of institutional seal certificates, which left seals expired from May 2023, has been resolved, which a later audit or ministry report would confirm (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0135; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0111).
