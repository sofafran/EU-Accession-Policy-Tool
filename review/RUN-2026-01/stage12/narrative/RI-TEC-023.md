## In brief

The Commission asks state institutions to strengthen their cyber infrastructure and expertise and the national Computer Incident Response Centre to strengthen its staffing and operational capacity significantly, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to operational response teams, a staffed competent authority and a secure government network (MKD-EU-2025-001-0141; MKD-EU-2025-001-0142; MKD-EU-2024-007-0003; MKD-EU-2024-007-0004; MKD-EU-2024-007-0007). North Macedonia adopted a cyber security strategy and action plan in January 2025 and a law on network and information security in June 2025, which establishes the national and government response teams (MKD-GOV-2025-007-0025). The State Audit Office reports, in machine-translated findings, that only 3 of 11 MKD-CIRT posts were filled and that 67% of surveyed institutions had no response team, and the disaster recovery centre is used by 3 of about 140 bodies (MKD-GOV-2024-008-0054; MKD-GOV-2024-008-0068; MKD-GOV-2025-004-0043). In April 2026 the Commission still reports major deficiencies (MKD-EU-2026-003-0009). The validated assessment is that the gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High and tractability is Low.

## How the EU requirement has changed

From 2020 to 2023 the Commission frames cybersecurity mainly at regional level, as a priority for capacity building and resilience against hybrid threats (MKD-EU-2020-002-0019; MKD-EU-2021-002-0006; MKD-EU-2022-002-0021). From 2023 its country wording is essentially unchanged: state institutions need stronger cyber capacity, infrastructure and expertise, and the national response centre needs more staff (MKD-EU-2023-001-0147; MKD-EU-2024-001-0096; MKD-EU-2024-001-0097; MKD-EU-2025-001-0141). The 2025 wording sharpens this to significant strengthening of the centre (MKD-EU-2025-001-0142). In October 2024 the Reform Agenda adds concrete payment conditions on response teams, the competent authority and a secure network (MKD-EU-2024-007-0003; MKD-EU-2024-007-0004; MKD-EU-2024-007-0007). The Commission's reform programme assessments move from large deficiencies in 2025 to major deficiencies in 2026 (MKD-EU-2025-012-0009; MKD-EU-2026-003-0008).

## Open questions

- No item shows the national or government response teams operating to the Reform Agenda standard, and the Reform Agenda's 2024 baseline of not operational sits against statements that MKD-CIRT is in place, so a Commission payment assessment after June 2026 would settle their status (MKD-GOV-2024-002-0004; MKD-EU-2025-004-0093; MKD-GOV-2025-007-0057).
- Incident figures do not show a trend in capacity, since sources give conflicting counts for 2020 (1,443 and 1,441) and later figures use different definitions, so consistent mandatory incident reporting under the new law would be needed (MKD-GOV-2022-003-0006; MKD-GOV-2024-008-0055; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0048).
- The audit evidence on staffing and protection is machine translated, capped at Medium confidence, and based on a survey of 18 institutions, so an updated audit or staffing data would test whether capacity has changed (MKD-GOV-2024-008-0043; MKD-GOV-2024-008-0068).
- The new 40% salary supplement for cyber security staff is adopted but no item shows its effect on hiring or retention, so later vacancy data for MKD-CIRT and institutions would settle this (MKD-GOV-2025-007-0048; MKD-GOV-2022-004-0009).
