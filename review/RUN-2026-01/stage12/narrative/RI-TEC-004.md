## In brief

The Commission asks North Macedonia to put in place the basic systems that e-government needs, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to three steps: legislation allowing electronic documents to be archived, data storage infrastructure with an e-platform for record keeping and archival work, and a single document management system for all central institutions (MKD-EU-2023-003-0044; MKD-EU-2024-007-0011; MKD-EU-2024-007-0012; MKD-EU-2024-007-0013; MKD-GOV-2024-002-0014; MKD-GOV-2024-002-0015; MKD-GOV-2024-002-0016). Parliament adopted the Law on Archival Material and Archival Activity in June 2025 and the Commission considers that step achieved, but the law applies only from 1 June 2026 and its by-laws remain proposed (MKD-EU-2025-011-0039; MKD-EU-2025-011-0045; MKD-EU-2025-011-0043). The Commission's technical checks found the document management system only partially operational, and the government reports that 22 institutions use a very small part of it, on machine-translated figures (MKD-EU-2025-011-0046; MKD-GOV-2025-004-0076). The Commission's statement that adoption facilitates e-government is judged too early to show success (MKD-EU-2025-001-0047). The validated assessment is that the gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

In 2020 and 2023 the Commission's wording in this area concerns the human resources management information system, whose use it says increased but could be further exploited (MKD-EU-2020-001-0010; MKD-EU-2023-001-0214). In July 2023 the Commission states more broadly that basic systems such as a digital mailbox need to be put in place to advance e-government (MKD-EU-2023-003-0044). From October 2024 the requirement becomes concrete and conditional: the Reform Agenda sets three payment conditions on archiving legislation, storage infrastructure with an archival e-platform, and a single document management system for all central institutions (MKD-EU-2024-007-0011; MKD-EU-2024-007-0012; MKD-EU-2024-007-0013). In October 2025 the Commission explains the purpose, stating that the legal framework and a complete document management system will let institutions exchange and store documents electronically without printing (MKD-EU-2025-011-0044).

## Open questions

- No item shows whether the June 2026 and December 2026 Reform Agenda deadlines for the storage platform and the single document management system were met, so a Commission payment assessment or technical check after those dates would settle the status (MKD-GOV-2024-001-0083; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0084).
- The success of the archival law is judged too_early and its attribution insufficient_evidence, because no item shows use of e-government tools after the law began to apply; data on electronic archiving in practice after 1 June 2026 would settle this (MKD-EU-2025-001-0047; MKD-EU-2025-011-0043).
- The figures on how many institutions use the document management system come from machine-translated national strategies capped at Medium confidence, so an English or independent count of users would strengthen the evidence (MKD-GOV-2023-002-0062; MKD-GOV-2025-004-0076).
- The amendment of the archival law planned in 2023 and the general e-government framework are rated insufficient_evidence, so a source linking the 2023 plan to the 2025 law, or describing the framework, would settle their status (MKD-GOV-2023-002-0081; MKD-EU-2020-001-0122).
