## Reading this brief
This brief compares North Macedonia's position with three countries that faced the same Commission finding: Croatia before its accession in 2013, Montenegro, which closed Chapter 2 provisionally in 2026, and Serbia, where the finding has persisted. Each case keeps apart what was reformed, how it was implemented, what resulted and whether the result can be attributed to the reform. None of the three cases shows what happened to EU workers in practice: Croatia's completed alignment is not linked by any source to its Aliens Act, Montenegro's reform is too recent and its closing terms are not public, and Serbia is a stalled case. The cases therefore show what was tried and what to avoid, not a proven route. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses; no source states them as lessons for North Macedonia.

## Lessons across the cases
Three points recur across the cases. First, two countries wrote rules for EU nationals as a separate regime inside a general foreigners law, giving equal treatment with nationals and work without a permit: Croatia in its 2011 Aliens Act and Montenegro in its December 2025 amendments (HRV-EU-2007-001-0015; MNE-GOV-2026-001-0005). This answers the same legal mechanism the Commission names for North Macedonia, whose law treats EU citizens the same as other foreigners (MKD-EU-2025-001-0097). Second, both countries deferred some or all of these provisions to accession, and no case shows that deferral is enough: Croatia's rating is not attributed to the Act (HRV-EU-2012-001-0001), Montenegro's deferred clauses cannot yet have been applied, and Serbia reported a clause of this kind in 2015 while the Commission still found misalignment in 2015 and partial alignment in 2024 and 2025 (SRB-EU-2015-001-0002; SRB-EU-2015-001-0010; SRB-EU-2025-001-0005). Third, Serbia suggests that simplifying permits for all foreigners can earn a finding of limited progress while the difference in treatment the Commission names remains. No case addresses posts in private companies reserved for nationals, which is part of North Macedonia's gap (MKD-EU-2025-001-0013).

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (open) and on comparator cases validated at G6b, and each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and none is shown by the evidence to work.

**Option 1: Write equal treatment for EU nationals into the law, as a distinct regime.**
- *Gap addressed:* the law on access to the labour market does not give EU workers equal treatment with nationals (MKD-EU-2025-001-0097; MKD-EU-2025-003-0033).
- *Mechanism targeted:* legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).
- *Comparator evidence:* Croatia (relevance High) and Montenegro (relevance High) each placed EU nationals in a separate regime within their foreigners law, with equal treatment and permit-free work (HRV-EU-2007-001-0015; MNE-GOV-2026-001-0005). Transferability conditional: the design answers the same mechanism, but neither case shows that it earned the Commission's assessment or changed access for EU workers.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* North Macedonia has a separate law on employment of foreigners, so the regime would need to cover that law as well as any residence rules, not one act only; the provisions to be changed would first need to be listed.
- *Main risks:* drafting a clause that the Commission does not accept as full alignment, as in Serbia; leaving the reserved private sector posts untouched, since no case covers them.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government, through the ministry responsible for the law on employment of foreigners, now the Ministry of Economy and Labour (MKD-GOV-2025-001-0003, machine translated), with adoption by the Assembly.

**Option 2: Settle the commencement design early, rather than assume that deferral to accession is enough.**
- *Gap addressed:* the requirement that EU citizens can access the labour market without restrictions by accession (MKD-EU-2025-003-0033).
- *Mechanism targeted:* legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).
- *Comparator evidence:* all three cases. Croatia and Montenegro deferred provisions to accession, and Serbia reported a deferred clause while misalignment findings continued (SRB-EU-2015-001-0002; SRB-EU-2024-001-0004). Transferability conditional: this is a warning drawn from the tool's reading of three cases, not a tested lesson, and what closed Montenegro's benchmark is not public.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a clear view, through the negotiation process, of which provisions must apply before accession and which may start on accession.
- *Main risks:* a law that is aligned on paper but leaves EU workers inside the foreigner regime until accession, which may not satisfy a closing benchmark.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and its negotiating structures, with the ministry responsible for labour.

**Option 3: Do not treat permit simplification or the general non-discrimination rule as the answer.**
- *Gap addressed:* the Commission's finding that EU citizens are treated as any other foreigner (MKD-EU-2025-001-0097). North Macedonia points to the reference to nationality discrimination in its Law on Labour Relations (MKD-EU-2025-003-0019), but the validated gap judgement finds that this, being the country's own account, does not close the gap.
- *Mechanism targeted:* legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).
- *Comparator evidence:* Serbia (relevance Medium) simplified permits for all foreigners and reported a general non-discrimination clause, while the Commission still found that EU citizens were covered by the rules for third-country nationals (SRB-EU-2025-001-0005; SRB-EU-2025-001-0006). Transferability conditional: the point rests on the Commission's wording, not on a stated lesson, and Serbia's reasons for its slow pace are not in the evidence.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* any permit reform would be paired with an explicit provision on EU nationals, as in Option 1.
- *Main risks:* reporting progress on procedures while the gap the Commission names remains.
- *Who would need to act:* the ministry responsible for labour, and the Employment Service Agency where permit procedures change.

**Related step outside the comparator evidence.** Removing posts in private companies reserved for nationals is part of the gap but is not covered by any case, so no option is drafted on it here. The laws that reserve these posts are not identified in the evidence, and identifying them is an open question for the next run.
