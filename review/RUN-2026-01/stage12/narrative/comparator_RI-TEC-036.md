## Reading this brief
This brief compares North Macedonia's national e-services portal with three countries: Moldova, which digitalised services through a long donor project with assisted access; Albania, which placed most services on one portal under a law obliging institutions to use it; and Montenegro, which launched a new portal in December 2024. Moldova and Montenegro were rated of High relevance and Albania of Medium relevance, as reported use there is high rather than low. No case shows that a reform raised use of a portal: Moldova does not show which measure raised use, Albania's law is not shown to have caused its range or use, and Montenegro's results are too early. The two narrow results judged as having worked, one overlap removed in Albania and better information on Montenegro's portal, do not concern use or access. The cases show what was tried, what to measure and what to avoid. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses.

## Lessons across the cases
Three points recur. First, counts of digital services say little about use. In 2022 Moldova had 128 of 226 reported digital services with no users, although 51 of them had no requests on any channel (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0012; MDA-CSO-2023-001-0013). A Moldovan civil society source states that launch does not bring use, and this carries an item-level transferability judgement of conditional (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0007). Second, a portal without the most-needed services may improve information without improving the service: in Montenegro the monitor reports better information on the new portal (MNE-CSO-2025-001-0019) while none of seven essential sampled services was on it (MNE-CSO-2025-002-0002). This matters because the Commission found that North Macedonia's most commonly requested services were not yet online (MKD-EU-2024-001-0021). Third, a legal duty on institutions can sit alongside payment that often fails: Albania's law requires e-payment through the portal (ALB-CSO-2025-001-0040), but the monitor says users usually cannot pay (ALB-CSO-2025-001-0043). North Macedonia's 2025 strategy proposes a similar duty to integrate services on its portal (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0194, machine translated).

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (partly addressed) and on comparator cases validated at G6b, and each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and none is shown by the evidence to raise use.

**Option 1: Agree one service-by-service count, with use, and repeat it.**
- *Gap addressed:* the missing agreed count of fully transactional services, on which progress against the Reform Agenda target of a 50% increase cannot be fixed, because the government's baseline conflicts with the Commission's figures (MKD-EU-2024-007-0015; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0092; MKD-EU-2024-001-0023).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11), and unreliable data on what exists.
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova's 2022 count, made service by service and separating fully from partly digital services, showed the gap between availability and use (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0012), though it was not repeated and Moldova's counts still differ (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0002; MDA-CSO-2023-001-0005). Montenegro suggests that a delivered count may need an independent check on whether services are transactional (MNE-CSO-2025-001-0014). Transferability conditional: a count could help settle North Macedonia's conflicting figures if repeated, but neither case shows that it did so.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a shared definition of a fully transactional service, agreed with the Commission for the Reform Agenda, and data on use per service.
- *Main risks:* a count produced once for a payment condition and then dropped; a definition loose enough to inflate progress.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which leads digitalisation projects (MKD-EU-2023-003-0045), with the institutions that own the services.

**Option 2: Put the most-requested services online in full before widening the range.**
- *Gap addressed:* the much wider set of fully transactional, commonly requested services that people use (MKD-EU-2026-003-0007; MKD-EU-2024-001-0021).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11) and weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro (relevance High), where a portal without the most-needed services improved information only (MNE-CSO-2025-001-0019; MNE-CSO-2025-002-0002), and Moldova, where many digital services had no users (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0012). A Moldovan source states that building without beneficiaries' views harms use; its item-level transferability is conditional, on user involvement in design, a condition the evidence does not show North Macedonia meets (MDA-CSO-2023-001-0024). Transferability conditional: Montenegro's portal is newer than North Macedonia's, so results at its stage may not predict results at a later one.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a list of the most-requested services and their owners, which North Macedonia's strategy plans as an action but does not show delivered.
- *Main risks:* the selected services depend on registers and data exchange that are not ready, which ties this option to the interoperability issue (RI-TEC-028).
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation with the service-owning ministries and agencies.

**Option 3: Pilot assisted access and measure its effect on use.**
- *Gap addressed:* use of the portal and equal access, including the State Audit Office's finding that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0136, machine translated).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11).
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova reports use rising alongside service redesign and assisted access through its service centres (MDA-GOV-2025-001-0002), but does not show which measure raised it. North Macedonia so far plans only an analysis for DigInfo centres (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0127, machine translated). Transferability conditional: the effect of assisted access is unmeasured in Moldova, so it transfers only as an idea to test.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a measure of use set before the pilot starts; accessibility treated as a separate requirement, since assisted access does not by itself meet it.
- *Main risks:* centres that help people with information but not with completing services; cost without evidence of effect.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which has said it will carry out the DigInfo analysis.

**A warning rather than an option: a legal duty to integrate services will not by itself raise use.**
North Macedonia's 2025 strategy proposes making integration of e-services on the portal mandatory (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0194, machine translated). Albania (relevance Medium) shows such a duty alongside most services on one portal and high reported use, but no source shows that the duty caused either, and payment through the portal often fails (ALB-CSO-2025-001-0016; ALB-CSO-2025-001-0043). Transferability conditional: if North Macedonia adopts such a duty, the comparator evidence supports pairing it with working payment and signature and with the resources the lead ministry lacks (MKD-EU-2023-003-0045), not expecting it to drive use on its own.
