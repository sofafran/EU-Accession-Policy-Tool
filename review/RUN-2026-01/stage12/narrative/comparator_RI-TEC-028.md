## Reading this brief
This brief compares North Macedonia's underused interoperability platform with three countries: Moldova, which put a legal duty and sanctions behind its exchange platform; Serbia, which has a procedural duty to exchange data and a central exchange system; and Montenegro, which reports counts of active exchange alongside connected institutions. All three cases were rated of Medium relevance, so this issue has no closely matching case. None of the cases measures whether institutions use their platform more because of a reform: Moldova's evidence stops at the text of the law, Serbia's evidence is on the citizen side, and Montenegro's figures are two unverified government points. The cases show what was tried, what to measure and what to avoid, not a proven route. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses. Several Montenegrin and Moldovan sources are machine translated under LIM-0030 and capped at Medium confidence.

## Lessons across the cases
Three points recur. First, a connection count does not show regular use. North Macedonia's own figures separate connected from active institutions (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0064, machine translated), and Montenegro's government reports active register pairs rising from 18 to 37 while institutions with access stayed at 21 (MNE-GOV-2025-002-0003; MNE-GOV-2026-003-0011, both machine translated). Second, a legal duty, even with sanctions, may sit alongside continued underuse or continued requests for documents: Moldova added fines and disqualification from 18 January 2021 with no evidence on use afterwards (MDA-GOV-2021-002-0004; MDA-GOV-2021-002-0005), and in Serbia about half of surveyed citizens still reported submitting documents the state holds, under a duty dating from 2016 (SRB-CSO-2025-001-0003). Third, the stated obstacles are institutional: Montenegro names data not available through web services and slow legal and organisational preparation, which resemble North Macedonia's own reasons of IT staff, coordination, and register, process and software changes (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0065, machine translated). A sanction does not supply any of these.

## Draft policy options
These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (partly addressed) and on comparator cases validated at G6b, all of Medium relevance, and each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and none is shown by the evidence to raise use.

**Option 1: Measure use and ex officio evidence, not only connection.**
- *Gap addressed:* regular use of the platform by the institutions obliged to use it, so that evidence is obtained ex officio (MKD-EU-2025-001-0004; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0097).
- *Mechanism targeted:* coordination failure (MEC-08) and the confirmed conflict between the country's statement that once-only is applied and its Reform Agenda's statement that institutions do not gather evidence ex officio (MKD-EU-2023-003-0029; MKD-GOV-2024-001-0097).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro counts active register pairs as well as institutions with access (MNE-GOV-2026-003-0011), and Serbia's civil society monitor pairs a citizen survey on documents resubmitted with a review of sampled services (SRB-CSO-2025-001-0013). A source-stated point from the Serbian monitor, that compliance with the procedural duty needs monitoring, carries an item-level transferability judgement of conditional (SRB-CSO-2025-001-0014). Transferability conditional: the measures are institutional and could be applied in North Macedonia, but none of the cases shows that measuring raised use.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* an agreed definition of active use per institution and per register, and a body that collects it regularly; North Macedonia's own figures for 2025 differ (33 and 37 active institutions), which shows the need (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0064; MKD-GOV-2025-004-0186, both machine translated).
- *Main risks:* a new indicator that is reported but not acted on; government self-reporting without independent checks.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which runs the platform and leads digitalisation projects (MKD-EU-2023-003-0105; MKD-EU-2023-003-0045), with the institutions obliged to use it. The body that oversees the Law on General Administrative Procedure is not identified in the evidence.

**Option 2: Address the institutional preconditions before relying on enforcement.**
- *Gap addressed:* as in Option 1.
- *Mechanism targeted:* coordination failure (MEC-08) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro's stated obstacles of data not available through web services and unsettled legal and organisational arrangements (CMP-0006), and Moldova's case, where a sanction was added without evidence that it supplied capacity or raised use (MDA-GOV-2021-002-0003). Transferability conditional: the obstacles are institutional and resemble North Macedonia's, but scale and starting point differ, and the Montenegrin figures are unverified. An item-level judgement on a Montenegrin source, that capacity must be tackled along with the technology, is also conditional and rests on a source rated Low (MNE-CSO-2025-002-0008).
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a costed plan for connecting the obliged institutions, which the State Audit Office found missing for about 1,300 institutions (MKD-GOV-2022-004-0051, machine translated), and filled IT posts.
- *Main risks:* the plan is drafted but not funded; staff outflow continues.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and the Ministry of Digital Transformation, with the institutions that hold the registers.

**Option 3: Consider an enforcement mechanism for the duty to use the platform, as an untested option.**
- *Gap addressed:* as in Option 1. North Macedonia already has a legal duty to use the platform (MKD-GOV-2022-004-0031), and its 2023 strategy says there is no accountability without an ICT inspection competence (MKD-GOV-2023-002-0029); both are machine translated.
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04), which is not among the mechanisms recorded for this issue, so this option goes beyond the validated issue definition and is offered for discussion only.
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova's fines and temporary disqualification for refusing data or asking for documents available on the platform (MDA-GOV-2021-002-0004). Transferability conditional and untested: no source shows that the sanctions were applied or that they raised use.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* Options 1 and 2 in place, so that a duty can be met and breaches can be seen.
- *Main risks:* sanctioning institutions that lack the staff and software to comply; enforcement on paper only.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and the Assembly for any change to the law, and a body given the inspection competence.
