## In brief

No Commission recommendation on third-country workers under Chapter 2 appears in the evidence, so this issue is raised mainly by international organisations, partly at regional level (MKD-IO-2024-001-0075; MKD-IO-2026-001-0004). They report an ageing population and skilled emigration that increase the need for foreign workers, alongside regulatory barriers to their immigration (MKD-IO-2024-001-0003; MKD-IO-2024-001-0098). For North Macedonia they report quota-based admission, passport retention, wage deductions of up to 30% and systematic underreporting of exploitation (MKD-IO-2026-001-0004; MKD-IO-2026-001-0012; MKD-IO-2026-001-0017). They also describe permits tied to one employer and under-resourced, reactive labour inspection (MKD-IO-2026-001-0011; MKD-IO-2026-001-0015; MKD-IO-2026-001-0023). No intervention by North Macedonia is linked to this issue and no outcome is recorded. The validated unresolved gap is insufficient_evidence and the direction of travel is insufficient_evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium, the latter a provisional view.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The fact pack contains no EU requirement item from a Commission document for this issue, so there is no Commission wording whose change between 2020 and 2025 or 2026 can be traced. The concerns about third-country workers come from international organisations and one research study rather than from the Commission (MKD-IO-2024-001-0075; MKD-RES-2023-001-0036). The validated judgements note that the Commission's Chapter 2 assessment concerns the access of EU workers, not workers from outside the EU, and that a regional claim that laws are EU-aligned does not name the country (MKD-IO-2026-001-0023; MKD-EU-2025-003-0031).

## Open questions

- Whether conditions for third-country workers in North Macedonia are improving or worsening is unsettled, because no measure repeats over time for the country and the doubling of work permits is a regional figure (MKD-IO-2026-001-0001); country-level permit and complaint series over several years would settle it.
- The size of the remaining gap cannot be assessed without an EU requirement item, so a Commission position on third-country workers, or a confirmed decision that none applies, is needed.
- The scale of rights violations after legal entry rests on international organisation reports that point to underreporting (MKD-IO-2026-001-0012; MKD-IO-2026-001-0017); labour inspectorate records or a country survey of third-country workers would settle it.
- The Commission's permit counts have unclear bases (MKD-EU-2022-001-0068; MKD-EU-2025-001-0104), so official permit statistics with stated definitions are needed.
