## In brief

The Commission has found since 2021 that North Macedonia's Personal Data Protection Agency depends on government for staff and funds, and in 2026 it states that the Agency is inadequately funded, that its financial autonomy remains restricted and that its capacities have not improved (MKD-EU-2021-001-0032; MKD-EU-2024-001-0040; MKD-EU-2026-007-0034; MKD-EU-2026-007-0037). The Agency drafted amendments to secure its autonomy in April 2022, but its own machine-translated report says adoption had still not started in 2025 (MKD-GOV-2026-002-0032; MKD-GOV-2026-002-0037). The Commission reports staff falling from 21 to 20, and the Agency reports 18 at the end of 2024 and 17 at the end of 2025 (MKD-EU-2023-001-0036; MKD-EU-2024-001-0041; MKD-GOV-2025-010-0011; MKD-GOV-2026-002-0016). In 2026 the Commission finds the Agency critically understaffed, with seven staff directly enforcing the law, which contributes to systemic underperformance in inspections and infringement proceedings; that count covers a narrower group than the staff totals (MKD-EU-2026-007-0039; MKD-EU-2026-007-0040). The sources conflict on the director: the 2025 report found none appointed since January 2025, while the 2026 report states that a director was appointed in December 2025, a date the Agency gives as 5 December 2025 (MKD-EU-2025-001-0204; MKD-EU-2026-007-0038; MKD-GOV-2026-002-0016). The 2020 data protection law is in practice, with weak application, and no outcome is recorded. What remains is autonomy in hiring and spending, and resources matching the Agency's tasks. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's concern has stayed the same in substance, and its findings became more severe up to 2025. In 2021 it described the Agency's dependence on the Ministry of Finance and another ministry for recruitment and organisation (MKD-EU-2021-001-0032). In 2022 and 2023 it said understaffing seriously harmed implementation, and in 2023 it called for alignment on the Agency's independence and noted that it is not financially autonomous (MKD-EU-2022-001-0023; MKD-EU-2023-001-0028; MKD-EU-2023-001-0037). In 2024 it said the Agency lacks independence to hire staff and spend its budget (MKD-EU-2024-001-0040). In 2025 it added that no director had been appointed and called for improved capacities, while a general communication asked for sustained resources and stronger independence (MKD-EU-2025-001-0204; MKD-EU-2025-001-0211; MKD-EU-2025-002-0002). In 2026 it records a director appointed in December 2025 but finds that the Agency's capacities have not improved, that it remains underfunded and critically understaffed, and that its implementation of the 2025 to 2030 strategy is severely hampered (MKD-EU-2026-007-0034; MKD-EU-2026-007-0037; MKD-EU-2026-007-0038; MKD-EU-2026-007-0043).

## Open questions

- The latest staff totals, budget and funding refusals rest on the Agency's own machine-translated reports capped at Medium confidence, some at Low (MKD-GOV-2025-010-0012; MKD-GOV-2026-002-0016; MKD-GOV-2026-002-0017). The Commission's 2026 figure of seven enforcement staff measures a different group (MKD-EU-2026-007-0039), so Commission or Ministry of Finance figures on total staff and funding would settle them.
- How far the 2025 to 2030 data protection strategy has been carried out is unclear: the Agency says implementation began in 2025, while the Commission says it was held back in 2025 and severely hampered in 2026 (MKD-GOV-2026-002-0086; MKD-EU-2025-001-0069; MKD-EU-2026-007-0043). A progress report against the strategy's actions would settle it.
- No outcome evidence shows whether the Agency's supervision improves compliance (MKD-GOV-2026-002-0040), so data on compliance rates over time are needed.
