## Summary
This overview lists the 48 reform issues identified for North Macedonia in RUN-2026-01: 14 under Chapter 2 (Freedom of movement for workers) and 34 under the cross-cutting technology and AI theme. All values shown are judgements validated by the analyst at G6. Nineteen issues are rated High priority (4 in Chapter 2 and 15 in the technology theme, one of them set to High by the analyst at G6), 24 Medium and 5 Low. The dominant pattern is persistence: 33 of 48 issues have a persistent direction of travel, and no Chapter 2 issue is judged to be improving. Five technology issues are improving: electronic public procurement tools, electronic communications law, the cybersecurity framework, electronic identification and trust services law, and customs IT systems. Where the improvement is in law only, the issue briefs say so. Two technology issues are worsening and one is new. No issue's gap is closed: 20 gaps are open, 27 partly addressed, and one, on third-country workers, cannot be judged on the evidence. Most issues are of Medium tractability. The comparator pilot covered three High priority issues: nationality discrimination in access to employment (RI-FMW-001), the underused interoperability platform (RI-TEC-028) and the national e-services portal (RI-TEC-036).

## Notes on evidence coverage
Coverage is uneven between the two strands. The technology theme draws on far more evidence than Chapter 2, because the theme makes the whole of each annual report and every cluster's screening report relevant. Within Chapter 2, social security coordination and work permits are well covered, while the rights of foreign workers rest on few sources, and no monitoring report on foreign workers after 2021 was found. Evidence on artificial intelligence comes mainly from one national audit, as the Commission's reports say almost nothing specific on it. Most national government sources are in Macedonian only, so much of the evidence on the country response is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence; the share for each issue is shown in the tables. The newest annual Commission report in the run is from 2025, as the 2026 package had not appeared by the evidence freeze of 29 October 2026. The evidence count shows how much material exists, not how important the issue is: priority did not depend on it.
