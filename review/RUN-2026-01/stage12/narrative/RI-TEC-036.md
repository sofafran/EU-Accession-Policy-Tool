## In brief

The Commission asks North Macedonia to offer many more fully digitalised services on its national e-services portal and to treat the digitalisation of public services as a priority, and a Reform Agenda payment condition requires a 50 per cent increase in the offer of e-services (MKD-EU-2023-003-0037; MKD-EU-2025-004-0103; MKD-EU-2024-007-0015). The Commission states that the portal was upgraded, its offer widened with services most demanded by citizens, and some certificates put online (MKD-EU-2025-001-0135; MKD-EU-2025-001-0136). The State Audit Office, in machine translation, found only 0.556 requests per registered user and about 140 laws not aligned with the digitalisation package (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0194; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0002), and the 2025 strategy reports about 13 of 100 adults registered (MKD-GOV-2025-004-0075). In April 2026 the Commission found digital government quite underdeveloped, with most e-services rarely used (MKD-EU-2026-003-0007). There is no outcome evidence. The validated judgements are priority High, direction of travel persistent, unresolved gap partly_addressed and tractability Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

The Commission's message has stayed essentially the same from 2020 to 2026. It first asked institutions to commit more to the portal and to encourage its use (MKD-EU-2020-001-0015; MKD-EU-2021-001-0011). From 2022 it asked for a wider offer of services and noted that fully electronic services had barely grown (MKD-EU-2022-001-0045; MKD-EU-2022-001-0046; MKD-EU-2023-003-0106). In 2023 and 2024 it asked for the 135 new services to be completed and found the most common services, such as personal documents, still not online (MKD-EU-2023-001-0011; MKD-EU-2024-001-0023; MKD-EU-2024-001-0090). From 2024 the Reform Agenda added the 50 per cent target, and in 2025 the screening asked for digitalisation to be treated as a priority (MKD-EU-2024-007-0015; MKD-EU-2025-004-0103). In 2026 the Commission still describes rare use and few fully transactional services (MKD-EU-2026-003-0007).

## Open questions

- Sources conflict on the number of services, with the Reform Agenda baseline of 264 against Commission figures of 392, 246 and 124 in 2024, so progress against the 50 per cent target cannot be measured until an agreed count and definition is published (MKD-GOV-2024-001-0092; MKD-EU-2024-008-0015; MKD-EU-2024-009-0014; MKD-EU-2024-001-0072).
- The Commission states that the portal caters to people with special needs, while the State Audit Office, in machine translation capped at Medium confidence, finds that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services, and an independent accessibility test of the portal would settle this conflict (MKD-EU-2024-001-0020; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0136; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0026).
- The Commission states that most administrative services are offered online and offline, while the State Audit Office records 1,120 services available only in person, and a complete service catalogue showing the channel for each service would settle this conflict (MKD-EU-2024-001-0088; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0055).
- Use figures come mainly from machine-translated national audit and strategy texts and no recent usage series exists, so regular portal statistics on transactions per service would be needed to show whether the 2025 upgrade changed use (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0006; MKD-GOV-2025-004-0075; MKD-EU-2025-001-0135).
