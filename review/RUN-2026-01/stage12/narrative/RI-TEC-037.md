## In brief

The Commission's requirement here is general: it states that ongoing digitalisation, including expanding services in sectors such as energy regulation and veterinary services, is essential to maintain progress (MKD-EU-2025-004-0132). The Commission reports that the energy regulator, pharmaceutical chamber and pension insurance fund developed electronic services, and that the Cadastre Office's new digital services cut unresolved cases by 20% and the backlog by 10%; that result is assessed as worked with Medium attribution, for one period only (MKD-EU-2024-001-0113; MKD-EU-2025-001-0075; MKD-EU-2025-001-0076). The national audit, machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, describes five One Stop Shops, some not used effectively outside Skopje, portals requiring separate profiles and no public use statistics (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0209; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0120; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0088; MKD-GOV-2024-007-0089). A civil society study finds slow progress in municipal e-services (MKD-CSO-2025-001-0003). The validated assessment is partly_addressed, with priority Low, direction of travel insufficient_evidence and tractability Medium.

## How the EU requirement has changed

There is no repeated Commission wording to trace for this issue. The Commission's items are isolated additions: in 2024 it noted that companies can be registered online within a single day and reported electronic services developed by three sector bodies, and in 2025 it noted the move to digital platforms for construction permits and stated that expanding services in sectors such as energy regulation and veterinary services is essential (MKD-EU-2024-008-0030; MKD-EU-2024-001-0113; MKD-EU-2025-004-0138; MKD-EU-2025-004-0132). In November 2025 it reported the Cadastre Office's new digital services (MKD-EU-2025-001-0075). None of these sets a specific requirement on sector or municipal services, and no Commission item on this issue is recorded for 2020 to 2023.

## Open questions

- Use statistics for independent sector and municipal portals are not public, according to the machine-translated audit, so published data on services delivered and users would settle how far these services are used (MKD-GOV-2024-007-0089).
- Government reports on e-Rabota point in different directions, with the employer share of registrations rising from 60% to 69% and the private agency share falling from 67% to 62%, and these Low or Medium confidence machine-translated figures would need independent confirmation to show a trend (MKD-GOV-2022-002-0010; MKD-GOV-2025-003-0009; MKD-GOV-2022-002-0011; MKD-GOV-2025-003-0010).
- The evidence on municipal services rests on one Low confidence civil society study, and a systematic count of municipal e-services and their use would be needed to judge progress at local level (MKD-CSO-2025-001-0003).
- The Cadastre Office result covers one period and the sentence does not state where the figures come from, so a further year of data from a stated source would show whether it holds (MKD-EU-2025-001-0076).
