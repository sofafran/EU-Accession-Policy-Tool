Nothing in this review is decided until Sofia decides at G8, using `review/RUN-2026-02/G8_decisions.csv`, `G8_proposed_changes.csv`, `G8_lesson_statuses.csv` and `G8_limits.csv`. ## 1. What the run produced

RUN-2026-02 was the first country run of the controlled scaling phase (decisions E19 and E20). It applied the method validated in the North Macedonia alpha to Moldova, with the default scope: all of Chapter 2 (Freedom of movement for workers) in its EU meaning and the cross-cutting technology and AI theme, with evidence from 1 January 2020 to the freeze date. The 2026 Moldova report, published on 4 November 2026, is inside the window. The run started on 3 November and passed G7 on 6 November 2026. It was also the first test of the shared multi-country architecture, and the scaling note (`runs/RUN-2026-02/scaling_note.md`, points S1 to S10) records what having two countries changed.

- **Sources:** 77 Moldova sources registered (56 available, 17 inaccessible, 4 excluded), plus the four annual Communications on enlargement from 2023 to 2026, registered under North Macedonia and used for their Moldova and horizontal passages. In the comparator step, 6 new comparator sources available, 2 inaccessible and 1 excluded, with 9 held sources reused.
- **Evidence:** 3,534 items for this run (3,479 in the Moldova evidence files and 55 horizontal items in files registered under North Macedonia), of which 3,173 are in scope; 1,712 are machine-translated Romanian. Every quote verified word for word. 73 comparator items.
- **Structure:** five new shared reform issues (RI-TEC-038, RI-FMW-015, RI-TEC-039 and RI-TEC-040 at G4; RI-FMW-016 at G5), taxonomy 1.3 and theme list 1.4; 53 issues judged for Moldova (16 Chapter 2, 37 technology); 710 new interventions, 20 new actors, 842 validated links of this run.
- **Judgements:** 8,107 validated at G6, plus the comparator judgements validated at G6b. Priority: 17 High, 24 Medium, 12 Low. Direction: 28 improving (16 of them in law only), 15 persistent, 9 insufficient evidence, 1 new.
- **Comparator step:** six new cases (CMP-0010 to CMP-0015) and two reused alpha cases (CMP-0008, CMP-0009) on RI-FMW-015, RI-TEC-038 and RI-TEC-036, drawn from Croatia, Montenegro, Serbia, Albania and North Macedonia.
- **Outputs (signed off at G7):** a scope overview, 53 issue briefs, a coverage and limits report and three comparator briefs, whose draft policy options stay internal drafts.

The main finding differs from North Macedonia's. Moldova's dominant pattern is improvement, much of it in law only: 28 of 53 issues improve, but no gap is closed, and five High priority issues are persistent (EHIC preconditions, digital communication with the courts, the data protection authority, 5G security and the outdated electronic procurement system). The comparator step found no case of an outdated e-procurement system actually replaced.

## 3. Error patterns and how often they occurred

**Extraction.**
- *Missed claims* remain the main weakness, but the second pass catches most of them. At G3, 711 sample items were checked: 668 correct, 23 minor field errors, 8 quote boundary errors, 4 wrong types, 4 that should not have been extracted, 3 scope errors and 1 date or actor error; type accuracy 99%. The second pass added 123 items to the sample (17%), mainly from pages that first-pass extractors did not read in long sources such as the Reform Agenda and a thesis (ALN-0037). Blind hand-coding of three passages matched 23 of 29 claims and found 6 missed.
- *Second-pass yield across the full run*, computed from `review/RUN-2026-02/second_pass/` against the run's items: about 404 items added to some 3,075 first-pass items (13%). By family: EU about 8%, IO about 7%, GOV and CSO about 15%, RES about 34% (on a small base). The gap between EU and national sources is narrower than in the alpha (2.7% against 13.6%), but EU and IO remain the lower families that PC-05 asks to watch.
- *Quote cut mid-word:* one serious error in a spot check of 60 flagged items (MDA-GOV-2025-002-0467), with an untrue note that the page text broke there. The automatic check could not catch it (ALN-0038). The other 59 were 49 correct and 10 minor.
- *Unsettled types:* 20 pass 1 queries remain (G5-D10): 9 on whether a statement of an institution's mandate is a finding or an intervention, which M2 did not settle; 5 on EU steps in the accession process (cluster opening, release decisions) typed as country interventions; 2 conversion artefacts; 2 apparent source misprints.
- *Inconsistent coverage of horizontal documents:* the 2023 to 2025 Communications were first extracted for Moldova passages only, although REQ-076 covers horizontal passages too; corrected at stage 8 (G4-D15, 33 items).
- *Scope call by the quality check:* its view that two release-decision annexes were out of scope was wrong (62 in-scope Reform Agenda items).
- *Comparator extraction:* the G5b spot check of 29 items found 22 correct, 3 minor, 2 serious and 2 missed.

**Classification and linking.**
- Of a 10% sample of 317 automatic classifications, 285 were correct, 30 arguable and 2 wrong; of 100 out-of-scope decisions, 91 were correct, 8 arguable and 1 wrong (the alpha's figure was 82). Arguable patterns: mandates read as implementation, small stretches of RI-FMW-005, RI-TEC-033 and RI-TEC-038, and uneven scope calls on IT service management.
- *Contradicts links were over-proposed:* of 61, 8 were confirmed, 49 were apparent differences (different measures, definitions or dates) and 4 were not conflicts (ALN-0039). At the same time, writers at stage 12 found about five real differences stated in validated statuses or reasons with no contradicts link (open data law dates, EVOSign, the eIDAS draft law, Law 72/2025, a register's launch date). The link step both over-proposes and under-records.
- *Same-sentence check (PC-06):* 72 groups of identical sentences, 45 classified differently, of which only one was a real inconsistency (HRMIS, MDA-EU-2026-012-0035). The rest were legitimate splits of one sentence into two claims, so the check is noisy.
- *Parallel batches:* one inconsistency between batches on the government-wide HR system, caught by the same-sentence check.
- *Entities:* 36 possible duplicate actor and intervention pairs at G5, about 20 intervention pairs still kept separate at G6, and one possible duplicate that reached a brief (INT-MDA-0232 and INT-MDA-0568 on 5G security). A renamed or merged institution can only be recorded as a possible duplicate (ALN-0040).
- *Shared registers:* tools mixed runs because the requirements table and the register have no run field (S10, fixed in the tools at stage 7).
- *Gate application:* the G4 application did not set the ratings confirmed at G4 to validated; found and corrected before stage 10.

**Judgement.**
- *Reason check before G6 (PC-08, PC-09):* of 286 tier 3 reasons, 215 were sound, 46 had wording problems (mostly claims of absence without the search behind them), 13 had doubtful values and 12 had citation errors. 123 rows were corrected and 5 values changed (RI-FMW-004 to High, RI-TEC-028 to Medium, RI-TEC-033 and RI-FMW-009 direction, one attribution lowered).
- *Parallel batches* proposed the same leads_to link four times, so four links carried two conflicting success and attribution pairs; eight rows were superseded (ALN-0042).
- *Search scope stated two ways:* "1,451 Moldova items" for success judgements and "3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files" for issue judgements, the latter including 152 alpha comparator items.
- *Priority calibration:* the test was read with one added principle, that a Reform Agenda condition already fulfilled and not repeated in 2026 does not alone make an issue High (G6-D01, RI-TEC-011 and RI-TEC-023). The written test does not say this. Moldova has at most four standard annual reports (2023 to 2026), so the three-report tests for priority and for a persistent direction rarely apply (G6-D02).
- *Comparator cases:* the independent check found 6 serious and 32 minor errors, all corrected (against 24 serious in about 480 claims in the alpha's first drafts).
- *Writers at stage 12* found about 20 problems in validated material, and eight rows were flagged for re-review (GATE-0029): dates or speakers misattributed (RI-TEC-002, RI-TEC-004, RI-TEC-022, RI-TEC-038, INT-MDA-0041), a reason addressed to the analyst (INT-MDA-0608), a horizontal passage rated as concerning the same country (JDG-018540), and "reversed" used for laws repealed and replaced (INT-MDA-0424, INT-MDA-0428). Writers also found that two shared issue definitions name features drafted from North Macedonia that the Moldova evidence does not show (RI-TEC-018 "can be left without a director", RI-TEC-021 biometric data).

**Tools and process.**
- Single-run assumptions in tools and tests were the most frequent technical error: at stage 0 (tests, page labels, links; S1, ALN-0035), at stage 7 (counts and packages read every run's rows; S10), and at G6b, where a Moldova comparator link appeared in North Macedonia's rebuilt brief (ALN-0043). Each was fixed, and North Macedonia's outputs and pages rebuild identical.
- A write to `decisions/decision_log.md` emptied the file after G7; the test suite caught it and it was restored from the previous commit.

**Overall pattern.** As in the alpha, errors of fact and reference were rare. What was new came from having two countries: shared files read without a run filter, horizontal documents filed under one country, and shared definitions drafted from the first country. The independent checks before each gate and the output writers again found what routine validation did not.

## 8. Scaling readiness

The Moldova run shows that the shared architecture carries to a second country. The multi-run evidence model (E21) worked on 11 held files and on the horizontal Communications with no change to the alpha's records. The shared taxonomy fitted 299 of 393 Moldova EU requirement items, and five new shared issues were added without changing any validated North Macedonia finding. The chain ran from G0 to G7 in four days. What the run teaches about scaling is mostly about shared files, not about the analytical method.

**Must change before Montenegro**
- **Run scoping in tools:** a two-run test fixture and run scoping of every builder (ALN-0035, ALN-0043; PC-31). Montenegro has records from both earlier runs, so this is the highest risk.
- **Parallel agents:** judge links once after batches merge, and use one stated search scope (ALN-0042; PC-19, PC-20).
- **Contradicts links:** one test for a real conflict (ALN-0039; PC-18). With rich national statistics in every country, apparent differences will otherwise crowd out real ones.
- **Horizontal documents and run columns:** stated, not inferred (S8, S10; PC-26, PC-27).
- **Priority test:** the fulfilled-condition reading and short report series written into the rules, so that countries are judged by the same test (G6-D01, G6-D02; PC-21; scaling note S7).

**Should change before later countries**
- **Territorial coverage:** a general rule before Ukraine, Georgia and Kosovo (S6; PC-29).
- **Shared definitions:** reviewed at each G4 for features drawn from earlier countries (S3, S4; PC-25). The risk grows with each country added.
- **Entities:** successor fields for reorganised institutions (ALN-0040; PC-28). Institutional reorganisations are frequent in every candidate country.
- **Long national sources in new languages:** whole-text screening (ALN-0037; PC-24), word-boundary quote checks (ALN-0038; PC-30), and text-recognition data checked at each stage 0.

**Still open from the alpha**
- **Languages:** about half of Moldova's items are machine translated and capped at Medium, and no Romanian-reading reviewer was named (LIM-0042; ALN-0007 in the alpha). The same will hold for Montenegro, Albania, Ukraine, Serbia, Georgia and Türkiye. Either name language reviewers or accept explicitly that country-response evidence stays at Medium.
- **Assurance:** one person validated all judgements, and no person read the sources (G0-D04; ALN-0027 in the alpha). Independent agent checks and output writing remain the main defence and caught real errors at every gate.

**Can scale as it is**
