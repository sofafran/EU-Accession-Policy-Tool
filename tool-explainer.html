EU Accession Reform Intelligence Tool

From EU accession assessments to evidence-backed reform priorities and practical policy options.

A structured, reviewable method for following each reform issue from what the EU asks, through what a country has done, to what could realistically come next.

1Why this tool exists

Accession reports say what needs fixing. They rarely show what happened next.

The evidence exists, but it is spread across many documents and years. It is not joined up into a view a decision-maker can use.

EU assessments and official EU material
National government material
International and institutional evidence
Research and analytical evidence
Other relevant evidence
No systematic link
?Has the country responded?
?Is the response applied, and did it work?
?What remains open, and what have others done?
?What should be prioritised next?

The tool closes that gap. It follows each reform issue end to end, keeps every claim tied to its source, and puts the important judgements in human hands.

2What the tool does

It turns scattered evidence into reviewed priorities and options.

Evidence in

Collect and register

Relevant sources are gathered and logged. Every document gets a permanent ID, and originals are never edited.

→
Structured analysis

Extract, classify, link

Exact quotes become evidence items. These are tagged to shared reform issues and connected along one analytical chain.

→
Priorities and options out

Judge and propose

Proposed judgements are reviewed by analysts. They feed priorities, comparator lessons and draft policy options.

Stated vs assessedWhat a source says is kept apart from what we conclude.
Exact quotesEvery item quotes its source word for word, with a page or section, and is checked automatically.
Human gatesEach phase closes with a blocking review. Nothing crosses a gate on its own.
Fully traceableOutputs, judgements and evidence are linked and versioned.
Default scope of a country run Chapter 2 · Freedom of movement for workers Technology and AI recommendations, wherever they occur Evidence from 1 January 2020 English primary

    5Human judgement + AI

    The tool proposes. It never concludes on its own.

    AI extracts, organises and proposes. Analysts decide.

    Authority is set in three levels. Every substantive analytical judgement is a human decision.
    Level 1

    AI performs and logs

    AIHuman
    • File naming and IDs
    • Format conversion
    • Exact-duplicate detection
    • Retrieval failures
    • Quote verification
    • Structural tags

    Mechanical operations. The log can always be checked.

    Level 2

    AI decides, analyst reviews at the next gate

    AIHuman
    • Clear-cut exclusions
    • Clear-cut theme tags
    • Extraction under approved rules
    • Source quality
    • Evidence confidence
    • Relevance
    • Implementation status where sources agree

    Lower-risk decisions, surfaced for review. Doubtful cases are flagged.

    Level 3

    AI proposes, humans decide

    AIHuman
    • Unresolved gap
    • Direction of travel
    • Success and attribution
    • Priority and tractability
    • Policy options
    All level 3 matters
    • Source requirements
    • Judgement-based exclusions
    • Taxonomy changes
    • Ambiguous classifications
    • Comparator choices
    • Comparator relevance
    • Transferability
    • Any change to method, rules or scope

    7One method, many countries

    A shared core, country-specific evidence, and a regional view.

    Each country is run one at a time with the same method. Its evidence, judgements and outputs are its own.

    Shared methodological core

    One method, reused for every country

    Seven codebooksProcedures and versioned rulesOne shared taxonomyCross-cutting themesRun templatePage generator
    ⇄ Comparator evidence is drawn first from countries already in the project
    Regional comparison layer

    Validated country judgements, side by side

    Only judgements that have passed each country's G6 are compared.

    Checked afreshAccession status, Chapter 2 context, documents, languages and evidence base are checked for each country. Nothing is assumed from another.
    Never stretchedEvidence is matched to a shared reform issue only where it genuinely fits. New issues are proposed for review.
    Learning carried forwardLessons from each run are logged and confirmed at a gate before the method changes.

    Shared across countries

    • Method, codebooks and procedures
    • Versioned extraction, classification and judgement rules
    • Reform issue taxonomy and theme list
    • Run template and page tooling

    Specific to each country

    • Run record, scope deviations and limits
    • Sources and evidence items
    • Judgements and their reviews
    • Country page and outputs
