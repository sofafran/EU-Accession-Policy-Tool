# Issue brief: Open data and re-use of public sector information incomplete

RI-TEC-016 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Institutions do not regularly publish and update datasets on the open data portal, and legislation is only partly aligned with the Open Data Directive and related EU data rules. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks that all institutions regularly provide and update data, that proactive disclosure of datasets be encouraged, and that North Macedonia step up alignment with the Open Data Directive [1][2][3]. The Law on the Use of Public Sector Data is adopted, and the government reports 580 datasets from 74 institutions on the open data portal by December 2022 [4][5]. Amendments to align the law with the Directive have been with a working group since 2023 and are assessed as proposed [6][7][8]. The Commission stated in 2020 that access to public information improved thanks to the transparency strategy that set up the portal, assessed as a mixed success with Medium attribution, because the State Audit Office reports that the portal has not functioned since early 2025 owing to procurement problems, in machine-translated text [9][10]. The portal's status is therefore unclear. The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is Medium and tractability is High.

**What remains.** A Law on the Use of Public Sector Data is adopted and 580 datasets were reported by 2022, but the amendments and the Directive alignment action are proposed only, and these statuses are themselves proposals for G6. What remains is alignment with the Open Data Directive and regular publication by institutions on a functioning portal. Sources conflict, since the October 2025 strategy says the country is not aligned while the screening report's country presentation says partially aligned, so both are the country's own statements (the conflict register attributes the second to the Commission), and the Commission's own finding is only that further efforts are needed; the machine-translated items are capped at Medium confidence.

**Direction of travel.** Commission items ask for regular data updates in 2020 and more proactive disclosure in 2022, while amendments for Open Data Directive alignment were in preparation in 2023 and still with a working group in 2025; the 2020 sub-area rating of some progress concerns transparency more widely and, like the chapter scale, was not decisive. The State Audit Office reports that the portal has not functioned since early 2025, in machine-translated text capped at Medium confidence, and the country's published screening presentation describes the portal favourably without addressing this. That outage rests on one source with no later status, so it does not yet support worsening, though confirmation would.

**Priority.** The Commission raises the matter in 2020, 2022 and 2025, but not in its main findings, so it is a repeated requirement that is not central. Most items here come from one national audit and one national strategy, and that volume was not treated as importance.

**Tractability.** One ministry leads, the portal and 74 publishing institutions already existed by 2022, and the stated cause of the outage is a procurement problem rather than a structural one. Progress does not depend on the EU, although the amendments have been slow since 2023. This view leans on machine-translated items capped at Medium confidence, and government reporting on datasets does not itself show success.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement has moved from practice to legal alignment. In 2020 the Commission recorded some progress on transparency, including an operational open government data portal, and asked all institutions to provide and update data regularly so the system works smoothly [11][1]. In 2022 it asked for proactive disclosure of information and datasets to be further encouraged [2]. In 2025 the screening reports state that the Law on the Use of Public Sector Data is partially aligned with the Open Data Directive, ask the country to invest further efforts in aligning with it, and ask it to monitor other EU data legislation [12][3][13]. The matter does not appear among the Commission's main findings.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The country's February 2025 presentation describes the portal as in line with best practice while the State Audit Office says it has not functioned since early 2025, in machine-translated text capped at Medium confidence, and a later source on whether the portal was restored would settle both its status and whether the 2020 result has lasted [18][10].
- The October 2025 strategy states that the country is not aligned with the Open Data Directive while the February 2025 screening report states that the law is partially aligned, a confirmed conflict that a Commission table of concordance or alignment assessment would settle [14][4].
- The ITU profile of 2022 lists the network and information security law as an existing enabler while the Commission's 2022 report says the draft had not been adopted, a confirmed conflict that dated legislative records would settle [15][16].
- There are no data on use of the portal or on how regularly institutions update their datasets after 2022, which portal statistics or an Open Data Maturity assessment would provide [5][20].
