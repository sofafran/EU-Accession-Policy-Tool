# Issue brief: Weak compliance with data protection rules

RI-TEC-020 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Data controllers, including public bodies, do not apply data protection law in practice, breaches are frequent and not always reported, awareness is low, and sanctions are rare. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission finds that, in general, data controllers do not implement the Law on Personal Data Protection, calls for awareness raising in all sectors, and reports in 2025 that breaches are becoming more frequent, some go unreported and state bodies show low awareness [1][2][3][4]. The law is assessed as in_practice, and the Agency reports 196 supervisions in 2025, with 121 decisions ordering violations to be removed [5][6]. The Agency adopted a new rulebook on security of processing and took over an e-classroom platform, but the data protection officer network and the sanction amendments remain proposed [7][8][9][10]. Success on awareness and on the e-classroom is insufficient_evidence, and no independent evidence shows changed behaviour. The Agency reports its Misdemeanour Commission disrupted by staff departures [11]. Much Agency evidence is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence. The gap is assessed as open. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is persistent and tractability is Low.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |
|---|---|
| Unresolved gap | open |
| Direction of travel | persistent |
| Priority | Medium |
| Tractability | Low |

**What remains.** The security rulebook has been adopted and an e-classroom is in place, but the data protection officer network and the sanction amendments are still only planned, no independent evidence shows changed behaviour, and the 2026 report again finds digital safeguards in state bodies insufficient and severe under-reporting of breaches. What remains is for controllers, including public bodies, to apply the law, report breaches and face effective sanctions. Two conflicts are reported and not resolved: reprimand counts differ between the Commission's report (10 for 2022, 17 for 2021) and the country presentation (4 for each), and the 2025 finding of increasingly frequent breaches conflicts with the 2026 finding of declining reported incidents, which low notification numbers (19 for 2022, 11 for 2025) and the Agency's under-reporting estimate do not settle.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission called fines low in 2020, found that controllers do not implement the law in 2023, found low awareness and more frequent breaches in 2025, and in 2026 still finds digital safeguards in state bodies insufficient, so the default of persistent applies; the chapter scale was not decisive. On breaches the reports conflict, and both are cited: the 2025 report finds breaches increasingly frequent and the 2026 report finds reported incidents declining, while the Agency's estimate of severe under-reporting, given in 2026, means neither figure shows whether compliance changed, so the conflict is reported and not resolved. Supervision counts measure enforcement rather than compliance, and the machine-translated Agency findings are capped at Medium confidence.

**Priority.** Compliance is central to whether the law has effect, but the Commission's direct findings on it are thin, mainly 2023 and 2025, and it is not a named key recommendation. The large volume of items comes from the Agency's own reports and was not treated as importance.

**Tractability.** Progress needs behaviour to change across very many public and private controllers, and it depends on an Agency whose supervision and misdemeanour capacity is shrinking. The evidence shows awareness work and enforcement activity, but no sustained improvement in compliance. The Agency's items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's concern has stayed the same while its findings have become broader. In 2020 it notes that misdemeanour fines for data protection breaches remain low [12][13]. In 2023 it states more generally that controllers do not implement the law and that there is a strong need for awareness raising and a culture of data protection in all sectors [1][2]. By November 2025 it adds that breaches are increasingly frequent, some are not reported as the law requires, and state administrative bodies still show low awareness and insufficient IT protection of personal data [3][4]. Compliance has not been a named key recommendation in these reports.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- For 2021 and 2022 the Commission's report gives 17 and 10 reprimands while the country presentation gives 4 for each year, and this confirmed conflict needs the Agency's annual reports for those years to settle it [17][19][18].
- Success of the law on awareness and of the e-classroom is insufficient_evidence because the only evidence is the Agency's own reporting, so independent awareness surveys or user figures would be needed [15][16].
- No evidence measures compliance among controllers over time, and repeated supervision findings on comparable controllers, or independent audits, would show whether behaviour is changing [31][28].
- Whether the sectoral amendments on misdemeanour sanctions and the data protection officer network have been delivered is not shown, and later Agency or Commission reports would settle it [10][9].
