# Issue brief: Sector and local online public services limited

RI-TEC-037 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** Online public services run by sector bodies or municipalities, outside the national portal, are limited in range, use or quality. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission's requirement here is general: it states that ongoing digitalisation, including expanding services in sectors such as energy regulation and veterinary services, is essential to maintain progress [1]. The Commission reports that the energy regulator, pharmaceutical chamber and pension insurance fund developed electronic services, and that the Cadastre Office's new digital services cut unresolved cases by 20% and the backlog by 10%; that result is assessed as worked with Medium attribution, for one period only [2][3][4]. The national audit, machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, describes five One Stop Shops, some not used effectively outside Skopje, portals requiring separate profiles and no public use statistics [5][6][7][8]. A civil society study finds slow progress in municipal e-services [9]. The validated assessment is partly_addressed, with priority Low, direction of travel insufficient_evidence and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** Sector services exist, and One Stop Shops are proposed as implemented, while the analysis on e-services there, the mobile offices and the employment plan are proposed or at insufficient evidence; these statuses are themselves proposals, and the national items are machine translated, capped at Medium confidence. The Commission's requirement is general, not specific. What remains is a wider range and use of sector and local services, including municipal ones, with published statistics on delivery.

**Direction of travel.** No repeated Commission wording or comparable series exists: EU items are isolated additions (2024, 2025), and the Chapter 10 scale is context only and was not decisive. The other signals are partial and mixed, since e-Rabota's share of employer registrations rose from 60% to 69% but fell from 67% to 62% for agencies in government reports, and a civil society study finds general slow progress at municipal level. Government and civil society items here are Low or Medium confidence, and the Macedonian ones are machine translated.

**Priority.** The Commission mentions sector services only briefly, and most of the concern is raised by the national audit (machine translated, capped at Medium confidence), national plans and civil society, so the issue meets the test of being mainly raised by others. The Reform Agenda target falls on the national portal under the G5 split, and item volume was not counted.

**Tractability.** Individual sector bodies can and do make progress, as e-Rabota and the Cadastre Office show. Across many institutions and municipalities, however, the audit and ICT strategy (machine translated, capped at Medium confidence) describe parallel systems built without clear guidance, and no single actor has control.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* There is no repeated Commission wording to trace for this issue. The Commission's items are isolated additions: in 2024 it noted that companies can be registered online within a single day and reported electronic services developed by three sector bodies, and in 2025 it noted the move to digital platforms for construction permits and stated that expanding services in sectors such as energy regulation and veterinary services is essential [10][2][11][1]. In November 2025 it reported the Cadastre Office's new digital services [3]. None of these sets a specific requirement on sector or municipal services, and no Commission item on this issue is recorded for 2020 to 2023.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Use statistics for independent sector and municipal portals are not public, according to the machine-translated audit, so published data on services delivered and users would settle how far these services are used [8].
- Government reports on e-Rabota point in different directions, with the employer share of registrations rising from 60% to 69% and the private agency share falling from 67% to 62%, and these Low or Medium confidence machine-translated figures would need independent confirmation to show a trend [15][16][17][18].
- The evidence on municipal services rests on one Low confidence civil society study, and a systematic count of municipal e-services and their use would be needed to judge progress at local level [9].
- The Cadastre Office result covers one period and the sentence does not state where the figures come from, so a further year of data from a stated source would show whether it holds [4].
