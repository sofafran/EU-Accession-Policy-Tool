# Issue brief: Readiness for electronic exchange of social security information

RI-FMW-010 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The institutions lack the IT and human capacity to exchange social security information electronically with Member States (the EESSI system) and to use electronic health insurance data. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to develop human capacity and upgrade its IT capacity so that it can exchange social security data with Member States through EESSI, and to step up its capacity to use EHIC data electronically so that it is ready at accession [1][2][3]. The Commission finds the country still at an early stage on EESSI [4]. The country reports plans for a single unified registry of insured persons within two years, financed by a World Bank loan, and for an analysis of law, capacity and IT infrastructure leading to defined EESSI access points within five years [5][6]. Both are assessed as proposed, and no source shows either in operation. The country also reports that its healthcare facilities have no EHIC reader, so the data cannot be processed electronically [7]. The unresolved gap is assessed as open. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is insufficient_evidence and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is the human and IT capacity to exchange social security data through EESSI and to process EHIC data electronically. The registry and the EESSI preparations are only proposed (statuses that are themselves proposals), and the country reports no EHIC reader in its healthcare facilities.

**Direction of travel.** The sub-area rating 'still at an early stage' matches this issue but appears only in the 2025 screening report, and the annual key recommendation adds electronic exchange only in 2025. The word 'still' suggests the issue is not new, so I do not call it new, and a single year cannot show a trend.

**Priority.** Electronic exchange is central to applying coordination at accession, and the Commission asks for stepped-up effort. The support is thin, however, as it comes from the screening report and one annual key recommendation.

**Tractability.** The planned registry has a stated World Bank loan and two-year horizon, but it rests on the country's own account. Several institutions are involved, EESSI access points follow later analysis over five years, and connection also involves the EU side, with no past progress recorded.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement appears in the evidence only from 2025, so no change over 2020 to 2025 can be traced. The February 2025 screening report states that North Macedonia is "still at an early stage" on EESSI and asks it to step up alignment, develop human capacities, upgrade IT capacities and increase its capacity to use EHIC data electronically [4][8][1][2][3]. The November 2025 annual report then adds administrative preparation for electronic exchange of social security data to its key recommendation, alongside concluding bilateral agreements [9]. The word "still" suggests the issue predates 2025, but the earlier reports in the evidence do not state it.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Direction of travel is insufficient_evidence because the rating rests on a single year of Commission assessment, and earlier Commission or government material on EESSI readiness would be needed to show a trend.
- The registry and its World Bank financing rest on the country's own account in the screening report, and loan documents or project progress reports would show whether the plan has moved forward [5].
- No source reports progress on defining EESSI access points or on equipping healthcare facilities with EHIC readers, and later Commission reports or ministry and Health Insurance Fund updates would be needed to settle whether either has begun [6][7].
