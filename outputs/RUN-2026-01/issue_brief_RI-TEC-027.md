# Issue brief: Interoperability framework not aligned with EU

RI-TEC-027 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The national interoperability framework is not aligned with the latest European Interoperability Framework or the Interoperable Europe Act. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align its national interoperability framework and legislation with the European Interoperability Framework and the Interoperable Europe Act [1][2]. North Macedonia stated at the 2023 screening that its framework was aligned with European Interoperability Framework 2.0 and that it planned further alignment with the updated version, and its 2025 digital strategy, in machine translation, says care will be taken to align [3][4][5]. The State Audit Office found in 2022 that no study supported the ministry's claim to follow that framework [6]. The alignment itself is rated proposed, and no aligned text is shown. The Commission links outdated legislation with under-use of the interoperability platform as an obstacle to digital services [7]. The validated assessment is that the gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is Medium and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The only responding intervention, alignment with the framework, has proposed status at the freeze, and this status is itself a proposal, so no aligned text is shown. Items are in tension and this is not recorded in the contradictions file: the 2023 screening says the framework is aligned with framework 2.0, while the 2023 report asks for alignment with the 2017 revision and the audit (machine translated, capped at Medium) found no study confirming alignment. What remains is a national framework shown to be aligned with the current European framework and with the Interoperable Europe Act.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission repeated the recommendation to align with the European Interoperability Framework and the Interoperable Europe Act in near-identical terms in 2023, 2024 and 2025, which sets the persistent default under the rules. The 2025 wording ('efforts need to continue') reports no result. The chapter scale is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** This is a repeated EU requirement across three reports, but no item places it in a benchmark, a Reform Agenda payment condition or the main findings list of a report. It supports the interoperability theme, yet the Commission itself treats use of the platform as the wider obstacle.

**Tractability.** The mechanism is legal and framework alignment, which one ministry can lead without depending on others. The caution is the record: the recommendation has not moved in three years and the plan is still only stated in a government strategy.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement has barely changed. In July 2023 the screening report asks North Macedonia to align with the latest European Interoperability Framework and to seek alignment with the proposed Interoperable Europe Act [8][9]. The 2023 and 2024 reports repeat in identical words that the country should align with both the framework, latest revised in 2017, and the Interoperable Europe Act, and the 2024 report specifies national legislation [10][11][1]. In 2025 the wording becomes a call to continue efforts to align with both, which reports no result [12][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The 2023 screening states that the national framework is aligned with European Interoperability Framework 2.0, while the Commission asks for alignment with the latest framework and the audit found no alignment study; this tension is not recorded as a confirmed conflict, and a Commission assessment of the national framework text would settle it [3][10][6].
- The only evidence of current national plans is a machine-translated strategy capped at Medium confidence, so a draft or adopted legal text aligning the framework would settle whether work has started [5].
- The Commission treats under-use of the interoperability platform as part of the obstacle, so evidence on whether alignment changes how many institutions exchange data would show whether alignment matters in practice [7].
