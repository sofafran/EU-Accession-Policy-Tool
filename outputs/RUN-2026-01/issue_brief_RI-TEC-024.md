# Issue brief: Security of 5G networks not ensured

RI-TEC-024 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** The EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, including supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers, is not fully transposed or applied when 5G networks are deployed. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to implement the EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, including supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers, and the Reform Agenda requires a fully implemented 5G security by-law by December 2027 [1][2][3]. April 2021 amendments gave the regulator prior security approval of 5G equipment, and the Commission reported the regulator adopted by-laws in 2022 [4][5], but the screening report says not all the amendments were fully implemented [6]. The 2025 Law on Electronic Communications, validated as adopted, empowers the regulator to assess suppliers and restrict high-risk ones, according to a machine-translated text [7]. The 5G security by-law and the supplier risk assessment remain proposed, and no item shows an assessment carried out. No outcome is recorded. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The latest EU items ask the country to continue implementing the toolbox, including assessing suppliers and restricting high-risk ones, and the Reform Agenda requires the security by-law fully implemented by December 2027; the 2025 law is proposed as adopted, the by-law and supplier assessment as proposed, and the 2021 amendments as reversed because the new law replaces them, all of which are proposals for review. What remains is adopting and applying the by-law and actually carrying out supplier risk assessments and any restrictions, since the law gives the power (machine translated, capped at Medium) but no item shows it used. The Reform Agenda's statement that the step is largely achieved sits against its own partial compliance baseline and the Commission's later wording, and it appears to rest mainly on spectrum and legislation, so it is not treated as closure; the Commission's finding that this law's reform step was not achieved concerns the Code and the Gigabit Act and not the toolbox, and the law allows 15 months for by-laws, so the finding does not decide this gap.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission has named the 5G toolbox in its recommendations in four successive reports (2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, two of these items being classified under the framework issue), and its November 2025 wording still asks the country to continue, so the default of persistent applies; the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. Legal steps did occur (2021 amendments, 2022 by-laws, the June 2025 law with supplier risk provisions), but the screening report says not all the 2021 amendments had been implemented. A reviewer could read the 2025 law as the start of improvement, but it began to apply only in June 2026 and no later EU item assesses it.

**Priority.** Toolbox compliance is a Reform Agenda step and has been named in the Commission's key recommendations in four successive reports, and the enlargement communication presents it as a basis of secure digital infrastructure. The issue is narrow, one clause among several in the chapter's key recommendation, so centrality is the weakest part of this rating and a reviewer could reasonably set Medium. The small number of items did not lower the rating, as volume is not a criterion.

**Tractability.** Few actors are needed, mainly the regulator and the Ministry working with operators, the law already provides the power and by-laws under the 2021 amendments were reported adopted by the following year, which all favour progress. However, not all the 2021 amendments were implemented four years on, a supplier assessment planned from 2023 is not shown as done, and operators complained of having no consultation in 2021. The evidence does not show what restrictions would cost operators, so the rating is not set higher.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has named the 5G toolbox in successive recommendations from 2022 to 2025, and the core requirement has not changed [8][9][10][11]. In 2024 the Reform Agenda turned it into a payment condition linked to a 5G security by-law and 5G roll-out [2]. In 2025 the wording became more specific, asking the country to continue implementing the toolbox, including assessing suppliers' risk profiles and restricting or excluding high-risk suppliers [12][1]. The Commission's 2025 enlargement communication also presents secure 5G infrastructure under the toolbox as a regional priority [13].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the regulator has carried out any supplier risk assessment or restriction is not shown, although one was planned from 2023 [16]; regulator supervision reports or a Commission assessment would settle it.
- The supplier assessment power rests on a machine-translated legal text capped at Medium confidence [7], so the official text or an English Commission summary is needed.
- The Reform Agenda says the step is largely achieved while its own baseline records partial compliance [18][17]; a Commission assessment of the 5G toolbox step itself would settle how far it has been met.
- No EU item assesses the 2025 law's application since June 2026, so later Commission reporting is needed to judge whether the direction of travel is changing.
