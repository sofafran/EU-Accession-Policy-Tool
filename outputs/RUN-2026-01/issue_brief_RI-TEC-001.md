# Issue brief: Artificial intelligence framework not aligned or supported by structures

RI-TEC-001 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** The rules on artificial intelligence are not aligned with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, or the enforcement structures and wider capacity needed to apply them and support trustworthy uptake are not in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission invites North Macedonia to align fully with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act and to put in place the necessary enforcement structures [1]. The State Audit Office reports that a working group formed in 2021 met only once and that no national AI strategy or legislation for public sector AI projects has been adopted [2][3][4]. The 2025 digital strategy lists drafting an AI strategy and legislation aligned with the AI Act as planned actions, assessed as proposed [5][6]. The audit reports that the ADA digital assistant is not functioning after about EUR 150 thousand was spent, and the strategy route and ADA are each assessed as failed [7]. The Commission welcomed the signed AI Factory Antenna grant, but no activity under it is reported [8]. Much of the national evidence is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence. The gap is assessed as open. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is new (as an EU requirement) and tractability is Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |
|---|---|
| Unresolved gap | open |
| Direction of travel | new |
| Priority | Medium |
| Tractability | Medium |

**What remains.** Against the EU requirement of 2025 and 2026 to develop AI regulation and the governance and enforcement structures required by the AI Act, the 2026 report records only initial alignment work and preparatory steps to identify those structures. The signed Council of Europe convention, the adopted ICT strategy and the established AI Factory Antenna do not supply alignment or enforcement, and the national AI legislation and AI strategy remain plans, as national sources confirm (a machine-translated audit, capped at Medium). What remains is AI regulation aligned with the AI Act and an authority with the means to enforce it, none of which yet exists; the audit's statement that data protection law is aligned with the GDPR conflicts with the Commission's statement that full alignment is pending, and this is left unresolved.

**Direction of travel.** The EU requirement first appears in 2025, as an invitation to align fully with the AI Act, and the 2026 report restates it as developing AI regulation and the governance and enforcement structures the Act requires; two reports are too few for the persistent default, and the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. The 2026 report records first steps (initial alignment work including preparatory steps to identify those structures, signature in May 2026 of the Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence, which the report does not say was ratified, and a national AI Factory Antenna), but these are preparatory and do not yet show alignment, so they do not support improving. National sources show the gap is older than the EU ask, so the value means new as an EU requirement, not new as a national gap.

**Priority.** The rating rests on the EU layer: the AI requirement sits in body text in the 2025 and 2026 reports, is not a benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, and is absent from the Chapter 10 key recommendations of both years, which in 2026 name digital services, cybersecurity and digital trust but not AI. Repetition in two reports is short of the three the test asks for, so Medium reflects a requirement central to the AI theme with thin but growing support, and a third report repeating it, or its entry into the key recommendations, would support High. The much larger national audit, strategy and research evidence was given no weight.

**Tractability.** Drafting is within government control, with the EU Act as a template, and the 2026 report records initial alignment work, signature of the Council of Europe convention and an AI Factory Antenna established under an EU grant signed in April 2026, which show that first steps can be taken. Enforcement structures still need a designated authority, specialist staff and funding across several bodies, and the earlier record is weak (one working group meeting since 2021 and a digital assistant no longer functioning, in machine-translated national evidence capped at Medium). The Commission also finds that the data protection agency cannot effectively police the increased use of AI and biometric data because of its limited oversight capacity.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement is recent, so there is no repeated Commission wording to compare. The first Commission item appears in the November 2025 report, which invites North Macedonia to "fully align" with the AI Act and to ensure the necessary enforcement structures [1]. The only later Commission item, from April 2026, does not mention the AI Act: it welcomes the AI Factory Antenna grant and encourages outreach to neighbouring countries, a regional ecosystem of experts and users, and use of the European Digital Innovation Hubs [9]. The requirement is therefore new as an EU ask, although national sources show that the gap itself is older [10][3].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The State Audit Office says data protection in AI projects is governed by law aligned with the GDPR, while the Commission says full GDPR alignment is still pending, and this confirmed conflict is left unresolved pending a comparison of the law against the GDPR [13][14].
- The success of the AI pilot in the 112 emergency service is insufficient_evidence, and measured data on response times or call handling would be needed to judge it [28][29].
- Key evidence comes from the machine-translated State Audit Office report and digital strategy, capped at Medium confidence, so checking the Macedonian originals or finding independent English sources would strengthen the assessment [4][6].
- The audit finds that no public sector AI project has been implemented while also reporting the Buki platform at a state university, and a clarification of scope from the audit office would settle whether these differ only in scope [30][31].
