# Issue brief: Artificial intelligence framework not aligned or supported by structures

RI-TEC-001 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The rules on artificial intelligence are not aligned with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, or the enforcement structures and wider capacity needed to apply them and support trustworthy uptake are not in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission invites North Macedonia to align fully with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act and to put in place the necessary enforcement structures [1]. The State Audit Office reports that a working group formed in 2021 met only once and that no national AI strategy or legislation for public sector AI projects has been adopted [2][3][4]. The 2025 digital strategy lists drafting an AI strategy and legislation aligned with the AI Act as planned actions, assessed as proposed [5][6]. The audit reports that the ADA digital assistant is not functioning after about EUR 150 thousand was spent, and the strategy route and ADA are each assessed as failed [7]. The Commission welcomed the signed AI Factory Antenna grant, but no activity under it is reported [8]. Much of the national evidence is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence. The gap is assessed as open. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is new (as an EU requirement) and tractability is Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |
|---|---|
| Unresolved gap | open |
| Direction of travel | new |
| Priority | Medium |
| Tractability | Medium |

**What remains.** Against the single EU alignment ask of 2025, the responding AI legislation and AI strategy are still proposed, and the adopted ICT strategy and signed Antenna grant do not supply alignment or enforcement; these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is alignment with the AI Act and the enforcement structures it needs, none of which yet exists. National sources confirm the absence (machine-translated audit, capped at Medium), and the audit's statement that data protection law is aligned with the GDPR conflicts with the Commission's statement that full alignment is pending, which is left unresolved.

**Direction of travel.** The EU requirement first appears in 2025 (AI Act alignment and enforcement structures) and the only later EU item, in 2026, concerns the AI Factory Antenna, so there is no repeated EU wording to compare; the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. Separately, national sources show the gap is older than the EU ask: a 2024 government plan mentions AI as a future need, and the 2025 audit traces a stalled strategy to a 2021 working group, but these are one-year snapshots rather than a trend. The value therefore means new as an EU requirement, not new as a national gap.

**Priority.** The rating rests only on the EU layer: one body-text invitation in the 2025 report to align fully with the AI Act, plus a 2026 readout that encourages outreach and does not mention the Act. It is not a benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, is absent from the 2025 Chapter 10 key recommendations and is not repeated, so it cannot be High; Medium reflects a requirement central to the AI theme with thin support. The much larger national audit, strategy and research evidence (over 200 of 229 items, mostly from a few documents) describes the gap but was given no weight here.

**Tractability.** Drafting is within government control, with the EU Act as a template and AI legislation already listed in an adopted action plan, but enforcement structures need a designated authority, specialist staff and funding, across several bodies. The record is weak: one working group meeting since 2021, a digital assistant no longer functioning and no line of accountability between the ministry and the innovation fund, though a data protection law gives some base and an EU-funded Antenna grant is signed. This leans on machine-translated national evidence (capped at Medium), and the audit's finding that no public sector project has been implemented sits against its report of the Buki platform at a state university, a difference of scope it does not explain.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement is recent, so there is no repeated Commission wording to compare. The first Commission item appears in the November 2025 report, which invites North Macedonia to "fully align" with the AI Act and to ensure the necessary enforcement structures [1]. The only later Commission item, from April 2026, does not mention the AI Act: it welcomes the AI Factory Antenna grant and encourages outreach to neighbouring countries, a regional ecosystem of experts and users, and use of the European Digital Innovation Hubs [9]. The requirement is therefore new as an EU ask, although national sources show that the gap itself is older [10][3].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The State Audit Office says data protection in AI projects is governed by law aligned with the GDPR, while the Commission says full GDPR alignment is still pending, and this confirmed conflict is left unresolved pending a comparison of the law against the GDPR [13][14].
- The success of the AI pilot in the 112 emergency service is insufficient_evidence, and measured data on response times or call handling would be needed to judge it [25][26].
- Key evidence comes from the machine-translated State Audit Office report and digital strategy, capped at Medium confidence, so checking the Macedonian originals or finding independent English sources would strengthen the assessment [4][6].
- The audit finds that no public sector AI project has been implemented while also reporting the Buki platform at a state university, and a clarification of scope from the audit office would settle whether these differ only in scope [23][24].
