# Issue brief: Legal and organisational framework for joining EURES

RI-FMW-004 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** The country has not set up the legal and organisational arrangements needed to join the European network of employment services, such as a system for appointing EURES members and partners and access for private employment agencies. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to establish a system for appointing EURES members and partners and to give private employment agencies access to it, and its key recommendation asks the country to strengthen the Employment Service Agency and align legislation and good practice with the EURES and European Labour Authority regulations [1][2][3]. At screening, North Macedonia stated that the Agency will be the main EURES implementing body and National Coordination Office, and that it foresees an EU-funded analysis of harmonisation needs and stronger Agency capacity [4][5]. The amendments needed to comply fully have a deadline of 2030, with implementation upon accession [6]. All four responding measures remain proposed, and the Commission reported no developments on EURES in November 2025 [7]. There is no outcome evidence. The validated judgements are priority High, direction of travel persistent, unresolved gap partly_addressed and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is a legal system for appointing EURES members and partners and access for private employment agencies. The Commission credits good alignment on IT and structural levels, but every responding intervention is only proposed (these statuses are themselves proposals), with legislative amendments on a 2030 deadline and implementation at accession.

**Direction of travel.** Sub-area wording on EURES, which matches this issue, says no developments or measures in 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025, and the 2023 key recommendation still asks for alignment. The screening report's 'good level of preparation' sits in tension with these and with its own finding that no appointment system exists; it is the only rating of this kind, so I do not read it as improvement, and I report the tension rather than resolve it.

**Priority.** Alignment with EURES is named in the key recommendation in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports, and EURES is one of the chapter's main strands. The recommendation is a composite one, so the repetition is of one sentence and not of independent findings.

**Tractability.** One lead body is planned and the IT and structural base is credited, which helps. However, no legal step has been reported in six years, the country's own timetable runs to 2030 and implementation is tied to accession.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* From 2020 to 2022 the Commission recorded only that nothing had been done to prepare for joining EURES on accession [8][9][10]. From 2023 the key recommendation asked the country to strengthen the Employment Service Agency and align legislation and good practice with EURES and European Labour Authority rules, in near-identical words in 2023, 2024 and 2025 [11][12][3]. The 2025 screening report made the requirement specific: an appointment system for EURES members and partners, and access and adequate connection for private employment agencies [1][2][13]. It also rated preparation as good and alignment as good at IT and structural levels [14][15].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The screening report's rating of a good level of preparation sits in tension with its own finding that no appointment system exists and with the annual reports of no developments, and the analysis reports this tension rather than resolving it; the Commission's next assessment or a statement of the basis for the rating would settle it [14][17][7].
- No source shows whether the planned analysis of harmonisation needs, the designation of the Agency as National Coordination Office or the capacity strengthening has started, and Agency or government progress reports would settle this [4][5].
- The content and timing of the legislative amendments within the 2030 deadline are unknown, and a draft law or a dated legislative plan would show whether the appointment system and private agency access are covered [6].
