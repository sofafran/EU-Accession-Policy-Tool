# Issue brief: Electronic communications law not aligned with EU rules

RI-TEC-011 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The law on electronic communications is not fully aligned with the European Electronic Communications Code, the Gigabit Infrastructure Act and related rules on cost reduction, spectrum, roaming and open internet access. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align its electronic communications law with the European Electronic Communications Code and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act, and ties adoption of an aligned law to a Reform Agenda payment [1][2]. A new Law on Electronic Communications was adopted in June 2025 and amended in December 2025, with application from 1 June 2026 and by-laws due within 15 months [3][4][5]. Both measures are validated as adopted, not as applied. The Commission found the Reform Agenda step not achieved in 2025 and 2026 while the country considers it achieved, a confirmed conflict [6][7][8]. Separately, the Regional Roaming Agreement is judged to have worked, on Commission statements without price data [9]. What remains is closing the gaps with the Gigabit Infrastructure Act in two laws and adopting the by-laws. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The Commission's May 2026 assessment considers the Reform Agenda step not achieved and the beneficiary considers it achieved, as it did in 2025, so the two sides conflict and the Commission's finding is the requirement compared here. Responding interventions carry proposed statuses that the reviewers check at G6: the 2025 law and December amendments adopted, the by-laws and 2026 draft amendments only proposed, and the law applicable only from 1 June 2026, with no evidence on the outcome of the grace period ending 30 June 2026. What remains is closing the harmonisation gaps with the Gigabit Infrastructure Act in both laws and adopting and applying the by-laws; the ePrivacy conflict is left to its own issue under the taxonomy.

**Direction of travel.** Commission wording that the law needs further alignment repeated from 2020 to 2022, and the matching sub-area wording in 2023 was no progress, but the wording then changed: the 2025 assessment found significant gaps after the new law, and the 2026 assessment found alignment partially achieved after the December 2025 amendments. The chapter-wide limited progress of 2024 is context only and was not decisive. Sources conflict on the Broadband Cost Reduction Directive: the Commission said in 2024 that the country was yet to align, while the country's reported view in the screening is full alignment, and this is not resolved here.

**Priority.** Adoption of an aligned law is a Reform Agenda step with a payment provision, and alignment with the Code recurs in the Commission's chapter recommendations from 2022 to 2025. It is central to the digital infrastructure theme because other reforms in the area rest on this legal framework. The roaming and net neutrality parts of the issue are largely positive and do not drive this rating.

**Tractability.** The mechanisms are legal alignment and implementing rules, led by one ministry with the regulator and parliament, and a new law and amendments were adopted in 2025, which favours progress. Against that, the Commission assessed the Reform Agenda step as not achieved in both 2025 and 2026, with gaps remaining in two laws, and the by-laws are described as technically demanding and were not yet due. Adoption has been quick but alignment has twice fallen short, which does not support High.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* From 2020 to 2022 the Commission repeated that the Law on Electronic Communications needed further alignment with the EU acquis [10][11][12]. From 2022 its recommendations named the Electronic Communications Code and the Broadband Cost Reduction Directive, and in 2023 it found no progress [13][14]. In 2024 the Reform Agenda made adoption of a new law aligned with the Gigabit Infrastructure Act a payment condition [1]. After the 2025 law, the Commission found significant harmonisation gaps [15], and in May 2026, after the December amendments, it found alignment only partially achieved and asked for amendments to both the electronic communications and construction laws [16][17].

## Conflicting evidence

- North Macedonia's reported view in the screening report is that it is fully aligned with the Broadband Cost Reduction Directive, while the Commission's 2024 report says it is yet to align with that directive. [26][27]
- On the same Reform Agenda step on the Law on Electronic Communications in the same 2025 assessment, the beneficiary considers it fully achieved and the Commission considers it not achieved. [28][6]
- On the same Reform Agenda step in the same 2026 assessment, after the December 2025 amendments, the beneficiary considers it achieved and the Commission considers it not achieved. [7][8]
- The regulator's 2025 work report dates the start of the roam like at home regime to 1 June 2021, while the Commission dates the regional roaming-free zone to 1 July 2021. [29][30]
- North Macedonia stated in the screening published in February 2025 that the Law for Electronic Communications is fully aligned with the ePrivacy Directive, while the Commission's 2024 Report states that further efforts are needed for alignment with that Directive. [31][32]

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The outcome of the Reform Agenda grace period ending 30 June 2026 is not in the evidence [35], so a later Commission assessment of the step is needed to settle whether alignment was reached.
- Whether the 2025 law actually began to apply on 1 June 2026 and whether its by-laws were adopted is not shown, since the dates rest on the legal text in machine translation capped at Medium confidence [46][5]; regulator or Commission reports after June 2026 would settle it.
- Two confirmed conflicts remain unresolved: on the Broadband Cost Reduction Directive between the Commission and the country's screening view [27][26], and on the start date of roam like at home [29][30]; a Commission statement on the current alignment and an authoritative date source would settle them.
- The roaming results rest on Commission statements, and later reductions towards 2028 are judged too_early [25], so actual roaming price data for North Macedonia are needed.
