# Issue brief: Low digital adoption by businesses

RI-TEC-033 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** Digitalisation of the economy and adoption of digital technologies by small businesses remain low compared with the EU and undermine competitiveness, and EU programmes and digital innovation hubs are little used. **Problem mechanisms:** Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission finds that digitalisation of the economy remains low and restrains the competitiveness of domestic companies, and it asks North Macedonia to support companies through the European Digital Innovation Hub and to take part actively in Digital Europe Programme calls [1][2][3]. The country signed an association agreement to the Digital Europe Programme in June 2023, and the Commission reports that the first European Digital Innovation Hub was set up [4][5]. No item shows actual participation in calls. SME vouchers, a support portal and a grant mechanism appear only as plans in a machine-translated strategy [6][7][8]. The Commission notes that SME digital adoption has accelerated but remains uneven and largely basic [9]. No outcome is linked to any intervention. The validated assessment is that the gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |

| Direction of travel | persistent |
| Priority | Medium |
| Tractability | Medium |

**What remains.** The latest EU asks concern support to companies through the innovation hub and active use of Digital Europe calls; the hub is set up and the association agreement signed, while vouchers, the support portal and the grant mechanism are proposed, and these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is actual use of these channels and SME adoption beyond basic technologies, as the Commission still reported low digitalisation in 2026.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission's wording is close to unchanged across reports: advancing but low in 2020 to 2023, advancing slowly in 2024 and 2025, and low in 2026, with one 2025 note that SME adoption has accelerated but stays uneven and basic. The chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. The 54.5% website figure repeats across reports and was not treated as new evidence, and the 3% and 19.7% online sales figures use different populations, so they show no trend.

**Priority.** The finding is repeated in most years, largely in economic governance documents, but it has few specific recommendations; the only key recommendation found is the 2025 hub item, and no benchmark or payment condition names it in the input. It is therefore a repeated EU concern that is not central to accession steps.

**Tractability.** Government can fund and steer support, and EU programmes and a first hub already exist, but results depend on many small firms' demand, skills and finance. Past effort is small and not evaluated: EUR 0.5 million budgeted in 2023, and the strategy itself says there has been no support mechanism for projects of greater national importance; that source is machine translated and capped at Medium.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording is close to unchanged. From 2020 to 2023 it states that digitalisation of the economy is advancing but remains low compared with the EU [10][11][12][13]. In 2024 and 2025 the wording becomes advancing slowly, and economic governance documents link low digitalisation and weak innovation to weak business competitiveness [14][15][16][17]. In 2025 the Commission adds more specific asks, on active participation in Digital Europe calls and support to companies through the European Digital Innovation Hub, and notes accelerated but uneven SME adoption [3][2][9]. In 2026 it still describes digitalisation as low [1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows North Macedonia taking part in Digital Europe Programme calls or how many companies the innovation hub has supported, so participation and hub activity data would settle whether these channels are used [3][5].
- The online sales figures of 3% of SMEs and 19.7% of enterprises use different populations and the 54.5% website figure is repeated across reports, so a consistent time series on enterprise digital adoption would be needed to show any trend [20][21][18][19].
- The C-JET grant to assess SME digital needs is rated insufficient_evidence, so a World Bank report on its status and findings would settle it [26].
- The evidence on planned support schemes and on the absence of a support mechanism comes from a machine-translated strategy capped at Medium confidence, so programme documents or budget data showing schemes in operation would strengthen the record [25][6].
