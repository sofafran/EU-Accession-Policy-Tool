# Issue brief: National social security law not aligned with the coordination regulations

RI-FMW-009 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National pension, health and other social security laws are only partly aligned with the EU coordination regulations, for example through citizenship conditions, and the administrative measures for applying them at accession are not identified or in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to put in place administrative measures to prepare for accession and for electronic exchange of social security data, and to align its pension and disability and health insurance laws with the coordination regulations by accession, including on nationality conditions [1][2]. The Commission finds the national framework partly aligned and notes that both laws in some cases require citizenship for access to insurance [3][4][5]. The pension, health insurance and unemployment insurance laws and the 2023 law on the register of mandatory social insurance are assessed as adopted, with no evidence on how they are applied or that the register exists [6][7][8]. The country plans within five years to analyse its law, capacity and IT infrastructure, amend the law and define EESSI access points, assessed as proposed [9]. The Commission rates preparation on coordination as good because of experience with bilateral agreements, assessed as worked with Medium attribution but narrow in scope [10][11]. The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is removing citizenship conditions from the pension and health insurance laws, aligning with the regulations and putting administrative measures in place by accession. The Commission finds the framework partly aligned, but the plan to amend the legislation is only proposed within five years (a status that is itself a proposal).

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was context only, and the 2025 ratings have nothing earlier to compare with. The recommendation on administrative measures recurs from 2020 to 2025, and the shift from identifying measures to putting them in place reflects wording, not a reported step.

**Priority.** Administrative measures for coordination are in the key recommendation in each report from 2020 to 2025, and alignment with the regulations is central to this strand of the chapter. The screening report requires alignment, including on nationality conditions, by accession.

**Tractability.** The named legal changes are within national control, and the register law adopted in 2023 shows some related legislative activity. However, it spans the pension and health systems, needs prior analysis and has a plan of up to five years.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The key recommendation on administrative measures has run through every report since 2020. In 2020 to 2022 the Commission asked the country to identify which administrative measures would be needed before accession, with 2021 and 2022 adding further bilateral agreements on coordination and health insurance [12][13][14]. From 2023 the wording shifts from identifying measures to putting them in place, which was assessed as a change of wording rather than a reported step [15][16]. The 2025 report adds electronic exchange of social security data [1]. The February 2025 screening report adds a specific legal requirement: removing citizenship conditions from the pension and health insurance laws by accession [17][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows the register of mandatory social insurance established after the 2023 law, so evidence of the register in operation would settle whether the law has gone beyond adoption [6].
- The description of how the health insurance and unemployment insurance laws work comes only from the country's own presentation at screening, so independent evidence on their application, for example to EU or other foreign workers, is missing [7][8].
- The five-year plan to amend the legislation and define EESSI access points has no reported steps, and a draft amendment, the planned analysis or an EESSI readiness report would show whether it is moving [9].
