# Issue brief: Public sector posts closed to EU citizens beyond the public authority exception

RI-FMW-003 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** EU citizens are barred from public sector posts other than a short list of exemptions, rather than only from posts involving the exercise of public authority and safeguarding the general interests of the State. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to ensure that, by accession, restrictions on access to the public sector apply only to posts directly involving the exercise of public authority and the safeguarding of the general interest of the State, in line with the case law of the Court of Justice [1][2]. The Commission reports each year that EU citizens cannot access public administration posts other than a short list of positions in healthcare and higher education [3][4]. The screening report records that foreigners have no access to public employment, with exemptions for medical, dental and pharmacy experts in health and for higher education [5][6]. No intervention responding to this issue is linked in the evidence, so there is no implementation status and no outcome to assess. The gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is limiting the restriction to posts involving public authority and the general interest of the State, in line with case law of the Court of Justice. No responding intervention is linked, so there is no status to compare, and the existing exemptions for health and higher education are the country's account and are narrower than the requirement.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is part of the chapter and no sub-area wording matches. The finding that EU citizens cannot take public administration posts, apart from some in health and education, is unchanged in each report from 2020 to 2025.

**Priority.** This is a repeated finding central to the chapter, but public sector posts are named as a requirement only in the screening report and not in an annual key recommendation. If the reviewers read 'in all sectors' in the 2023 to 2025 key recommendation as covering them, this would be High.

**Tractability.** The work is national and depends on no outside party, but it requires a careful review of civil servant categories across the public service against case law. There is no evidence of past progress and no responding intervention.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's finding has stayed essentially the same. In 2020 it stated that EU citizens do not have access to public administration posts, and from 2021 it added the exception for some positions in health and education [7][8]. The 2022 and 2023 reports say EU citizens "still" cannot access these posts, and the 2024 and 2025 reports describe the exceptions as a short list in healthcare and higher education [9][10][3][4]. The explicit requirement appears only in the February 2025 screening report, which sets the public authority test, asks the country to examine which civil service categories could be reserved for nationals, and links both to case law [1][11][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- It is not settled whether the words "in all sectors" in the 2023 to 2025 key recommendation cover public sector posts, which would raise the priority to High, and a statement from the Commission or the reviewers' reading of the wider report would settle it [12][14].
- The evidence contains no government plan, review of civil servant categories or draft law on this issue, so a national response document would be needed before any status could be assessed [11].
- The scope of the health and higher education exemptions rests on the country's account in the screening report, and the legal texts or data on EU citizens employed under them would confirm how they work [5][15][6].
