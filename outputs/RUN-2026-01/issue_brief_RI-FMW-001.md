# Issue brief: Nationality discrimination in access to employment

RI-FMW-001 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** The law governing access to the labour market does not give EU workers equal treatment with nationals, for example by treating them as any other foreigner or reserving private sector posts for nationals. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align its law on access to the labour market with the EU acquis, in particular on non-discrimination on grounds of nationality against EU workers in all sectors, so that by accession EU citizens can work there without restrictions such as work permits [1][2][3]. The Law on Employment and Work of Foreigners is in practice, and it was amended in 2021 and in April 2025, but the 2025 amendment only renames ministries and bodies, and further amendments on the employment of foreigners are listed only as proposed in machine-translated government reports [4][5][6][7]. The Commission states that the law does not distinguish EU citizens from other foreigners and that some private sector posts are reserved for nationals [8][9]. North Macedonia points to the ban on nationality discrimination in the Law on Labour Relations, which is adopted but not shown in application [10][11]. No outcome has been assessed. The gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is equal treatment of EU workers in the law on access to the labour market, including an end to posts in private companies reserved for nationals. The foreigners law is itself the barrier, the 2025 amendment only renames bodies and the further amendments are only proposed with unknown content (all statuses are proposals; the two government items are machine translated, so capped at Medium), while the country's general ban on nationality discrimination is its own account and does not close the gap. The Commission's statement that the law makes no EU distinction conflicts with the country's account on quotas, which is reported and partly explained under RI-FMW-002.

**Direction of travel.** Sub-area wording on access to the labour market, which matches this issue, says no progress in 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, and the 2025 report says there were no legislative developments. The 2025 screening finding of partial alignment is a first assessment of that kind and does not show a change.

**Priority.** A key recommendation on non-discrimination against EU workers is repeated in the 2020 to 2025 reports, and equal access is central to the chapter. No benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition under this issue was found in the evidence.

**Tractability.** The change is legal and within the government's control, and the same law was amended in 2021 and 2025. Against that, six reports record no alignment and the requirement runs to the time of accession, so there is no evidence of past progress.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The core recommendation has barely changed. From 2020 to 2022 the Commission asked the country to continue adapting the legal framework on access to the labour market, in particular on non-discrimination on grounds of nationality against EU workers [12][13][14]. From 2023 it added the words "in all sectors", and in 2025 "adapt" became "align" [15][16][1]. Each report from 2020 to 2024 found no progress, and the 2025 report found no legislative developments [17][18][19]. The February 2025 screening report made the requirement more concrete: partial alignment, an end-point at accession with no work permit or residence permit for EU workers, and exclusion of EU citizens from the annual foreign worker quotas [20][2][21].

## Conflicting evidence

- The Commission states that the law does not distinguish between EU citizens and other foreigners, while North Macedonia states that under the same law the quotas do not apply to EU citizens following a reciprocity agreement with the EU. [22][23]

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The Commission states that the foreigners law makes no distinction for EU citizens, while North Macedonia states that the quotas do not apply to EU citizens under a reciprocity agreement, and this confirmed conflict could be settled by the text of the law or the agreement and data on how permits are issued to EU citizens [22][23].
- The content of the proposed amendments on the employment of foreigners, and whether they are the law adopted in April 2025, is not known from the machine-translated Employment Agency reports, so the draft or adopted text would settle it [6][7].
- No source shows how the non-discrimination rule in the Law on Labour Relations is applied to foreign or EU workers, which inspection, court or complaints data could show [10].
