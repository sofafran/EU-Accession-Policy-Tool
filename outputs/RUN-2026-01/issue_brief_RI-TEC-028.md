# Issue brief: Interoperability platform and data exchange underused

RI-TEC-028 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Institutions do not use the national interoperability platform regularly, so administrative data are not shared ex officio between institutions, including for checks such as asset declarations and inspections. **Problem mechanisms:** Coordination failure (MEC-08); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to fully implement the law on general administrative procedures by completing data exchange, and a Reform Agenda payment condition asks for more data exchange between institutions and documents obtained ex officio [1][2][3]. The interoperability platform is in practice, and the Commission reports that customs exchanges data regularly through it [4]. Government figures, machine translated, show connections rising from 39 institutions in 2021 to 58 or 63 in 2025 and transactions reaching 8 million in 2024, with the Commission noting more connections and transactions [5][6][7][8][9]. The Commission still finds only a few institutions using it, and the government's 2021 conclusions on connecting about 1,300 institutions are assessed as failed, with Low attribution, after the audit found only six new requests [10][11]. The planned upgrade remains proposed [12]. The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The platform operates and some institutions, such as customs, exchange data regularly, but the 2025 Commission recommendation still asks to complete data exchange and the responding upgrades and reforms are mainly proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals. There is a confirmed contradiction: the country told the 2023 screening that the once-only principle is applied, while its 2024 Reform Agenda says institutions still do not gather evidence ex officio. What remains is regular use of the platform by the institutions obliged to use it, so that evidence is obtained ex officio as the law prescribes.

**Direction of travel.** Commission wording that few institutions use the platform runs from 2020 through 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, which sets the persistent default; the 2025 report records only efforts and repeats the requirement. Connection and transaction figures rose (39 institutions in 2021 to 58 or 63 in 2025; 1.5 million transactions in 2022 to 8 million in 2024), and the Commission confirms the increases in 2023 and 2024, but the counts come from government documents, machine translated and capped at Medium, and most institutions remain outside. The 2020 Commission 'slight increase' and the audit's falling traffic are a noted difference of measure and period, and the chapter scale was context only and not decisive.

**Priority.** Data exchange and ex officio evidence sit in a Reform Agenda payment condition, and completing data exchange is a repeated recommendation on public administration reform in 2023, 2024 and 2025, in the main findings in the last two. It is central to the interoperability and data themes. The large number of items, mostly from one national audit, played no part in this rating.

**Tractability.** The platform exists and the government controls it, and use has risen somewhat, which favours progress. Against that, the audit found about 1,300 obliged institutions with no costed plan, IT pay at less than half the private sector and no coordinating body, findings the 2025 strategy partly repeats (all machine translated, capped at Medium), and the Commission cites missing software and lack of will. ITU's account of a ministers' council is a noted difference over a different body, leaving how far coordination worked in practice uncertain.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The diagnosis has hardly changed, while the requirement has become firmer. In 2020 the Commission found that many institutions did not use the system despite having the equipment, and asked for political will and funding [13][14]. In 2021 and 2022 it asked for administrative data sharing to be reinforced and simplified, citing missing communications software and lack of will [15][16][17]. From 2023 a key recommendation asks the country to complete data exchange under the law on general administrative procedures, repeated in 2024 and 2025, and in 2024 the Reform Agenda tied ex officio data exchange to a payment condition [18][19][1][2]. Specific demands also appeared for asset declaration checks and inspections [20][21].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- North Macedonia told the 2023 screening that the once-only principle is applied, while its 2024 Reform Agenda says institutions still do not gather evidence ex officio, a confirmed conflict that data on ex officio requests made through the platform would settle [22][23].
- Connection and transaction figures come from machine-translated government documents capped at Medium confidence and differ by measure and period, including a fall from about 3.6 million transactions in 2021 to 1.5 million in 2022, so a consistent independent series would settle the trend [29][28][8].
- The State Audit Office evidence behind the failed outcome is filed under the government family and machine translated, so the reviewers' confirmation of its independence and an English or verified text would firm up that judgement [10].
- ITU describes a ministers' council while the audit found no body coordinating systems used by several institutions, a noted difference over different bodies that leaves open how far coordination worked in practice, which evidence of that council's decisions on the platform would settle [34][35][31].
