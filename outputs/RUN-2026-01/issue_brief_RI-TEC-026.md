# Issue brief: Electronic identification and trust services not usable or recognised

RI-TEC-026 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Electronic identification and signature tools are costly, cumbersome and little used, qualified trust services are not widely available, and the country is not yet on the EU trusted list or in cross-border wallet arrangements. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

Under the Reform Agenda, the EU asks North Macedonia to join the EU Third Countries trusted list, make qualified trust services available, implement a Digital Identity Wallet and sign the Western Balkans mutual recognition agreement on trust services, and the Commission repeatedly finds the existing e-identification and e-signature tools not user friendly or free for citizens [1][2][3]. The 2019 law is in practice, two providers accredited in 2021 offer advanced and qualified signatures, and the Ministry of Digital Transformation has supervised them [4][5][6]. This provider outcome is assessed as a mixed success with Medium attribution. Electronic identity is in practice but little used, and the machine-translated audit found that high-level eID did not raise use [7][8][9]. The country requested trusted list inclusion on 30 October 2025, but the Commission considers the step not achieved, and the wallet and cross-border recognition remain proposed [10][11][12]. The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Two providers offer advanced and qualified signature services, as the Commission reports and as the Reform Agenda step asks, but the same step is not achieved, the tools remain not free or user friendly, and the wallet and mutual recognition are still proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals. The two sides conflict: North Macedonia reports the trusted list preparation complete and considers the step achieved, while the Commission says the self-assessment and published list are outstanding. What remains is inclusion in the trusted list (grace period to 31 December 2026), usable and free tools in common use, the wallet and cross-border recognition.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission said in 2024 that the tools were cumbersome and neither free nor user friendly, and in 2025 that they were still not, while its 2021 report recorded only a modest rise in electronic signature use. In 2026 it found the trusted list step not achieved despite the request of 30 October 2025, so steps were taken without a changed result. The chapter scale is context only and was not decisive, and the 2024 audit evidence that high-level eID did not raise use is machine translated and capped at Medium.

**Priority.** Joining the trusted list and implementing the wallet are Reform Agenda steps, the first with a stated payment provision, and the Commission repeated its concern about the tools in 2024 and 2025. Recognition and use are central to the electronic identification theme.

**Tractability.** The outstanding trusted list tasks are specific and technical, and two providers have passed ministry checks, which favours progress. Use and usability need many institutions and users, the wallet depends on regional partners and the mutual recognition agreement, and the list depends on Commission assessment. The audit found failed procurement of institutional seal certificates (machine translated, capped at Medium), which is a warning on delivery.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement has grown from usage to recognition. In 2021 the Commission noted only that electronic signature use had increased modestly [13]. In 2024 the Reform Agenda set payment conditions to join the trusted list, issue qualified certificates and implement a wallet and the regional agreement, and the annual report asked for alignment with the revised eIDAS Regulation and described the tools as cumbersome, not free and not user friendly [1][2][14][15]. The 2025 report says the tools are "still" not user friendly or free [3][16]. In May 2026 the Commission specified the outstanding trusted list tasks and set a grace period to 31 December 2026 [17][18].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- North Macedonia reports all trusted list preparations complete and considers the step achieved, while the Commission says the self-assessment and published list are outstanding and the step not achieved, a confirmed conflict that the Commission's decision by the end of the grace period would settle [20][19][17][11].
- The audit could not confirm use of the 150,000 digital identities announced through the Mastercard arrangement or who received 12,000 qualified signatures, in machine-translated text capped at Medium confidence, and certificate issuance and usage data would settle it [25][26].
- There are no current figures on how many citizens and businesses use eID and qualified signatures, and the latest benchmark score of 23% dates from 2022, so a newer benchmark or portal usage data would show whether use has changed [9].
- It is not shown whether the failed procurement of institutional seal certificates, which left seals expired from May 2023, has been resolved, which a later audit or ministry report would confirm [24][23].
