# Issue brief: Access and rights of third-country workers

RI-FMW-014 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Workers from outside the EU face barriers to accessing the labour market, or their rights after admission are not protected or monitored. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

No Commission recommendation on third-country workers under Chapter 2 appears in the evidence, so this issue is raised mainly by international organisations, partly at regional level [1][2]. They report an ageing population and skilled emigration that increase the need for foreign workers, alongside regulatory barriers to their immigration [3][4]. For North Macedonia they report quota-based admission, passport retention, wage deductions of up to 30% and systematic underreporting of exploitation [2][5][6]. They also describe permits tied to one employer and under-resourced, reactive labour inspection [7][8][9]. No intervention by North Macedonia is linked to this issue and no outcome is recorded. The validated unresolved gap is insufficient_evidence and the direction of travel is insufficient_evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium, the latter a provisional view.

**What remains.** No EU requirement item exists for this issue, so the comparison the gap requires cannot be made and no responding intervention is linked. The international organisations report, for North Macedonia, regulatory barriers, passport retention, wage deductions, underreporting and overlapping laws, which is what remains on their account; other items are regional. A regional claim that laws are EU-aligned does not name the country and sits uneasily with the Commission's finding of partial alignment for EU workers.

**Direction of travel.** The evidence is from international organisations, with one research study, and partly regional, and no measure of this issue repeats over time for the country. The doubling of permits is regional and cannot alone support a finding here, and the Commission's permit counts have unclear bases. The chapter scale was not decisive, as it concerns EU workers' access.

**Priority.** The issue is mainly raised by others: the evidence is from international organisations and partly regional, with no Commission recommendation on third-country workers in Chapter 2. The labour shortage it describes may matter for the country, but that is not the test for importance to accession.

**Tractability.** The rules and inspection are national matters, without dependence on the EU, but the international organisation evidence, partly regional, points to several institutions, under-resourced inspection and permits tied to one employer. No item shows past progress for the country, so this is a provisional view.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether conditions for third-country workers in North Macedonia are improving or worsening is unsettled, because no measure repeats over time for the country and the doubling of work permits is a regional figure [13]; country-level permit and complaint series over several years would settle it.
- The size of the remaining gap cannot be assessed without an EU requirement item, so a Commission position on third-country workers, or a confirmed decision that none applies, is needed.
- The scale of rights violations after legal entry rests on international organisation reports that point to underreporting [5][6]; labour inspectorate records or a country survey of third-country workers would settle it.
- The Commission's permit counts have unclear bases [14][15], so official permit statistics with stated definitions are needed.
