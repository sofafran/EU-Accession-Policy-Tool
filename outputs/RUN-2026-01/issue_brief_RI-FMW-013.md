# Issue brief: Weak corruption prevention in the administration of worker mobility

RI-FMW-013 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The bodies administering free movement of workers, notably the employment service, lack a specific integrity policy, internal controls and audit capacity to prevent corruption. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to strengthen the framework for preventing corruption in free movement of workers, and in particular asks the Employment Agency to sign an integrity policy, nominate a person to implement it, reinforce its internal audit staff and ensure controls, documentation and monitoring [1][2][3][4]. The Commission states that the agency applies the code for civil servants but has not adopted an integrity policy, and that it had planned to sign one but had not done so [5][6][7]. No country intervention responding to this issue is linked in the evidence, so there is no implementation status or outcome to report. The Commission describes the framework as weak and the country as still at an early stage in this domain [8][9][10]. The validated assessment is that the gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is a signed integrity policy, a person to implement it, a reinforced internal audit department and controls in the institutions concerned. No responding intervention is linked, so there is no status to compare, and the country's stated plan to sign has not been shown to be carried out.

**Direction of travel.** The sub-area rating 'still at an early stage' matches this issue but appears only in 2025, so it is used with the issue's own items. The integrity policy is outstanding in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and the framework is called weak in 2024 and again in the screening report.

**Priority.** The requirement is repeated by the Commission in three reports, but it is not in a key recommendation for the chapter or central to it. The issue's scope is limited to free movement of workers, and general anti-corruption policy is outside it.

**Tractability.** Signing the policy and naming a person are small steps for one agency, but no report shows them done, though the agency reportedly planned to do so. Reinforcing audit staff, ministry capacity and controls across institutions is wider.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement first appears in the evidence in 2023, when the Commission states that the Employment Service Agency needs to adopt an integrity policy document [11]. In 2024 the Commission adds that the specific framework for preventing corruption in free movement of workers remains weak [8][5]. The 2025 screening report widens and sharpens the wording: it asks for the policy to be signed, for a person to be nominated to implement it, for internal audit staff to be reinforced, for controls within the institutions involved and for the Ministry of Labour and Social Policy's capacities to be reinforced, and it rates the country as still at an early stage [2][3][4][12][10]. The November 2025 report repeats that no integrity policy has yet been adopted [7].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The evidence contains only Commission statements, so it does not show whether the agency has since signed the integrity policy or named a person to implement it; a government or agency document recording the signature and appointment would settle this [6][7].
- No source reports the current staffing of the agency's internal audit department or the controls in place, so the scale of the remaining capacity gap is unknown; audit reports or staffing data for the agency would settle it [2].
- Two screening report items rest on Medium confidence evidence, so a second source confirming the planned signature and the weakness of the framework would strengthen the record [6][9].
