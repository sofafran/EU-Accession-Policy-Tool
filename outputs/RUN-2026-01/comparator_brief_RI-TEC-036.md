# Comparator brief: National e-services portal limited in range and use

RI-TEC-036 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. **Draft policy options at the end are internal drafts** and stay so after sign-off at G7 until a separate decision adapts them for an external audience.

## The issue in North Macedonia (validated at G6)

**Unresolved gap (partly_addressed).** The portal is proposed as in practice and its upgrade as implemented, while the Reform Agenda reform is proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals, and the upgrade status sits uneasily with the Commission's 2024 note that the 135 services were not yet configured. Progress against the 50% target cannot be fixed, because the 264 baseline conflicts with the Commission's 392 and 124 figures, and 246 also conflicts with 124, so sources are cited both ways. What remains is a much wider set of fully transactional, commonly requested services that people actually use, on an agreed count, and equal access, where the Commission's statement that the portal caters for special needs conflicts with the audit's finding (machine translated, capped at Medium confidence) that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services.

**Priority High, tractability Medium, direction of travel persistent.**

## Reading this brief (agent synthesis)

This brief compares North Macedonia's national e-services portal with three countries: Moldova, which digitalised services through a long donor project with assisted access; Albania, which placed most services on one portal under a law obliging institutions to use it; and Montenegro, which launched a new portal in December 2024. Moldova and Montenegro were rated of High relevance and Albania of Medium relevance, as reported use there is high rather than low. No case shows that a reform raised use of a portal: Moldova does not show which measure raised use, Albania's law is not shown to have caused its range or use, and Montenegro's results are too early. The two narrow results judged as having worked, one overlap removed in Albania and better information on Montenegro's portal, do not concern use or access. The cases show what was tried, what to measure and what to avoid. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses.

### CMP-0007: Moldova

**Comparator relevance: High.** Moldova shows the same mechanism as North Macedonia (MEC-11), with the only portfolio-wide service-level use data in the three cases, though these date from 2022. The Commission still named low demand in 2025, the same report that says access and use 'significantly increased', and the agency's 71% of requests online in 2026 is unverified, so the later reported rise qualifies the match without removing it. The context differs: EVO is described as a modular front end over the portal with a recent, untested single-portal claim, a long donor project and a dedicated agency.

**Problem addressed.** Moldova counted many services as digital while few were fully digital and many were unused: a 2023 brief found 36 of 157 services that could be requested online were provided in digital format, and 128 of the 226 services reported as digital had no users in 2022. The Commission still named low demand, especially in rural areas, as limiting use of e-government services in its 2025 report.

**Reform or mechanism used.** The main measures are the World Bank-financed Modernization of Government Services project run by the e-Governance Agency, the Public Services Portal, the EVO app launched in June 2024, assisted access through Unified Public Service Centres (CUPS), and a strategy approved in September 2023 with digitalisation targets. The approach is service-by-service digitalisation with assisted access. No extracted item records a legal duty on institutions or users to use one portal.

**How it was implemented.** Offered: the Commission reports 15 online services launched on the electronic services platform in its 2024 report and two in 2025, and over 50% of mapped services available 'fully or partially'. Fully transactional: the only figure with a stated definition is the 2023 brief's 36 of 157, because the agency's 283 e-services include partly automated ones and its 303 of 648 'fully digitalized' (2025) and 445 of 636 'accessed digitally' (2026) are unverified. EVO lists all services 'regardless of how they are provided', so a listing there is not a transaction. The Commission confirms 105 CUPS, but no extracted item says how many people they served.

**Evidence of results.** Used: the 2022 service-level data show 128 of 226 digital services with no users, 77 of them with requests at counters and 51 with no requests on any channel, and this count has not been repeated. The agency's survey reports online access passing offline access (54.5% against 52.6%), and the agency reports 71% of 4.5 million requests submitted online in January to June 2026, up 7 points on 2025. The Commission states that access and use 'significantly increased', with no figure and no named cause. No extracted item measures use of the Public Services Portal or EVO themselves.

**Limitations of the evidence.** Figures on online requests and access after 2023 come from the agency, a survey it commissioned or the project's funder, or have no stated source (the observatory's 'only 20% of requests' at counters for businesses in June 2024, [1]), and no independent check on portal use was found in the extracted items; the request series does not say which services it counts. The Commission's e-signature user figures measure signature use only; they sit in the unmatched queue [2][3], stay unused under G5b and are listed for the analyst. The rise from 25% to over 54% of citizens has no stated base, and a similar 2023 survey figure is reported for people who used public services, not all citizens. Blocked sources, including the SIGMA 2024 reports, are a recorded gap (LIM-0028). Use figures are listed as outcome items here although pass 2 classed them as implementation, because use is the result this issue asks about. The Court of Accounts report relied on in the MPass and MSign rows [4] is machine translated, capped at Medium and flagged possible_misread; it is an audit report and not a legal text, so it is outside LIM-0029 as worded, for the analyst to confirm at G6b. Claims of absence in this case refer to the extracted items, not the full source text.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: North Macedonia's stated mechanism includes low uptake of a working portal (MEC-11), and the Commission's 2024 report gives 292,179 users and 2,070 monthly applications, with no reference period beyond 'currently' [5]. If North Macedonia's position resembles Moldova's, a count of digital services would say little about use, since more than half of those reported for 2022 had no users, although 51 of the 128 had no demand on any channel. Moldova also reports use rising alongside service redesign and assisted access, but it does not show which measure raised it, so any of them would be a hypothesis to test.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** A one-off count, made service by service and separating fully from partly digital services, showed the gap in Moldova in 2022, and it could help with North Macedonia's conflicting service counts if repeated. Moldova has not repeated it and still reports differing counts (283, 303 of 648, 445 of 636), so it has not shown that such a count settles a conflict. Assisted access answers a barrier for which North Macedonia so far plans only an analysis for establishing DigInfo centres or upgrading one-stop-shop centres [6], machine translated), and its effect on use in Moldova is unmeasured, so it transfers only as an idea to test.

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** The Moldovan results sit within a seven-year donor project and a dedicated agency, while North Macedonia's ownership is described as fragmented, with 101 of 242 foreseen posts unfilled [7][8]. The observatory factsheet describes EVO as a modular app with the Public Service Portal as one module and the business portal as an extension of MCabinet, and the agency calls EVO the single government portal and plans to centralise all access under it [9][10][11][12][13]. That single-portal claim is recent and unverified, and no extracted item measures use of EVO or the portal, so Moldova offers no tested single-portal model. North Macedonia's increase in e-services is a Reform Agenda condition [14], and no extracted item shows the same condition for Moldova; relative size was not assessed because no extracted item gives it.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a one-off, service-by-service count separating fully from partly digital services, and assisted access, are ideas North Macedonia could test against its low uptake. Conditional, because Moldova has not shown which measure raised use, its results sit within a long donor project and a dedicated agency, and its own service counts still differ.

### CMP-0008: Albania

**Comparator relevance: Medium.** Albania matches on one portal, depth of transactions, inclusion and coordination (MEC-08), but it differs on the core symptom because reported use is high, not low. It is a contrast case for consolidation and for range as the Commission reports it, although the RCC says Albania lags the regional average on service scope and sophistication (50 against 53), and it cannot explain what produces use.

**Problem addressed.** Albania's gap is reported mainly as depth and inclusion: the Commission records 95% of applications for 1,237 services offered online in 2024 and 1,253 in 2025, yet accessibility for vulnerable groups remains unaddressed. The sources conflict on range, because the RCC also says Albania 'continues to lag behind the regional average in certain aspects of service scope and sophistication', with a citizen services score of 50 against 53 from desk research in 2025. It also asks Albania to improve access to the use of e-services and to address an emerging digital divide. The national monitor finds digitalisation largely complete but citizens not at the centre of design and delivery.

**Reform or mechanism used.** Law No. 43/2023 on Electronic Governance, adopted in June 2023, requires public institutions to use e-Albania as the primary point of access and requires e-payment through the portal; as stated in the sources, the duty falls on institutions, not users. The Digital Agenda 2022-2026 sets a 95% target. ADISA was absorbed by the National Agency for Information Society, which concentrates policy and operational control.

**How it was implemented.** Offered: the monitor records more than 90% of services placed on the portal since its previous cycle and most physical counters closed, while municipal offices still handle services that need in-person steps. Fully transactional: no extracted item gives a national count, and of seven sampled services the monitor calls two end-to-end, while its own footnote says the payment option usually does not work, and others, such as vehicle registration and VAT, need in-person payment. No service is yet delivered as a life event. Vehicle registration and VAT are described as offered outside the portal in one passage and as sampled services needing in-person payment in another.

**Evidence of results.** Used: the Regional Cooperation Council (RCC) reports e-government users at 78.5% of internet users in 2023 and 88.6% in 2024, with 3.43 million registered users and 15.2 million services provided in 2024 and 17 million in 2025. The monitor's perception survey has 66.9% agreeing that they can access services easily online and 30.5% disagreeing. No extracted item gives the share of those services that were completed online from start to finish.

**Limitations of the evidence.** The RCC gives no origin for the portal figures and does not define a registered user or a service provided; its text says 89% of the population where its table says 88.6% of internet users. The e-government user indicator counts any online contact with authorities, not portal use [15]. The extracted items hold no series from before the June 2023 law, so use before and after it cannot be compared. The monitor's informant scores are points awarded only if all informants agree [16]; 0 of 3 on accessible service delivery, 3 of 3 on ease of use), so they are not counts of people. The monitor states that 'these reforms explain why' informants describe transactional reliability and ease of access [17], but this rests on three informants, concerns their views and not use, and conflicts with its footnote that payment 'usually does not work' [18]. The monitor also reports the law's once-only duty both ways, as effectively implemented [19] and as partial [20][21][22][23]; these items sit in the unmatched queue, stay unused under G5b and are listed for the analyst. Claims of absence in this case refer to the extracted items, not the full source text.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: North Macedonia's 2025 strategy proposes updating the law towards mandatory integration or connection of each e-service on the portal [24], machine translated), and its stated mechanisms include coordination failure (MEC-08) and low uptake (MEC-11). Albania shows that a duty on institutions can sit alongside most services being placed on one portal and high reported use, but no extracted item shows that the duty caused either, and the legal e-payment duty is reported as limited in practice. If North Macedonia adopted such a duty, this case would not support expecting it to drive use; it supports, at most, the RCC's untested statement that uptake is linked to the portal's central role.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** The monitor reports that absorbing ADISA into the agency removed one named overlap, on monitoring, evaluation and reporting between ADISA and the Agency for Dialogue and Co-governance [25][26]. This is a narrow result with no measure, and North Macedonia already has a lead ministry that lacks resources and cooperation [7], so naming one body would not by itself address its problem. A legal duty on institutions to integrate services is an untested option: no extracted item shows that it caused Albania's range, and Albania's experience shows that working payment and signature cannot be assumed.

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Closing counters cannot be assumed to transfer: the source gives no count, date or effect on users, and North Macedonia has 1,120 services available only in person and an audit finding that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services [27][28], both machine translated). Albania's own unaddressed accessibility issues show that its route has not solved this. Relative size was not assessed because no extracted item gives it. In these items the Commission's ask of Albania is a recommendation, while North Macedonia's increase in e-services is also a Reform Agenda condition [14].

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a legal duty on institutions to integrate services on one portal is an option North Macedonia's 2025 strategy already proposes, but no extracted item shows that the duty caused Albania's range or use. Conditional, because working payment and signature cannot be assumed, the one stated result is a narrow removal of an overlap between two bodies, and naming a lead body would not by itself supply the resources North Macedonia's ministry lacks.

### CMP-0009: Montenegro

**Comparator relevance: High.** Montenegro is the closest match in problem: a live portal, too few transactional services, low use, services run outside the portal and capacity limits (MEC-03, MEC-11). The match is on the problem and not on a solution, because the results are too early and unverified and its portal is newer than North Macedonia's.

**Problem addressed.** Montenegro's government names low use of e-services and weak interoperability as key challenges, and the Commission repeatedly asks it to prioritise a platform for transactional services. The national monitor reports that the government's own Digital Services Plan 2025-2027 puts no service at the fifth level (full online service). E-government users were 37.5% of internet users in 2024, below the Western Balkans average of 54%.

**Reform or mechanism used.** Sources date the new eUprava portal differently in kind: the government adopted an information note on it in December 2024, when the monitor says it 'was not functional'; the Commission and the event report say it launched that month; and first services were published in April 2025. It runs under a plan for transactional services 2025-2027 covering 171 services and 21 institutions, revised on 18 December 2025; the RCC says the same plan sets out 162 additional services and the revised plan's table sums to 175, and these figures are not reconciled. The Law on Electronic Government gives the legal basis, and the government launched an initiative for all institutions to publish their services on the portal. A proposed Open Government action plan targets 150 transactional services by 2029.

**How it was implemented.** Offered: the government reports 106 services from 14 institutions on the portal, without saying how many are transactional, and eFirma and eTaxis run outside it. Fully transactional: the government's action plan states 45 transactional services [29], while its Digital Services Plan 2025-2027, as cited by the monitor, puts no service at level 5 [30]. These are two government statements not shown to be at the same date (the action plan is dated 12 October 2025; the monitor, dated September 2025, does not date the plan's state of play), not reconciled, and no extracted item holds an independent count of transactional services. Delivery against the plan was 25 of 57 by July 2025, then 57 of 57 on the government's own report, with no independent check in the extracted items. The ministry reports two officials in the responsible directorate, a refusal of budget reserve for Interior services and Employment Agency services waiting on a new law. The ministry's report on the Digital Transformation Strategy gives 43.14% of planned 2024 activities fulfilled, for the whole strategy and not only e-services (government reporting, machine translated; repeated by the monitor, [31]).

**Evidence of results.** Used: e-government users fell from 48.4% of internet users in 2023 to 37.5% in 2024, on an indicator of any online contact with authorities [15], and an oral UNDP figure puts actual use at 8%. No extracted item gives use of eUprava itself. The monitor's service delivery grades reflect findings to the end of 2024, when the portal was under construction [32][33]. Its statements that information improved and that none of the seven sampled services was on the portal are notes made at the time of writing, after the first services of April 2025. The government states that satisfaction is around 50%.

**Limitations of the evidence.** The portal is young. The monitor, a non-government body co-funded by the monitored ministry (SQ-00180), sets its findings to the end of 2024, when the portal was under construction, with later notes to July 2025 [32][33], so its grades largely predate the first services, and the government's report of full delivery and the 106 count are untested. The RCC text says use continues to grow, against the fall in its own table. The 8% figure is oral, second-hand and has no reference period, and the event report is rated Low. The 2024 user figures predate almost all of the portal's life, so they measure the starting position. The 106 services, 25 and 57 of 57, the 43.14%, the two officials, the budget refusal and 'key challenges' come from machine-translated Montenegrin government reports, capped at Medium; they are not legal texts, so they are outside LIM-0029 as worded, for the analyst to confirm at G6b, and item [34] is also flagged possible_misread. Claims of absence in this case refer to the extracted items, not the full source text.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Montenegro is the closest parallel to North Macedonia's position of a live portal, the most-needed services absent and capacity gaps (MEC-03, MEC-11; [35]), but its results are too early and unverified. It suggests a hypothesis, not a finding: a portal launched without the most-needed services on it may improve information without improving the service, which is the monitor's statement, drawn from seven sampled services, with grades that largely predate the first services. It also suggests that where a plan reports a delivered count, an independent check on whether services are transactional may be needed, given North Macedonia's own conflicting counts.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** As a conditional hypothesis only: a portal may add little where the services people need sit elsewhere, and North Macedonia's Commission findings say the most commonly requested services are not yet online [35]. It rests on one monitor and seven sampled services, whose grades largely predate the first services, that monitor also reports a gain in information, and no extracted item measures use of eUprava. The stated constraints of too few staff and refused budget have a parallel in North Macedonia's unfilled posts and limited resources for the ministry [8][7].

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** The Montenegrin portal launched in December 2024 on the Commission's account, while North Macedonia's has run since 2019 [36] and has a longer record of low use, so results at Montenegro's stage may not predict results at a later one. The cybersecurity crisis that the Commission dates to 2023 and says delayed services and eroded trust (the government's action plan refers instead to 'the 2022 attacks', [37], classed out_of_scope at pass 2) has no stated equivalent in North Macedonia's evidence here. Montenegro's different accession stage was not used as a reason, because no extracted item links it to the portal.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a portal launched without the most-needed services may improve information without improving the service, and a delivered count may need an independent check on whether services are transactional. Conditional and too early, because it rests on one monitor and seven sampled services, and North Macedonia's portal has run since 2019, so results at Montenegro's stage may not predict results at a later one.

## Lessons across the cases (agent synthesis)

Three points recur. First, counts of digital services say little about use. In 2022 Moldova had 128 of 226 reported digital services with no users, although 51 of them had no requests on any channel [38][39]. A Moldovan civil society source states that launch does not bring use, and this carries an item-level transferability judgement of conditional [40]. Second, a portal without the most-needed services may improve information without improving the service: in Montenegro the monitor reports better information on the new portal [41] while none of seven essential sampled services was on it [42]. This matters because the Commission found that North Macedonia's most commonly requested services were not yet online [35]. Third, a legal duty on institutions can sit alongside payment that often fails: Albania's law requires e-payment through the portal [43], but the monitor says users usually cannot pay [18]. North Macedonia's 2025 strategy proposes a similar duty to integrate services on its portal [24], machine translated).

## Draft policy options (agent synthesis, internal draft)

These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (partly addressed) and on comparator cases validated at G6b, and each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and none is shown by the evidence to raise use.

**Option 1: Agree one service-by-service count, with use, and repeat it.**
- *Gap addressed:* the missing agreed count of fully transactional services, on which progress against the Reform Agenda target of a 50% increase cannot be fixed, because the government's baseline conflicts with the Commission's figures [14][44][45].
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11), and unreliable data on what exists.
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova's 2022 count, made service by service and separating fully from partly digital services, showed the gap between availability and use [38], though it was not repeated and Moldova's counts still differ [46][47]. Montenegro suggests that a delivered count may need an independent check on whether services are transactional [48]. Transferability conditional: a count could help settle North Macedonia's conflicting figures if repeated, but neither case shows that it did so.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a shared definition of a fully transactional service, agreed with the Commission for the Reform Agenda, and data on use per service.
- *Main risks:* a count produced once for a payment condition and then dropped; a definition loose enough to inflate progress.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which leads digitalisation projects [7], with the institutions that own the services.

**Option 2: Put the most-requested services online in full before widening the range.**
- *Gap addressed:* the much wider set of fully transactional, commonly requested services that people use [49][35].
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11) and weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro (relevance High), where a portal without the most-needed services improved information only [41][42], and Moldova, where many digital services had no users [38]. A Moldovan source states that building without beneficiaries' views harms use; its item-level transferability is conditional, on user involvement in design, a condition the evidence does not show North Macedonia meets [50]. Transferability conditional: Montenegro's portal is newer than North Macedonia's, so results at its stage may not predict results at a later one.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a list of the most-requested services and their owners, which North Macedonia's strategy plans as an action but does not show delivered.
- *Main risks:* the selected services depend on registers and data exchange that are not ready, which ties this option to the interoperability issue (RI-TEC-028).
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation with the service-owning ministries and agencies.

**Option 3: Pilot assisted access and measure its effect on use.**
- *Gap addressed:* use of the portal and equal access, including the State Audit Office's finding that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services [28], machine translated).
- *Mechanism targeted:* low uptake (MEC-11).
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova reports use rising alongside service redesign and assisted access through its service centres [51], but does not show which measure raised it. North Macedonia so far plans only an analysis for DigInfo centres [6], machine translated). Transferability conditional: the effect of assisted access is unmeasured in Moldova, so it transfers only as an idea to test.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a measure of use set before the pilot starts; accessibility treated as a separate requirement, since assisted access does not by itself meet it.
- *Main risks:* centres that help people with information but not with completing services; cost without evidence of effect.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which has said it will carry out the DigInfo analysis.

**A warning rather than an option: a legal duty to integrate services will not by itself raise use.**
North Macedonia's 2025 strategy proposes making integration of e-services on the portal mandatory [24], machine translated). Albania (relevance Medium) shows such a duty alongside most services on one portal and high reported use, but no source shows that the duty caused either, and payment through the portal often fails [52][18]. Transferability conditional: if North Macedonia adopts such a duty, the comparator evidence supports pairing it with working payment and signature and with the resources the lead ministry lacks [7], not expecting it to drive use on its own.
