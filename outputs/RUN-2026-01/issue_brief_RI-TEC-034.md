# Issue brief: Digital single market rules not aligned

RI-TEC-034 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Legislation is not aligned with EU rules on online platforms and services, such as the Digital Services Act, the Digital Markets Act, the platform-to-business and geo-blocking regulations, and e-commerce is developing slowly. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align with the Digital Services Act and the Digital Markets Act, to align fully with electronic commerce rules and to harmonise its legislation with the Geo-blocking Regulation, and its screening report finds no alignment with the platform-to-business regulation [1][2][3]. The country states it plans to align with the platform-to-business and geo-blocking rules by 2027 and with the Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act by 2028, and it has asked to join the related EU expert groups, which the Commission will explore [4][5][6][7][8]. The only delivered step is training from European services on the Digital Services Act in June 2024 [9]. No draft law and no outcome evidence are reported. The validated judgements are priority Medium, direction of travel persistent, unresolved gap open and tractability Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

**What remains.** The latest EU items still require alignment with the DSA, DMA and Geo-blocking Regulation, and the responding alignment measures are all proposed, with national targets of 2027 and 2028 and only training delivered; these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is the alignment itself for all these acts, plus full alignment on electronic commerce. The country's report of being largely aligned with the e-Commerce Directive and the Commission's call to align fully differ in degree and speaker, and were not treated as closure.

**Direction of travel.** The ask to align with the Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act recurs with little change in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports, and e-commerce was described as slow in 2021, 2022 and 2023. The chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. Growth figures for online transactions are country-reported, partly repeated and on differing bases, so they were not read as closing the issue.

**Priority.** The requirement is repeated in three reports and relates to binding acquis, but it sits in Chapter 10 body text, not in the 2024 or 2025 key recommendations, and is not named as a benchmark or payment condition in the input. It is therefore a repeated EU requirement that is not central under the criteria.

**Tractability.** The work is mainly legislative with EU texts as templates, a government timetable and a request for expert group participation, which depends partly on the Commission. No draft is reported and past progress is limited to training, and applying platform rules will need capacity that the input does not evidence.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* From 2021 to 2023 the Commission's concern was the slow pace of e-commerce [10][11][12]. From 2023 it asked for alignment with the Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act to be stepped up, first to give businesses predictability, then in 2024 alongside equal access to online services, and in November 2025 as something the country still needs to do [13][14][1]. The 2025 screening widened the requirement: it found legislation largely aligned with the e-Commerce Directive but not aligned with the platform-to-business or geo-blocking rules, and asked for full alignment on electronic commerce and geo-blocking [15][3][16][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No draft law transposing any of these acts is reported, so a published draft or legislative programme entry would show whether the 2027 and 2028 targets are realistic [17][4].
- The finding that legislation is largely aligned with the e-Commerce Directive and the call to align fully differ in degree and were not treated as closure, and a table of concordance for the e-commerce law would settle what is missing [15][2].
- Growth figures for internet transactions are country-reported, partly repeated and on differing bases, so a consistent e-commerce statistical series would be needed to judge whether e-commerce is still slow [18][19].
- The input does not show which authorities would apply platform rules or whether they have the capacity, and an institutional plan for enforcement would settle this [9].
