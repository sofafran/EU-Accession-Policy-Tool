# Issue brief: Broadband and high-capacity network coverage uneven

RI-TEC-013 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Small businesses and rural or non-urban areas face obstacles to fixed broadband, and high-capacity and 5G networks are still being rolled out. **Problem mechanisms:** Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has repeatedly called for wider broadband access, and the Reform Agenda ties a payment to 5G roll-out and a 5G security by-law [1][2]. The Commission reports continued 5G expansion, and a machine-translated national source gives 96.99% population coverage [3][4]; 5G spectrum assignment and roll-out are validated as in_practice. The national broadband plan is validated as implemented, but its success is insufficient_evidence, and the national optical network is only proposed. The Commission reports household internet access rising to 90.8% in 2024 [5], yet in 2025 it found that smaller companies and rural areas still face significant obstacles [6], and take-up of the fastest connections remains very low [7]. Sources conflict on fixed coverage against the EU average. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Latest EU items still ask for more access for smaller companies and rural areas, while the compared statuses, which are proposals for G6, show 5G roll-out in practice but the national optical network only proposed. Sources conflict on the size of the gap: the ICT strategy gives 77% fixed coverage against an EU average of 78%, the Commission gives 79.6% against 93.1%, and the strategy elsewhere gives 77% as very high capacity coverage after stating 69%, so the comparison with the EU cannot be relied on until the measures are checked. What remains is fixed broadband for small firms and rural areas, very low take-up of the fastest connections, and completing the 5G roll-out named in the Reform Agenda.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission's sentence that smaller companies still face obstacles repeats from 2021 to 2026 with the same figures, so the default applies, though repeats are not independent evidence, and in 2025 the wording hardened to significant obstacles for smaller companies and rural areas. National averages did rise, with home internet access at 90.8% in 2024, 5G coverage at 76% in 2023 and 97% in a machine-translated 2025 national source, and some progress on ICT infrastructure in matching sub-area wording in 2025, while differences between earlier household figures reflect different measures, not conflict. No series tracks the rural and small-business gap itself, so the averages do not show that the unevenness has narrowed, and the chapter scale was not decisive.

**Priority.** Roll-out of 5G network and services is named in a Reform Agenda step, and the Commission repeats the particular need to increase access to broadband in its assessments from 2021 to 2026, which makes it central to the digital infrastructure theme. The payment condition covers the 5G part, not the rural and small-business part, which appears in repeated assessments and not in the chapter's key recommendations. The large volume of items on this issue, much of it repeated wording and national statistics, did not raise the rating.

**Tractability.** Past progress is clear on 5G and on general access, where operators have invested and the Commission records continued expansion. The harder part depends on resources and on others: rural and low-demand areas lack commercial interest, the national network is at feasibility stage on a timetable to 2030 with mixed financing, and the government's own strategy, in machine translation, names lack of finance and high prices as challenges. Delivery therefore needs the ministry, the public broadcasting enterprise, operators and outside funders.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has been largely stable. From 2021 to 2023 it repeated that there is a particular need to increase access to broadband and that smaller companies still face obstacles to fixed broadband [1][8][9]. From 2024 it added fast internet for small businesses and non-urban areas and named rural areas alongside smaller companies [10][11]. The 2024 Reform Agenda added a payment condition on 5G roll-out [2]. In 2025 the wording hardened to significant obstacles for smaller companies and rural areas [6], while the 2026 assessment repeated the earlier sentence [12].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The size of the gap with the EU is unsettled because the ICT strategy and the Commission give conflicting fixed coverage figures and EU averages [15][16]; checking the definitions behind each measure would settle it.
- No series tracks the rural and small-business gap itself, as the 2023 rural figure is a single point [17], so repeated coverage and take-up data by area and firm size are needed.
- The outcome of the 2025 spectrum tender rests only on machine-translated regulator reporting capped at Medium confidence, and its success and attribution are insufficient_evidence [14]; a published award decision would settle it.
- Whether the national broadband plan caused the 5G expansion is insufficient_evidence [13], so evidence on use and on the plan's own contribution, as distinct from operator investment, is needed.
