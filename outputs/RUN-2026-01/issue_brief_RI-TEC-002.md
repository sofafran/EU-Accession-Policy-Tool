# Issue brief: Digital transformation strategy and governance weak

RI-TEC-002 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** There is no adopted long-term strategy for digital transformation aligned with EU targets, or the ministry or agency responsible lacks the capacity, staff and inter-institutional coordination to lead and monitor it. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Coordination failure (MEC-08); Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asked North Macedonia from 2020 to 2024 to finalise and adopt a long-term ICT strategy, and since 2022 it has asked for stronger ministry capacity to coordinate digital transformation [1][2][3][4]. The Commission reports that a Ministry of Digital Transformation was set up and that a long-term ICT strategy and action plan were adopted in 2025 [5][6]. Adoption is not improvement in governance: national audit and strategy texts, machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, report under half of ministry IT posts filled, no confirmed National Council activity in 2023 and no central register of ICT systems [7][8][9]. The Commission still asks for more IT staff and better coordination, and encouraged a digital agency whose need the country said it would reassess [10][11][12]. The validated assessment is partly_addressed, with priority High, direction of travel persistent and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** The latest EU items report the strategy adopted but still ask for stronger ministry capacity, more IT staff and the digital agency, which remains proposed and which the country has said it will reassess; these statuses are themselves proposals, and that of the National Council is unclear because the 2024 audit found no 2023 activity while the strategy cites its formation. What remains is applying the strategy, building coordinating capacity and IT staffing, and settling the agency question. The Commission's well-structured set-up and the national vacuum are a noted difference of scope, not a conflict.

**Direction of travel.** The only movement is that the key recommendation to finalise and adopt the ICT strategy, unchanged from 2020 to 2024, ended in 2025 because the strategy was adopted, and adoption is not improvement in governance. The Commission's wording that the ministry needs stronger capacity is unchanged across 2022 to 2025, which sets the default to persistent, and the 2025 report adds a call for more IT staff. The chapter scale (some progress 2020, limited 2021 to 2024, some 2025) is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** The ICT strategy was a Chapter 10 key recommendation in five successive reports, and the ministry capacity ask has recurred in four reports to 2025, on an issue central to the digital transformation theme. The key recommendation itself left the list after adoption in 2025 and the capacity ask sits in body text, so High rests on treating adoption as the start rather than the end of the requirement. National plans and audits were not counted towards this rating.

**Tractability.** A new ministry and an adopted strategy show that institutional steps can be taken, but the strategy took over five years and coordination depends on many institutions that have offered limited cooperation. IT posts are about half filled and public pay rules limit competition with the private sector, which the ministry cannot change alone. The staffing and working group evidence is machine translated and capped at Medium.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The key recommendation to finalise the long-term ICT strategy appeared in 2020 and, as finalise and adopt, in every report from 2021 to 2024, with the 2022 and 2024 versions adding alignment with electronic communications and network security rules and, in 2024, adoption of the cybersecurity strategy [1][13][14][15][2]. It left the list in 2025, when the Commission reported the strategy adopted [6]. The capacity request has been stable since 2022, moving from the Ministry of Information Society and Administration to the Ministry of Digital Transformation [3][16][17][4]. In 2025 the Commission added a call to recruit and retain more IT staff, while its screening assessment described the institutional set-up as well structured [10][18].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The Commission's 2025 statement that a long-term ICT strategy was adopted is linked to SMART/MK: 2030 only by timing, and whether the earlier unadopted drafts became that strategy is not stated, so a text naming the adopted strategy would settle it [6][25].
- Sources differ on the National Council for Digital Transformation, which the 2025 strategy describes as formed to plan and coordinate while the State Audit Office could not confirm any activity in 2023, and records of its meetings and decisions since then would show whether it functions [19][8].
- Whether a national digital agency will be set up is unresolved, since the Commission encouraged it and the country said it would reassess the need, and a government decision either way would settle it [11][12].
- The staffing and coordination evidence rests on machine-translated national texts capped at Medium confidence, and current IT staffing figures and evidence of the adopted strategy being carried out would be needed to judge whether governance is improving [7][23][26].
