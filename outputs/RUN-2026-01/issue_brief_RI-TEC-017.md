# Issue brief: Personal data protection law not aligned with EU rules

RI-TEC-017 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The personal data protection framework is not fully aligned with the General Data Protection Regulation, the Law Enforcement Directive and the ePrivacy Directive, rules on international transfers are missing, and sector laws are not harmonised. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align fully with the General Data Protection Regulation, to adopt a law transposing the Law Enforcement Directive, to ensure safeguards for international transfers and to harmonise sector laws [1][2][3][4]. The 2020 Law on Personal Data Protection has applied in full since August 2021, and the Agency reports supervisions and new rulebooks [5][6]. A draft law transposing the Law Enforcement Directive was withdrawn in September 2022, and the Agency reports that neither that transposition nor its own draft amendments had started moving in 2025, in machine-translated reporting [7][8]. In 2025 Parliament amended the law to allow transfers to NATO countries without prior consultation of the Agency, and the Commission found no progress and an insufficient framework [9][10][11]. The validated assessment is that the gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High (an analyst override) and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2020 law is proposed as in practice, with the Commission confirming full application from August 2021, while the amendments and the Law Enforcement Directive law are proposed only, the earlier draft was withdrawn in 2022, and all these statuses are themselves proposals for G6. What remains is full alignment with the General Data Protection Regulation, a law transposing the Law Enforcement Directive, safeguards for transfers, harmonised sector laws and confirmed ePrivacy alignment. Sources conflict, since the State Audit Office and the Agency's strategy describe the law as aligned while the Commission calls the framework insufficient, and the country claims full ePrivacy alignment while the Commission seeks further efforts; this judgement does not rely on Article 122 of the unofficial translation [12], which may be mistranslated and, read literally, would suspend core chapters until accession.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission found no concrete progress on the Law Enforcement Directive in 2020, partial alignment and a need for full alignment in 2023, and pending alignment in 2024 and 2025, so the requirement has not changed across three or more reports. The sub-area rating of no progress in 2024 and 2025 covers more than this issue and was not decisive. The 2025 transfer amendment without equivalent safeguards is a further concern but a single event.

**Priority.** Full alignment with the data protection rules is central to the data theme and is repeated by the Commission in 2023, 2024 and 2025, but none of the 2020 to 2025 reports names it in the key recommendations, and it is in no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition. The 2024 and 2025 main findings record no progress, but that is a finding and not a key recommendation, so this is a central requirement with thin support under the test. National and agency items were not counted. The analyst set this value at G6: G6-D02: full alignment is core acquis, is repeated in 2023 to 2025, and the 2024 and 2025 main findings record no progress; the working test that lowered it was stricter than the judgement rules.

**Tractability.** Texts already exist: the Agency drafted amendments in April 2022 and a Law Enforcement Directive draft reached Parliament with, as the Agency reports, a positive informal Commission opinion. Yet the Government withdrew that draft in 2022, neither process moved in 2025, and sector laws need several ministries. The Agency's annual report items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2020 the Commission's wording centres on the Law Enforcement Directive, recording no concrete progress on alignment [13]. From 2023 the requirement broadens: the screening report calls the framework partially aligned and asks for the recast of the law to be completed and a new law on the Law Enforcement Directive, and the 2023 report adds transfers, the Agency's independence and the harmonisation of sector laws [14][15][16][17][3]. In 2024 and 2025 the Commission records no progress and states that full alignment remains pending, and it adds ePrivacy alignment [18][19][20][21]. In 2025 it also calls the framework insufficient and notes the absence of safeguards for transferred data [11][4].

## Conflicting evidence

- In 2025 the State Audit Office says personal data protection in AI projects is regulated by legislation aligned with European standards, especially the GDPR, while the Commission says adoption of legislation to fully align with the GDPR is still pending. [23][20]
- The State Audit Office's June 2025 report states that national data protection legislation is aligned with EU legislation, while the Commission's 2025 Report states that adoption of legislation to fully align with the General Data Protection Regulation and the Law Enforcement Directive is still pending. [24][20]
- The Agency's 2025-2030 strategy describes a robust and comprehensive legal framework that aligns closely with the acquis, while the Commission's 2025 Report states that the legal framework for the protection of personal data is insufficient. [25][11]
- North Macedonia stated in the screening published in February 2025 that the Law for Electronic Communications is fully aligned with the ePrivacy Directive, while the Commission's 2024 Report states that further efforts are needed for alignment with that Directive. [26][19]
- The State Audit Office report dates the Law on Personal Data Protection to 2018, while the Commission's 2020 Report states that the new law was adopted in February 2020. [27][28]
- The ITU profile of February 2022 lists the Law on the Security of Network and Information Systems as an existing enabler, while the Commission's 2022 report says the draft of that law has not yet been adopted. [29][30]

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Sources conflict on alignment: the State Audit Office and the Agency's strategy describe the legislation as aligned with EU law, while the Commission calls the framework insufficient and full alignment pending, and only a Commission assessment of a new or amended law would settle this [24][25][20][11].
- North Macedonia states that its electronic communications law is fully aligned with the ePrivacy Directive, while the Commission seeks further efforts and could not yet fully evaluate it, so a completed Commission evaluation would settle this conflict [26][19][31].
- Article 122 of the unofficial translation of the 2020 law reads as suspending core chapters until accession, which contradicts the other evidence of application and may be a translation fault, so a check against the Macedonian text would settle it [12].
- Much of the evidence on what the Agency has done comes from its own machine-translated reports, capped at Medium confidence or lower, and the effect of the Law on financial companies is rated insufficient_evidence for success, so independent evidence on supervision results and complaint trends would strengthen the record [8][22].
