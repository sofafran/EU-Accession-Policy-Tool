# Issue brief: Electronic communications regulator independence and market oversight weak

RI-TEC-012 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** The electronic communications regulator lacks guaranteed independence in appointments and funding and sufficient capacity, and competitive safeguards in a concentrated telecom market are not applied. **Problem mechanisms:** Lack of institutional independence (MEC-05); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Political economy and vested interests (MEC-10).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to strengthen the independence and capacity of the electronic communications regulator and to improve competition in the telecom sector [1][2][3]. Parliament adopted a new Law on Electronic Communications in June 2025, which states that the Agency is independent, but that is a legal provision and no item confirms its application [4][5]. The Commission reports that parliament dismissed all five members of the regulator's commission in September 2024 [6]. On competition, machine-translated regulator reporting states that a spectrum tender received a single compliant bid, with the award decision postponed to 12 January 2026, and the Commission says a new operator took part in a tender [7][8][9]. Success and attribution for that tender are insufficient_evidence, and the mobile market remains concentrated [10]. The validated assessment is that the gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2026 report again asks the Agency for Electronic Communications to increase its institutional independence, and the 2025 law's statement that the Agency is independent is a legal provision, not evidence of practice. The competition part has moved, as a new operator was licensed in January 2026 as the third mobile network operator after a tender with a sole bidder, although no item yet shows it operating or its effect on a market held mainly by two operators. What remains is independence shown in appointments and practice, the third operator's actual entry, and resolving the financing provisions for the Operational and Technical Agency, on which the Commission and the country disagree and which concern that agency and not the regulator itself; the gap stays open because the requirement repeated in 2026 concerns independence, on which nothing has changed.

**Direction of travel.** Commission concern about the regulator's independence recurs in nearly unchanged terms in each report from 2020 to 2025, with no recorded improvement, so the default applies; in September 2024 parliament dismissed all five commission members, which the source reports without saying what it meant for independence. The chapter scale was not decisive. The competition part has moved a little, from no relevant developments in 2020 and 2021 to a market still shared by two operators in 2023 and a new operator taking part in a frequency tender in 2025, so the two parts of this issue do not move together.

**Priority.** Strengthening the independence and capacity of this regulator was a key Commission recommendation in every report from 2021 to 2025, and an independent regulator is central to the digital infrastructure theme. The 2026 key recommendation names only the media regulator and the public service broadcaster, and the call for the Agency for Electronic Communications to increase its independence moves to body text, so High rests on five earlier reports and a requirement still stated in 2026; it is not a Reform Agenda step, and if the 2027 report also leaves it out of the key recommendations, Medium would fit. The competition part is supported by repeated but less prominent findings and did not drive the rating.

**Tractability.** The two parts differ. Independence looks hard, because it depends on appointments by parliament and exposure to political interests, and six years of reports record no progress, while competition looks more realistic because a tender has reached a compliant bid. That tender rests on one bidder and was held up by elections and a missing opinion from the anti-corruption commission, as machine-translated regulator reporting states, so Medium is a blend that would not hold if the issue were split.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's concern about the regulator's independence recurs in nearly unchanged terms from 2020 to 2025: concerns over appointments in 2020 and 2021, concerns over independence in 2022, calls to ensure full independence in 2023 and 2024, and a call to bolster independence in 2025 [11][12][13][14][15][2]. The key recommendation on the regulator's independence and capacity appears in every report from 2021 to 2025 [16][1]. On competition, the wording moves from no relevant developments in 2020 and 2021, to a market mainly shared by two operators in 2023, to a new operator taking part in a tender in 2025 [17][18][9]. In 2025 and 2026 the Commission adds points on the new law, including concerns about its provisions on financing the Operational and Technical Agency [5][19][20].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows whether the regulator's independence has improved in appointments or in practice, so evidence on appointments to the new commission and on how the 2025 law is applied would settle this [6][4].
- The outcome of the spectrum tender is unknown, with success and attribution rated insufficient_evidence on machine-translated regulator reporting capped at Medium confidence, so an award decision and later market share data would settle whether competition improved [7][8].
- The Commission and North Macedonia disagree on whether the law's provisions on financing the Operational and Technical Agency are consistent with the EU telecoms code, and the evidence does not resolve this; a later Commission assessment or an amendment to the law would settle it [19][23].
- The planned third mobile network operator rests on a machine-translated strategy and no item shows it introduced, so a regulatory decision or licence would settle its status [31].
