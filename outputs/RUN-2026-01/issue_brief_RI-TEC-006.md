# Issue brief: Electronic public procurement tools underused

RI-TEC-006 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The electronic public procurement system works, but some of its modules, such as the e-marketplace for low-value contracts and e-catalogues, are incomplete or little used by contracting authorities, and its data are not yet used for red-flag analysis. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to complete its e-procurement platform, build the confidence of contracting authorities to use the e-market for low-value contracts, and develop the red flag system and data analysis to report on possible corruption or bid-rigging [1][2][3]. The Commission states that the electronic procurement system works efficiently and that its use is obligatory, and the system is assessed as in_practice [4][5][6]. The e-market was launched in 2022, and the Commission reports 70 awards through it in 2022 and 258 in 2023 [7][8][9]. The Commission credits e-appeals and the electronic system with a high level of transparency, assessed as worked, though not with a fall in corruption [10]. What remains is raising e-market use to the 800 awards set as a Reform Agenda payment condition for 2026, wider use of e-catalogues and stronger data analysis [11][12]. The gap is assessed as partly_addressed. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is improving and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The system is working and the e-market and red-flag modules are proposed as in practice, with the low-value bylaw at insufficient evidence; these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is raising e-market use from 350 to the required 800 awards in 2026, wider take-up of e-catalogues, and the data analysis the Commission asked for in 2023.

**Direction of travel.** The e-market moved from development in 2021 to launch in 2022, and awards rose from 70 in 2022 to 258 in 2023 (259 in the Reform Agenda, a minor difference of count), with 350 small-value transactions in 2024, though that item does not name the e-market. The Commission's finding of poor use in 2023 is not repeated later, and no later item shows change on e-catalogues or red-flag analysis. The Chapter 5 'Limited progress' wording of 2022 and 2025 is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** E-market use is a Reform Agenda payment condition, which meets one limb of the High test, but the Commission finds the core system functions efficiently and the shortfall concerns low-value contracts and add-on modules. It is therefore not central to Chapter 5 or the theme, and the proposal rests on that reading of centrality.

**Tractability.** One bureau runs a platform that is already obligatory for all contracting authorities, awards rose about fivefold in two years, and a payment is attached to the target. The main limit is the confidence of contracting authorities, and the target is still more than double the 2024 count.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has moved from building the platform to getting it used. In 2020 it asks for the e-procurement platform to be completed and the portal upgraded with new modules, and in 2021 for adoption of the bylaw on the low-value e-marketplace [1][13][14]. In 2023 the focus turns to use: the Commission finds poor use of the e-market, little interest from procuring entities and e-catalogues only just starting, and asks for further development of the red flag system and data analysis [2][15][12][3]. From 2024 the requirement becomes a quantified payment condition of at least 800 e-market awards in 2026, while the Commission repeats that the core system functions efficiently [11][5].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The Commission's 2020 report says the e-appeals section was still being developed, while North Macedonia reports it fully operational from 1 April 2019, and this confirmed conflict remains unresolved pending a dated record from the Bureau or the State Appeals Commission [19][20].
- The 2024 figure of 350 small-value transactions does not name the e-market, so e-market award data for 2024 to 2026 would be needed to judge progress towards the 800 target [21].
- Whether the bylaw on the low-value e-marketplace was ever adopted is insufficient_evidence, and the official gazette or a Bureau statement would settle it [27].
- No evidence after 2023 shows how e-catalogues or the red flag system are used, and usage figures or Commission findings on data analysis would be needed [28][12].
