# Issue brief: Gaps in bilateral social security and healthcare agreements

RI-FMW-008 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Bilateral social security coordination and healthcare agreements do not yet cover all EU Member States or all branches, so workers moving before accession lack coordinated protection. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to negotiate and conclude bilateral social security coordination and healthcare insurance agreements with EU Member States and to put in place administrative measures to prepare for accession [1][2][3]. The ministry lists agreements it describes as in force and applied, including with 13 Member States, but this list is machine translated, capped at Medium confidence and gives no case data [4][5]. Negotiations with Italy, the USA and Greece began in 2023, yet the Commission reports no new agreements signed in any year from 2021 to 2025 [6][7][8][9][10][11]. The agreements are judged to have worked, with Medium attribution, in a narrow sense: the Commission rates preparation on coordination as good because of that experience [12]. What remains is agreements with the other Member States and administrative measures for accession. The validated assessment is partly_addressed, with priority High, direction of travel persistent and tractability Low.

**What remains.** What remains is agreements with the other Member States and the administrative measures to prepare for accession, which the 2026 key recommendation repeats. Agreements with 13 Member States were in force by the government's list and the Commission's count, the 2014 agreement with Italy came into force in December 2025 and an information exchange agreement with the Austrian pension authority was signed in June 2026, but the report does not say whether the Italy agreement adds to the 13 or updates the existing convention, and the negotiations with Greece have no recorded outcome. Further agreements would not by themselves close the gap.

**Direction of travel.** The key recommendation to negotiate and conclude bilateral agreements is unchanged from 2021 to 2026, and the chapter scale is context only. The 2026 report records two steps, the entry into force in December 2025 of the social insurance agreement with Italy signed in 2014 and an information exchange agreement signed in June 2026 between the pension fund and the Austrian pension authority, but the first took eleven years from signature and the 2025 report found no new agreement signed in its period, so they do not yet show a change of direction. The two confirmed count contradictions (14 against 13 EU agreements; 23 against 22 in total) still stand, and the 2026 report does not say whether the Italy agreement adds a Member State or replaces the 1961 convention the government lists.

**Priority.** Concluding agreements is in the key recommendation in the 2021 to 2025 reports, and social security coordination is a main strand of the chapter. The Commission's good preparation rating lowers the urgency but not the repeated requirement, so reviewers may reasonably weigh centrality differently.

**Tractability.** Progress depends on other states agreeing, not only on the government. No new agreement is recorded from 2021 to 2025, including after the negotiations started in 2023.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The core request has been stable since 2021, with small shifts in verb and scope. In 2021 the Commission asked the country to take steps to conclude further bilateral agreements and to identify administrative measures for accession [1]. In 2022 it asked it to step up signature of agreements with EU Member States, now naming healthcare insurance [13]. From 2023 the wording became to continue negotiating and concluding agreements and to put in place administrative measures [14][2]. The 2025 report adds preparation for electronic exchange of social security data [3]. The 2025 screening report states that further agreements need to be signed, specifically with EU Member States [15][16].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Two confirmed conflicts on the number of agreements stand unresolved, 14 against 13 with EU Member States between the 2020 and 2021 reports and 23 against 22 in total between the 2023 report and the screening report, and a current official list checked against the Commission's count would settle them [18][19][20][5].
- The ministry's list of agreements in force and applied is machine translated and gives no case data, so figures on claims handled, periods aggregated or benefits exported under each agreement would be needed to show the agreements working in practice [4].
- No outcome of the negotiations with Italy and Greece begun in 2023 is recorded, and a report of signature or ratification would settle their status [6][11].
- The Commission's good preparation rating is a single 2025 finding with no earlier rating to compare, and later Commission assessments would show whether readiness is changing [12].
