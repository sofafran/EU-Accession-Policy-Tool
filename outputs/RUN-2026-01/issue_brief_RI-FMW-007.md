# Issue brief: Information and support for mobile workers on their rights

RI-FMW-007 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** There is no designated body supporting EU mobile workers in their rights and the system for informing them of their rights is weak. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

In its 2025 screening report the Commission asks North Macedonia to improve the system that informs EU workers of their rights in the country and to designate an institution responsible for supporting EU mobile workers [1][2][3]. No country intervention responding to this requirement is linked in the evidence, so there is no implementation or result to report. A 2026 regional report by an international organisation states that complaint mechanisms are available only in local languages in most Western Balkan contexts and that workers have no way to navigate the several institutions sharing responsibility for their protection, but these items concern migrant workers generally and cannot alone support a finding about North Macedonia [4][5]. What remains is designating the institution and improving the information system. The validated assessment is open, with priority Low, direction of travel insufficient_evidence and tractability High.

**What remains.** What remains is designating an institution to support EU mobile workers and improving the system that informs them of their rights. No responding intervention is linked, so there is no status to compare.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was context only, and the issue's EU items come from one report, the 2025 screening report, so there is no sequence to read. The 2026 items are regional, concern migrant workers generally and cannot alone support a finding about the country.

**Priority.** The requirement appears in one report, in repeated sentences, and is not named in a key recommendation. The regional items do not raise this, as they concern other workers.

**Tractability.** Designating an institution is a single national decision, with no dependence on others and few actors. There is no evidence of past progress, so this rests on the nature of the task and is less certain for the wider information system.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement cannot be traced over time, because all the Commission's items on this issue come from one document, the February 2025 screening report [1][2][3]. In that report the Commission states that efforts should be made to improve the system informing EU workers about their rights and that an institution responsible for supporting EU mobile workers should be designated, and it repeats the call to designate such an institution in a separate sentence [1][3]. No item from the annual reports of 2020 to 2025 is recorded for this issue, and the requirement is not named in a key recommendation, so there is no earlier wording to compare.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Direction of travel cannot be assessed because the requirement appears in only one report, and the Commission's next assessment of this point would show whether it is moving [1].
- No source says whether any body has been designated or is preparing to support EU mobile workers, and a government decision or Commission finding on such an institution would settle whether the gap is being addressed [3].
- The regional evidence on complaint mechanisms and on navigating institutions concerns migrant workers generally, so country-specific evidence on how EU workers in North Macedonia obtain information on their rights would be needed before it can be used [4][5].
