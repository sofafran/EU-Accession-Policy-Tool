# Issue brief: Broadband planning and investment framework incomplete

RI-TEC-014 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** National broadband targets, plans and investment are not aligned with EU Digital Decade targets, and EU funding instruments, State aid rules and backhaul studies are not used or in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align its national broadband targets with the Digital Decade, to align further with EU rules on aid for broadband deployment, and to strengthen the capacity of the public broadcasting enterprise mandated to build optical infrastructure [1][2][3]. The National Operational Broadband Plan remains in force, but the Commission reported its implementation slowed in 2021 and further delayed in 2022 [4][5][6]. The country states that its targets will be aligned with the Digital Compass and that a mapping study is in preparation [7][8]. A feasibility study for a national transport optical network has started, but no network is built [9][10]. The Broadband Competence Office, in machine translation, reports that none of the three measures towards the 100 Mbps target was implemented [11]. The validated judgements are priority Medium, direction of travel persistent, unresolved gap partly_addressed and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** The latest EU items ask for targets aligned with the Digital Decade, further alignment on aid for broadband and stronger capacity at the public broadcasting enterprise. The compared statuses are proposals for G6: the ICT strategy with its 2030 goals is adopted, but its alignment with the Digital Decade is the government's own statement in machine translation, and target alignment, State aid and the national network are only proposed, while none of the three plan measures towards the 100 Mbps target has been implemented. What remains is updating the plan's targets, settling the aid rules and financing the backhaul network; the reported non-use of the Western Balkans Investment Framework for infrastructure sits beside technical assistance from it for a feasibility study, which appears to be a difference between study and construction but should be checked.

**Direction of travel.** The plan's implementation slowed in 2021 and was further delayed in 2022, a strategic approach to broadband was found lacking in 2022 and 2023, the plan was said only to remain valid in 2024 and 2025, and the 2025 screening asks for its targets to be aligned with the Digital Decade; the 2020 chapter-wide some progress is context and was not decisive. Newer Reform Agenda investment and a feasibility study are plans and do not yet show change, so this is not improving, and no 2026 Commission item addresses the framework. Sources conflict on the plan's period, with ITU and the 2025 report giving 2019-2029 and the 2022 and 2024 reports giving 2019-2023, which affects how far the delays can be measured against targets.

**Priority.** The requirement is repeated by the EU, in the 2022 and 2023 assessments and the 2025 screening, but it is not named in a benchmark, a Reform Agenda payment step or the chapter's key recommendations. The Reform Agenda plan does call the broadband network the most important digital project, but as an investment, so the issue matters for the theme with thinner support than the legal and regulator issues.

**Tractability.** Updating targets is within the government's control and a mapping study is the stated first step, but financing the network depends on outside funders, State aid alignment and a public enterprise whose capacity the Commission says needs strengthening. Past progress is weak: the plan was delayed in 2021 and 2022, and the national broadband competence office, reported in machine translation, finds none of three measures implemented. The financing figures come from a table flagged as possibly misread, so they support only the general point that funding is mixed.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2020 the Commission recorded some progress, citing ICT strategy preparations and the new Broadband Competence Office [12]. In 2022 and 2023 its assessments found a strategic approach to broadband access lacking, the 2023 one adding national strategic investment and the targets of the National Broadband Plan [13][14]. The 2025 screening made the requirement more specific: align the targets with the Digital Decade and align further on State aid for broadband, while noting that the Western Balkans Investment Framework and the Connecting Europe Facility had not been used for infrastructure [1][2][15]. In November 2025 the Commission added the need to strengthen the public broadcasting enterprise [3]. No 2026 Commission item addresses the framework.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Sources conflict on the plan's period, with ITU and the 2025 report giving 2019 to 2029 and the 2021, 2022 and 2024 reports giving 2019 to 2023, and the adopted plan text would settle how delays should be measured against its targets [16][5][6][23][4].
- The claim that the 2030 ICT strategy goals are aligned with the Digital Decade, and the Office's finding on the three unimplemented measures, rest on machine-translated government text capped at Medium confidence, so official English versions or the Office's report itself would be needed [17][18][11].
- The reported non-use of the Western Balkans Investment Framework sits beside its technical assistance for the network feasibility study, which appears to separate study from construction but should be checked against funding records [15][21].
- The financing figures for the network come from a table flagged as possibly misread, so a confirmed financing plan is needed before the funding gap can be assessed [10].
