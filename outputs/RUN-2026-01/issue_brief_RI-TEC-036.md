# Issue brief: National e-services portal limited in range and use

RI-TEC-036 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The national e-services portal offers too few transactional services, is little used, or does not serve all users. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Coordination failure (MEC-08); Low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to offer many more fully digitalised services on its national e-services portal and to treat the digitalisation of public services as a priority, and a Reform Agenda payment condition requires a 50 per cent increase in the offer of e-services [1][2][3]. The Commission states that the portal was upgraded, its offer widened with services most demanded by citizens, and some certificates put online [4][5]. The State Audit Office, in machine translation, found only 0.556 requests per registered user and about 140 laws not aligned with the digitalisation package [6][7], and the 2025 strategy reports about 13 of 100 adults registered [8]. In April 2026 the Commission found digital government quite underdeveloped, with most e-services rarely used [9]. There is no outcome evidence. The validated judgements are priority High, direction of travel persistent, unresolved gap partly_addressed and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** The portal is proposed as in practice and its upgrade as implemented, while the Reform Agenda reform is proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals, and the upgrade status sits uneasily with the Commission's 2024 note that the 135 services were not yet configured. Progress against the 50% target cannot be fixed, because the 264 baseline conflicts with the Commission's 392 and 124 figures, and 246 also conflicts with 124, so sources are cited both ways. What remains is a much wider set of fully transactional, commonly requested services that people actually use, on an agreed count, and equal access, where the Commission's statement that the portal caters for special needs conflicts with the audit's finding (machine translated, capped at Medium confidence) that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services.

**Direction of travel.** From 2020 to 2026 the Commission repeats the same shortfall: too few fully transactional services, low use and below-expectation results, ending with 'quite underdeveloped' in 2026, and the world index rank falls from 72 to 80 to 84 (the last from a machine-translated strategy table, capped at Medium confidence). The 2025 report does record a real change, with most-demanded services and some certificates now online, so the case for 'improving' is open but rests on one report that the 2026 finding does not bear out. The Chapter 10 scale (Some, Limited, Limited, Some progress in 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025) is context only and was not decisive, and service counts were not used for trend because their definitions differ.

**Priority.** The e-services increase is a Reform Agenda payment condition, and widening the portal's services is a recommendation repeated across three or more reports, including 'treat as a priority' in the 2025 screening. It is central to the digital government theme and Chapter 10. The rating rests on these, not on the large number of national audit and strategy items.

**Tractability.** A single platform, EU funding and a dated payment step favour progress, and the Commission reports new services in 2025. Against that, the Commission's own wording shows the 135-service upgrade slipping for years and fragmented ownership, and the State Audit Office (machine translated, capped at Medium confidence) reports about 140 laws unaligned and 101 of 242 posts unfilled, which means many institutions must act.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's message has stayed essentially the same from 2020 to 2026. It first asked institutions to commit more to the portal and to encourage its use [10][11]. From 2022 it asked for a wider offer of services and noted that fully electronic services had barely grown [12][13][14]. In 2023 and 2024 it asked for the 135 new services to be completed and found the most common services, such as personal documents, still not online [15][16][17]. From 2024 the Reform Agenda added the 50 per cent target, and in 2025 the screening asked for digitalisation to be treated as a priority [3][2]. In 2026 the Commission still describes rare use and few fully transactional services [9].

## Conflicting evidence

- For the same Reform Agenda target on e-services in October 2024, the Agenda gives a 2024 baseline of 264 while the Commission assessment gives a current figure of 392. [18][19]
- The Reform Agenda gives 264 e-services as the 2024 baseline, while the Commission report gives 124 services accessible online by September 2024. [18][20]
- Commission documents of June and October 2024 give 246 services available online and 124 services accessible online by September 2024. [21][20]
- The Commission states in 2024 that most administrative services are offered online and offline, while the State Audit Office in 2024 records 1,120 services available only in person. [22][23]
- North Macedonia told the 2023 screening that the platform allows access to people with disabilities, while the State Audit Office finds that visually impaired persons cannot use the portal e-services. [24][25]
- The Commission states in 2024 that the portal caters to people with special needs, while the State Audit Office concludes in 2024 that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services. [26][27]
- The 2023 report says eIDAS alignment was further delayed, while the 2023 public administration reform strategy says the 2019 law on electronic documents, identification and trust services is aligned with European legislation on digitalisation. [28][29]

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Sources conflict on the number of services, with the Reform Agenda baseline of 264 against Commission figures of 392, 246 and 124 in 2024, so progress against the 50 per cent target cannot be measured until an agreed count and definition is published [18][19][21][20].
- The Commission states that the portal caters to people with special needs, while the State Audit Office, in machine translation capped at Medium confidence, finds that persons with disabilities cannot use e-services, and an independent accessibility test of the portal would settle this conflict [26][27][25].
- The Commission states that most administrative services are offered online and offline, while the State Audit Office records 1,120 services available only in person, and a complete service catalogue showing the channel for each service would settle this conflict [22][23].
- Use figures come mainly from machine-translated national audit and strategy texts and no recent usage series exists, so regular portal statistics on transactions per service would be needed to show whether the 2025 upgrade changed use [46][8][4].
