# Issue brief: Base registers incomplete, unreliable or not digitalised

RI-TEC-015 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Key registers, above all the population register, hold data of poor quality or are little used, and many institutional, property and intellectual property registers are not yet digitalised or kept up to date. **Problem mechanisms:** Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to complete the digitalisation of institutions' registers and data exchange, to improve the quality of population register data, and to digitalise intellectual property registration further [1][2][3]. The Central Population Register is assessed as in practice: the Commission reported it operational in 2020 and used to cross-check the 2021 census [4][5]. The Commission nonetheless found its use still limited in 2024, and the government's machine-translated 2025 strategy also describes it as underused and in need of a larger data set [6][7][8]. The platform for digitalising registers, the Register of Registers and the digital register of charges are assessed only as proposed, and the 2023 strategy's baseline was no digital registers in 2022 [9][10][11][12]. The Commission still finds municipal property registers analogue [13]. No outcome has been assessed. The gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The population register is proposed as in practice, but the register platform, the Register of Registers and the wider e-services reform are all proposed only, and these statuses are themselves proposals for G6. What remains is to complete the digitalisation of institutional, municipal property and intellectual property registers and to raise the quality and use of population register data. The fully digital National Real Estate Register and the Commission's analogue property registers are a noted difference of register and level, not a conflict, and the machine-translated strategy on low use is capped at Medium confidence.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission repeated the need to improve population register data in 2021, 2022 and 2023, and the same recommendation to complete the digitalisation of institutions' registers in 2023, 2024 and 2025, with use of the register still limited in 2024. The 2025 mention of efforts does not change the recommendation, so the default of persistent applies. The Chapter 10 scale is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** The Commission's main findings gave the same key recommendation to complete register digitalisation in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and base registers are central to the digital government theme. The recommendation is one clause of a wider one on administrative procedures and is shared with the data exchange and e-services issues, which reviewers may weigh. The Reform Agenda payment step on a register of charges is flagged as doubtful for this theme and was not relied on.

**Tractability.** The operating population register shows that delivery is possible, and a register platform was begun with EU support. Against that, completion needs many institutions and municipalities to act, and the strategy's baseline of no digital registers in 2022 shows how much remains. The two strategy items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's focus has moved from the population register to registers in general. In 2021 it asked for population register data to be fully uploaded and checked and used to cross-check census data, and in 2022 and 2023 it asked for better data quality, continuously verified against other registers [14][15][2]. From 2023 a key recommendation asks the country to complete the digitalisation of institutions' registers and data exchange as part of fully implementing the law on general administrative procedures, repeated almost word for word in 2024 and 2025 [16][17][1]. The 2025 report notes efforts but keeps the recommendation [18]. The 2025 screening report adds intellectual property registration [19][3].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No later source confirms that the register digitalisation platform came into use in 2023 as planned, and a count of digitalised registers against the strategy's baseline and targets would settle progress [9][12].
- The 2023 report says eIDAS alignment was further delayed while the 2023 strategy states that the 2019 digital laws are aligned with European legislation, a confirmed conflict that a Commission alignment assessment of the law would settle [20][21].
- The fully digital National Real Estate Register rests only on the country's own account, while the Commission finds municipal property registers analogue, so independent evidence on use of each register is missing [25][26].
- Much of the evidence on register plans and on the underuse of the population register comes from machine-translated government strategies, capped at Medium confidence, which an official English version or independent usage data would strengthen [7][23].
