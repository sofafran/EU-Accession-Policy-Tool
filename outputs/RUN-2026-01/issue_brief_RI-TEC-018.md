# Issue brief: Data protection authority lacks independence and resources

RI-TEC-018 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The data protection supervisory authority depends on government for staffing and funding, has capacity below its tasks, and can be left without a director. **Problem mechanisms:** Lack of institutional independence (MEC-05); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has found since 2021 that North Macedonia's Personal Data Protection Agency depends on government for staff and funds, and in 2025 said its capacities need to improve [1][2][3]. The Agency drafted amendments to secure its autonomy in April 2022, but its own machine-translated report says adoption had still not started in 2025 [4][5]. The Commission reports staff falling from 21 to 20, and the Agency reports 18 at the end of 2024 and 17 at the end of 2025 [6][7][8][9]. The Commission also found no director appointed from January 2025 [10]. The 2020 data protection law is validated as in_practice, with weak application, and no outcome is recorded. What remains is autonomy in hiring and spending, and resources matching the Agency's tasks. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is worsening. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The amendments meant to secure the Agency's autonomy are proposed only, and the 2025-2030 strategy's proposed status of implemented means only that work has begun, with the Commission finding it was held back; these statuses are themselves proposals for G6. What remains is autonomy in hiring and spending, and funding and staff that match the Agency's tasks. Statements that the Agency is independent describe its legal status, while the Commission and the Agency's own reports describe its operating dependence, so this is a noted difference and not a conflict.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission moved from dependence on ministries in 2021 and understaffing in 2022 and 2023 to staff falling further in 2024 and no director in 2025, with reported staff of 21, then 20, then 18 at the end of 2024 and 17 at the end of 2025. The last two counts are the Agency's own, machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, but they run the same way as the Commission's findings. The Agency reports a director from 5 December 2025, which is a later date than the Commission's finding, not a conflict, and the chapter scale was not decisive.

**Priority.** The Commission has raised the Agency's dependence and capacity from 2021 to 2025 and an independent authority is central to the data theme, but the requirement appears only in body text, in 2023 and 2025 and in the 2025 communication in general terms. It is in no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, and the main findings do not mention it, so it is a central requirement with thin support, on the same footing as RI-TEC-019.

**Tractability.** Only a few domestic actors are needed, a draft has existed since April 2022 and the budget is small. Past progress is poor, however: the draft has not moved, the Ministry of Finance repeatedly refused funds or returned the employment plan, and the director post took most of 2025 to fill. The Agency's reports are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, and they are a party's account of its own constraints.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's concern has stayed the same in substance but its findings have become more severe. In 2021 it described the Agency's dependence on the Ministry of Finance and another ministry for recruitment and organisation [1]. In 2022 and 2023 it said understaffing seriously harmed implementation, and in 2023 it called for alignment on the Agency's independence and noted that it is not financially autonomous [11][12][13]. In 2024 it said the Agency lacks independence to hire staff and spend its budget [2]. In 2025 it added that no director had been appointed and called for improved capacities, while a general communication asked for sustained resources and stronger independence [10][3][14].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The latest staffing, budget and funding refusals rest on the Agency's own machine-translated reports capped at Medium confidence, some at Low [23][9][22]; Commission or Ministry of Finance figures would settle them.
- Whether the director reported by the Agency from December 2025 has improved its functioning is not shown, so later Commission assessments of the Agency's operation are needed.
- How far the 2025 to 2030 data protection strategy has been carried out is unclear, since the Commission says implementation was held back [16]; a progress report against the strategy's actions would settle it.
- No outcome evidence shows whether the Agency's supervision improves compliance [24], so data on compliance rates over time are needed.
