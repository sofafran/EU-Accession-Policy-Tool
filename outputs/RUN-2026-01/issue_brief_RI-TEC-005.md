# Issue brief: Limited taxpayer e-services

RI-TEC-005 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The tax administration offers a limited range of electronic services to taxpayers. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has asked every year from 2020 to 2026 for the Public Revenue Office to extend or keep expanding e-services for taxpayers, alongside better business processes and compliance risk management [1][2][3]. The Commission reports that the Office issues pre-filled annual returns through its electronic personal tax platform and names the e-Tax system and the e-VAT application as working e-services; all three are assessed as in practice [4][5][6]. The Commission states that e-Tax simplified tax reporting, assessed as worked with Medium attribution though no measure is given; for e-VAT, success and attribution are insufficient_evidence [6][7]. The electronic tax administration and e-invoicing remain at the planning and draft legislation stage, in machine-translated plan items capped at Medium confidence [8][9][10]. The validated assessment is partly_addressed, with priority Medium, direction of travel persistent and tractability High.

**What remains.** The Commission still asks in 2026 for expansion, while e-Tax, e-VAT and pre-filled returns are proposed as in practice and the electronic tax administration only as proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals, and the Macedonian-language plan items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence. What remains is a wider range of taxpayer e-services, with the electronic tax administration still at project documentation and draft legislation stage.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission's recommendation to keep extending taxpayer e-services recurs every year from 2020 to 2026 with almost no change, moving only from 'further extend' to 'continue the process of expansion', so the default applies; this is one repeated view, not independent evidence. The Chapter 16 'Some progress' wording of 2021 is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** The recommendation is repeated across more than three reports, but taxpayer e-services are one clause beside business processes, the integrated IT system and compliance risk, so the issue is not central to Chapter 16 or the theme. The Reform Agenda's e-services step names the fiscal sector only as an example, and this was not read as a payment condition on this issue.

**Tractability.** One agency leads, working platforms exist, and upgrades are reported by the Commission, so progress depends little on other actors or the EU. A caution is that the source does not say how far further e-services depend on the integrated IT system, which has been outstanding since 2020.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The request has changed very little. From 2020 to 2022 the Commission asked the country to develop a new integrated IT tax model or system, improve risk management and further extend e-services for taxpayers [1][11][12]. In 2023 it said the Public Revenue Office still had to replace its fragmented IT system with an integrated one and continue to expand e-services [13]. From 2024 the annual reports speak of continuing the process of expansion of e-services and no longer name the integrated IT system in that sentence, although the February 2025 screening report still asks for it [14][2][3][15]. The 2021 report recorded some progress through upgraded e-tax services [16].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No usage figures are given for the e-Tax system, the e-VAT application or pre-filled returns, so data on the number and share of filings made online would be needed to judge results beyond the Commission's statements [6][5].
- The Commission's statement that e-Tax simplified tax reporting gives no measure and the 2023 wording repeats 2022, so evidence such as filing time or compliance cost data would be needed to confirm the result independently [6][7].
- Sources differ on whether an integrated tax information system exists to be expanded or has still to be built, with the Commission asking in 2025 for a new system and an international report referring to expanding the existing one, and a clear statement from the Public Revenue Office or the Commission would settle it [15][20].
- Progress on the electronic tax administration and e-invoicing is known only from machine-translated plan rows, and evidence of the project documentation, the law and the systems being delivered would be needed [8][9][10].
