# Issue brief: Core e-government building blocks missing

RI-TEC-004 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Shared systems that every institution needs to work digitally, such as a single document management system, legal and technical arrangements for electronic archiving, a digital mailbox and shared administrative information systems, are missing or little used. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to put in place the basic systems that e-government needs, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to three steps: legislation allowing electronic documents to be archived, data storage infrastructure with an e-platform for record keeping and archival work, and a single document management system for all central institutions [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]. Parliament adopted the Law on Archival Material and Archival Activity in June 2025, the Commission considers that step achieved, and the law applies from 1 June 2026 [8][9][10]. In 2025 the Commission's technical checks found the document management system only partially operational, and the government reported, on machine-translated figures, that 22 institutions used a very small part of it [11][12]. In 2026 the Commission records some progress through an upgraded document management system and states that by the end of June 2026 44 institutions were using the new electronic archiving and document management system [13][14][15]. This is a change in use but short of all central institutions, and the earlier and later counts may not measure the same system. No 2026 item reports the storage platform, the digital mailbox or the by-laws. The Commission's statement that adoption of the archiving legislation facilitates e-government is judged too early to show success [16]. The assessment is that the gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is improving, priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Of the three Reform Agenda requirements, the archiving law is adopted and applies from June 2026, and the Commission reports a central document management system in use by 44 institutions by the end of June 2026, which is short of the single system across all central institutions that the Reform Agenda requires. No 2026 item for the country reports the storage and record-keeping platform, the digital mailbox or the by-laws, or says whether the 2026 deadlines were met. What remains is the platform, the digital mailbox, the by-laws and extending the document system to all central institutions.

**Direction of travel.** The public administration rating is context only, but the Commission attributes its some progress in 2026 partly to the upgraded document management system, and it records a central document management system launched and in use by 44 institutions by the end of June 2026, which is a change in use and not only in law. Earlier figures were 21 institutions in 2022 and 22 in 2025, from machine-translated national sources capped at Medium, and the 2026 report calls the system new, so the counts may not measure the same system; the direction is clear but its size is uncertain. The Reform Agenda requires the system across all central institutions, so this is improving and not largely resolved.

**Priority.** The archiving law, the storage platform and the single document system are named Reform Agenda steps with payments attached, and these shared systems are central to the digital government theme. The rating rests on those EU conditions, not on the many national plan items.

**Tractability.** The tasks are bounded, led by one ministry and backed by payment incentives, and the legal step was achieved by its 2025 deadline. Roll-out is harder: it needs hardware, licences, training and change of working habits in many institutions, and earlier piloting left 22 institutions using a small part of the system. The national evidence on use is machine translated and capped at Medium.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2020 and 2023 the Commission's wording in this area concerns the human resources management information system, whose use it says increased but could be further exploited [17][18]. In July 2023 the Commission states more broadly that basic systems such as a digital mailbox need to be put in place to advance e-government [1]. From October 2024 the requirement becomes concrete and conditional: the Reform Agenda sets three payment conditions on archiving legislation, storage infrastructure with an archival e-platform, and a single document management system for all central institutions [2][3][4]. In October 2025 the Commission explains the purpose, stating that the legal framework and a complete document management system will let institutions exchange and store documents electronically without printing [19]. In 2026 the Commission attributes its finding of some progress in public administration reform partly to the upgraded system for electronic management of documents [13].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows whether the June 2026 Reform Agenda deadline for the storage infrastructure and archival e-platform was met, so a Commission payment assessment or technical check after that date would settle its status [2][20].
- The 44 institutions using the document management system at the end of June 2026 fall short of all central institutions, and the deadline for that step is December 2026, so a later Commission assessment would show whether the step was met [21][15].
- The success of the archival law is judged too_early and its attribution insufficient_evidence [16]. The law applies from 1 June 2026 and no source reports its by-laws adopted, so evidence of electronic archiving under the law after that date would settle this [10].
- The counts of 21 users in 2022 and 22 in 2025 come from machine-translated national strategies capped at Medium confidence, while the count of 44 in 2026 is the Commission's for a system it calls new, so a consistent count for one defined system would show the size of the change [24][12][14].
- The amendment of the archival law planned in 2023 and the general e-government framework are rated insufficient_evidence, so a source linking the 2023 plan to the 2025 law, or describing the framework, would settle their status [27][28].
