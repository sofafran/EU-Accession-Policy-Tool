# Issue brief: Core e-government building blocks missing

RI-TEC-004 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Shared systems that every institution needs to work digitally, such as a single document management system, legal and technical arrangements for electronic archiving, a digital mailbox and shared administrative information systems, are missing or little used. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to put in place the basic systems that e-government needs, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to three steps: legislation allowing electronic documents to be archived, data storage infrastructure with an e-platform for record keeping and archival work, and a single document management system for all central institutions [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]. Parliament adopted the Law on Archival Material and Archival Activity in June 2025 and the Commission considers that step achieved, but the law applies only from 1 June 2026 and its by-laws remain proposed [8][9][10]. The Commission's technical checks found the document management system only partially operational, and the government reports that 22 institutions use a very small part of it, on machine-translated figures [11][12]. The Commission's statement that adoption facilitates e-government is judged too early to show success [13]. The validated assessment is that the gap is partly_addressed, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High and tractability is Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |
|---|---|
| Unresolved gap | partly_addressed |
| Direction of travel | persistent |
| Priority | High |
| Tractability | Medium |

**What remains.** Of the three Reform Agenda requirements, the law is adopted and the Commission treats that step as achieved, but it applied only from June 2026 with by-laws still proposed, the e-platform is proposed and the document system is only partially operational; these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is application of the law, the storage and record-keeping platform, the single document system across all central institutions and the digital mailbox. No item in the input shows whether the 2026 deadlines were met.

**Direction of travel.** The change since the Commission's 2023 call for basic systems is the adoption of the electronic archiving law in 2025 and the Reform Agenda steps themselves, and adoption alone does not show improvement. The Commission's technical checks found the document system only partially operational, and use has barely moved, from 21 institutions in 2022 to 22 in 2025, on machine-translated national figures capped at Medium. The chapter scale is context only and was not decisive.

**Priority.** The archiving law, the storage platform and the single document system are named Reform Agenda steps with payments attached, and these shared systems are central to the digital government theme. The rating rests on those EU conditions, not on the many national plan items.

**Tractability.** The tasks are bounded, led by one ministry and backed by payment incentives, and the legal step was achieved by its 2025 deadline. Roll-out is harder: it needs hardware, licences, training and change of working habits in many institutions, and earlier piloting left 22 institutions using a small part of the system. The national evidence on use is machine translated and capped at Medium.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2020 and 2023 the Commission's wording in this area concerns the human resources management information system, whose use it says increased but could be further exploited [14][15]. In July 2023 the Commission states more broadly that basic systems such as a digital mailbox need to be put in place to advance e-government [1]. From October 2024 the requirement becomes concrete and conditional: the Reform Agenda sets three payment conditions on archiving legislation, storage infrastructure with an archival e-platform, and a single document management system for all central institutions [2][3][4]. In October 2025 the Commission explains the purpose, stating that the legal framework and a complete document management system will let institutions exchange and store documents electronically without printing [16].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows whether the June 2026 and December 2026 Reform Agenda deadlines for the storage platform and the single document management system were met, so a Commission payment assessment or technical check after those dates would settle the status [17][18].
- The success of the archival law is judged too_early and its attribution insufficient_evidence, because no item shows use of e-government tools after the law began to apply; data on electronic archiving in practice after 1 June 2026 would settle this [13][10].
- The figures on how many institutions use the document management system come from machine-translated national strategies capped at Medium confidence, so an English or independent count of users would strengthen the evidence [20][12].
- The amendment of the archival law planned in 2023 and the general e-government framework are rated insufficient_evidence, so a source linking the 2023 plan to the 2025 law, or describing the framework, would settle their status [23][24].
