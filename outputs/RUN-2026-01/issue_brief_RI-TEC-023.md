# Issue brief: Operational cybersecurity capacity of public institutions weak

RI-TEC-023 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The national incident response centre and state institutions lack staff, expertise and secure infrastructure, so government services and digital infrastructure remain exposed to attacks. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks state institutions to strengthen their cyber infrastructure and expertise and the national Computer Incident Response Centre to strengthen its staffing and operational capacity significantly, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to operational response teams, a staffed competent authority and a secure government network [1][2][3][4][5]. North Macedonia adopted a cyber security strategy and action plan in January 2025 and a law on network and information security in June 2025, which establishes the national and government response teams [6]. The State Audit Office reports, in machine-translated findings, that only 3 of 11 MKD-CIRT posts were filled and that 67% of surveyed institutions had no response team, and the disaster recovery centre is used by 3 of about 140 bodies [7][8][9]. In April 2026 the Commission still reports major deficiencies [10]. The validated assessment is that the gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, priority is High and tractability is Low.

**What remains.** The latest EU items, of November 2025 and April 2026, still call for significant strengthening and report major deficiencies, while responses are proposed as adopted on paper (the law, strategy and action plan, the national and government response teams) or only planned (government data centre, secure network, security operations centre), and the disaster recovery centre is used by 3 of about 140 bodies; these statuses are themselves proposals for review. What remains is staffed and trained response teams working to the Reform Agenda standard, security officers and tested plans in institutions, and secure government infrastructure in actual use. The Reform Agenda's 2024 baseline of CERTs not operational sits against statements that MKD-CIRT is in place, possibly because the baseline means NIS2 readiness, and the audit evidence behind this finding is machine translated, capped at Medium confidence, with percentages from a survey of 18 institutions.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission's wording is essentially unchanged across three reports, that state institutions need stronger capacity and infrastructure and the national centre more staff (2023, 2024, 2025), and its reform programme assessments repeat large or major deficiencies in 2025 and 2026, so the default of persistent applies; the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. Incident counts cannot show a trend in capacity, because reporting was not mandatory and the figures are not comparable (1,441 or 1,443 for 2020, 2,804 for 2022, about 170 in 2023 on an unstated definition). The World Bank's advanced rating and the audit's finding of poor protection are a noted difference and not a conflict, since an index of national commitments and a test of practice measure different things, and the 2024 index itself lists technical and capacity measures as areas for growth.

**Priority.** Operational national and government CERTs, a staffed competent authority and a secure government network are Reform Agenda payment steps, and the Commission has repeated the need for capacity in its 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports. The Commission's reform programme assessments also name these deficiencies, and without operating capacity the legal framework has little effect, so the issue is central to the theme. The rating does not rest on the volume of audit and action plan items.

**Tractability.** Progress needs people the state has struggled to hire and keep (IT posts about half filled, 3 of 11 MKD-CIRT posts filled, a shortage the strategy says extends across the region), action across about 140 bodies, and budgets the strategy itself calls at risk, while the main infrastructure is at pre-feasibility stage and looks to outside funding. Past progress is weak, as most surveyed institutions had no response team five years after the previous strategy called for them, and the new 40% salary supplement is adopted with no item yet showing its effect. Strengthening MKD-CIRT alone would be more achievable than system-wide capacity, and the audit and strategy items are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* From 2020 to 2023 the Commission frames cybersecurity mainly at regional level, as a priority for capacity building and resilience against hybrid threats [11][12][13]. From 2023 its country wording is essentially unchanged: state institutions need stronger cyber capacity, infrastructure and expertise, and the national response centre needs more staff [14][15][16][1]. The 2025 wording sharpens this to significant strengthening of the centre [2]. In October 2024 the Reform Agenda adds concrete payment conditions on response teams, the competent authority and a secure network [3][4][5]. The Commission's reform programme assessments move from large deficiencies in 2025 to major deficiencies in 2026 [17][18].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No item shows the national or government response teams operating to the Reform Agenda standard, and the Reform Agenda's 2024 baseline of not operational sits against statements that MKD-CIRT is in place, so a Commission payment assessment after June 2026 would settle their status [25][26][23].
- Incident figures do not show a trend in capacity, since sources give conflicting counts for 2020 (1,443 and 1,441) and later figures use different definitions, so consistent mandatory incident reporting under the new law would be needed [21][22][31].
- The audit evidence on staffing and protection is machine translated, capped at Medium confidence, and based on a survey of 18 institutions, so an updated audit or staffing data would test whether capacity has changed [28][8].
- The new 40% salary supplement for cyber security staff is adopted but no item shows its effect on hiring or retention, so later vacancy data for MKD-CIRT and institutions would settle this [43][35].
