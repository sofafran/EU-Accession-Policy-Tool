# Issue brief: Alignment with the European Labour Authority regulation

RI-FMW-006 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft, rebuilt after G7 (GATE-0012) with evidence or judgements added since, for sign-off at the next G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007 and GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. Agent synthesis sections written for the version signed off at G7 may not reflect what was added since, unless they say so; values marked (proposed) are tier 2 ratings still to be reviewed.

**Issue definition.** National law is not aligned with the regulation establishing the European Labour Authority, including the arrangements for cooperating with it. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to further align its legislation and exchange of good practice with the EURES and European Labour Authority regulations, and its screening report finds national law not aligned with the regulation establishing the Authority [1][2][3]. The Commission reports that capacity building for civil servants on EURES and the Authority took place, and this training is assessed as implemented, but no source shows the knowledge in use and it does not align the law [4][5]. The country states medium-term plans to harmonise, with full implementation on accession, and to prepare the State Labour Inspectorate for tasks coordinated by the Authority, including inspections and undeclared work; both are assessed as proposed [6][7][8]. No outcome has been assessed. The gap is open, the direction of travel is persistent, the priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** What remains is aligning national law with the regulation and preparing the State Labour Inspectorate for coordinated tasks. Both responses are only proposed for the medium term, and the implemented training does not align the law; these statuses are proposals.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was context only. The composite recommendation naming the European Labour Authority is nearly unchanged in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports, and the only direct findings, in the screening report, say national law is not aligned.

**Priority.** The authority is named in a key recommendation in three reports, but only as part of a sentence mainly about the employment service. The direct support is one source and the issue is not central to the chapter.

**Tractability.** Harmonisation is within national control and plans exist, but the plans only run in the medium term, with full implementation at accession. They also need institutional and procedural preparation by the labour inspectorate as well as legal change.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The European Labour Authority first appears in the evidence in the 2023 report, inside a key recommendation mainly about strengthening the Employment Service Agency through training, further alignment of legislation and exchange of good practice in line with the EURES and Authority regulations [9]. The 2024 and 2025 reports repeat this recommendation almost word for word, with the 2025 report only spelling out the full names of EURES and the Authority [10][1]. The only direct findings on the Authority come from the February 2025 screening report, which states twice that North Macedonia is not aligned with the regulation establishing it [2][3]. The requirement is therefore unchanged in substance since 2023.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- No source shows that the training on EURES and the Authority is being used in practice, so evidence of trained staff applying it, for example in the Employment Service Agency or the labour inspectorate, would settle whether it has had any effect [5].
- The content and timing of the medium-term harmonisation plan are not set out, so a draft law, action plan or timetable would show whether alignment is under way [6][8].
- No item shows the institutional, procedural and administrative preparations of the State Labour Inspectorate being carried out, which inspectorate reports or Commission findings could confirm [7].
