# Comparator brief: Interoperability platform and data exchange underused

RI-TEC-028 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. **Draft policy options at the end are internal drafts** and stay so after sign-off at G7 until a separate decision adapts them for an external audience.

## The issue in North Macedonia (validated at G6)

**Unresolved gap (partly_addressed).** The platform operates and some institutions, such as customs, exchange data regularly, but the 2025 Commission recommendation still asks to complete data exchange and the responding upgrades and reforms are mainly proposed; these statuses are themselves proposals. There is a confirmed contradiction: the country told the 2023 screening that the once-only principle is applied, while its 2024 Reform Agenda says institutions still do not gather evidence ex officio. What remains is regular use of the platform by the institutions obliged to use it, so that evidence is obtained ex officio as the law prescribes.

**Priority High, tractability Medium, direction of travel persistent.**

## Reading this brief (agent synthesis)

This brief compares North Macedonia's underused interoperability platform with three countries: Moldova, which put a legal duty and sanctions behind its exchange platform; Serbia, which has a procedural duty to exchange data and a central exchange system; and Montenegro, which reports counts of active exchange alongside connected institutions. All three cases were rated of Medium relevance, so this issue has no closely matching case. None of the cases measures whether institutions use their platform more because of a reform: Moldova's evidence stops at the text of the law, Serbia's evidence is on the citizen side, and Montenegro's figures are two unverified government points. The cases show what was tried, what to measure and what to avoid, not a proven route. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses. Several Montenegrin and Moldovan sources are machine translated under LIM-0030 and capped at Medium confidence.

### CMP-0004: Moldova

**Comparator relevance: Medium.** Moldova has the closest lever to the question, a legal duty and sanctions behind an existing platform, and the agency named resistance from data owners in 2020. But no extracted item shows current underuse, the Commission reports searched in full do not name MConnect, and the audit that might address it is blocked. Coordination and capacity appear in general audit findings, not tied to platform use.

**Problem addressed.** In July 2020 the e-Governance Agency named resistance from data owners, inconsistent data and incompatible systems as obstacles to data exchange. The Court of Accounts, restating its audit of public sector information systems adopted in December 2020, says a large part of those systems do not exchange information [1] and that misalignment with government platforms, including MConnect, caused interoperability difficulties [2]; this predates the 2021 amendments, so it describes the problem and not their implementation. A full-text search of the Commission 2024 and 2025 reports finds no mention of MConnect. The 2025 sentence asking for stronger interoperability between verification systems and state registries concerns asset declarations under Chapter 23 and is flagged theme_doubtful [3]. A 2024 sentence on 14 fragmented systems in the labour ministry [4] bears on this point but is in the unmatched queue, so it stays unused and is listed for the analyst.

**Reform or mechanism used.** MConnect was piloted from 2014 to 2018, and Interoperable Europe says Law 142/2018 defines how data exchange happens through it. Amendments effective on 18 January 2021 wrote into Article 6 of that law the duty not to ask for documents available through the platform, and added fines and temporary disqualification from a post to the Code of Contraventions. The operator also states a free data rule between public participants, a duty on participants to make their data sets available, and, in 2020 slides, an access route intended for participants with no information system, with no later confirmation that it operates.

**How it was implemented.** The operator reports 480+ public and private institutions connected, 2.6+ million requests a day and 600 million exchanges in total, under 2024 figures on its own page. The only independent evidence is the Court of Accounts follow-up of September 2024, which concerns public sector information systems and not MConnect as such. It reports that connecting one named system was still under analysis, and that it was shown no evidence that a planned exchange between two systems was carried out or works. A full-text search of the Moldovan sources held finds no statement that any fine or disqualification has been applied.

**Evidence of results.** No outcome evidence on the 2021 amendments or on use by institutions found. Searched in full text: the operator's MConnect page, its 2020 slides, 2021 notice and 2025 project summary, the 2026 state news report, the agency's 2023 survey summary (MDA-GOV-2024-002), the civil society analysis (MDA-CSO-2023-001), the Commission 2024 and 2025 reports, the 2024 Interoperable Europe supporting document and the Court of Accounts follow-up. MDA-CSO-2023-001, MDA-GOV-2024-002 and MDA-GOV-2026-001 do not name MConnect. None measures use by institutions over time, documents no longer requested, or any effect of the 2021 amendments. Separately, the Commission says in 2025 that access and use of digital public services significantly increased [5], with no figure and no named platform; this is on the citizen side and is not linked to the amendments.

**Limitations of the evidence.** Every use figure is the operator's own, is machine translated and capped at Medium, combines public and private bodies and has no earlier figure to compare with. The two volume figures do not reconcile: 2.6 million a day would pass 600 million in about 230 days, so they may count different things. A Court of Accounts page whose URL refers to an examination of MConnect (MDA-GOV-2021-001; title from the URL, page not read) returned HTTP 403 and the MConnect statistics dataset returned 404, so neither is evidence here, and the wording seen only in search excerpts at country selection is not used (LIM-0028). The operator's page and the Court of Accounts follow-up are machine translated, capped at Medium and not legal texts, so their use is outside LIM-0029 as worded, for the analyst to confirm at G6b. Interoperable Europe's general remark that Moldova is on the right track [6] is not evidence of implementation and is not relied on. Claims of absence in this case rest on a full-text search of the eleven Moldovan source texts held for MConnect, interoperability and the sanctions, and on the extracted items classified to this issue.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: North Macedonia already has a legal obligation to use its platform [7], and its 2023 strategy says competence to inspect laws on ICT use is not established [8], so Moldova's sanctions may be the closest parallel in these cases. The evidence stops at the text of the law: it does not show that the sanctions were applied or that they raised use. If North Macedonia considered a sanction in law, this case could show only that such a mechanism exists on paper and is untested here, and that a connection count, such as 480+, may not show regular use.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** The wording of a duty on officials and bodies backed by a personal sanction could be copied into comparable administrative law, but the case gives no evidence that it would raise use, and no item states a lesson. The access route stated in 2020 for participants with no information system speaks to a related barrier, North Macedonia's lack of communications software [9], but the only source is a 2020 slide with no later confirmation.

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Moldova's connection count includes private bodies and has no activity threshold, so it cannot be set against North Macedonia's 58 connected and 33 active institutions [10]. North Macedonia's stated barriers are IT staff, software, coordination and process change [11], which a sanction does not itself supply. In the full text of its 2024 and 2025 reports, the Commission does not name MConnect, whereas in North Macedonia data exchange sits in a Reform Agenda payment condition [12].

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a legal duty on bodies to use the exchange platform, backed by a personal sanction, could be written into comparable law in North Macedonia. Conditional and untested, because the evidence stops at the text of the law, a sanction does not supply the IT staff, software and process change North Macedonia names as barriers, and Moldova's connection count cannot be compared with North Macedonia's.

### CMP-0005: Serbia

**Comparator relevance: Medium.** Serbia matches on the procedural duty, the exchange system and the ex officio problem. It differs in that connection is widening and the evidence is about citizens' experience, not whether institutions use the platform. Commission wording on fragmented responsibilities [13] is in the unmatched queue and is not relied on.

**Problem addressed.** The Ministry describes citizens acting as couriers between administrations, collecting certificates from several counters. WeBER reports that the once-only principle is not applied consistently, with around 53 to 54% of surveyed citizens saying they had to submit documents the state already held the last time they requested a service. Two Commission sentences, on fragmented responsibilities and on aligning sector law with the procedure law [13][14], bear on this point but are in the unmatched queue, so they stay unused and are listed for the analyst.

**Reform or mechanism used.** The Law on General Administrative Procedure of 2016 obliges administrations to exchange data and documents from official records among themselves, and introduced the once-only principle. The e-ZUP system was established in June 2017 on the basis of that law and is now the Government Service Bus. An improved Bus was launched in December 2023, and the Commission says an interoperability framework is in place.

**How it was implemented.** The Commission states in 2024 and 2025 that more databases are connected to the Bus, with no number and no volume of use. The Ministry reports more than 170 million data exchanged in six years, and on an undated page 18 databases of eight institutions. WeBER says its research confirmed the once-only principle is implemented in a sample of seven services, except property registration, where it is unclear whether it is or can be applied. The report says points come from observing seven sample services, but it does not say what was observed for its 3/3 score, and it does not name the Bus or eZUP.

**Evidence of results.** The Ministry claims nearly 500,000 citizen hours and about RSD 20 million saved in the first year, and describes eEnrolment as removing paper documents, with no method given. WeBER's February 2025 survey (N = 1006) found around 53 to 54% had to submit documents already held by the state at their last request, while 68% agreed in general terms that they are not required to, and WeBER scores citizen experience 0.5/2. No source text searched gives use of the Bus by institution over time. As a proxy for data reuse, the Regional Cooperation Council scores Serbia's pre-filled forms at 61 in 2023 and 62 in 2025, against a regional average of 65 and an EU average of 71, and says this indicates potential to enhance data reuse [15][16][17]. The index does not name the Bus, and no cause is inferred.

**Limitations of the evidence.** The savings are a Ministry self-report with no comparison group, and they describe a period before the run's window (flagged out_of_window). The survey measures citizen perception at one date, is not linked to any database or service, and sits against the 3/3 score on sampled services. That score is an implementation indicator with no basis stated in this report; on the same indicator the Montenegrin report gives points because document lists are published [18], so it is not shown to rest on observed exchange. The 170 million figure is a volume of exchange and is treated here as implementation, although second-pass classification noted it as outcome. The survey items are listed under results although second-pass classification noted them as implementation, because WeBER itself labels E 1.14 as outcomes and impact. WeBER is EU-funded with the Ministry named as a project associate. The Ministry's 2023 statement that buying real estate is now done automatically [19] may conflict with WeBER on property registration [20], or they may concern different steps; this is not settled. The pre-filled forms score is from a source rated partly independent; the 2025 value comes from desk research, so the method may differ from 2023. The same index scores North Macedonia at 73 and 75 [21], so it may not track use of an exchange platform. Claims of absence in this case rest on a full-text search of the eight Serbian source texts held for the Bus, e-ZUP and the system's other name, and on the extracted items classified to this issue.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Serbia does not show what raises institutional use, because no source text searched measures it. It suggests that a legal duty and a live exchange system, with more databases connected, may sit alongside about half of surveyed citizens reporting they were still asked for documents. If so, this would be relevant to North Macedonia's conflict between telling the screening that once-only is applied and its Reform Agenda saying institutions do not gather evidence ex officio [22][23]. The lesson goes no further than this: connection and a review of sampled services may not settle whether evidence is obtained ex officio.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** The mechanism of a procedural duty to exchange data, with a central system built on it, matches North Macedonia's own Law on General Administrative Procedure and platform. WeBER's measures, a citizen survey on documents resubmitted alongside a review of sampled services, could be applied wherever the same monitor runs; the Serbian and Montenegrin reports share the indicators.

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** North Macedonia's stated problem is institutions not using the platform, while Serbia's evidence is on the citizen side and does not name the platform. The undated 18 databases of eight institutions cannot be set against North Macedonia's 58 connected and 33 active institutions, because they count different things. No source read connects Serbia's position to its accession stage or to an EU payment condition on data exchange, so that difference is not tested.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a procedural duty with a central exchange system matches North Macedonia's own law and platform, and the monitor's paired measures (citizen survey and sampled-service review) could be used to test whether evidence is obtained ex officio. Conditional, because Serbia's evidence is on the citizen side and does not name the platform, so it shows no route to higher institutional use.

### CMP-0006: Montenegro

**Comparator relevance: Medium.** Montenegro matches North Macedonia on stated institutional obstacles [11], and on a platform where access and active exchange are counted [24][25]. Commission wording on once-only has no match on the North Macedonian side, where the term appears only in the country's own screening presentation [22], although its Reform Agenda says institutions do not gather evidence ex officio [23]. It differs in a smaller count of institutions, a platform reported as under development in 2025 and established in 2026, no evidence of underuse by connected institutions, and resources appearing through staffing of the inspection, not IT staff in institutions.

**Problem addressed.** The Commission says in 2024 and 2025 that the Law on Administrative Procedure is not consistently implemented, notably on once-only and electronic communication. In 2025 it adds that integrating all public registers into the exchange system is incomplete; in 2024 it recommended working towards interoperability of registers through the Reform Agenda. The Ministry names insufficient data available through web services and slow settling of legal and organisational preconditions as the obstacles to full interoperability. Its official also says some public bodies are not meeting their obligations under the Law on Electronic Administration, and citizens contact institutions one by one.

**Reform or mechanism used.** The Ministry of Public Administration established JSERP, a single system for electronic data exchange between state bodies, and a Metaregister. A new X-Road platform has since been established, according to a government text of 2026. The Law on Electronic Government, the once-only principle and the Reform Agenda sit around it, and expanding JSERP to connect all registers is proposed in the 2026 to 2029 open government plan.

**How it was implemented.** Government reporting gives 21 institutions with access in both the 2024 report and the 2026 text, which is marked as a proposal, while active register pairs are reported as 18 and then 37, and 8 million queries in 2024. The share of catalogue e-services that can draw on more than one register in one query was 0 in 2023 and 2024, against a target of 10% by 2024 [26]. The Commission reports some progress on data exchange in 2024 and incomplete register integration in 2025. Strengthening the information society inspection was not achieved, which an official puts down to delays in the staffing plan.

**Evidence of results.** The Ministry claims that JSERP has significantly improved electronic service delivery, with no figure. The Institute Alternative survey of February 2025 (1010 citizens) found 53.6% aware they need not provide documents the state holds and 19.9% reporting once-only in practice, and scores citizen experience 0/2. These concern the once-only principle, and no source text searched links them to JSERP. As a proxy for data reuse, the Regional Cooperation Council scores Montenegro's pre-filled forms at 24 in 2023 and 49 in 2025, against a regional average of 65 and an EU average of 71, and describes this as weaker than the regional average [27][28][17]. The index does not name JSERP, and no cause is inferred.

**Limitations of the evidence.** All direct figures on platform use are government self-reports, machine translated and capped at Medium; the implementation report and the second contribution to the Commission report are not legal texts, so their use is outside LIM-0029 as worded, for the analyst to confirm at G6b. The 2026 text is marked as a proposal (PREDLOG), and an unchanged 21 alongside the Metaregister jump may be a figure carried forward. The only indicator of data reuse from outside national government is the pre-filled forms score, from a source rated partly independent: it is not a measure of platform use, the 2025 value comes from desk research so the method may differ from 2023, and the same index scores North Macedonia at 73 and 75 [21], so it may not track use of an exchange platform. The base of the 19.9% is unclear, and the 3/3 score for practice rests on published lists of documents, not on observed exchange [18]. The survey items are listed under results although second-pass classification noted them as implementation, because the source labels E 1.14 as outcomes and impact. Institute Alternative's report names the Ministry, which established JSERP, as a project associate, and its event report names the Ministry as a co-funder. The Metaregister figures of 45, 170+ and 265 may count different things and are not a trend. The SIGMA finding on special laws reaches us second-hand, since SIGMA 2024 was blocked (LIM-0028). A major cybersecurity crisis delayed digital services, which further clouds any before and after reading; sources conflict on its date, 2023 in the Commission's 2025 report [29] and 2022 in its 2024 report [30] and the open government plan [31]. Claims of absence in this case rest on a full-text search of the Montenegrin source texts held for JSERP, X-Road, the Metaregister and electronic data exchange, and on the extracted items classified to this issue.

**Practical lesson for North Macedonia (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Montenegro gives two reported points on use, not a series: active register pairs of 18 and then 37 while institutions with access stay at 21, on unverified government figures, the later from a proposal text. No source text searched says why, although a new X-Road platform is reported in between. Its stated obstacles, data not available through web services and slow settling of legal and organisational preconditions, resemble North Macedonia's need to digitalise registers, change processes and integrate software [11]. An official reports that strengthening the inspection was not achieved, and no extracted item shows whether it has acted on data exchange, so the case says nothing on inspection as a lever. If the figures hold, the case suggests that counts of connected institutions and of active exchanges may move separately.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** The underlying mechanism, a central exchange system with a catalogue of registers and a count of active pairs, is close to North Macedonia's platform and could be measured the same way. The stated preconditions, data available through web services and settled legal and organisational arrangements, are institutional rather than specific to Montenegro.

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Montenegro's 21 institutions have access to a platform reported as under development in the April 2025 report and as established in the September 2026 text, whereas North Macedonia's audit refers to connecting around 1,300 institutions [32] and its platform has been in place since 2015 [25], so scale and starting point differ. The active register pair is not the same measure as North Macedonia's active institution [24]. Both countries have a Reform Agenda, but Montenegro's is cited only in a Commission recommendation, and no source says how it affected use.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: counting active register pairs alongside connected institutions, and treating web-service availability and settled legal and organisational arrangements as preconditions, could apply to North Macedonia's platform. Conditional, because the figures are two unverified government points, scale and starting point differ, and the active pair is not the same measure as North Macedonia's active institution.

## Lessons across the cases (agent synthesis)

Three points recur. First, a connection count does not show regular use. North Macedonia's own figures separate connected from active institutions [10], machine translated), and Montenegro's government reports active register pairs rising from 18 to 37 while institutions with access stayed at 21 [33][34], both machine translated). Second, a legal duty, even with sanctions, may sit alongside continued underuse or continued requests for documents: Moldova added fines and disqualification from 18 January 2021 with no evidence on use afterwards [35][36], and in Serbia about half of surveyed citizens still reported submitting documents the state holds, under a duty dating from 2016 [37]. Third, the stated obstacles are institutional: Montenegro names data not available through web services and slow legal and organisational preparation, which resemble North Macedonia's own reasons of IT staff, coordination, and register, process and software changes [11], machine translated). A sanction does not supply any of these.

## Draft policy options (agent synthesis, internal draft)

These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (partly addressed) and on comparator cases validated at G6b, all of Medium relevance, and each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and none is shown by the evidence to raise use.

**Option 1: Measure use and ex officio evidence, not only connection.**
- *Gap addressed:* regular use of the platform by the institutions obliged to use it, so that evidence is obtained ex officio [38][23].
- *Mechanism targeted:* coordination failure (MEC-08) and the confirmed conflict between the country's statement that once-only is applied and its Reform Agenda's statement that institutions do not gather evidence ex officio [22][23].
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro counts active register pairs as well as institutions with access [34], and Serbia's civil society monitor pairs a citizen survey on documents resubmitted with a review of sampled services [39]. A source-stated point from the Serbian monitor, that compliance with the procedural duty needs monitoring, carries an item-level transferability judgement of conditional [40]. Transferability conditional: the measures are institutional and could be applied in North Macedonia, but none of the cases shows that measuring raised use.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* an agreed definition of active use per institution and per register, and a body that collects it regularly; North Macedonia's own figures for 2025 differ (33 and 37 active institutions), which shows the need [10][24], both machine translated).
- *Main risks:* a new indicator that is reported but not acted on; government self-reporting without independent checks.
- *Who would need to act:* the Ministry of Digital Transformation, which runs the platform and leads digitalisation projects [41][42], with the institutions obliged to use it. The body that oversees the Law on General Administrative Procedure is not identified in the evidence.

**Option 2: Address the institutional preconditions before relying on enforcement.**
- *Gap addressed:* as in Option 1.
- *Mechanism targeted:* coordination failure (MEC-08) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* Montenegro's stated obstacles of data not available through web services and unsettled legal and organisational arrangements (CMP-0006), and Moldova's case, where a sanction was added without evidence that it supplied capacity or raised use [43]. Transferability conditional: the obstacles are institutional and resemble North Macedonia's, but scale and starting point differ, and the Montenegrin figures are unverified. An item-level judgement on a Montenegrin source, that capacity must be tackled along with the technology, is also conditional and rests on a source rated Low [44].
- *Conditions and preconditions:* a costed plan for connecting the obliged institutions, which the State Audit Office found missing for about 1,300 institutions [32], machine translated), and filled IT posts.
- *Main risks:* the plan is drafted but not funded; staff outflow continues.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and the Ministry of Digital Transformation, with the institutions that hold the registers.

**Option 3: Consider an enforcement mechanism for the duty to use the platform, as an untested option.**
- *Gap addressed:* as in Option 1. North Macedonia already has a legal duty to use the platform [45], and its 2023 strategy says there is no accountability without an ICT inspection competence [8]; both are machine translated.
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04), which is not among the mechanisms recorded for this issue, so this option goes beyond the validated issue definition and is offered for discussion only.
- *Comparator evidence:* Moldova's fines and temporary disqualification for refusing data or asking for documents available on the platform [35]. Transferability conditional and untested: no source shows that the sanctions were applied or that they raised use.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* Options 1 and 2 in place, so that a duty can be met and breaches can be seen.
- *Main risks:* sanctioning institutions that lack the staff and software to comply; enforcement on paper only.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and the Assembly for any change to the law, and a body given the inspection competence.
