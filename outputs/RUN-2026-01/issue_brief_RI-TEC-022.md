# Issue brief: Cybersecurity legal and strategic framework incomplete

RI-TEC-022 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Network and information security law is not fully aligned with the NIS2 Directive and the Cybersecurity Act, the national cybersecurity strategy is late or fragmented in implementation, and responsibilities are split without effective coordination. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align its network and information security law with the NIS2 Directive and the Cybersecurity Act, to adopt and implement a cybersecurity strategy, and to improve coordination, and the Reform Agenda ties payments to the law and strategy, an operational competent authority and the NIS2 disclosure and crisis frameworks [1][2][3][4][5][6]. After a 2019 draft stalled, the Government adopted the Cyber Security Strategy 2025-2028 in January 2025 and Parliament adopted the law in June 2025, both assessed as adopted [7][8][9]. The Commission found the law sufficient for the Reform Agenda step but not fully aligned with the acquis [10][11]. The 15 by-laws, entity lists, disclosure and crisis frameworks and working coordination are not shown in place, and the State Audit Office found the National Cyber Security Council met only once [12][13]. No outcome has been assessed. The gap is assessed as partly_addressed. Priority is High, direction of travel is improving and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The Commission found the 2025 law sufficient for the Reform Agenda step, which required alignment of primary legislation only, yet says it does not fully align with the acquis and still asks the country to continue aligning; the law, strategy and competent authority are proposed as adopted and the by-laws and disclosure and crisis frameworks as proposed, and these statuses are themselves proposals for review. What remains is the outstanding NIS2 provisions and Cybersecurity Act certification alignment (last raised before the law passed), the 15 by-laws legally due within six months but not shown as adopted, the entity lists, the disclosure and crisis frameworks, working coordination, and application to the sectors the law defers until accession. The law's text is machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, and the regulator's one-line statement that the strategy is applied is government reporting without specifics, so neither is treated as closure.

**Direction of travel.** Commission items moved from no further progress on the 2019 draft law in 2021, through repeated calls to adopt the law and strategy in 2022, 2023 and 2024, to adoption of the strategy in January 2025 and the law in June 2025, which the 2025 report counts as some progress. The chapter scale (limited progress 2021 to 2024, some progress 2025) is context only and was not decisive, and the improvement lies in the legal and strategic framework, since no item shows better coordination after the 2023 call, the 2024 audit finding of a Council that met once and the 2025 strategy's own description of coordination as critical. A February 2022 ITU item lists the law as existing while the Commission's 2022 report says it was still a draft, and later Commission and audit items support the draft reading; the differing dates for the Council (2019 or 2020) and the action plan's end (2027 or 2028) do not affect this value.

**Priority.** The law and strategy, an operational competent authority and the NIS2 frameworks are each Reform Agenda payment steps, and NIS2 or cybersecurity legislation recurs in the Commission's key recommendations from 2022 to 2025. The issue is the legal base on which the rest of the cybersecurity theme rests. The rating rests on these EU requirements and not on the large number of national plan and audit items.

**Tractability.** Much of what remains is drafting that the Ministry controls, and adoption followed within a year of the Reform Agenda step, which favours progress; against this, the first draft law stalled from 2019 to 2025, the Council barely functioned and no item shows the by-laws adopted after their deadline. Coordination needs many bodies, the strategy itself names political support and budget as key risks, and one reporting obligation to ENISA cannot be met outside the EU. Tractability is therefore higher for legal alignment than for coordination.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has moved from adoption to completeness of alignment. In 2021 it records no progress on the 2019 draft law and calls for more effort on a fragmented strategy [7][14]. From 2022 to 2024 it repeatedly asks North Macedonia to adopt the law, aligned with NIS and then NIS2, and a new cybersecurity strategy, adding in 2023 a call for better coordination and a stronger National Cybersecurity Council [15][16][17][3][1]. The 2024 Reform Agenda turns these into payment conditions [4]. In 2025, after adoption, the Commission records some progress but asks the country to continue aligning with NIS2 and the Cybersecurity Act [18][19][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the 15 by-laws, the entity lists and the response team and network required by the 2025 law were adopted or set up by their deadlines is not shown, and official gazette entries or ministry reports would settle it [29][30][44].
- The regulator states that the strategy is applied, but this is government reporting without specifics, and the half-yearly implementation reports the strategy promises would show what has been delivered [33][45].
- Confirmed conflicts remain on whether the law existed in February 2022, on whether the Council was formed in 2019 or July 2020, and on whether the action plan runs to 2027 or 2028, and the official gazette and Government decisions would settle them [20][21][22][23][24][25].
- The law's text and the audit findings are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, and no evidence shows the competent authority staffed or performing supervisory checks, which later Commission Reform Agenda assessments would show [32][5].
