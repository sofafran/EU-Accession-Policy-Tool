# Issue brief: Digital communication with courts and justice system connections incomplete

RI-TEC-010 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Digital communication with courts is incomplete, or justice system IT does not connect between institutions. **Problem mechanisms:** Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has asked North Macedonia to digitalise its justice system, including digital communication with courts, videoconferencing for hearings and connections between justice IT systems [1][2][3]. The Commission's 2026 Rule of Law report describes the digital interconnection of the Administrative Court, the Higher Administrative Court and other public bodies as a key advancement that enables electronic exchange of data and documents [4]. Its implementation status is validated as implemented, but this rests on one statement with no usage data, and no outcome is recorded, so no result can be claimed. The Luris software, which North Macedonia said in 2023 needed connecting between the Ministry of Justice and the Public Prosecutor's Office, is at insufficient_evidence [5]. The validated unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium, a tentative rating.

**What remains.** The administrative courts' interconnection is proposed as implemented and the Commission calls it a key advancement, but no status is recorded for the judicial IT strategies and council, and Luris is at insufficient evidence; these statuses are themselves proposals. What remains is digital communication with courts generally, videoconferencing for hearings, and connections between other justice institutions, including the Ministry of Justice and the prosecution.

**Direction of travel.** Commission calls for digital court communication and connected IT recur in 2020, 2023, 2024 and 2025, with identical wording in the last two, and the single 2026 item on the administrative courts cannot alone show a change of direction. Support is thin (eight items, none for 2021 or 2022, most flagged theme_doubtful), and no chapter scale was decisive.

**Priority.** The recommendation is repeated, including in the 2024 and 2025 reports, within the important Chapter 23 area, but digital communication is a supporting part of judicial reform and no benchmark or payment condition on it appears in the input. Evidence is thin and most items are flagged theme_doubtful.

**Tractability.** One interconnection shows that progress is possible, but several independent justice institutions must act together, and the Commission points to weak coordination. The evidence on actors and resources is thin, so this rating is tentative.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has narrowed over time. In 2020 it said that IT connections needed to be prioritised, including full digitalisation of cases and summoning, with training for judges and judicial staff [1]. In 2023 it said that digitalisation, including interoperability between IT systems, and coordination between the relevant authorities needed to improve [6]. In 2024 and 2025 it used identical wording, calling for more efforts to digitalise the justice system, in particular communication with courts and videoconferencing for court hearings [2][3]. No Commission item from 2021 or 2022 is in the evidence.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the administrative courts' interconnection is used and has improved coordination is not settled, because the only evidence is one statement without usage data [4]; system usage figures or case processing data would settle it.
- Whether the Luris software works and was connected between the Ministry of Justice and the Public Prosecutor's Office is at insufficient_evidence [5]; a later Commission or government statement on its status would settle it.
- Progress on digital communication with courts generally and on videoconferencing for hearings is not shown by any intervention item, so government or Commission reporting on these specific measures is needed.
- The evidence base is thin, with eight items, none for 2021 or 2022 and most flagged theme_doubtful, so further Commission or justice sector sources would strengthen the direction of travel judgement.
