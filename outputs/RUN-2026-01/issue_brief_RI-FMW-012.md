# Issue brief: Supplementary pension rights of mobile workers

RI-FMW-012 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National law is not aligned with the EU directives on supplementary pension rights, such as the acquisition and preservation of rights for workers who move between Member States. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission's 2025 screening report finds North Macedonia's legislation not aligned with Directive 98/49/EC or Directive 2014/50/EU on supplementary pension rights, apart from cross-border membership and equal treatment of incoming and outgoing EU workers in voluntary pension funds [1][2]. Those two elements are provided in national law but enter into force only on the date of accession [3]. The country states that the Law on Voluntary Fully Funded Pension Insurance sets the legal framework for the voluntary scheme; the law is assessed as adopted, and no evidence of its application is given [4]. The screening report also states that the responsible unit has only one person working in it and calls for thorough training of the teams in charge [5][6]. What remains is alignment with both directives beyond those two elements. The validated assessment is open, with priority Low, direction of travel insufficient_evidence and tractability Medium.

**Direction of travel.** All items come from one report, the 2025 screening report, so there is no sequence to read and the chapter scale cannot help.

**Priority.** The requirement is raised only in the screening report and in no key recommendation, and supplementary pensions are peripheral to the chapter compared with its main strands. It remains a stated acquis gap, so Low does not mean it can wait past accession.

**Tractability.** Legal alignment is within national control and part of the framework is already drafted into law. However, the responsible unit is reported to have only one person and the Commission calls for thorough training.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement cannot be traced over time, because every Commission item on this issue comes from one document, the February 2025 screening report. There the Commission states that the legislation is not aligned with Directive 98/49/EC or Directive 2014/50/EU, and that the country is not aligned with most of the supplementary pensions acquis apart from cross-border membership and equal treatment in voluntary funds [1][2]. It also describes features of the current scheme, such as access for people aged 15 to 70, keeping an occupational account after employment ends and limits on early withdrawal, and recommends thorough training [7][8][9][6]. No item from the annual reports of 2020 to 2025 is recorded for this issue.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Direction of travel cannot be read from a single report, and the Commission's next assessment of supplementary pensions would show whether alignment is moving [1].
- No source reports a plan, draft law or timetable for aligning with the two directives, and a government plan or draft amendment would show whether the gap is being addressed [2].
- No evidence shows how the Law on Voluntary Fully Funded Pension Insurance is applied, for example to workers leaving or joining occupational funds, and supervisory or fund data would settle this [4].
- The staffing constraint rests on one Medium-confidence statement, and current staffing figures for the responsible unit would confirm whether it remains a constraint [5].
