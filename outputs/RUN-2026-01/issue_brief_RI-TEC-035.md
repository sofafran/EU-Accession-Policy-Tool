# Issue brief: Digital tools in company law not fully aligned

RI-TEC-035 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0022), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0020) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Company law is not fully aligned with EU rules on the use of digital tools and processes, such as online formation, electronic filing and director checks. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to align further with Directive (EU) 2019/1151 on the use of digital tools and processes in company law, including scrutiny of a company's object and verification of the appointment and disqualification of directors [1][2][3]. The country cites the Law on Trade Companies and the Law on Central Registry as regulating this area, both assessed as adopted [4]. The Commission states that company registration and filing can be carried out fully online through the Central Registry's one-stop shop, which is assessed as in_practice, and it finds a high level of alignment with disclosure requirements and digital tools [5][6][7]. No outcome has been assessed. What remains is the narrower set of legal amendments, with no draft reported. The gap is assessed as partly_addressed. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is persistent and tractability is High.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

| Judgement | Value |
|---|---|

| Direction of travel | persistent |
| Priority | Medium |
| Tractability | High |

**What remains.** Fully online company registration is in practice and the Commission finds high alignment, on a status that is itself a proposal. What remains is further alignment with Directive (EU) 2019/1151, including scrutiny of a company's object and verification of directors' appointment and disqualification. The country's statement that it is aligned and the Commission's statement that further alignment is necessary are a confirmed contradiction, reported here and not resolved.

**Direction of travel.** The alignment ask on digital tools in company law recurs in near-identical wording in 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, so the default applies; the chapter scale is context only and was not decisive. The 2025 screening finding of a high level of alignment shows the remaining ask is narrow, but it is not evidence of change over time.

**Priority.** It appears in the key recommendations of three reports, but always bundled with other company law items, and digital tools are a narrow strand on which the Commission already finds high alignment. It is therefore a repeated EU requirement that is not central to the chapter, and it is not a benchmark or payment condition in the input.

**Tractability.** The remaining points are specific legal amendments in a field with few actors and a working one-stop-shop registry, and they depend on no other institution's roll-out. The ask has nonetheless stood since 2021 with no reported draft, which is why the rating depends on the small scope and not on past momentum.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording is essentially unchanged. In 2021 and 2022 it states that further alignment is necessary with the 2019 acquis on digital tools and cross-border operations, and from 2023 to November 2025 the key recommendations ask for alignment on the use of digital tools and processes in company law, always bundled with cross-border operations and, in some years, shareholder engagement [8][9][10][11][2]. The ask becomes more specific from 2024, naming scrutiny of the company's object and checks on directors [12][1]. The February 2025 screening report adds that alignment is already high, which narrows the remaining ask but does not show change over time [7].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- North Macedonia states it is aligned with the acquis on digital tools in company law while the Commission says further alignment is necessary, and this confirmed conflict needs a provision-by-provision comparison with Directive (EU) 2019/1151 to settle it [13][3].
- No source reports a draft amendment on object scrutiny or director checks, so a government legislative plan or draft law would show whether work has started [1].
- Free online access to basic company information rests on the country's own presentation, and an independent check of the registry would be needed to confirm it is in practice [14].
