# Issue brief: Personal data protection in electoral processes weak

RI-TEC-021 · North Macedonia · **Status: Draft.** Prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7. It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The processing of personal data in elections, including biometric data and campaign use, lacks oversight and was introduced without assessing compliance or consulting the data protection authority. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission states that data protection in the electoral sector needs improvement, that there is no assessment of whether the Electoral Code is harmonised with the data protection law, and that oversight of personal data used in election campaigns is inadequate [1][2][3]. Amendments supplementing the provisions on biometric voter identification were adopted in April 2021 [4], and the Commission notes that the Electoral Code brought in biometric data without prior consultation of the Personal Data Protection Agency [5][6]. The amendments are assessed as adopted, and no item shows biometric identification used at an election. The Commission reports that the State Election Commission addressed some irregularities identified in supervision [7][8], but this does not reach the assessment of the Code or campaign data oversight. No evidence after 2023 addresses the issue. The validated judgements are priority Low, direction of travel insufficient_evidence, unresolved gap open and tractability Medium.

**What remains.** The latest requirement, from 2023, is unmet on the record: no intervention is recorded as responding and the Electoral Code's biometric provisions have a proposed status of adopted, and these statuses are themselves proposals for G6. What remains is an assessment of the Code against data protection law and proper oversight of personal data used in campaigns. The State Election Commission addressed some irregularities, but that does not reach these two points, and this finding rests on evidence no newer than 2023.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission used the same wording in three documents, its 2022 and 2023 reports and the 2023 screening report, but no item after 2023 addresses the issue, so the record shows neither that it continues nor that it has eased. The three-report default for persistent was therefore not applied, because the repeats span only two years and the 2024 and 2025 reports are silent. The chapter scale was not decisive.

**Priority.** The Commission raised it only in 2022 and 2023, it is not in the main findings, and it is peripheral to the data theme.

**Tractability.** Few actors are needed and an assessment of the Code is a limited task, and the State Election Commission has already acted on some irregularities. Oversight of campaign data and any change to the Electoral Code are likely to be harder, and the evidence on this is thin.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission first raised the issue in its 2022 report, stating that several improvements were needed in data protection in the electoral sector [1]. The 2023 screening report and the 2023 report repeated this in almost the same words and added specific points: no assessment of the Code against data protection law, the need to ensure respect for privacy, and inadequate oversight of campaign data [9][2][10][11][3]. The finding on the missing prior consultation appears in both years [12][13]. No Commission item from 2024 or 2025 addresses the issue, so the requirement has not been restated since 2023.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The direction of travel is insufficient_evidence because the 2024 and 2025 Commission reports are silent, and a later Commission or Personal Data Protection Agency assessment would show whether the problem continues or has eased [11].
- Whether any assessment of the Electoral Code against the data protection law has since been carried out is not known, and a published assessment or Agency opinion would settle it [2].
- The record does not show whether oversight of personal data in election campaigns has improved, and Agency supervision reports covering recent elections would be needed [3][8].
