# Issue brief: Online access for service providers and regulated professions incomplete

RI-TEC-007 · North Macedonia · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0012), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0007) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0011). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The point of single contact does not give service providers full online information and procedures as the Services Directive requires, and there is no transparent electronic register of regulated professions and qualifications. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks North Macedonia to upgrade its point of single contact so that service providers have full online information and procedures under the Services Directive, especially on licences, and to set up a transparent electronic register of all regulated professions [1][2][3]. The Commission reports that the point of single contact was upgraded and reached 312 online services by September 2025, and the platform is assessed as implemented, not in practice, as no source gives evidence of use [4][5]. The Commission still finds it not fully in line with the acquis, and states that the list and register of regulated professions are yet to be put in place [6][7]. Whether the new Central Register portal has simplified procedures is insufficient_evidence [8]. The gap is assessed as partly_addressed. Priority is Medium, direction of travel is persistent and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** A contact point platform exists and is proposed as implemented, but the Commission finds it not fully in line with the acquis and the list and register still absent; these statuses are themselves proposals. Sources conflict on which body is the contact point (Central Register or uslugi.gov.mk), and the 238 (2023) and 124 (2024) service counts are not explained. What remains is full online information and procedures under the Services Directive, especially on licences, and a transparent electronic register of all regulated professions.

**Direction of travel.** The requirement that the point of single contact be upgraded to meet the Services Directive is unchanged in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and the register of regulated professions moves from unfinished data validation (2020, 2021) to 'yet to be put in place' (2024, 2025). The contact point itself did advance, from early stage in 2021 to a platform with 312 online services in 2025, so the two limbs of this issue point different ways. No Chapter 3 scale rating is linked to the issue, and the scale would be context only.

**Priority.** The point of single contact was a key recommendation only in 2021, 2022 and 2023, with the 2021 item flagged as doubtful for the theme, and later Commission items on it and the 2025 item on the register of professions are in body text, so the requirement is repeated. It is a sector portal under the Services Directive and is not central to the digital government theme, which is the same reading applied to taxpayer e-services (RI-TEC-005) and e-procurement (RI-TEC-006). Its importance to Chapter 3 is not the test for a technology issue, and no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition names it.

**Tractability.** The platform is in place and service numbers have been added, which favours progress on the contact point. Against that, sectoral laws must be aligned, the responsible body is unclear between sources, and a register of all professions needs input from many regulators.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has changed little in substance. On the register of regulated professions, it asks in 2020 and 2021 for data validation to be finalised, notes in 2022 that a transparent list is missing, and from 2024 states that the list and register are "yet to be put in place", adding in November 2025 a call for a transparent electronic register of all regulated professions [9][10][11][12][3]. On the point of single contact, it moves from asking for one to be established in 2021 to asking, in nearly identical words in 2023, 2024 and 2025, for www.uslugi.gov.mk to be upgraded to meet the Services Directive, with the November 2025 report adding licences and business procedures [13][14][1][15][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- In the same screening report the country says the Central Register is the point of single contact while the Commission names www.uslugi.gov.mk, and this confirmed conflict needs a statement of the legal designation to settle it [19][15].
- Sources give conflicting counts of online services for 2024 (264, 246 and 124) and the fall from 238 in 2023 is unexplained, so a consistent series with stated definitions would be needed [16][18][17][20].
- Whether the 2024-2025 action plan on the Services Directive and the business licence portal upgrade were completed is insufficient_evidence, and government reports on these measures would settle it [24][26].
- No source reports how far service providers actually use the online services, so usage data for the portal would be needed to judge results.
