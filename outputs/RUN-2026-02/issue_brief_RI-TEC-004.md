# Issue brief: Core e-government building blocks missing

RI-TEC-004 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Shared systems that every institution needs to work digitally, such as a single document management system, legal and technical arrangements for electronic archiving, a digital mailbox and shared administrative information systems, are missing or little used. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU's requirement under this issue centres on the civil service human resources information system: the Commission found national HR systems non-functional, and the Reform Agenda requires a human resource management information system for all central government institutions, with at least three modules operational, by December 2027 [1][2]. Moldova has a long-standing set of shared e-government platforms, including MCloud, MPass, MPay and MConnect, which the Reform Agenda records as already implemented, and a World Bank-supported project added platforms such as MDelivery, MPower and MDocs [3][4][5]. Some of these are shown in use: the Commission reported 42 more services integrated with MDelivery in 2023, and the permits system had issued permits through 16 of the 31 issuing institutions when audited [6][7]. The HR system has not followed: the earlier system failed through problems with its private provider, and its replacement was in incipient design in 2025 and still in development in 2026 [8][9][10]. Institutions not subordinate to the Government declined to use the permits system, and the eArhiva electronic archive is still a planned action [11][12]. Sources disagree on whether all state information systems are registered in the state register. What remains is a working HR management system and consistent use of shared systems by all institutions. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Most shared building blocks are implemented and in use, but the human resources management system, which the Reform Agenda requires with at least three modules by December 2027, is still in development, the permits system was used fully or partly by only 16 of 31 issuing institutions at the 2024 audit, and the eArhiva system is planned. Sources conflict on the register of state information systems: SIGMA says all resources and systems were registered, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed. What remains is a working human resources management system and consistent use of shared systems by all institutions, including those not subordinate to the Government.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is a cross-cutting e-government issue. The Commission found the civil service human resources information system not functional in 2023 and 2024, its replacement only in incipient design in 2025 and still in development in 2026. Shared platforms such as MCloud, MPass, MPay and MConnect were already in place before the period, as the Reform Agenda notes, so they show no change.

**Priority.** The human resources management system is a Reform Agenda payment condition and is reported in each year from 2023 to 2026, but it mainly serves civil service management rather than shared e-government services, and the Commission records the shared platforms as in place.

**Tractability.** Moldova has a long record of building shared platforms through the e-Governance Agency, and the human resources system has a Reform Agenda deadline. Against that, the earlier system failed through problems with its private provider, its replacement has moved slowly since 2023, and institutions not subordinate to the Government declined to use the permits system (machine translated).

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording concerns the civil service HR system rather than the shared platforms as a whole; the fact pack holds no Commission item on this issue from the 2022 Opinion. In 2023 the Commission reported that the national HR information system was not functional because of maintenance and updating problems with its private provider [8]. In 2024 it described civil service management as fragmented and understaffed, with non-functional national HR systems [1]. The 2025 Reform Agenda turned this into a payment condition, an HR management information system with at least three operational modules by December 2027, while recording MCloud, MPass, MPay and MConnect as platforms already in place [2][3]. The Commission's 2025 and 2026 reports then recorded the new system as only in incipient design and later as still in development [9][10].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the new HR management system will have three modules operational by December 2027 is open; the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step would settle it [2][10].
- SIGMA states that all state information systems are registered in the state register, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed; a follow-up audit of the register would settle the question [17][16].
- How widely institutions use the permits system after the 2024 agreement on its new version is not reported; current usage data across all issuing institutions would show it [19][7].
- The savings attributed to MPay rest on the e-Governance Agency's own account with no method given; an independent evaluation would settle it [13].
