# Issue brief: Data protection authority lacks independence and resources

RI-TEC-018 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The data protection supervisory authority depends on government for staffing and funding, has capacity below its tasks, and can be left without a director. **Problem mechanisms:** Lack of institutional independence (MEC-05); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to increase the capacity of its data protection supervisory authority, the National Centre for Personal Data Protection, especially its staff [1][2]. Moldova adopted Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, which entered into force in August 2026, widens the authority's mandate and creates a data protection inspector function [3][4]. Strengthening the authority's administrative capacity, especially its human resources, is set as an objective for 2026 but has not yet been carried out [5]. No increase in capacity is shown. The Commission found 39 staff for 45 posts in 2023, and the authority reports 36 staff at the end of 2025, nine short of its limit, with a staff turnover rate of 18% [6][7][8]. In 2026 the Commission found that the authority's human, technical and financial capacities had not grown with its expanded mandate [3]. In its own account, the authority reports pay below comparable supervisory bodies, too few staff in its surveillance and legal departments, and pressure, intimidation and abusive challenges against its staff without adequate safeguards [9][10][11]. What remains is to give the authority the staff, pay, budget and safeguards it needs to supervise independently under the new law. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The Commission's latest finding is that capacities remain limited while Law No. 195/2024 has widened the mandate, and the response aimed at capacity, strengthening the authority's administrative and human resources, has not yet been carried out. The authority itself reports pay below comparable supervisory bodies, staff overload and pressure on staff, and gives this as its own account. What remains is giving the National Centre for Personal Data Protection the staff, pay, budget and safeguards it needs to supervise independently under the new law.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is only part of the chapter. The 2023 report finds 39 staff for 45 posts with high turnover, the 2024 and 2025 reports call for more capacity, especially staff, and the 2026 report says the authority's human, technical and financial capacities have not grown with its expanded mandate. The authority's own 2025 report gives 36 staff at the end of 2025 and a turnover rate of 18%, which confirms the finding but is its own account.

**Priority.** An adequately resourced and independent supervisory authority is a core requirement of the EU data protection acquis, and the Commission records the shortfall in the 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports with no increase in capacity recorded, which meets the test for High without a benchmark. The 2025 Enlargement Communication's call for more resources and independence for data protection authorities applies to all enlargement countries and only supports this.

**Tractability.** The remedy is within the government's control, through the staffing limit, pay and budget, and Law No. 195/2024 was adopted. Against that, staff numbers fell from 39 in the 2023 report to 36 at the end of 2025 despite repeated recommendations, and the authority links turnover to pay below comparable bodies, which needs wider pay decisions.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has moved from description to a repeated recommendation and then to a finding tied to the new law. In 2021 it recorded that civil society and the media had criticised the draft data protection law for limiting access to information and widening the authority's powers [12]. The 2023 report described the authority's position: 39 staff for 45 authorised posts, the lowest since 2018, high turnover and difficulty attracting and retaining staff because of low salaries and the expertise required [6]. The 2024 report recommended that the authority's capacity be increased, especially its staffing, and the 2025 report repeated this, noting that the authority was examining a growing number of complaints [1][13][2]. The 2025 Enlargement Communication asked for additional and sustained resources and stronger independence for such bodies in all enlargement countries [14]. In 2026 the Commission linked the shortfall to Law No. 195/2024, finding that the law had significantly expanded the mandate while capacities remained limited [3]. The finding has been recorded in four successive reports with no increase in capacity, but it is not a benchmark or a Reform Agenda payment condition in the evidence [3].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the authority's staff numbers and pay rise to match its wider mandate would be shown by staffing and remuneration figures for 2026 and later [7][9].
- Whether data protection inspectors are appointed under Law No. 195/2024 and the law is applied in practice would be shown by appointment records and the authority's later activity reports [4].
- The authority's account of pressure and intimidation against its staff rests on its own report, and independent evidence, such as civil society monitoring or Commission findings on independence, would settle how serious it is [11].
- Whether the authority's budget grows with its mandate would be shown by the approved budgets after 2025 [16].
