# Issue brief: Limited taxpayer e-services

RI-TEC-005 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The tax administration offers a limited range of electronic services to taxpayers. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission's 2026 report states that it is essential for Moldova to further increase the digitalisation of tax services, and the Reform Agenda sets a target of 18 of 19 taxpayer services, or 90%, digitalised for businesses and citizens by December 2026 [1][2]. The digitalisation of State Tax Service services is planned, with at least two services to be digitalised by 2025 and two more in 2026, but no source reports which services have so far been digitalised, so the measure remains proposed [3][4]. The e-Invoice service on the Electronic Fiscal Services platform is in place and is assessed as implemented [5]. On use, the Government's own machine-translated indicator gives 64% of companies using electronic invoices against a target above 70%, a figure not independently corroborated, and SIGMA found that for fiscal year 2022 81% of businesses declared corporate income tax digitally but only 10% of citizens filed personal income tax returns electronically [6][7]. These figures describe uptake, not whether the services meet the EU requirement. The Reform Agenda names limited staff and time, and many taxpayers' preference for in-person service, as barriers [8][9]. What remains is completing the digitalisation of taxpayer services and raising their use, especially by citizens. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The Reform Agenda sets a target of 18 of 19 taxpayer services digitalised by December 2026 and the 2026 report asks for further digitalisation, while the Government's indicator gives 64% of companies using e-invoices against a target above 70%. SIGMA found that only 10% of citizens filed personal income tax returns electronically for 2022, and the Reform Agenda names limited staff and taxpayers' preference for in-person services as barriers. What remains is completing the digitalisation of taxpayer services and raising their use, especially by citizens.

**Direction of travel.** The 2023 and 2024 reports find that the tax administration improved its electronic services, and the 2025 and 2026 reports record good progress in Chapter 16 that includes the digitalisation of tax administration services. The 2026 report still finds it essential to digitalise tax services further. The Chapter 16 statements cover the whole chapter, so the issue's own items were decisive.

**Priority.** The digitalisation of taxpayer services is a Reform Agenda payment condition due by December 2026 and is repeated in the 2026 report, but it is a narrow strand of digital government on which the Commission records good progress in 2025 and 2026. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no key recommendation naming taxpayer e-services; the 2026 key recommendation on the tax administration concerns the State Tax Service's digital transformation programme and compliance risk management.

**Tractability.** The work lies with one agency, the Reform Agenda sets yearly targets with payments attached, and the Commission records improvement in every report from 2023 to 2026. Uptake depends partly on taxpayers' habits, which the Reform Agenda names as a barrier.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2022 Opinion contains no item on taxpayer e-services in the evidence. From 2023 the Commission's wording is a finding of improvement rather than a demand: the 2023 report states that the tax administration improved its services to taxpayers, including electronic tax returns, and the 2024 report that it improved its electronic services and promoted voluntary compliance [10][11]. The 2025 report and the 2026 report record good progress in Chapter 16 that includes the digitalisation of tax administration services, though these statements cover the whole chapter [12][13]. The Reform Agenda turned the matter into a payment condition with a numerical target for December 2026 [2]. The 2026 report then returns to a requirement, stating that further digitalisation of tax services is essential alongside an automated compliance risk management system [1]. No key recommendation names taxpayer e-services.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Which of the 19 taxpayer services have been digitalised is not reported, and a State Tax Service or Reform Agenda payment request report listing them would show whether the December 2026 target was met [2][3].
- The share of companies using electronic invoices comes only from the Government's own machine-translated indicator, and independent data, for example from the Commission or SIGMA, would be needed to confirm it [6].
- Whether electronic filing by citizens has risen from the 10% recorded for fiscal year 2022 is not known, and more recent filing statistics would settle it [7].
- Whether the staffing constraint named in the Reform Agenda has been eased is not recorded, and evidence on State Tax Service resources for digitalisation would be needed [8].
