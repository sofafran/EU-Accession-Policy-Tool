# Issue brief: Legal and organisational framework for joining EURES

RI-FMW-004 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The country has not set up the legal and organisational arrangements needed to join the European network of employment services, such as a system for appointing EURES members and partners and access for private employment agencies. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to prepare to join the European Network of Employment Services (EURES) upon accession, and in 2025 added that this should include reinforcing the Public Employment Service's IT infrastructure [1][2]. Moldova has created a Labour Migration and EURES Department in the National Employment Agency, adopted Government Decision No. 617/2024 and Government Decision No. 607/2025 setting the national legal framework, designated the Agency as National Coordination Office and approved a procedure for admitting EURES members and partners [3][4][5][6][7]. The Commission also reports ten territorial EURES members, implementing acts on data collection and monitoring, and technical standards for matching vacancies and CVs with the EURES portal [8][9][10]. These are preparatory steps: no source reports any organisation admitted under the new procedure, and the Government itself describes its decisions as only partial alignment with the EURES Regulation [4][11]. What remains, on the Government's own account, is better data exchange between the Agency and the European platform and the completion of legislative alignment, with an order on interoperability planned for December 2027 [12][13]. There is no outcome evidence. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The Commission asked in 2023, 2024 and 2025 for the preparatory work for EURES to continue, and in 2026 it records the legal framework, coordination office and admission procedure as in place. The Government describes its 2024 and 2025 Decisions as only partial transposition of the EURES Regulation, says data exchange with the European platform and legislative alignment still need to be completed, and lists further agency orders, including on interoperability with EURES, as planned. What remains is completing legal alignment with the EURES Regulation and the technical connection and data exchange with the EURES platform, ahead of membership on accession.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 2 rating covers more than this issue and is context only. The 2023 report and Analytical Report found the preparatory work for EURES not completed, the 2024 report recorded its start with an action plan and the National Employment Agency designated as coordination body, and the 2025 report recorded a EURES department and the September 2025 Government Decision setting the national legal framework. The 2026 report adds the National Coordination Office, the procedure for admitting members and partners, 10 territorial EURES members, implementing acts on data and monitoring and technical standards for matching with the EURES portal.

**Priority.** Preparing to join EURES is a key recommendation in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports, and the 2026 key recommendation asks for the measures, capacity and IT systems needed for full participation in EURES, while the Commission states that the previous year's recommendations remain valid. The requirement therefore runs through four reports and joining EURES is central to the chapter, although the Commission records the main legal and organisational steps as taken in 2026.

**Tractability.** The work lies mainly with one body, the National Employment Agency, and uses Government Decisions and agency orders; the steps planned in 2024 and 2025 were reported as taken by the Commission in the following report. The remaining technical connection depends on the employment service's IT capacity, which the 2025 recommendation asks Moldova to reinforce.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The fact pack holds no Commission item on EURES from the 2022 Opinion. In February 2023 the Analytical Report found that Moldova had not completed the preparatory work needed for access to EURES, and the 2023 report repeated that finding and asked Moldova to undertake that work for joining upon accession [14][15][1]. In 2024 the Commission recorded limited progress, noted an action plan and the designation of the Agency as coordination body, and asked Moldova to continue the preparatory work [16][17][18]. The 2025 recommendation kept the same wording but added the reinforcement of the employment service's IT infrastructure [2]. By 2026 the Commission's language had moved from asking for preparation to recording it: Moldova "has taken a series of measures to prepare for joining the network", with the legal framework and the coordination office cited as the basis for good progress [19][20]. The Commission's 2026 key recommendation asks for the measures, capacity and IT systems needed for full participation in EURES [21].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether any organisation has been admitted as a EURES member or partner under the November 2025 procedure is unknown; Agency records of admissions or the Commission's next report would settle it [24].
- Whether the Agency's information system can exchange vacancy and CV data with the EURES portal is not shown; evidence of a working technical connection, or the planned interoperability order, would settle it [10][13].
- Which provisions of the EURES Regulation remain to be transposed is not set out; a transposition table or the Government's next accession programme report would show the remaining gap in legal alignment [11][12].
- The findings of the Agency's internal audit of how the EURES network is being implemented are not reported; publication of the audit would show whether the arrangements work as intended [25].
