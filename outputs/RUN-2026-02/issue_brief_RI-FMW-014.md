# Issue brief: Access and rights of third-country workers

RI-FMW-014 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Workers from outside the EU face barriers to accessing the labour market, or their rights after admission are not protected or monitored. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

This issue is not raised as a Chapter 2 requirement in EU material. The Commission records the residence rules and counts residence permits for work, such as 2,991 issued in 2023, and its 2023 call to register foreigners and give them access to services sits under Chapter 24 and does not concern employment [1][2][3]. Moldova grants displaced persons from Ukraine temporary protection with the right to work, its employment agency signed cooperation agreements with partner organisations on refugee employment, and a draft law on the admission, stay and supervision of foreigners would replace Law No. 200/2010 [4][5][6]. The agency reports that 450 displaced jobseekers registered and 164 found work from January to September 2025, figures not corroborated by another source [7]. UNHCR finds that only 29 percent of working-age refugees are in paid work, with language gaps, non-recognition of diplomas and scarce childcare as barriers, and that formal employment services remain underused [8][9][10]. Research describes the procedure for employing foreigners as particularly bureaucratic, and foreigners residing for work or study cannot use employment services or register as unemployed [11][12]. Consultation comments on the draft law note that work permits are tied to one employer and that labour inspection is not separated from immigration control [13][14]. The draft law would enter into force only on 1 June 2027 [15]. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** No EU item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files states a Chapter 2 requirement on third-country workers, so the gap is judged on the issue's own items. The draft law on admission of foreigners and the draft amendment extending employment services to more categories of foreigners are proposals, and consultation comments on the draft law point to work permits tied to one employer and no clear separation between labour inspection and immigration control, while refugees still face language, recognition and childcare barriers. What remains is a law in force that eases third-country workers' access to the labour market and protects their rights after admission, and support that reaches refugees in practice.

**Direction of travel.** The issue is not raised as a Chapter 2 finding or requirement in EU material: across all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files, the Commission items on third-country nationals record the temporary residence rule and counts of residence permits, and its 2023 call to register foreigners and give them access to services sits under Chapter 24, so the standard scale was not decisive. The issue's own items describe continuing barriers to third-country workers' access from 2024 to 2026: the migration inspectorate's 2024 proposals to remove problems found in the foreigners law, a 2025 study finding the procedure for employing foreigners bureaucratic, and UNHCR's 2026 finding that only 29 percent of working-age Ukrainian refugees are in paid work, with language, recognition of qualifications and childcare as barriers. The new law on admission of foreigners that would change the rules is a draft due to apply from June 2027.

**Priority.** The issue is not raised as a requirement in EU material, which counts residence permits and records the residence rules, and this keeps priority from being High: a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no Commission recommendation on third-country workers' access to the labour market, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, and the 2023 Chapter 24 recommendation on registering foreigners and giving them access to services does not concern employment. The evidence comes from national, international organisation and research sources, which do not raise priority.

**Tractability.** A full draft law on admission of foreigners and a draft amendment to the employment law have been prepared and consulted, and the employment agency already supports refugees with partner organisations. Against that, the barriers named by UNHCR (language, recognition of diplomas, childcare) need several ministries and funding, and the draft law would apply only from June 2027.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The issue is not raised as a Chapter 2 finding or requirement in the Commission material in the evidence, from the 2022 Opinion to the 2026 report. Across its reports the Commission records the temporary residence rule and residence permit numbers, noting in 2024 that most permits for work in 2023 went to citizens of Ukraine, Türkiye, Russia and Nepal, and in 2026 that most permits went to citizens of Nepal, Türkiye, Bangladesh and Uzbekistan [1][2]. Its only related call, in 2023, to register foreigners and give them access to services, falls under Chapter 24 [3][16]. There is no Commission recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on third-country workers' access to the labour market, so the analysis rests on national, international organisation and research sources.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the draft law on admission of foreigners will be adopted, and whether it will allow a change of employer and separate labour inspection from immigration control, is open; the adopted text would settle it [15][13][14].
- Whether foreigners residing for work or study will gain access to employment services depends on the draft amendment to the employment law; its adoption and the agency's registration data would show it [17][12].
- The effect of the agency's refugee support is shown only by its own figures; independent data on refugee registration and employment through the agency would settle it [7][10].
- Whether the referral platform built with Helvetas became functional in the second half of 2025 is not reported; agency activity reports for 2025 would show it [22].
