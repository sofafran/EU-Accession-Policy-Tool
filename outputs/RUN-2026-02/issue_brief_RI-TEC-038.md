# Issue brief: Electronic public procurement system outdated and in need of replacement

RI-TEC-038 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The electronic public procurement system is outdated or functionally inadequate: it does not support all procedures, tools or stages of the procurement cycle set out in law, hampers centralised procurement, and has to be re-engineered or replaced by a new system. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to finalise the development of a new electronic procurement system and make it operational, and the Reform Agenda sets as a payment condition a pilot of the new system, with eTendering, eAwarding, eInvoicing, eComplaint and e-catalogues, by December 2027 [1][2]. The current MTender system, launched in 2018, has enabled electronic procurement and public access to documents, but it lacks annual planning and contract management modules and does not offer all the procedures and award instruments set out in law [3][4][5]. Its re-engineering is part of the Government's public procurement development programme 2023-2026, and the Commission reports that development of a new system advanced with funding from international partners [6][7]. Government monitoring, in machine translation, records that technical specifications were received in September 2025, tender documents went to the World Bank in October 2025, and the tender to develop the new system was published in December 2025 with bids due on 10 February 2026 [8][9][10][11]. The new system has not yet been built, and no outcome has been assessed. In 2026 the Commission still found the outdated MTender limiting the procurement agency's monitoring, since delivery, invoices, payments, amendments and performance are not captured digitally [12]. What remains is designing, building and putting into operation a new system that covers every procedure and stage of the procurement cycle. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The new system is still being procured: technical specifications were received in September 2025 and the call for bids was published in December 2025 with a deadline in February 2026, while the Reform Agenda sets a pilot of the new system by December 2027. MTender remains in use without planning, contract management and some procedures. What remains is designing, building and putting into operation a new e-procurement system that covers every procedure and stage of the procurement cycle.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since the issue comes from a cross-cutting theme. The Commission finds the e-procurement system inadequate in 2021, calls for it to be overhauled or re-engineered in 2023, describes MTender as outdated in 2025, and in 2026 records limited progress on digitalising procurement and an outdated MTender that limits monitoring; a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no item from the 2024 report on e-procurement.

**Priority.** The new e-procurement system is a Reform Agenda payment condition, recalled in the 2025 release decision, and the Commission calls for it in the 2023 report and in the key recommendations of the 2025 and 2026 reports. It is central to digital public procurement.

**Tractability.** Funding from international partners is secured, a concept and programme exist, and procurement of the system has started with World Bank review. Against that, the system has been called for since 2021 with limited progress recorded in 2026, and building a full new system is a large technical project.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2021 the Commission found centralised procurement held back by inadequate e-procurement systems, called for improvements in e-procurement, and recorded that the macro-financial assistance memorandum required a re-design of the e-procurement system [13][14][15]. No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2024 report on e-procurement was found. The February 2023 analytical report found legal and technical problems limiting the system and called for it to be re-engineered, and the November 2023 report said it must be overhauled, as it did not cover all procedures or the whole procurement cycle [16][17][18][19]. From 2025 the wording assumes a new system: the Reform Agenda set the December 2027 pilot, the 2025 release decision called for designing and developing the new system, and the 2025 key recommendation asked Moldova to speed up its design and development, describing MTender as outdated and a main bottleneck [2][20][21][22]. In 2026 the key recommendation moved to finalising the system and making it operational, while the Commission recorded limited progress on digitalising procurement processes [1][23].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether a developer was contracted after the February 2026 bid deadline, and on what timetable, would be settled by the contract award notice [11].
- Whether the concept of the new e-procurement information system has been approved by Government decision is not clear; the published decision would settle it [24][25].
- Whether the pilot will meet the December 2027 Reform Agenda condition, with all the required modules, would be shown by the Commission's assessment of that payment request [2].
- Whether the operator has enough staff to run the new system is not assessed; only 18 of its staff work on electronic procurement, and a staffing plan for the new system would show it [26].
