# Issue brief: Electronic communications regulator independence and market oversight weak

RI-TEC-012 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The electronic communications regulator lacks guaranteed independence in appointments and funding and sufficient capacity, and competitive safeguards in a concentrated telecom market are not applied. **Problem mechanisms:** Lack of institutional independence (MEC-05); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Political economy and vested interests (MEC-10).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The issue concerns the independence and capacity of the electronic communications regulator and competitive safeguards in a concentrated telecom market, where Moldtelecom, the national operator, holds more than half of the broadband market [1]. The Reform Agenda sets a step for June 2026 under which at least 3% of the incumbent's infrastructure is shared, and commits the incumbent to network-sharing agreements in line with the Gigabit Infrastructure Act [2][3]. Moldova adopted Electronic Communications Law No. 72/2025, in force from 1 January 2026, under which the regulator was renamed ARCOM, and the Commission finds that the new legal framework reinforced its administrative and financial independence [4][5]. Updated infrastructure access tariffs, between 16% and 49% lower, entered into force on 1 March 2026, and Law No. 106 of 11 June 2026 transposed the Gigabit Infrastructure Act [6][7][8]. The regulator had 105 staff for 114 posts in March 2026 and signed working arrangements with BEREC in March 2025 [9][10]. No source reports how the independence provisions are applied, the network-sharing agreements are still only a commitment, and no item reports the sharing rate after the 0.95% baseline [11]. The one outcome assessed, from the BEREC arrangements, rests only on the regulator's own report, so its success is insufficient evidence [12]. What remains is independence shown in practice and safeguards that actually raise infrastructure sharing. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only for independence. Priority is Medium and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The Reform Agenda requires at least 3% of the incumbent's infrastructure to be shared and network-sharing agreements by the incumbent; access tariffs have been cut and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act transposed in June 2026, but the agreements are still planned and no item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files reports the sharing rate after the 0.95% baseline. The law reinforcing the regulator's independence is adopted, and the regulator had filled 105 of 114 posts by March 2026. What remains is independence shown in practice and competitive safeguards that actually raise infrastructure sharing in a market where the incumbent holds more than half of broadband.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 10 scale is context only, since this is part of the chapter: some level of preparation from 2023 to 2025 and moderately prepared in 2026. The 2026 report finds that the new legal framework reinforced the regulator's administrative and financial independence, and the Commission's 2026 release decision records access tariffs for the incumbent's infrastructure cut by 16% to 49% from 1 March 2026, against minimal network sharing (0.95%) in the 2025 baseline. The gain on independence is legal only, and an ITU finding reported in the Government's 2025 programme still describes independence and accountability as insufficient (machine translated).

**Priority.** The Reform Agenda conditions on infrastructure sharing touch the competition side of this issue, but they are framed as implementing the Electronic Communications Code and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act, whose alignment belongs to the legal alignment issue. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found regulator independence in no benchmark, key recommendation or Reform Agenda payment condition; the Commission mentions it only in its 2026 finding that the new law reinforced it.

**Tractability.** The levers are legal and regulatory and within national control, and recent delivery is strong: a new law in force from January 2026, updated access tariffs in force within the Reform Agenda timetable, working arrangements with BEREC from 2025 and a regulator close to full staffing. The main constraint is market concentration, with the incumbent holding more than half of broadband.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* No item from the 2022 Opinion, and no benchmark or key recommendation, addresses the regulator's independence; the Commission mentions it only in its 2026 finding that the new law reinforced it [5]. Against that, the Government's 2025 programme reports, in machine translation, an ITU finding that the regulator's independence and accountability are insufficient [13]. The competition side was given concrete terms by the Reform Agenda in 2025, which found network sharing with the incumbent minimal at 0.95% of infrastructure, driving up deployment costs and discouraging new entrants, and set the target of at least 3% shared by June 2026 through implementation of the Electronic Communications Code [14][2]. By 2026 the Commission was recording delivery against these conditions: lower access tariffs and the transposing law on access to property and infrastructure [7][8]. The Commission's rating of the whole of Chapter 10 moved from some level of preparation in 2023, 2024 and 2025 to moderately prepared in 2026, which covers more than this issue [15][16][17][18].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The share of the incumbent's infrastructure used by other operators after the tariff cuts is not reported; an updated sharing figure against the 3% target would settle whether the safeguards work [11][2].
- Whether the incumbent has concluded network-sharing agreements is not known; the agreements or a regulator report on them would show it [3].
- How the reinforced independence is applied, for example in board appointments and the regulator's budget, is not described; an independent review, such as an updated ITU or BEREC assessment, would settle it [5][13].
- The effect of the BEREC working arrangements rests on the regulator's own account; an EU or BEREC assessment of its participation would show it [20][12].
