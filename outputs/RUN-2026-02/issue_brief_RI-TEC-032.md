# Issue brief: Not ready to connect to other EU sectoral information systems

RI-TEC-032 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Systems in other sectors are not yet ready to interconnect with EU information systems, such as the business registers interconnection system, the Internal Market Information System, the civil protection system, EU taxation systems and the own resources reporting system. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to prepare its systems in several sectors to connect to EU information systems: above all to advance technical interoperability with the VAT Information Exchange System and the VAT in the Digital Age solutions and to develop digital solutions for the excise systems EMCS and SEED, but also to connect to the civil protection system CECIS through TESTA and to join the radiological exchange systems ECURIE and EURDEP [1][2][3][4]. Some connections already exist, such as access to Tenders Electronic Daily since October 2020, trademark data in the EU Intellectual Property Office's systems and participation in TRACES, but they lie outside the latest requirements [5][6][7]. In response, Moldova approved a State Tax Service IT development programme for 2025 to 2027 in October 2025 and a roadmap for tax interoperability with EU systems for 2026 to 2029 in November 2025 [8][9]. These are adopted plans, and no source reports their delivery. Connection to the CCN Gateway, interoperability with VIES and other tax systems, connection to TESTA and a liaison point for the Internal Market Information System are all planned, with deadlines from December 2026 to January 2028 [10][11][12][13]. No source records a new connection to an EU tax or excise system. What remains is building and connecting the tax, excise, radiological, civil protection and internal market systems to the EU systems the Commission names. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Some connections exist, such as access to TED since 2020, trademark data in EUIPO systems and use of TRACES, but they lie outside the latest requirements. The responses to those requirements are a tax IT programme and roadmap that are adopted, and connections to the CCN gateway, VIES and other tax systems, TESTA and IMI that are planned. What remains is building and connecting the tax, excise, radiological, civil protection and internal market systems to the EU systems the Commission names.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scales were not decisive, since this issue spans several chapters and is part of each. The main thread is taxation: the Commission finds no IT strategy for interoperability with EU tax systems in 2023 and 2024, records a programme prepared in 2025, and in 2026 records a roadmap for 2026 to 2029 while finding that EMCS and SEED solutions remain to be developed and that VIES interoperability must advance. The plans are adopted but no item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files records a new connection to an EU tax or excise system, and the 2026 report adds that Moldova is not yet in ECURIE or EURDEP.

**Priority.** Tax interoperability is a core acquis requirement raised in the 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports, but the Commission records good progress in the taxation chapter in 2025 and 2026, so the test for High on lack of progress is not met. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on it: the Reform Agenda's tax objective refers to EU taxation IT systems, but its step concerns taxpayer services. The issue groups many sectors, each with one or two items.

**Tractability.** The tax service now has an approved IT programme and a roadmap with a timetable, which is evidence of planning progress. Against that, the work spreads across many agencies and EU systems, and some connections depend on EU arrangements open only to Member States, as the government notes for the AGORA system.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's analytical report of February 2023 found that Moldova had yet to adopt the excise movement control system and that significant efforts were needed to equip the tax administration with IT systems, procedures and staff [14][15]. The 2023 report found no technical capacity or IT strategy to prepare for interoperability with EMCS and VIES and asked Moldova to set up TESTA to connect to CECIS [16][3]. In 2024 the Commission still found no IT strategy for interoperability and found Moldova not yet familiar with VIES [17][18]. The 2025 report noted that the tax IT programme had been prepared but not approved and asked Moldova to align its legislation with the IT solutions for EMCS and SEED [19][20]. The 2026 report records the roadmap but states that EMCS and SEED solutions remain to be developed and that VIES interoperability must advance, and it adds ECURIE and EURDEP [2][1][21]. The Reform Agenda refers to aligning with EU taxation IT systems, but its payment step concerns taxpayer services rather than interoperability [22].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the State Tax Service has connected to the CCN Gateway, planned for December 2026, is not known, and a Commission or tax service report of a working connection would settle it [10].
- No source describes a Moldovan plan or step for EMCS or SEED, and a dated plan or a Commission finding on them would show whether excise interoperability has started [2].
- Whether the TESTA connection and the IMI liaison point have been set up is not recorded, and evidence of the connection or designation would be needed [12][13].
- The Commission described Moldova as an active participant in TRACES in 2023 but reports it still preparing for full integration in 2026, and a statement of which TRACES functions are in use would clarify the position [7][28].
