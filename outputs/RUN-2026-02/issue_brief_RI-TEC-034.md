# Issue brief: Digital single market rules not aligned

RI-TEC-034 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Legislation is not aligned with EU rules on online platforms and services, such as the Digital Services Act, the Digital Markets Act, the platform-to-business and geo-blocking regulations, and e-commerce is developing slowly. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to align with EU digital single market rules on geo-blocking, platform-to-business relations, the Digital Services Act and the Digital Markets Act, and to build the capacity and legal basis to enforce the two Acts from accession [1][2]. Moldova adopted two laws in 2025 that partly transpose the platform-to-business and geo-blocking regulations, and the Competition Council is designated for the first [3][4][5]. Moldova is broadly aligned with the e-Commerce Directive [6]. A digital services law is being drafted with EU4Digital experts, and the Government plans to align with the Digital Services Act and set up mechanisms for the Digital Markets Act by the end of 2026, with new supervisory units in the communications regulator and the Competition Council [7][8][9][10]. These are adopted or planned measures, not shown results. In 2026 the Commission found alignment on geo-blocking and platform-to-business relations still partial and only initial steps on the institutional framework for the two Acts [11][12]. The Government notes that no authorities yet supervise digital services and markets, that enforcement rules under the platform-to-business regulation are missing, and that one ministry post covers the area [13][14][15]. Most Government sources are machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Two 2025 laws partly transpose the platform-to-business and geo-blocking regulations, although Government sources give conflicting law numbers for them, the Competition Council is designated for the first, and the e-Commerce Directive is broadly aligned. The digital services law is still being drafted, the Digital Markets Act mechanism needs an amendment to the competition law, no supervisory authority for digital services exists yet, and the Government notes missing enforcement rules under the platform-to-business regulation. What remains is a legal basis and enforcement capacity for the Digital Services Act and the Digital Markets Act, and full alignment on geo-blocking and platform-to-business relations.

**Direction of travel.** The 2023 and 2024 reports found Moldova not aligned with the acquis on geo-blocking, platform-to-business relations, the Digital Services Act and the Digital Markets Act, and the 2025 report still found no alignment on the first two and the Digital Markets Act while noting that work on the Digital Services Act had started. The 2026 report finds alignment on geo-blocking and platform-to-business relations partial, with additional legislation in force in 2026, and initial steps on the institutional framework for both Acts. The improvement is legal and partial only.

**Priority.** The 2024 and 2025 key recommendations ask Moldova to adopt and implement digital services legislation, bundled with media legislation, and the 2026 report repeats the need in its body text on binding acquis. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found it in no key recommendation in three reports, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition.

**Tractability.** Two laws were adopted in 2025, and the Government plans to align with the Digital Services Act by the end of 2026, with EU4Digital experts helping on the draft. Enforcement needs new units in two regulators, while the ministry has one post for this area.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has moved from a list of missing alignment to enforcement capacity. In February 2023 and again in the 2023 and 2024 reports it found Moldova broadly aligned with the e-Commerce Directive but not aligned on geo-blocking, platform-to-business relations, the Digital Services Act or the Digital Markets Act [16][17][18][1]. The 2024 and 2025 key recommendations asked Moldova to adopt and implement media and digital services legislation, including the Digital Services Act, bundled with the Media Freedom Act [19][20]. In 2025 the Commission dropped the Digital Services Act from its list of non-alignment, noting that legislative work had started, and asked Moldova to continue preparing the draft [21][22][23]. The 2026 report found alignment on geo-blocking and platform-to-business relations partial after new legislation entered into force, and asked Moldova to build the institutional capacity and legal basis to enforce both Acts from accession [11][2]. No benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses the issue [2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Government sources give conflicting numbers for the two 2025 laws: the accession programme lists Law No. 54/2025 as the geo-blocking law, while the 2026 progress report gives that title to Law No. 53/2025, and the Official Gazette would settle this confirmed conflict [4][24].
- Whether the digital services law is adopted, and leaves enough time to apply before the planned supervisory structures are needed, would be shown by its adoption date and text [26][27].
- Whether the communications regulator and the Competition Council set up and staff their digital units would be shown by organisational and staffing decisions [9][10].
- A civil society source says a 2024 law on digital content contracts aimed to transpose the Digital Services Act, which other sources do not mention, and its text would settle what it covers [28].
