# Issue brief: Electronic public procurement tools underused

RI-TEC-006 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The electronic public procurement system works, but some of its modules, such as the e-marketplace for low-value contracts and e-catalogues, are incomplete or little used by contracting authorities, and its data are not yet used for red-flag analysis. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The issue concerns fuller use of Moldova's electronic public procurement tools: modules such as the e-marketplace for low-value contracts and e-catalogues, and use of procurement data for red-flag analysis. No item states an EU requirement on fuller use of the existing modules or on red-flag analysis; the Commission's requirements on the MTender system concern its replacement by a new system, which is a separate issue. In 2021 the Commission found that the use of the online tender platform for COVID-19 related procurement, and the monitoring of those tenders, was a positive step towards transparency in health sector procurement, while the increased use of emergency procedures heightened the risk of favouring or disadvantaging certain economic operators [1]. In 2023 the Commission reported a regulation obliging contracting authorities to carry out all stages of most low-value procurement through the State Registry of Public Procurement information system [2]. The regulation is adopted, but no source reports how it is applied or how far the low-value module is used, and no outcome has been assessed. What remains is not known from the evidence held. The unresolved gap is insufficient evidence and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is insufficient evidence.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has set no requirement specific to this issue. Only two Commission items fall under it, and neither is a recommendation. The 2021 report treated the online tender platform as a transparency tool in emergency health procurement, and its concern was the use of emergency procedures rather than the electronic tools themselves [1]. The 2023 report recorded the regulation routing most low-value procurement through the State Registry of Public Procurement system, as a country development rather than a finding of a shortfall [2]. The Commission's later findings that MTender lacks planning and contract management modules and does not offer all procedures are framed around replacing the system, and are held under that issue [3][4]. No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2026 report, and no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, addresses fuller use of the existing modules or red-flag analysis, so no change in wording can be traced.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- How far contracting authorities use the low-value procurement module required by the 2023 regulation is not reported; system usage statistics from the procurement agency would settle it [2].
- Whether the e-marketplace and e-catalogue modules are complete and in use is not stated in any item held; a technical review or annual procurement report would show it.
- Whether procurement data are used for red-flag analysis by any oversight body is not known; reports from the procurement agency or the bodies that oversee procurement would settle it.
- How the planned replacement of MTender affects these modules is not clear; the specification of the new system would show whether it covers low-value procurement and data analysis [3][4].
