# Issue brief: Customs IT systems not aligned with EU electronic customs

RI-TEC-029 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National customs IT systems are not yet aligned with the EU electronic customs strategic plan, and upgrades such as the New Computerised Transit System phases, export and import control systems are delayed or understaffed. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to build customs IT systems that enable a fully digital customs environment interoperable with the EU, to adopt Customs Code amendments that give these systems a legal basis, and to advance its customs IT programme, prioritising core interconnectivity systems and a single window [1][2]. Moldova migrated nationwide to Phase 5 of the New Computerised Transit System in October 2024 and completed the Phase 6 rollout in September 2025 [3][4]. The Customs Decisions System was given its legal framework by Government Decision No. 222/2026 and was fully operational by June 2026, with all 17 types of applications and authorisations handled electronically [5][6]. In October 2025 the Government approved a programme for harmonising customs information systems for 2025-2030 [7]. Beyond adoption, there is shown effect: the Commission reports that the transit system made possible Moldova's full accession to the Common Transit Convention in November 2025 and significantly enhanced the interoperability of its customs operations, and the evidence attributes this result firmly to the transit system [8]. The Commission also judged the related Reform Agenda steps satisfactorily fulfilled [9][10]. Only three of the programme's 25 systems are operational [11], implementation of the Union Customs Code remains incomplete because of transitional provisions and a lack of IT tools [12], and the Government states that the programme has no budget coverage [13]. What remains is the Customs Code amendments and most of the programme's systems, including the core interconnectivity systems and a single window. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The 2026 report asks Moldova to adopt the Customs Code amendments that give the legal basis for EU-interoperable IT systems and to advance the national customs IT programme approved in October 2025, of whose 25 systems three are operational. The Commission notes that implementation remains incomplete because of transitional provisions and a lack of IT tools, and the Government states that the programme has no budget coverage. What remains is the Customs Code amendments and most of the programme's systems, including the core interconnectivity systems and a Single Window Environment for Customs.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 29 rating covers more than this issue and is context only; it recorded good progress in 2025 and 2026, naming the New Computerised Transit System both times. The issue's own items move from uncertainty about the transit system in 2021 and requests for an IT strategy in 2023 and 2024, to migration to NCTS Phase 5 in October 2024, Phase 6 in September 2025, full accession to the Common Transit Convention in November 2025 and a fully operational Customs Decisions System by June 2026.

**Priority.** Customs IT is a key recommendation in every Commission report from 2023 to 2026, and accession to the Common Transit Convention, the transit system and the Customs Decisions System are steps of the Reform Agenda. Interoperability with the EU electronic customs environment is central to the customs chapter and to the interoperability theme.

**Tractability.** The Customs Service delivered the transit system, Convention accession and the Customs Decisions System close to the dates set, the Commission judged the related Reform Agenda steps satisfactorily fulfilled, and EU expert support continues. The lack of budget coverage for the 2025 to 2030 programme, reported by the Government, is the main risk to keeping that pace.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2021 the Commission described Moldova's accession to the Common Transit Convention and the transit system as uncertain [14]. The 2023 analytical report said customs IT needed to be brought in line with EU standards, and the 2023 report asked Moldova to prepare an IT strategy for a paperless environment interoperable with EU systems and to finalise the IT system needed to join the Convention [15][16][17]. In 2024 the request became approval and implementation of the Customs Service IT strategy and full operation of the transit system [18][19]. The 2025 report found good progress on the transit system and asked for a multi-annual IT programme and a fully operational Customs Decisions System [20][21], matching Reform Agenda steps for December 2025 and June 2026 [22][23]. With those delivered, the 2026 report moves on to the Customs Code amendments, the IT programme's core interconnectivity systems and a single window [1][2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- How the 2025-2030 customs IT programme will be funded is not settled; a budget allocation or donor commitment covering the programme would show whether the pace can be kept [13].
- The draft law amending the Customs Code is not shown adopted; the adopted law would provide the legal basis for full automation that the Commission asks for [26].
- The technical arrangements for the Import Control System 2 were pending in 2025; a later monitoring mission or Commission finding would show whether they are in place [27].
- No source reports how far traders use the Customs Decisions System; data from the Customs Service on applications and authorisations processed would show its use in practice [6].
