# Issue brief: Artificial intelligence framework not aligned or supported by structures

RI-TEC-001 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The rules on artificial intelligence are not aligned with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, or the enforcement structures and wider capacity needed to apply them and support trustworthy uptake are not in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to develop the governance, regulatory and enforcement structures needed for artificial intelligence in line with the EU AI Act [1]. Moldova has published a White Paper on data governance and AI, which the Government itself describes as declarative and not a policy document as such, and in 2024 it set up a sub-council on AI and data governance within the National Digitalisation Council [2][3][4]. A law on AI, a law on AI testing spaces, a 2026 to 2030 national programme and an AI governance institution are all planned or being drafted, with transposition of the AI Act foreseen by the end of 2026 or by 2027 [5][6][7][8]. Little is shown to have happened: a civil society report finds that the sub-council produced no initiative for a legal framework on AI in 2024 and 2025 as planned [9]. The Government reported in 2025 that no institution responsible for AI policy or supervision had been identified and that no ministry employee covered AI, and in 2026 it described the field as at an early stage, without a competent authority or an AI-specific legal framework [10][11][12][13][14]. Funding for the national programme will depend on the annual budget process [15]. What remains is a law aligned with the AI Act, a designated and staffed supervisory authority, and the governance structure to apply it. Most Government sources are machine translated. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The Commission requires governance, regulatory and enforcement structures in line with the AI Act. A draft AI law, a law on AI testing spaces, the 2026 to 2030 national programme and an AI governance institution are all planned or being drafted, and the Government reported in 2025 that no institution responsible for AI policy or supervision had been identified. What remains is a law aligned with the AI Act, a designated and staffed supervisory authority, and the governance structure to apply it.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 10 scale is context only, as this issue is part of the AI theme and the scale was not decisive. The Government recorded a lack of legal acts on AI in 2023, the Commission found in 2025 that Moldova is not yet aligned with the AI Act and in 2026 asked it to develop governance, regulatory and enforcement structures, and the Government's 2026 programme document still describes the field as at an early stage with no competent authority or AI-specific framework. The 2024 White Paper is declarative, and a 2026 civil society report says the AI sub-council produced no legislative initiative.

**Priority.** AI Act alignment is raised by the Commission in 2025 as a finding and in 2026 as a recommendation, and a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found it in no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition. It is central to the AI theme but not yet repeated across three reports.

**Tractability.** The National Accession Programme plans transposition of the AI Act and a governance institution by 2027, and a consultative sub-council exists. In 2025 no ministry employee covered AI policy, the competent authority was yet to be chosen, and funding for the national programme depends on the annual budget process.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement is recent. The Commission did not raise AI in the evidence before its 2025 report, which found that Moldova was not yet aligned with the EU AI Act [16]. The 2026 report turned this finding into a recommendation that Moldova develop the necessary governance, regulatory and enforcement structures in line with the AI Act [1]. The ask has therefore widened from legal alignment to the institutions that would enforce it. The Commission has also recorded supporting steps: association to the Digital Europe Programme, ratified in May 2024, the AI and data sub-council, and an AI factory antenna set up in Moldova [17][18][19]. The national gap is older than the EU ask: the Government's 2023 digital strategy already named the lack of legal acts on AI as a problem [20]. AI Act alignment is not in any benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition in the evidence [1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the AI law is drafted and adopted on the planned timetable, and how closely it follows the AI Act, would be shown by the draft text and its adoption record [22][6].
- Which body will supervise AI, and whether it has staff and budget, would be settled by a designation decision and staffing figures for it and for the planned ministry post [10][23].
- Whether the National Programme on AI and Data Governance 2026 to 2030 is approved and funded would be shown by the Government decision and budget allocations [24][15].
- Whether the sub-council has influenced policy beyond the White Paper, which no independent source shows, could be settled by its minutes or outputs [25][9].
