# Issue brief: Supplementary pension rights of mobile workers

RI-FMW-012 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National law is not aligned with the EU directives on supplementary pension rights, such as the acquisition and preservation of rights for workers who move between Member States. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU requirement is that workers who move between Member States can acquire and preserve their supplementary pension rights [1]. The Commission finds that these EU rules are not incorporated into Moldovan law, and that Moldova has no occupational pension scheme, with voluntary private schemes supplementing public pensions [2][3]. Moldova adopted Law No. 198/2020 on voluntary pension funds, but the Government states that effective implementation of the occupational pension system remains at an early stage [4]. The Government undertakes to transpose Directive 98/49/EC fully and plans an amendment to Law No. 198/2020 for both directives [5][6]. Neither is shown to have been adopted, and nothing is shown to have happened in practice. Moldova currently has no occupational supplementary pension system linked to employment, so the rules have limited practical reach [7]. Sources disagree on how far the law is already aligned: the Government says, on the basis of the financial market regulator's conformity tables, that Moldovan law is aligned with Directive 2014/50/EU and partly transposes Directive 98/49/EC, while the Commission says the EU rules are not incorporated [8][2]. The Government sources are machine translated. What remains is incorporating the EU rules on acquiring and preserving supplementary pension rights into Moldovan law in a form the Commission accepts. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Sources conflict: the Commission in 2024 says the EU rules are not incorporated into Moldovan law, while the government in 2025 says, on the basis of the financial market regulator's conformity tables, that the law is aligned with Directive 2014/50/EU and partly transposes Directive 98/49/EC. The responses named by the government, full transposition of Directive 98/49/EC and a draft amendment to Law No. 198/2020, are not yet adopted. What remains is incorporating the EU rules on acquiring and preserving supplementary pension rights into Moldovan law in a form the Commission accepts.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is only part of the chapter and the only Chapter 2 sub-area wording among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files concerns legal alignment in general (2023) and the European Health Insurance Card (2025). The 2023 report, the 2023 Analytical Report and the 2024 report all find that EU rules on supplementary pension rights of mobile workers are not incorporated into Moldovan law and that there is no occupational pension scheme, and in 2025 the government still describes the occupational system as at an early stage and plans full transposition of Directive 98/49/EC.

**Priority.** The finding is repeated in the 2023 and 2024 reports, but a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on supplementary pension rights. Moldova has no occupational pension scheme, so the rules have limited practical reach, and the issue is a small part of the chapter.

**Tractability.** The change is legal and lies with the government and the financial market regulator, and a draft amendment to the voluntary pension funds law is identified. Against that, the government and the Commission disagree on how far the law is already aligned, and no adoption date is given for the draft.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording has not changed. The February 2023 Analytical Report found that the EU rules on supplementary pension rights of mobile workers were not incorporated in Moldovan law, and noted that Moldova had no occupational pension scheme [1][9]. The 2023 report and the 2024 report repeated both findings in almost the same words [10][11][3][2]. The finding is stated as a gap, not as a recommendation, and no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses supplementary pension rights [11]. No Commission item in the evidence after 2024 returns to the issue, so the Commission has not yet responded to the Government's 2025 statement that the law is aligned with one directive and partly transposes the other [8].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The Commission says the EU rules on supplementary pension rights are not in Moldovan law, while the Government says the law is aligned with Directive 2014/50/EU and partly transposes Directive 98/49/EC; the regulator's conformity tables, or a later Commission assessment of them, would settle this confirmed conflict [2][8].
- Whether the amendment to Law No. 198/2020 is adopted, and whether it completes transposition of both directives, would be shown by the adopted text [6][5].
- Whether an occupational pension scheme develops, which would give the rules practical reach, would be shown by data on schemes and members under Law No. 198/2020 [4][7].
