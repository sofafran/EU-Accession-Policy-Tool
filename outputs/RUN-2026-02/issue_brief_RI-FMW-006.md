# Issue brief: Alignment with the European Labour Authority regulation

RI-FMW-006 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National law is not aligned with the regulation establishing the European Labour Authority, including the arrangements for cooperating with it. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

Under the Chapter 2 acquis, national law has to be aligned with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 establishing the European Labour Authority, including the arrangements for cooperating with it, but no Commission requirement, key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on the Authority was found in the evidence. The Commission records that Moldova signed a cooperation agreement with the Authority in May 2025 [1][2]. The Government reports, in machine-translated documents, that the agreement was signed in Bratislava on 2 May 2025 and that Moldovan authorities then started a dialogue with the Authority on priority areas, including the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information [3][4]. The Government also states that a draft law to harmonise national legislation with the regulation is being prepared, planned for October 2026 and covering the Authority's role as mediator, and that it is strengthening the capacity of the labour ministry, the National Employment Agency and the State Labour Inspectorate to cooperate with the Authority [5][6][7]. These are the Government's own account, no source shows the draft law adopted, and the capacity work names no specific measure. No outcome has been assessed. What remains is adopting and applying the law that aligns national legislation with the regulation, and building the capacity to cooperate with the Authority. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The Commission records that Moldova signed a cooperation agreement with the European Labour Authority in May 2025, which covers the arrangements for cooperating with it. The government states that the draft law to harmonise national legislation with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 is still being prepared, and gives this only as its own account. What remains is adopting and applying the law that aligns national legislation with the regulation, including the Authority's role as mediator, and building the capacity of the ministry, the employment agency and the labour inspectorate to cooperate with it.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is only part of the chapter and the only Chapter 2 sub-area wording among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files concerns legal alignment in general (2023) and the European Health Insurance Card (2025). A search of all items for the European Labour Authority and Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 found the Commission's two records of the May 2025 cooperation agreement and Government items on the agreement and a draft law; none is a finding or recommendation on alignment, so there is no sequence from which to read a direction.

**Priority.** A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no Commission requirement, key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on the European Labour Authority; the Commission mentions it only to record the 2025 cooperation agreement. Cooperation with the Authority is a small part of the chapter.

**Tractability.** The change is narrow, legal and within the control of the labour ministry, and the cooperation agreement was concluded in 2025, which shows recent progress with the Authority. A draft law has already been identified, although its adoption date is not given.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has not set a requirement on this issue. No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 and 2024 reports mentions the European Labour Authority. The Commission's only references, in the 2025 report, record that Moldova signed a cooperation agreement with the Authority in May 2025 [1][2]. Neither is a finding or recommendation on alignment with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149, and no Reform Agenda condition covers the issue. The only Chapter 2 sub-area wording found concerns legal alignment in general in 2023 and the European Health Insurance Card in 2025, so there is no sequence of Commission wording on the Authority from which to read a change. The issue therefore rests on the acquis itself and on the Government's 2025 accession programme, which plans the aligning law [5][6].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the draft law aligning national legislation with Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 has been adopted, and whether it covers the Authority's role as mediator, would be settled by the adopted text or the Government's legislative record [5][6].
- The capacity building of the labour ministry, the employment agency and the Labour Inspectorate is stated only in general terms; a list of training, staffing or procedures put in place would show what has been done [7].
- Whether the dialogue started after the cooperation agreement has led to joint activity with the Authority rests on the Government's account alone; an Authority record of joint inspections, exchanges or meetings with Moldova would confirm it [4].
- Whether the Commission regards alignment with the regulation as a requirement for Moldova is not known; a future report or screening finding on the Authority would settle it [2].
