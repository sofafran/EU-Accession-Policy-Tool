# Issue brief: Weak compliance with data protection rules

RI-TEC-020 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Data controllers, including public bodies, do not apply data protection law in practice, breaches are frequent and not always reported, awareness is low, and sanctions are rare. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU asks Moldova to align its data protection legislation further with the acquis, train public servants across all institutions and step up awareness campaigns, and in 2025 it asked Moldova to implement the National Health Strategy with a focus on digitalising health services and protecting personal data [1][2]. Moldova adopted Law No. 195/2024 to align with the General Data Protection Regulation, in force from 23 August 2026, which lets the data protection authority impose administrative sanctions directly [3]. In 2025 the authority organised 84 courses for over 3,600 people and prepared guidelines on applying the new law, on its own account [4][5]. Breaches continue: the authority found breaches in 105 of 230 decisions in 2022, 173 violations are reported for 2024 and again for 2025, and in 2025 a breach at the largest private laboratory leaked thousands of patients' test results [6][7][8][9]. Only about 50 public authorities have designated a data protection officer [10]. The Government states that data exchange takes place exclusively through MConnect, while the authority finds that state bodies still access personal data through COI technology, which does not identify individual users [11][12]. No source yet shows the new law being applied. What remains is compliance in practice by public and private controllers, including in health, with the new sanctions applied and access to state registers made traceable. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

## Assessment (validated at G6)

**What remains.** The 2023 requirement to train public servants and raise awareness has been answered by the authority's 2025 training campaign of 84 courses for over 3,600 people, and Law No. 195/2024, adopted and in force from 23 August 2026, lets the authority impose administrative sanctions directly. Sources conflict on data access: the Government says, in a machine-translated source, that data exchange takes place exclusively through MConnect, while the authority finds state bodies still using the COI technology outside it, and only about 50 public authorities had designated a data protection officer. What remains is compliance in practice by public and private controllers, including in health, with the new sanctions applied and access to state registers made traceable.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is a cross-cutting data protection issue. Breaches are confirmed every year: 105 in 2022 in the 2023 report and 173 violations, given by CSO Meter for 2024 and by the authority's 2025 activity report for 2025, which may be one count reported for two different years; the 2025 report records a major health data breach, and the authority reports in 2026 that state bodies still access personal data through the COI technology. Rising complaints are read by CSO Meter as growing awareness rather than worse compliance, so they are not used as a trend.

**Priority.** Data protection compliance is raised in the 2023 recommendation on training and awareness and in the 2025 key recommendation on health, which names the protection of personal data, but a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found it in no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition, and not across three or more reports. Applying data protection law is central to the data theme, but the volume of authority and civil society material does not raise priority.

**Tractability.** The new law gives the authority direct administrative sanctions, ending the duplicated procedures it blames for weak deterrence, and courts upheld its measures in 52 of 58 final cases in 2025. Against that, compliance depends on many public and private controllers, and the authority reports years of unanswered requests to move state bodies off the COI technology.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The evidence holds no Commission item on data protection compliance from the 2022 Opinion. In February 2023 the Analytical Report noted that the statistics bureau needed capacity and means to implement data protection rules, and the 2023 report set the main requirement: further alignment, training of public servants and awareness campaigns, while recording 105 breaches found in 2022 [13][1][6]. The 2024 report recorded only that the authority examined a growing number of complaints and issued penalties [14]. In 2025 the Commission's attention moved to health data: it reported the major laboratory breach and made personal data protection part of a key recommendation on the National Health Strategy [9][2]. Data protection compliance is not a benchmark or a Reform Agenda payment condition.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the authority applies the administrative sanctions available under Law No. 195/2024 from August 2026, and with what effect, is not yet shown; its sanctioning data for 2026 and 2027 would settle it [3][17].
- The Government and the data protection authority disagree on whether state bodies exchange data only through MConnect; an audit of access to state registers would settle it [11][12].
- The count of 173 violations appears for 2024 in a civil society report and for 2025 in the authority's report, and may be one figure given for two years; the authority's annual statistics would settle it [7][8].
- How many public bodies have designated a data protection officer since the new law took effect is unknown; an updated count from the authority would show it [10].
