# Issue brief: Public sector websites and apps not accessible to persons with disabilities

RI-TEC-039 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Public sector websites, mobile applications and other digital public services are not accessible to persons with physical, sensory or intellectual disabilities, and the EU accessibility requirements for them (the Web Accessibility Directive and the European Accessibility Act) are not applied or enforced. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to improve the accessibility of public sector websites, mobile applications and audiovisual services for persons with physical, sensory and intellectual disabilities [1]. Moldova has guidelines for government websites, and its digital strategy implementation programme includes an action to make ICT products and services accessible, including rules based on the WCAG 2.2 guidelines [2][3]. That action had not started by April 2026, because the e-Governance Agency order setting the WCAG 2.2 rules awaits a prior Government Decision on accessibility requirements [4]. In August 2026 Moldova adopted a law on accessibility requirements for certain products and services, including digital and e-commerce services, which the Commission describes as in line with the acquis [5][6]. This is an adopted law, not a shown result, and it concerns products and services rather than public sector websites. No improvement in practice is shown. In 2023 SIGMA found poor compliance with the WCAG 2.0 standard, with an average of 45 errors per page and only one of 26 sampled government websites faring well [7][8]. In 2026 the Commission found public sector websites and mobile applications still insufficiently accessible, and accessibility insufficiently enforced and funded [9][10]. What remains is binding accessibility rules for public sector websites and applications, with enforcement and funding. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2026 key recommendation asks Moldova to improve the accessibility of public sector websites and mobile applications. The law on accessibility requirements for products and services adopted in August 2026, which the Commission describes as in line with the acquis, is a legal step for products and services, while the order setting WCAG 2.2 rules for public websites had not started in 2026 because it awaits a Government Decision. What remains is binding accessibility rules for public sector websites and applications and their enforcement and funding.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 10 scale is context only, as this issue comes from a cross-cutting theme and was not decisive. SIGMA found in 2023 that government websites complied poorly with WCAG 2.0 despite guidelines, with an average of 45 errors per page across 26 tested sites, and the Commission found in 2026 that public sector websites and mobile applications remain insufficiently accessible and that accessibility is insufficiently enforced and funded.

**Priority.** Accessibility of public sector websites and applications is a key recommendation in the 2026 report, the first Commission report to raise it in a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files, and no Reform Agenda payment condition names it. It is a clear EU requirement but not yet repeated across reports.

**Tractability.** Guidelines for government websites already exist, and the WCAG 2.2 rules are a single order of the e-Governance Agency once the Government Decision is approved. The poor compliance SIGMA found across many sites in 2023 and the Commission's finding that accessibility is insufficiently enforced and funded show that bringing existing sites into line needs resources across many institutions.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The requirement is new as an EU ask. The Commission did not raise the accessibility of public sector websites and applications in any report in the evidence before 2026, although SIGMA, an international partner, had documented poor compliance in 2023 [1][2]. The 2026 report raised it at once as a key recommendation, asking Moldova to improve the accessibility of public sector websites, mobile applications and audiovisual services [1]. In the same report the Commission found that public sector websites and mobile applications remain insufficiently accessible, that accessibility is insufficiently enforced and funded across buildings, transport, websites and digital services, and that public and private job portals are largely inaccessible [9][10][11]. It also welcomed the August 2026 law on accessibility requirements for products and services [5]. No Reform Agenda payment condition names the issue, so there is no sequence of Commission wording to compare [1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the Government Decision on accessibility requirements is approved, and the e-Governance Agency then issues the WCAG 2.2 rules, would be shown by the adopted decision and order [4].
- Whether public sector websites have become more accessible since 2023 could be settled by a repeat of the SIGMA test or a similar independent audit [7].
- Whether the August 2026 law reaches digital public services, and who enforces it, would be shown by its text and implementing rules [5][6].
- Whether funding and enforcement for accessibility increase would be shown by budget allocations and enforcement records after 2026 [10].
