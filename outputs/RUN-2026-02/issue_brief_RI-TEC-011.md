# Issue brief: Electronic communications law not aligned with EU rules

RI-TEC-011 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The law on electronic communications is not fully aligned with the European Electronic Communications Code, the Gigabit Infrastructure Act and related rules on cost reduction, spectrum, roaming and open internet access. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asked Moldova to align its law with the European Electronic Communications Code and the EU roaming acquis, including their secondary legislation, so that it could join the Roam Like at Home area [1][2]. Parliament adopted Electronic Communications Law No. 72/2025 in April 2025 and roaming Law No. 81/2025 in the same month, the regulator adopted detailed roaming rules, and Law No. 106 of June 2026 transposes the Gigabit Infrastructure Act [3][4][5][6]. Law No. 72/2025 entered into force on 1 January 2026 [7]. The Commission assessed the legislation as correctly transposing the whole EU roaming acquis and judged the related Reform Agenda step satisfactorily fulfilled [8][9]. On that basis Moldova joined Roam Like at Home on 1 January 2026, and the Commission states that this removed roaming charges and brought immediate benefits to citizens and businesses; the roaming outcome is assessed as having worked [10][11]. Earlier, the voluntary operator agreement of 2023 led to reduced roaming tariffs from January 2024, which the Commission and an independent civil society source both report [12][13]. For the Code itself, effects on network roll-out are too early to judge, as the law has applied only since January 2026 [14]. The regulator is still drafting secondary acts under the new law and plans an end-user protection regulation for 2026, the Government plans to transpose the remaining electronic communications acts by the end of 2027, and a civil society monitor finds the law only partially compatible with the Code [15][16][17][18]. What remains is completing and applying the secondary legislation under the Code and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act and transposing the remaining related acts. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is High.

**What remains.** Law No. 72/2025, in force since January 2026, the roaming law and its secondary acts, and the 2026 infrastructure law respond to the requirements, and the Commission judged the related Reform Agenda step satisfactorily fulfilled. The regulator is still drafting secondary acts under the new law, an end-user protection regulation is planned for 2026, the Government plans to transpose the remaining electronic communications acts by the end of 2027, and a civil society monitor describes the new law as only partly compatible with the Code. What remains is completing and applying the secondary legislation under the Code and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act and transposing the remaining related acts.

**Direction of travel.** The 2022 Opinion found limited alignment with the EU electronic communications framework, and the 2023 report asked for alignment with the Electronic Communications Code and the Roaming Regulation. The 2024 report found that alignment had started and the 2025 report noted legislative advancements; in 2026 the Commission found the roaming acquis correctly transposed in full, the Code transposed in primary law with secondary legislation approximated, and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act reflected in a law adopted in June 2026. The Chapter 10 rating rates the whole chapter and was not decisive.

**Priority.** Alignment with the roaming acquis and the Code was a key recommendation in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports and part of a Reform Agenda step, which would usually mean High. The Commission now finds the roaming acquis fully transposed and the step fulfilled, and a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no recommendation on electronic communications or roaming in the 2026 report, so the remaining work is narrower.

**Tractability.** The main laws were adopted in 2025 and 2026 with EU technical support, and the Commission confirms the results, including entry into Roam Like at Home in January 2026. The remaining work is secondary legislation by one regulator and one ministry, which the regulator is already drafting.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2022 Opinion found Moldova's alignment with the EU electronic communications framework limited [19]. The 2023 report asked for alignment with the Electronic Communications Code and for alignment with and implementation of the Roaming Regulation [20][21]. In 2024 the key recommendation linked alignment with the roaming acquis and the Code to joining the Roam Like at Home area, and the report found that alignment had started [1][22]. The 2025 report narrowed the request to implementing secondary legislation and noted legislative advances on roaming [2][23]. The Reform Agenda step on electronic communications was then found satisfactorily fulfilled [9]. The 2026 report records the Code transposed in primary law, secondary legislation approximated and the Gigabit Infrastructure Act reflected in the June 2026 law, and it contains no recommendation on electronic communications or roaming [24][25][26].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the regulator has completed the secondary acts under Law No. 72/2025, including the end-user protection regulation, is not reported, and their adoption and a Commission assessment of them would settle it [15][16].
- The civil society monitor's finding that the law is only partially compatible with the Code is not matched by a detailed Commission analysis of the gaps, and a screening or transposition assessment would show which provisions remain [18].
- No source reports how Law No. 106 on the Gigabit Infrastructure Act is applied, and evidence on access disputes, tariffs and network sharing under it would be needed, given the reported uneven application of the earlier access law [6][31].
- No source gives data on roaming use under Roam Like at Home, and regulator or Commission statistics on traffic and prices would show its effect [10].
