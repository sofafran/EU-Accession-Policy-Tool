# Issue brief: Employment service capacity for EURES and labour mobility

RI-FMW-005 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The public employment service lacks trained staff or adequate IT and data systems to take part in EU labour mobility networks and vacancy exchange. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to ensure the capacity, resources and IT infrastructure, systems and databases needed for full participation in EURES, the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information system and the EHIC scheme [1]. Moldova has set up a Labour Migration and EURES department in the National Employment Agency from January 2025, with five of its six posts filled, assigned the Agency the role of EURES National Coordination Office, and developed technical standards, procedures and formats for matching vacancies and CVs with the EURES portal [2][3][4][5]. The Commission reports that the Agency developed a new integrated information system in December 2025, aiming at fully digitalised services by 2027, and a 2026 Government document says the system, funded by the EU Delegation, is still being developed [6][7]. No source shows the new system in use, and the Agency's own reports for 2025 and 2026 state that the lack of a modern information system affects the efficiency and quality of its services [8][9]. Interoperability with the EURES platform, staff training on EURES and minimum requirements for personal data in that exchange are planned for 2028 and are not reported as delivered [10][11][12]. What remains is a working connection between the Agency's information system and the EURES platform, operated by trained staff, before accession. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The employment agency has a Labour Migration and EURES directorate (five of six posts filled) and technical standards for exchange with the EURES portal, but the interoperability of its information system with EURES, staff training on EURES and the rules on personal data in that exchange are still planned. The agency's own reports for 2024 and 2025 say it lacks a modern information system, and the new system, funded through the EU Delegation, is still being developed. What remains is a working connection between the agency's information system and the EURES platform, operated by trained staff, before accession.

**Direction of travel.** The issue is first raised in the Commission's 2025 key recommendation to reinforce the employment service's IT infrastructure for EURES, when the 2025 report also recorded a new EURES department with a designated officer from January 2025. The 2026 report records technical standards for matching vacancies and CVs with the EURES portal and a new integrated information system developed in December 2025, aiming at fully digitalised services by 2027, while widening the recommendation to systems for EESSI and the EHIC. The chapter rating was not decisive, as the issue is only part of the chapter.

**Priority.** The employment service's IT capacity for EURES is named in the key recommendations of the 2025 and 2026 reports, two reports, and supports a chapter requirement that applies from accession. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on it.

**Tractability.** The work sits mainly with one agency, which has already set up a EURES directorate and technical standards, so there is evidence of past progress. The new information system depends on external funding and on technical connection work that is still planned, and the agency reports unfilled posts within its staff ceiling.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The issue does not appear in the evidence from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 and 2024 reports. It is first raised in the 2025 report, whose key recommendation asks Moldova to continue preparatory work for joining EURES upon accession, including by reinforcing the Public Employment Service's IT infrastructure [13]. The 2026 report widens the recommendation from EURES alone to the technical and organisational measures, administrative and financial capacity and IT systems and databases needed for full participation in EURES, EESSI and the EHIC scheme [1]. No Reform Agenda payment condition or benchmark addresses the issue.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the new integrated information system is in use is not reported, and an Agency activity report or Commission assessment showing it in operation would settle it [6][7].
- Interoperability between the Agency's Labour Market information system and the EURES platform is planned for November 2028, and evidence of a tested data exchange on vacancies and CVs would show whether it has been achieved [4][10].
- No source records that staff training on EURES or on serving EU citizens under Law No. 200/2025 has taken place, and training records or reports naming its content would be needed [16][11][6].
- The Agency reports unfilled posts within its staff ceiling, and later staffing data would show whether its capacity to run the EURES functions has grown [14][15].
