# Issue brief: Access to healthcare for EU citizens through the European Health Insurance Card

RI-FMW-011 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** EU citizens can use the European Health Insurance Card or access medical services only where a bilateral arrangement exists, so access is not yet available to citizens of all Member States. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The European Health Insurance Card is part of the Chapter 2 acquis on social security coordination, and the Commission's 2025 and 2026 key recommendations ask Moldova to develop the IT infrastructure and capacity for the Card scheme alongside the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information [1][2]. The Government stated in 2025, in machine-translated documents, that the EU acts on the Card are not transposed in Moldova, that Moldova is to apply the Card, and that it plans a law on cross-border medical care in EU Member States, the European Economic Area and Switzerland by December 2026 [3][4][5]. The Commission reports that Moldova conducted a feasibility study on introducing the Card, finalised in August 2026, and that an action plan on the Card was approved the same month as a roadmap for operational implementation [6][7][8]. No source shows the law adopted or the Card issued, and no item states whether EU citizens can obtain healthcare in Moldova with the Card, so no outcome has been assessed. The international cooperation section of the health insurance fund that deals with Member State institutions has three specialists [9]. What remains is a decision on introducing the Card and the legal and financial arrangements that would give EU citizens access to healthcare through it. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The Government stated in 2025 that the EU acts on the European Health Insurance Card are not transposed and listed applying the Card and a draft law on cross-border medical care as planned. In 2026 the Commission reported a feasibility study on the Card, finalised in August 2026, and an EHIC Action Plan approved the same month. What remains is a decision on introducing the Card and the legal and financial arrangements that would give EU citizens access to healthcare through it.

**Direction of travel.** Across all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files, the EU items on the European Health Insurance Card concern its domestic preconditions: the 2025 sub-area wording records no progress on the Card and the 2026 report a feasibility study and an action plan, and these are used for the issue on those preconditions. None states whether EU citizens can obtain healthcare in Moldova with the Card, and the Government stated in 2025 that the EU acts on the Card are not transposed, so two points in time with no assessed change in access are not enough to set a direction.

**Priority.** The Card is part of the Chapter 2 acquis on social security coordination and is named in the key recommendations of the 2025 and 2026 reports, as IT infrastructure and capacity for the EHIC scheme to be developed alongside the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information. Two reports fall short of the three-report test, which keeps it below High.

**Tractability.** Preparation has started: a feasibility study on the Card's impact on public finances was completed and a draft law on cross-border medical care is planned. The study's focus on public finances points to cost as the main constraint, and the international cooperation section dealing with Member State institutions has three specialists.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* No requirement item on the Card from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 and 2024 reports was found. The Card first appears in the 2025 report, whose key recommendation names IT infrastructure and capacity for the Card scheme to be developed together with the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information, and whose sub-area wording records no progress on the Card [1][10]. The 2026 report repeats the key recommendation and records the feasibility study and the action plan, so the Commission's attention moves from the absence of progress to the first preparatory steps [2][6][7][8]. Both reports address the domestic preconditions for the Card rather than whether EU citizens can use it in Moldova. No Reform Agenda payment condition on the Card was found in the evidence.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether EU citizens can obtain healthcare in Moldova with the Card, or only under bilateral arrangements, is not stated in any source; a statement by the health insurance fund or the Commission on current access would settle it [3].
- The findings and cost estimate of the feasibility study are not known; the published study would show whether cost is the main constraint [6][11].
- Whether the law on cross-border medical care has been adopted, and whether it transposes the EU acts on the Card, would be settled by the adopted text [5].
- Whether the three-person international cooperation section is enough to run the scheme is not assessed in any source; staffing plans in the action plan would show it [9][8].
