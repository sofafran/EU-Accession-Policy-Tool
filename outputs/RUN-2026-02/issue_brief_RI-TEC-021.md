# Issue brief: Personal data protection in electoral processes weak

RI-TEC-021 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The processing of personal data in elections, including biometric data and campaign use, lacks oversight and was introduced without assessing compliance or consulting the data protection authority. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

No Commission document in the Moldova evidence sets a requirement on the protection of personal data in electoral processes, and the issue rests on a single civil society report. That report, published in March 2026, states that illegal surveys during the parliamentary election campaign used personal data databases without clarity on how they were obtained, who collected them and where they are stored [1]. No source describes any measure Moldova has taken in response, and no source shows what has happened in practice beyond that one account. What remains, on the strength of that report alone, is oversight of how personal data are obtained, stored and used in election campaigns, a finding that other sources would need to confirm [1]. Because there is no EU benchmark, no responsible institution named and only one item from one year, the assessment cannot measure the gap against what the EU requires or say how realistic progress would be. The unresolved gap is insufficient_evidence and the direction of travel is insufficient_evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is insufficient_evidence.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the problem described in the civil society report is confirmed by other sources is not known, and an assessment by the national data protection authority, the Central Electoral Commission or an election observation mission covering the campaign would settle it [1].
- Which institution is responsible for overseeing the use of personal data in campaigns, and whether it has acted, is not recorded, and supervision or enforcement reports from the data protection authority would be needed to judge tractability.
- Whether the Commission regards electoral data protection as part of Moldova's alignment with the EU data protection acquis is not shown, and a statement in a later Commission report or screening document would establish an EU requirement against which the gap could be measured.
- With one item from 2026, no direction can be shown, and evidence from earlier or later election cycles would be needed to say whether the problem is persisting, worsening or easing.
