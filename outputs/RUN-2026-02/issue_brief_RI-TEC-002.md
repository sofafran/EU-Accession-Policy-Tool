# Issue brief: Digital transformation strategy and governance weak

RI-TEC-002 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** There is no adopted long-term strategy for digital transformation aligned with EU targets, or the ministry or agency responsible lacks the capacity, staff and inter-institutional coordination to lead and monitor it. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Coordination failure (MEC-08); Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to have a digital transformation strategy and the institutional capacity to implement it [1][2]. The Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030 was adopted in September 2023 [3], a National Council for Digital Transformation was set up in November 2023 to coordinate it [4], and a programme implementing the strategy for 2025 to 2027 was adopted in May 2025 [5]. The Commission reports tangible progress on the strategy's key performance indicators in 2025 and 2026 [6][7]. Earlier experience shows why delivery matters: the expired Digital Moldova 2020 strategy met only 6 of its 12 targets [8]. Capacity remains the weak point. The Government estimates the programme at 613 million lei, of which 225 million lei are uncovered [9], and states that implementation requires building capacity in the ministry and the e-Governance Agency [10]. It names the lack of centralised coordination of digitalisation initiatives as the main barrier [11], and a public body consulted on the programme warned that the two bodies are responsible for most of its 68 actions [12]. Government sources also cite low pay for IT staff, scarce resources and staff turnover after reorganisations [13][14][15], and the Court of Accounts found a recommendation on documenting information systems only partly implemented, citing insufficient institutional capacity [16][17]. What remains is the staff, funding and coordination capacity to deliver and monitor the strategy. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The strategy, the council and an implementation programme are in place, but the Commission repeats in 2024, 2025 and 2026 its key recommendation to ensure institutional capacity to implement the strategy. The Government reports 225 million lei of the programme's cost as uncovered, the ministry and the e-Governance Agency responsible for 53 and 37 of its 68 actions, and a lack of centralised coordination, and a 2024 follow-up audit by the Court of Accounts found its recommendation on documenting information systems only partly implemented, citing insufficient institutional capacity. What remains is the staff, funding and coordination capacity in the ministry and the e-Governance Agency to deliver and monitor the strategy.

**Direction of travel.** The 2023 key recommendation asked Moldova to adopt the Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030 and ensure capacity to implement it; the strategy was adopted in September 2023 and a National Council for Digital Transformation set up in November 2023, as the 2024 report records. The 2025 report notes an implementation programme for 2025 to 2027 and tangible progress on all key performance indicators, and the 2026 report again records progress on them. The Chapter 10 rating rose from some level of preparation in 2023 to 2025 to moderately prepared in 2026, but it rates the whole chapter and was not decisive.

**Priority.** A key recommendation on adopting the strategy and ensuring the institutional capacity to implement it appears in the 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports, and strategy and governance are central to the digital transformation theme. The later recommendations name data protection and cybersecurity as the main areas for that capacity, which are covered by other issues.

**Tractability.** The Government adopted the strategy, set up the council and approved the implementation programme within two years, and the Commission records progress on its indicators. Capacity is the harder part: the Government and the Court of Accounts report staff shortages, low pay for IT staff, staff turnover after reorganisations and reliance on external partners for funding.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2023 report asked Moldova to adopt the Digital Transformation Strategy 2023-2030 and ensure the institutional capacity to implement it, and to set up a National Digital Transformation Council [1][18]. Once the strategy was adopted, the 2024 key recommendation shifted to ensuring capacity to implement its priority areas, in particular alignment on electronic identification, data protection and cybersecurity, including the 5G Toolbox [19]. The 2025 and 2026 reports keep this wording, asking for capacity to "continue" implementation, with the focus on data protection and cybersecurity standards [20][2]. Over the same period the Commission's findings moved from some progress in 2024 to good progress in 2025 and 2026, on the strength of progress against the strategy's indicators [21][22][23].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- How the uncovered 225 million lei of the implementation programme will be financed is not known; budget allocations or donor commitments for the programme would show whether it can be delivered [9].
- It is not shown how well the National Council for Digital Transformation coordinates implementation; minutes of its meetings or an evaluation of its work would settle it [4].
- The sources disagree on whether all state information systems have been registered in the state register: SIGMA says they have, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed; an updated audit or register extract would resolve this [27][28].
- The sources also give different targets for the share of digitised public services, 75% by the end of 2024 in a civil society account against 80% and 100% in the strategy's indicator table; the Government's annual implementation report against the agreed indicators would clarify which target applies [25][26].
