# Comparator brief: Domestic preconditions for the European Health Insurance Card not in place

RI-FMW-015 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end. **Draft policy options at the end are internal drafts** and stay so after sign-off at G7 until a separate decision adapts them for an external audience.

## The issue in Moldova (validated at G6)

**Unresolved gap (open).** The 2025 and 2026 key recommendations still require IT infrastructure, systems and capacity for the EHIC scheme. Only the feasibility study is implemented: the EHIC module in the health insurance information system, the amendments to the health care and health insurance laws, the technical specifications of the card and the integrated health information system are planned or under development, and the Government states that the EU acts on the card are not yet transposed (machine translated). What remains is a national health insurance card and the IT, legal and administrative basis to issue and accept the European Health Insurance Card at accession.

**Priority High, tractability Medium, direction of travel persistent.**

## Reading this brief (agent synthesis)

This brief compares Moldova's missing domestic preconditions for the European Health Insurance Card with three countries: Croatia, which issued the card from its accession in 2013; Montenegro, which recognises the card in law, has a feasibility study and an action plan, and is now asked to adapt its existing health information system; and Serbia, a contrast case in which the Commission recorded no developments for three years. Montenegro was rated of High relevance, Croatia of Medium relevance, because it shows the end state rather than Moldova's problem, and Serbia of Low relevance. No case shows how to build a national health insurance card or an integrated health information system where none exists, which is the core of Moldova's gap [1]. Croatia already had both before accession [2], and Montenegro already has a system to adapt [3]. The Croatian evidence dates from 2011 to 2014 and is used by logged exception, and its results rest on the issuing body's own reports. The cases therefore show the route and the end point, not how to lay the foundations. The lessons and transferability judgements are the tool's synthesis, validated by the analyst at G6b as conditional hypotheses; the transferability of the Serbian case could not be rated.

### CMP-0010: Croatia

**Comparator relevance: Medium.** Croatia matches Moldova on the requirement, issuing and accepting the card at accession, and is the only case in which the card was issued. It does not match on the problem: a search of all 68 Croatian evidence passages in the project found none showing a missing national card or health information system, and by 2013 HZZO issued national health insurance cards and ran the infrastructure for using CEZIH, while the Commission had found Croatia advanced in e-health in 2012. Moldova's gap is the absence of both, rooted in weak administrative capacity and insufficient resources, and the Croatian evidence dates from 2011 to 2014.

**Problem addressed.** In October 2011 the Commission reported that preparations continued for the introduction of the card upon accession [4]. No Croatian source in the project describes a missing precondition of the kind Moldova faces: in 2012 the Commission found Croatia advanced in the area of e-health, without linking this to the card [5], and in 2013 one HZZO service issued national health insurance cards and smart cards alongside the EHIC and managed the public key infrastructure that is the basis for using CEZIH [2]. A search of all 68 Croatian evidence passages in the project for the card, health insurance cards and the health information system found none that states when the national card or CEZIH was introduced.

**Reform or mechanism used.** The reform is the introduction of the card upon accession (Introduction of the European Health Insurance Card in Croatia), with a Regulation on the EHIC adopted in December 2011 that set the card's validity (one year for all insured persons, longer if necessary for posted workers and their family members), its form as a separate card and the other details needed to issue it [6]; Regulation on the European Health Insurance Card (December 2011)). The source names neither the issuing body nor the regulation's Croatian title. HZZO also agreed with the foreign and defence ministries a procedure for issuing the card to their officials [7]; Procedure for issuing European Health Insurance Cards to officials of the foreign and defence ministries). Work on the information systems ran in parallel: a hardware contract and migration of CEZIH and of HZZO's central information system, to be completed by May 2013 [8]; Central health information system of primary health care (CEZIH); Central information system of the Croatian Health Insurance Fund (HZZO)), a tender for consolidating all registers in the health system [9]; Consolidation of all registers in the health system), and further upgrades of both systems planned for 2013 [10][11]. None of these sources links the systems to the card.

**How it was implemented.** In October 2012, nine months before accession, the Commission reported that preparations in relation to the card were reaching completion [12]. In 2013 one HZZO service issued and personalised the card together with national health insurance cards and smart cards [2]. Two HZZO regional offices give issuing figures for 2013: Rijeka processed 35,124 requests for European cards and 5,320 for certificates [13], and Split issued 8,781 cards and certificates together [14]; the Rijeka text does not spell out that 'European cards' are the EHIC or that 'certificates' are the provisional replacement certificate. Whether the procedure for officials was agreed before or after accession is not stated [7].

**Evidence of results.** The only statements of results come from HZZO, the body that issues the card. It says it was fully ready for accession thanks to intense preparations [15] and began to function without major obstacles in the European bodies for social security coordination [16]. It says a vast number of EU insured persons received health care during their stay in Croatia by using the card [17], and that, although accession at the height of the tourist season was an added challenge, all EU citizens on holiday who needed care received adequate care [18][19]; the last two passages do not name the card. None of these statements is quantified. A search of all 68 Croatian evidence passages in the project, including those from the Commission's 2015 report, found no independent account of the card after accession.

**Limitations of the evidence.** All Croatian evidence dates from 2011 to 2014, before the period normally covered, and is used by logged exception. The HZZO reports are the issuing body's own accounts; their Croatian passages are machine translated and capped at Medium confidence, while the English foreword and the English passage on EHIC use [15][17] are not translated. Issuing figures exist for two regional offices only, with no national total, and the Split figure does not separate cards from certificates. The passages on the information systems do not name the card, so their part in it is not shown, and the completion of the 2012 migration and of the planned 2013 upgrades is not reported. HZZO's statement that it adapted all its contracted providers [19] is not tied to any recorded intervention.

**Practical lesson for Moldova (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Croatia shows the end state that Moldova's preparations aim at, with the health insurer issuing both the national card and the EHIC by 2013, working from a central health information system, and a secondary act fixing the card's validity, form and issuing details about 18 months before accession. Moldova's draft CNAM order on the card's technical specifications and the provisional replacement certificate [20] is the nearest counterpart of Croatia's 2011 regulation. The case does not show how to build a national card or an integrated health information system where none exists, which is Moldova's gap [1].

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** A secondary act on the card's validity, form and issuing, adopted well before accession [6]; an identified issuing body, as HZZO was in Croatia, which in Moldova would be CNAM, already the coordinator of EHIC implementation [21]; provision for particular groups such as posted workers and officials [7]; and preparing contracted providers to treat visitors from Member States, which HZZO's own account presents as its main test at accession [18][19].

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Croatia's starting point: a central health information system already in use and migrated to new hardware in 2012, national health insurance cards issued by HZZO by 2013, and a Commission finding in 2012 that Croatia was advanced in e-health [8][2][5], against Moldova's lack of a national card and of an integrated health information system [1][22]. Croatia prepared for a fixed accession date [15]. The evidence is 12 to 15 years old, so the technology and costs of a card system then are no guide now.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: a secondary act fixing the card's validity, form and issuing, with the health insurer as the issuing body, could work in Moldova, where CNAM already coordinates the card and a draft order on its technical specifications exists. This is conditional on Moldova first building what Croatia had in place by 2013, a national health insurance card and a central health information system, which the Croatian case does not show how to do.

### CMP-0011: Montenegro

**Comparator relevance: High.** Montenegro matches on the issue and the capacity mechanism: the Commission asks it to adapt its health information system for the card, equip its health insurance fund and budget for reimbursements by accession, against Moldova's requirement for IT infrastructure, systems and capacity for the EHIC scheme, and both countries have a feasibility study and adopted an action plan in 2026. The context differs: Montenegro already has an integrated health information system and recognises the card in law, while Moldova has no such system and has not transposed the EU acts on the card, and Montenegro has provisionally closed Chapter 2.

**Problem addressed.** In 2025 the Commission's key recommendation was to prepare a feasibility study on the impact of the card on public finances by the end of 2025 [23][24]. As the Commission reports it, the updated study found a basic legal and administrative framework in place, with technical and information capacities still to be strengthened [25]. The 2026 key recommendation asks Montenegro to improve its existing Integrated Health Information System and adapt it for issuing and applying the card, to equip the Health Insurance Fund with technical and administrative capacity and resources, and to budget for future reimbursements, all by accession [3]. This is the same mechanism as Moldova's, weak administrative capacity, with a budget element that matches insufficient resources in substance.

**Reform or mechanism used.** Legal recognition of the card in the Law on compulsory health insurance, with its introduction deferred to accession [26][27][28]; Law on compulsory health insurance (Montenegro)); the adoption date of the provision is not stated. A feasibility study on introducing the card (Feasibility study on the introduction of the EHIC (Montenegro)). The Action Plan for the Coordination of Social Security Systems 2026-2028, defining key activities, deadlines and institutional responsibilities [29]; Action Plan for the Coordination of Social Security Systems 2026-2028 (Montenegro)). The existing Integrated Health Information System, which is to be adapted for the card [3]; Integrated Health Information System (Montenegro)).

**How it was implemented.** The feasibility study was updated with 2025 statistical data and, as the Commission reports it, confirmed no significant financial obstacles to implementing the card [30][31]. Its findings were integrated into the action plan, which outlines the technical and IT upgrades required before accession [32], and the Government adopted the plan in February 2026 [29]. The Commission rated progress in the chapter as good in 2026 and named preparation for the card through the plan among its reasons [33][34]. A search of all 204 Montenegrin evidence passages in the project for the card, the health information system and the Health Insurance Fund found none reporting any of the plan's EHIC upgrades carried out, and the dates of the original study and its update are not stated.

**Evidence of results.** No result yet: the card is to be introduced only after accession [28]. The same search of all 204 Montenegrin evidence passages found none reporting the card issued, the system adapted or the Fund equipped. Chapter 2 was provisionally closed at the Accession Conference [35], on 15 June 2026 according to the Government [36], which says the closure confirmed fulfilment of the closing benchmark [37]. Neither source names the card, and the Commission's 2026 key recommendation, issued after the closure, still asks for the card's preconditions by accession, so the closure is a negotiating step and not a result on this issue.

**Limitations of the evidence.** Everything on the card comes from the Commission's reports: the feasibility study and the action plan themselves are not among the sources, and the study's findings are known only as the Commission reports them. The terms of the closing benchmark are not public, and the EU may return to the chapter [38]. The Government's account of the closure is machine translated and capped at Medium, and gives the venue as Luxembourg in one passage and Brussels in another [36][37]. The Commission does not describe the existing Integrated Health Information System, so how far it falls short of what the card needs is not known.

**Practical lesson for Moldova (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Montenegro is some months ahead of Moldova on the same route, a feasibility study on public finances followed by an action plan that carries its findings; Moldova finalised its study and approved its EHIC Action Plan in August 2026 [39][40]. Once those were in place, the Commission's request to Montenegro became concrete: adapt the health information system for the card, equip the insurer and budget for reimbursements by accession. Moldova faces the same tasks with the added one of building an integrated system Montenegro already has. Montenegro had also recognised the card in its health insurance law before the chapter closed, while Moldova plans its law amendments for 2026 [41]; the case does not show that this recognition, or deferring the card to accession, was what the EU required.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** The sequence of a feasibility study on public finances followed by an action plan with deadlines and responsibilities, which Moldova already mirrors; legal recognition of the card in the health insurance law ahead of its introduction; and the three elements the Commission named for the next step (system adapted for the card, insurer equipped, reimbursement budget), which match the weak administrative capacity and insufficient resources behind Moldova's gap [3].

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Montenegro already has an Integrated Health Information System to adapt, while Moldova has none [22]. Montenegro's study found a basic legal and administrative framework in place [25], while Moldova states that the EU acts on the card are not transposed [42]. Montenegro is closing chapters under benchmark terms that are not public, and whether the EU would accept deferring the card to accession for Moldova cannot be judged from the evidence.

**Transferability judgement: conditional.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: the route of a feasibility study, an action plan and legal recognition of the card, followed by adapting the health information system, equipping the insurer and budgeting for reimbursements, fits Moldova, which has already taken the first two steps. This is conditional on Moldova having an integrated health information system to adapt, which Montenegro already had, and on the EU accepting any deferral of the card to accession, which the evidence does not show.

### CMP-0012: Serbia

**Comparator relevance: Low.** Serbia's 2025 key recommendation on IT infrastructure for the card is worded almost as Moldova's, and the Commission recorded no developments on the card in 2024, 2025 and 2026, as it recorded no progress for Moldova in 2025, but that is a match on the EU's request and on a stall, not on the problem. A search of all 90 Serbian evidence passages in the project found none on a national health insurance card, a health information system or a Serbian measure for the card, so whether Serbia faces Moldova's missing preconditions is unknown. Since Moldova finalised a feasibility study and approved an action plan in August 2026, the stall no longer matches its position either, and the case shows no route to closing the gap.

**Problem addressed.** The 2025 key recommendation asks Serbia to develop IT infrastructures for the EHIC and EESSI, and administrative capacities for the future EESSI network [43], in nearly the same wording as Moldova's 2025 key recommendation [44]; the 2026 report repeats it [45]. Only the EHIC IT part belongs to this issue, as a matter of administrative capacity. Whether Serbia has a national health insurance card or an integrated health information system is not stated: a search of all 90 Serbian evidence passages in the project for the card, a national health insurance card and a health information system found only the eight Chapter 2 passages of the Commission's 2024 to 2026 reports that mention the card, none of them describing Serbia's systems.

**Reform or mechanism used.** None in the evidence. Serbia applies agreements on the use of the card with nine Member States [46][47][48], but these concern access through bilateral arrangements, a separate issue outside the scope of this comparison, and are not used in this case.

**How it was implemented.** Nothing to report: the Commission found no developments on the card in each of three reporting periods [49][50][51].

**Evidence of results.** None. The unchanged Commission wording across three reports is the only evidence, and it records an absence of change rather than a result.

**Limitations of the evidence.** Only the Commission's reports cover the card for Serbia: the search of all 90 Serbian evidence passages in the project found no government, research or civil society source on it. The reports give no account of Serbia's systems, plans or reasons, so the case shows that the position stalled, not why.

**Practical lesson for Moldova (agent synthesis).** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: Serbia received nearly the same recommendation as Moldova in 2025, and the Commission recorded no developments in 2024, 2025 and 2026, including in the year after that recommendation, with no feasibility study or plan visible in the evidence. Moldova has already gone further, with a feasibility study and an EHIC Action Plan in August 2026 [39][40], so the contrast mainly shows what the Commission records when such steps are missing. It offers no lesson on the causes of a stall.

**What is transferable (agent synthesis).** Only as a warning: the Commission recorded no developments in 2024, 2025 and 2026, including in the year after its 2025 key recommendation, so the recommendation alone did not move the position within a year [49][43][51].

**What is not transferable (agent synthesis).** Serbia's starting point on the card, its health information systems and its negotiating context on this issue are not in the evidence, so no condition for comparison with Moldova can be stated.

**Transferability judgement: insufficient_evidence.** Agent synthesis, not stated by a source: the Serbian evidence contains no measure or mechanism that could be applied in Moldova, only three years of unchanged Commission wording with no stated reason. It serves as a contrast, but there is no lesson whose transfer can be rated.

## Lessons across the cases (agent synthesis)

Three points recur. First, once a feasibility study and a plan exist, the Commission's request becomes concrete. After Montenegro's updated study and its February 2026 action plan, the Commission asked it to adapt its health information system for the card, equip its Health Insurance Fund and budget for reimbursements, all by accession [32][29][3]. Moldova reached the same point in August 2026, when it finalised its feasibility study and approved an EHIC Action Plan [39][40], so the same three tasks are likely to follow, with the added task of building the integrated system that Montenegro already has [22]. Second, legal recognition is not delivery. Montenegro recognises the card in its health insurance law but will introduce it only after accession [28], and the Commission still asks for its preconditions after the chapter was provisionally closed; whether the EU would accept such a deferral for Moldova cannot be judged from the evidence. Third, a recommendation alone did not move the position in Serbia: the Commission recorded no developments on the card in 2024, 2025 and 2026, including in the year after a key recommendation worded almost as Moldova's [49][43][51][44]. Croatia adds that a secondary act fixing the card's validity, form and issuing was adopted about 18 months before accession [6], and that its insurer saw preparing contracted providers to treat visitors as the main test at accession [18][19].

## Draft policy options (agent synthesis, internal draft)

These options are internal drafts for discussion. Each rests on a validated unresolved gap (open) and on comparator cases validated at G6b; each transferability judgement is agent synthesis, rated conditional for Croatia and Montenegro. None is proposed because another country adopted it, and no case shows that any of these steps made the card work, since only Croatia issued it and its results come from its own insurer.

**Option 1: Set the national health insurance card and the integrated health information system as dated, budgeted steps that come before the card itself.**
- *Gap addressed:* the missing national health insurance card and integrated health information system, without which Moldova cannot issue or accept the European Health Insurance Card [1][22][52].
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* Croatia (relevance Medium) shows the end state, with its insurer issuing the national card and the European card together by 2013 from a central health information system [2][8]. Montenegro (relevance High) shows that, after the study and the plan, the Commission's next request is to adapt the information system, equip the insurer and budget for reimbursements [3]. Transferability conditional: both countries had the foundations Moldova lacks, so neither case shows how to build them, only that they come first.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the content of Moldova's EHIC Action Plan, which the evidence describes only as a roadmap for operational implementation [40]; a decision on how the card module under development in the health insurance information system relates to the wider integrated system [53].
- *Main risks:* building a card module before the national system it depends on; a plan that sets steps without a budget, given that the work depends on external and national funds.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government, which the Commission has asked since 2024 to develop an integrated e-health system [54], with the National Health Insurance Company (CNAM), which coordinates the card [21].

**Option 2: Adopt the legal basis and the secondary act early, with provision for particular groups.**
- *Gap addressed:* the legal basis for the card, since the EU acts on it are not transposed and the amendments to the health care and compulsory health insurance laws are planned for 2026 [42][41].
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); the mechanisms recorded for this issue do not include a legal gap, although the missing transposition is part of what remains.
- *Comparator evidence:* Croatia's 2011 regulation set the card's validity, its form as a separate card and the details needed to issue it, with longer validity for posted workers [6], and its insurer agreed a procedure for issuing the card to officials [7]. Moldova's draft CNAM order on the card's technical specifications and the provisional replacement certificate is the nearest counterpart [20]. Montenegro recognised the card in its health insurance law ahead of its introduction [28]. Transferability conditional: a secondary act could work in Moldova, but only on top of a national card and information system that Croatia already had.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the law amendments adopted before the CNAM order; decisions on groups such as posted workers and officials.
- *Main risks:* treating adoption as progress: Montenegro shows a card recognised in law but not issued, so adopted acts would need to be followed by evidence that the card is issued and accepted.
- *Who would need to act:* the Government and Parliament for the law amendments, and CNAM for the order on the card.

**Option 3: Staff and fund the insurer for issuing the card and reimbursing care, and prepare providers to treat visitors.**
- *Gap addressed:* the capacity and resources to run the scheme, which the 2026 key recommendation names [52].
- *Mechanism targeted:* weak administrative capacity (MEC-03) and insufficient resources (MEC-09).
- *Comparator evidence:* the Commission asks Montenegro to equip its Health Insurance Fund with technical and administrative capacity and to budget for future reimbursements by accession [3]. Croatia's insurer presented the treatment of visitors at the height of the tourist season as its test at accession [18][19], a self-report with no figures. CNAM's IT directorate had 3 of 8 planned specialists working on the card's IT components, according to a machine-translated government report [55]. Transferability conditional, as for Montenegro.
- *Conditions and preconditions:* the financial impact estimated in Moldova's finalised feasibility study [39], whose findings are not in the evidence.
- *Main risks:* posts planned but not filled; reimbursements not budgeted until after accession.
- *Who would need to act:* CNAM, with the Government for its budget.

**A warning rather than an option: a recommendation and a plan on paper will not by themselves move the position.**
Serbia (relevance Low) received in 2025 a key recommendation on IT infrastructure for the card worded almost as Moldova's, and the Commission recorded no developments in that year or the next [43][51]. The Serbian case gives no reason for the stall and its transferability cannot be rated, so it supports only a caution: Moldova recorded no progress on the card in 2025 [56], and its 2026 study and action plan are planning steps that do not yet show a change in the card or the health information system.
