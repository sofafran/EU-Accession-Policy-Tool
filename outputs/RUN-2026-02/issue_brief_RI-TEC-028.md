# Issue brief: Interoperability platform and data exchange underused

RI-TEC-028 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Institutions do not use the national interoperability platform regularly, so administrative data are not shared ex officio between institutions, including for checks such as asset declarations and inspections. **Problem mechanisms:** Coordination failure (MEC-08); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to strengthen interoperability between its automated asset verification systems and state registries, and the Reform Agenda requires all declarations to be filed by December 2026 in a new e-Integrity system that is interoperable through the MConnect platform [1][2]. More broadly, the issue is whether institutions use the national interoperability platform regularly. Moldova has had a law since 2018 requiring agencies to exchange data only through the interoperability solution, with an express duty to connect and sanctions in force since January 2021 [3][4][5][6]. SIGMA found MConnect well functioning in 2023, with base registers connected and 336 institutions exchanging data, and the Government reports 144 state and 252 private systems integrated, a different measure [7][8][9][10]. One result is shown: the Commission reports that data exchange through MConnect let the electoral commission check party financing more effectively, though it gives no measure of this [11]. Underuse persists. The World Bank finds that many ministries work in silos, the Government finds its systems fragmented, and the data protection authority reports that 13 entities still access state registers through COI technology outside the platform, with no progress despite repeated requests [12][13][14][15]. In 2026 the Commission found the integrity authority's interoperable verification tools still constrained [16]. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The platform and the legal duty to exchange data through it are in place, with sanctions since 2021, and SIGMA found the base registers connected to it in 2023. The data protection authority finds that state bodies still access personal data through COI technology outside the platform, which conflicts with the government's statement that data exchange takes place exclusively through MConnect, and the Commission in 2026 still finds the interoperable verification tools for asset declarations not fully functioning. What remains is regular use of the platform by all institutions, including an end to access outside it, and full data exchange for asset declaration checks.

**Direction of travel.** The Commission reported in 2023 that data exchange through MConnect let the electoral commission check party financing more effectively, but in 2025 it said interoperability between automated verification systems and state registries must still be strengthened, and in 2026 it noted constraints on the full practical functioning of interoperable verification tools. Other sources show connections continuing (336 institutions in 2023 according to SIGMA; 396 integrated state and private systems in 2025 according to the Government, a different measure) while the same underuse is reported: the World Bank in 2024 found interoperability yet to be streamlined, the government in 2025 found systems fragmented, and the data protection authority in 2026 found state bodies still using COI access outside the platform. The Chapter 10 rating was not decisive, as the issue is cross-cutting.

**Priority.** Interoperability for asset declaration checks is part of a Chapter 23 Reform Agenda step on the new e-Integrity system, with interoperability ensured through MConnect, is named in the 2025 main findings on Chapter 23 and is repeated as a finding in 2026. These EU items concern only asset declaration checks, and a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no benchmark, key recommendation or Reform Agenda payment condition on use of the platform as a whole, although regular use of the platform is central to the interoperability theme.

**Tractability.** The platform, the law and sanctions already exist and connections have grown, which is evidence of past progress. Against that, the remaining problem lies with many institutions that the World Bank says work in silos, and the data protection authority reports that bodies notified repeatedly have still not moved to the platform.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has raised interoperability mainly in the context of asset declaration checks under the rule of law chapter, not as a requirement on use of the platform as a whole [1]. In 2023 its only relevant item was positive, recording the benefit of MConnect for checks on party financing [11]. The Reform Agenda made interoperability through MConnect part of a step on the new e-Integrity system, due by December 2026 [2]. The 2025 report then said that interoperability between automated verification systems and state registries must be strengthened and the services adequately staffed [1]. The 2026 report repeated the concern as a finding, that the automated and interoperable verification tools remain constrained, while noting that the integrity authority had consulted on a new e-Integrity concept in August 2026 [16][17]. No benchmark or key recommendation addresses use of the platform generally [16].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- The Government states that all data exchange runs exclusively through MConnect, while the data protection authority finds that state bodies still access personal data through COI technology outside it; data from the e-Governance Agency on remaining COI connections would settle this confirmed conflict [22][21].
- SIGMA gives 665.7 million MConnect transactions in 2022 alone, while the e-Governance Agency gives 600 million exchanges in total, possibly different measures, and a definition of what each counts would settle it [20][19].
- Whether the new e-Integrity system is in place by December 2026, with working interoperability for asset checks, would be shown by the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step [2][17].
- Whether the fines for breaching the interoperability law have ever been imposed would be shown by enforcement records [5].
