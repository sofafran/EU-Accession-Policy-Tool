# Issue brief: Broadband and high-capacity network coverage uneven

RI-TEC-013 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Small businesses and rural or non-urban areas face obstacles to fixed broadband, and high-capacity and 5G networks are still being rolled out. **Problem mechanisms:** Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has asked Moldova to prioritise infrastructure investment to overcome the rural-urban digital divide, and it reports that 5G coverage remains well below the EU average [1][2]. Moldova's regulator set licence conditions for the 700 MHz and 800 MHz bands in August 2024, with coverage obligations from October 2027, and awarded spectrum to three operators in February 2025 and to Moldtelecom in a further 2025 competition [3][4][5]. A new law on electronic communications entered into force in January 2026, and it requires the regulator to survey broadband coverage [6]. Coverage and take-up are shown to have risen: home internet access went from 67.5% of households in 2023 to nearly 95% in 2026, fixed-broadband connections rose by 6.9% in the year to early 2026, and median download speeds rose by about a third [7][8][9][10]. The Commission estimates 5G population coverage at 25 to 35%, and the success of the spectrum awards is assessed as too early to judge [11][12]. What remains is high-capacity fixed and 5G coverage in rural and small localities: the Government counts 431 localities without fixed broadband, most of them small and isolated, and the plan to build networks there waits for the regulator's survey [13][14][15]. Much of the Government and regulator evidence is machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Coverage and take-up have risen, and the Commission's assessment for the third Reform and Growth Facility release records 6.9% more fixed-broadband connections and faster speeds between 2025 and 2026, but 5G coverage remains well below the EU average and the Reform Agenda describes a rural-urban digital divide. The government counts 431 localities in white areas without fixed broadband, and the plan to develop networks there is proposed and waits for the regulator's geographic survey. What remains is high-capacity fixed and 5G coverage in rural and small localities.

**Direction of travel.** In 2023 the Commission reported that 67.5% of households had internet at home (58% in rural areas), in 2025 that high-speed broadband reached 57% of the population by mid-2024 while regional disparities persisted, and in 2026 that nearly 95% of households had internet access. The 2026 sub-area wording on 5G deployment, which matches this part of the issue, is some progress, with coverage of 25 to 35%; the Chapter 10 rating was used as context only, as the issue is part of the chapter.

**Priority.** The rural-urban digital divide is named in a 2024 economic key recommendation on infrastructure investment, the Reform Agenda describes it and the 2026 report rates 5G deployment, but a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no Chapter 10 key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on coverage. The related Reform Agenda step concerns alignment with the Electronic Communications Code, which belongs to another issue.

**Tractability.** Spectrum was awarded through competitions in 2024 and 2025, operators are investing and the Commission records progress on 5G, which is evidence of past progress. Closing the remaining gaps depends on private operators and on public funding for small, isolated localities, which the government says require significant investment.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's concern has narrowed from a general one to a specific one. In 2023 it found that digitalisation in Moldova was lagging behind and that connectivity gaps remained, contrasting near-universal 4G coverage with home internet access of 67.5% of households, and 58% in rural areas [16][17][7][18]. In 2024 an economic key recommendation asked Moldova to prioritise infrastructure investment, including to overcome the rural-urban digital divide [1]. In 2025 the Commission found digitalisation steadily advancing while regional disparities persisted, and the Reform Agenda described a significant urban-rural divide [19][20]. By 2026 the focus had moved to 5G, with some progress recorded but coverage well below the EU average [11][2]. No Chapter 10 key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses coverage; the related Reform Agenda step on electronic communications, assessed as satisfactorily fulfilled, concerns legal alignment rather than coverage [21].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether operators meet the coverage obligations in the licence conditions by October 2027 would be shown by the regulator's compliance reports [3].
- Whether 5G coverage rises after the 2025 awards could be settled by independent coverage data, such as later European 5G Observatory figures, since the Moldtelecom 4G gains rest only on the regulator's report [2][23].
- Whether the geographic survey is carried out and networks are then built in the white areas would be shown by the survey and the list of localities connected [6][15].
- Whether the rural-urban gap in home internet access has closed is not shown, as the 2026 household figure is national, and rural and urban data would settle it [8][18].
