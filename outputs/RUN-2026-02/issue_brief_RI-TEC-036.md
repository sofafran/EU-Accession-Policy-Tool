# Issue brief: National e-services portal limited in range and use

RI-TEC-036 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The national e-services portal offers too few transactional services, is little used, or does not serve all users. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Coordination failure (MEC-08); Low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU expects Moldova to offer a full range of public services online through its national portal, used by all groups. The Reform Agenda set two conditions: an e-government ecosystem for business services by December 2025, and the EVO Sign digital wallet integrating at least 20 digital public services by December 2026 [1][2]. Moldova launched the EVO application in June 2024 and EVO 2.0 in June 2026, brought services together on the integrated EVO portal, and set up 118 Unified Public Service Centres to give rural residents access to electronic services [3][4][5]. The Commission reports that 447 of the 636 services on the EVO portal, 70.3 percent, are available online, and that by June 2026 82 percent of business services and 61 percent of citizen services were digitalised [4][6]. It verified that 310 of 414 business services are digitalised, meeting the 75 percent objective, although only 133 run fully end to end [7]. User uptake increased significantly, including in rural areas, but the Commission gives no rural figure and low demand persists there [8][9]. The portal lacks integration with EU systems, and its link to the Single Digital Gateway is still planned [10][11]. What remains is a fuller range of citizen services delivered end to end online, integration with EU systems, and use by people with weaker digital access or skills. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The Reform Agenda target of 75% of business services digitalised has been met, and EVO, the public services portal and 118 Unified Public Service Centres are in use; but in 2026 only 61% of citizen services are digitalised, only 133 of 414 business services run fully end to end, the Reform Agenda notes that the portal lacks integration with EU systems, and the link to the Single Digital Gateway is still planned. Demand remains low in rural areas, which is why the Commission says the centres were set up. What remains is a fuller range of citizen services delivered end to end online, integration with EU systems, and use by people with weaker digital access or skills.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 10 scale is context only, though its progress wording cites the digitalisation of public services in 2024 and progress on all key performance indicators in 2025. Commission figures are about 40% in both 2023 (business services online) and 2024 (mapped services) and rise from then on, on differing measures: over 50% in 2025 (64% for businesses and 54% for individuals in 2024), and in 2026 70.3% of the 636 services on the EVO portal online, 82% of business and 61% of citizen services digitalised and user uptake up significantly. Government counts of 2023 business service requests conflict between its 2024 and 2025 documents, so they are not used for the trend.

**Priority.** Digital public services are the subject of two Reform Agenda payment conditions: the EVO Sign wallet integrating at least 20 e-services by December 2026, which is still open and shared with the electronic identification issue, and 75% of business services digitalised, which the Commission found met in 2026. The Commission's 2024 Chapter 10 progress wording cites notable progress on digitalising public services, and the portal and its services are central to the digital government theme, so the rating rests on the open wallet condition rather than on the fulfilled step.

**Tractability.** Progress has been steady and measured by the Commission in every report from 2023 to 2026, the e-Governance Agency runs the shared platforms, and the work is tied to Reform Agenda funding. The main constraints are cost, which the Government says exceeds its own resources, and low demand among older and rural users.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2022 Opinion recorded only that the Government's Action Plan for 2021-2022 set objectives on extending electronic services for citizens and entrepreneurs [12]. In 2023 the Commission found digitalisation lagging behind, with about 40 percent of government services for businesses available online, and set this against the weak results of the Digital Moldova 2020 strategy, which reached 12 percent of services online against a 100 percent objective [13][14]. In 2024 it noted notable progress on digitalising public services but still put about 40 percent of mapped services online, with varying delivery conditions [15][16]. The 2025 Reform Agenda turned the requirement into dated payment conditions and noted the portal's lack of integration with EU systems, and the 2025 report found over half of mapped services available digitally [1][2][10][17]. By 2026 the Commission's language had moved from availability to uptake: it reported a significant increase in use while noting persistent low demand in rural areas [8]. The Commission's measures differ from report to report, so the percentages are not strictly comparable.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the EVO Sign wallet will integrate at least 20 digital public services by December 2026 is open; the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step would settle it [2].
- How many people use the Unified Public Service Centres, and whether rural use is rising, is not measured by any independent source; service counts by centre would settle it [5][8].
- Whether the portal will be integrated with the EU Single Digital Gateway, and when, is unclear; the adopted amendment to Law No. 234/2021 and a working connection would show it [11][10].
- Government counts of business service requests in 2023 differ between its 2024 and 2025 documents, possibly because they use different measures; a single published series would settle it [27][26].
