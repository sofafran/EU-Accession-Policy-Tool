# Issue brief: Broadband planning and investment framework incomplete

RI-TEC-014 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National broadband targets, plans and investment are not aligned with EU Digital Decade targets, and EU funding instruments, State aid rules and backhaul studies are not used or in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to raise investment in infrastructure, including to overcome the rural-urban digital divide, and the Reform Agenda required a broadband mapping system to support targeted investment [1][2]. The regulator operates a public broadband coverage platform, expanded since 2025, and Moldova has put into operation a map of very high capacity networks, built on a data-sharing agreement of March 2026 with the cadastre bodies and populated with data from the three mobile operators and the six largest fixed operators [3][4][5][6][7]. The Commission finds that the two platforms provide relevant monitoring for digital investment decisions [8]. The investment framework itself is less advanced. The regulator's geographical survey of broadband coverage has a legal deadline of 30 December 2030, and network development in white areas is to follow it [9][10]. Rules on state aid for broadband networks exist only as a planned Competition Council decision [11]. The Government notes that most white-area localities are small and isolated and need significant investment [12], and the Commission states that infrastructure investment remains significantly below needs [13]. What remains is a national investment framework, with state aid rules and funding, that brings broadband to rural and white areas in line with EU connectivity targets. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The mapping system is in operation, but the geographical survey of broadband coverage has a legal deadline of December 2030, the development of networks in white areas waits for it, and the state aid rules for broadband exist only as a draft. What remains is a national investment framework, with state aid rules and funding, that brings broadband to rural and white areas in line with EU connectivity targets.

**Direction of travel.** The 2023 and 2024 reports call for more investment in infrastructure, including to overcome the rural-urban digital divide, and the 2025 Reform Agenda notes the absence of a comprehensive broadband mapping system. In 2026 the Commission found that the regulator's coverage platform and a new map of very high capacity networks provide relevant monitoring for investment decisions, while the 2026 report says that infrastructure investment remains well below needs. The issue spans several chapters, so chapter ratings were not decisive.

**Priority.** The broadband mapping system was a Reform Agenda step, now fulfilled, and the 2023 and 2024 key recommendations on competitiveness ask for infrastructure investment, but they name digital infrastructure alongside energy and transport. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no key recommendation specific to broadband planning.

**Tractability.** The mapping step was delivered by 2026 through data sharing among the regulator, the cadastre agency and operators, and a World Bank project is analysing financing models. Extending networks to small and isolated localities needs substantial investment, and the Government's own timetable for the coverage survey runs to 2030.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2023 report asked Moldova to increase investment in infrastructure, especially for energy security, international trade and digitalisation [14]. The 2024 report sharpened this to prioritising investment to overcome the rural-urban digital divide, among other aims [1]. In both years digital infrastructure appears alongside energy and transport rather than as a separate recommendation on broadband. The 2025 Reform Agenda added a specific step, noting that the absence of a comprehensive broadband mapping system restricted monitoring, investment strategies and the targeting of public subsidies, and committing Moldova to develop one [15][2]. By 2026 the Commission recorded the mapping platforms as providing relevant monitoring, while the 2026 report observed that investment remained significantly below infrastructure needs [8][13].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the regulator will complete the geographical survey well before its 2030 deadline is not known; a published survey timetable or first results would show when white-area investment can start [9].
- The state aid regulation for broadband networks was planned for August 2026, and no source shows it adopted; the Competition Council decision itself would settle it [11].
- How investment in white areas will be financed is not set out; the financing models from the World Bank broadband project, or a funded investment plan, would answer it [17].
- It is not known whether the national broadband strategy mentioned in 2023 was adopted or is still applied; the strategy document or a Government statement on its status would clarify how targets relate to EU connectivity goals [18].
