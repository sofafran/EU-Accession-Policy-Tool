# Issue brief: Domestic preconditions for the European Health Insurance Card not in place

RI-FMW-015 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The country has no national health insurance card or integrated health information system, so it cannot yet issue or accept the European Health Insurance Card at accession. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to develop the IT infrastructure, systems, capacity and resources needed for full participation in the European Health Insurance Card scheme, and it has found in each report from 2023 to 2025 that no national health insurance card exists because there is no integrated health information system [1][2][3][4][5]. The National Health Insurance Company coordinates the work, and a feasibility study on the financial impact of the card was finalised in August 2026, when an EHIC Action Plan was also approved as a roadmap [6][7][8]. The feasibility study is the only step assessed as implemented. The card module in the health insurance information system is being developed, the amendments to the health care and health insurance laws are planned for 2026, and the order on the card's technical specifications is a draft, none of them shown adopted or in operation [9][10][11]. The Government states that the EU acts on the card are not yet transposed, and the Commission found in 2024 that an integrated health information system is not yet in place [12][13]. No card has been issued. What remains is a national health insurance card and the IT, legal and administrative basis to issue and accept the European Health Insurance Card at accession. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2025 and 2026 key recommendations still require IT infrastructure, systems and capacity for the EHIC scheme. Only the feasibility study is implemented: the EHIC module in the health insurance information system, the amendments to the health care and health insurance laws, the technical specifications of the card and the integrated health information system are planned or under development, and the Government states that the EU acts on the card are not yet transposed (machine translated). What remains is a national health insurance card and the IT, legal and administrative basis to issue and accept the European Health Insurance Card at accession.

**Direction of travel.** Sub-area wording on the European Health Insurance Card, which matches this issue, records no progress in 2025, and the finding that no national health insurance card exists because there is no integrated health information system is unchanged in the 2023 report, the 2023 Analytical Report and the 2024 and 2025 reports. In 2026 the Commission reports a finalised feasibility study and an approved EHIC Action Plan, which are planning steps and do not yet show a change in the card or the health information system.

**Priority.** The EHIC is named in the Chapter 2 key recommendations of 2025 and 2026, and the finding that its domestic preconditions are missing is repeated in each report from 2023 to 2025, with no progress recorded in 2025. Issuing and accepting the card is a core acquis requirement for accession, which is why the issue is rated High although the key recommendation itself appears in only two reports.

**Tractability.** The national health insurance company coordinates the work, the feasibility study is finished, an action plan sets a roadmap and the law amendments are planned for 2026. Against that, the card depends on an integrated health information system that the Commission found missing in each report from 2023 to 2025, and the insurer's IT directorate had 3 of 8 planned specialists (machine translated).

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's analytical report of February 2023 found that no national health insurance card exists because there is no integrated health information system, and that implementing the European Health Insurance Card might be challenging [14][15]. The 2023 and 2024 reports repeat both findings in the same words [3][16][4][17]. The 2024 report also recommended implementing the national health strategy, including an integrated e-health system [18]. The 2025 report records no progress on the card, repeats the finding on the national card and adds a key recommendation to develop IT infrastructure for the card [19][5][1]. The 2026 report widens this to the measures, capacity, resources and IT systems needed for full participation in the scheme, alongside EURES and EESSI, and reports the feasibility study and action plan [2][20][8].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the amendments to the health care and health insurance laws have been adopted is not shown, and their publication in the Official Gazette or a Commission finding would settle it [22][10].
- Whether the card module in the health insurance information system is operating is not reported, and evidence of cards or provisional replacement certificates being issued would be needed [9][23].
- No source records a plan or decision on the national health insurance card itself, and a Government decision or Commission finding on it would establish whether this precondition is being addressed [5].
- Whether the health insurance company has filled its planned IT posts is not known, and later staffing data would show whether it has the capacity to deliver the action plan [21][8].
