# Issue brief: Interoperability framework not aligned with EU

RI-TEC-027 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The national interoperability framework is not aligned with the latest European Interoperability Framework or the Interoperable Europe Act. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The issue concerns aligning Moldova's national interoperability framework with the latest European Interoperability Framework and with the Interoperable Europe Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/903. It is not raised as a requirement in the Commission's reports or the Reform Agenda; the EU material is a Growth Plan support measure that includes alignment with the European Interoperability Framework [1]. Moldova has a working legal and technical interoperability framework: Law No. 142/2018 on data exchange and interoperability obliges agencies to exchange data only through the interoperability solution, and the law and the decision on the MConnect platform are in force [2][3]. The Government stated in 2025, in machine-translated text, that the national framework follows the principles of the European Interoperability Framework but that the Interoperable Europe Act is not transposed [4]. It planned to amend Law No. 142/2018 to transpose the Act, and the start of drafting was announced on 3 February 2026 [5][6]. No source shows progress beyond that notice, and no outcome has been assessed. An international factsheet notes that Moldova is not yet part of the European Interoperability Framework data collection [7]. What remains is transposition of the Interoperable Europe Act into the interoperability law and alignment of the national framework with the latest European Interoperability Framework. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** By the government's own account the Interoperable Europe Act has not been transposed, and the law amending Law No. 142/2018 to do so was only initiated in February 2026; the Interoperable Europe factsheet notes that Moldova is not yet part of the European Interoperability Framework data collection. What remains is transposition of the Interoperable Europe Act into the data exchange and interoperability law and alignment of the national framework with the latest European Interoperability Framework.

**Direction of travel.** A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files for the Interoperable Europe Act and the European Interoperability Framework found no Commission finding or recommendation on the national framework's alignment; the EU items are a Growth Plan support measure and a 2026 note that the open data law is in line with the European Interoperability Framework. The government stated in 2025 that the national framework follows the Framework's principles but that the Interoperable Europe Act is not transposed, and drafting of the amending law was announced in February 2026, which is too short a record to show a direction.

**Priority.** The issue is not raised as a requirement in the Commission's reports or the Reform Agenda: a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no such item, which keeps priority from being High. It rests on the government's own gap analysis and an EU support measure under the Growth Plan.

**Tractability.** The change is one amendment to an existing law, prepared by the government, and Moldova already has a working legal and technical interoperability framework. Drafting began only in February 2026, and no item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files shows progress beyond the February 2026 drafting notice.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has not set a requirement on this issue. No item from the 2022 Opinion or the 2023 to 2026 reports contains a finding or recommendation on the alignment of the national interoperability framework, and no Reform Agenda condition covers it. The EU references are indirect. In 2024 the Growth Plan support for Moldova covered the EU Digital Identity Wallet and alignment with the EU Digital Identity framework and the European Interoperability Framework [1]. In 2026 the Commission noted that a law adopted in July 2026, partly aligning with the Open Data Directive, is in line with the European Interoperability Framework [8]. The issue therefore rests on the Government's own gap analysis, which since 2025 has listed alignment with the European Interoperability Framework among its e-government priorities and planned the transposition of the Interoperable Europe Act [9][10][5].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the law amending Law No. 142/2018 to transpose the Interoperable Europe Act has been drafted, submitted or adopted would be settled by the draft on the consultation portal or the adopted text [6].
- How far the national framework departs from the latest European Interoperability Framework is not shown; an independent assessment, or Moldova's inclusion in the European Interoperability Framework data collection, would settle it [7][4].
- Whether the planned changes to the MConnect platform decision, including interoperability regulatory sandboxes, have been made would be shown by the amended decision [11].
- Whether the Commission will treat the Interoperable Europe Act as a requirement for Moldova is not known; a future report finding on it would settle the issue's standing [1].
