# Issue brief: Information and support for mobile workers on their rights

RI-FMW-007 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** There is no designated body supporting EU mobile workers in their rights and the system for informing them of their rights is weak. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU requirement is a body that supports EU workers in their rights and informs them, and in 2023 the Commission found that Moldova had no specific body responsible for enforcing the right to free movement of workers [1]. Moldova's Law No. 200/2025 gives the Equality Council the competence to promote, monitor and support equal treatment of EU citizens and their family members, and makes it the national contact point for cooperation with similar bodies in EU Member States [2][3]. The Commission confirms that the law entered into force in March 2026 [4]. This is a designation in law, not a shown result. The steps needed to make it work are still planned: an amendment to the Equality Council law, a restructuring with more staff, training of Council and public authority staff, and information and referral measures by the General Inspectorate for Migration [5][6][7][8][9]. The Council had filled 19 of 33 planned posts, and its anti-discrimination directorate, which handles complaints, 4 of 7 [10][11]. No source shows the Council at work for EU workers. The Government sources are machine translated. What remains is a body that in practice supports EU workers and informs them of their rights, with the mandate, structure and staff to do so. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is Low and tractability is High.

**What remains.** Law No. 200/2025 is adopted and designates the Equality Council, which answers the 2023 finding in law. The amendment of the Equality Council law, the Council's restructuring and extra staff, training for public authority and Council staff, and information measures by the General Inspectorate for Migration are all still planned, and the Council had filled 19 of 33 posts (Government figures, machine translated). What remains is a body that in practice supports EU workers and informs them of their rights, with the mandate, structure and staff to do so.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this is part of the chapter and the only Chapter 2 sub-area wording among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files concerns legal alignment in general (2023) and the European Health Insurance Card (2025). The 2023 Analytical Report found no body responsible for enforcing the right to free movement of workers; Law No. 200/2025 gives the Equality Council that competence and the role of national contact point, as the Government reports in a machine-translated source, and the 2026 report confirms that the law entered into force in March 2026 to align with the acquis on workers' free movement rights. The improvement is legal only: the 2026 report does not assess the body, and the ten items of this run that name the Equality Council, all from the Government, concern its designation, staffing or planned measures, and none shows it at work for EU workers.

**Priority.** The missing enforcement body is raised only in the 2023 Analytical Report: a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no later EU item on the Equality Council or an enforcement body, and no benchmark, Reform Agenda payment condition or key recommendation naming it. It is a specific acquis obligation within the chapter but not central to it, and the 2026 key recommendations address alignment and equal treatment of EU workers in general terms.

**Tractability.** The legal designation is done, and the remaining steps (amending the Equality Council law, restructuring and recruiting) are within the Government's control, with one lead body already in place. Staffing is the main constraint: the Council had 19 of 33 planned posts filled and its anti-discrimination directorate 4 of 7 (Government figures, machine translated).

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has raised this issue only once, in its February 2023 Analytical Report, which found that no specific body was yet responsible for enforcing the right to free movement of workers [1][12]. No later Commission item in the evidence returns to the Equality Council or to an enforcement body, and no benchmark, Reform Agenda payment condition or key recommendation names it [12]. The 2026 report records that the law on free movement and residence of EU citizens entered into force in March 2026, aiming to align with the acquis on the rights of workers in the context of free movement, but it does not assess the body the law designates [4]. The 2026 key recommendations address alignment and equal treatment of EU workers in general terms [13][14]. The EU requirement has therefore not changed in wording since 2023; what has changed is that Moldova has answered the 2023 finding in law.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the amendment to the Equality Council law is adopted, giving the Council the powers to carry out its new tasks, would be shown by the adopted law [5].
- Whether the Council is restructured and recruits enough staff would be shown by its approved structure and staffing figures after 2025 [6][10].
- Whether the Council handles discrimination complaints from EU citizens and acts as national contact point would be shown by its activity reports or case data [3][15].
- Whether EU citizens arriving in Moldova are informed of their rights and referred would be shown by evidence of the Migration Inspectorate's information measures in operation [9].
