# Issue brief: Sector and local online public services limited

RI-TEC-037 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Online public services run by sector bodies or municipalities, outside the national portal, are limited in range, use or quality. **Problem mechanisms:** Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03); Insufficient resources (MEC-09); Low uptake of systems already in place (MEC-11).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission has asked Moldova in four successive reports to digitalise processes for businesses: to simplify and digitalise processes to cut administrative burden in 2023 and 2024, and to digitalise the processes of the Entrepreneurship Development Organisation in 2025 and 2026 [1][2][3][4]. The Reform Agenda required an e-government ecosystem for business services, a fully operational State Register of Legal Units and a life events methodology by December 2025 [5]. The register portal was launched on 29 December 2025 and the methodology approved on 18 December 2025, and the Commission verified that 310 of 414 business services are digitalised, meeting the 75% objective, and judged the step satisfactorily fulfilled [6][7][8][9]. That outcome is assessed as having worked, but only 133 of the 414 services are fully digital end to end [10][8]. By June 2026, 82% of business services and 61% of citizen services were digitalised, and the Commission reports that use of e-services increased significantly, including in rural areas, though it gives no rural figure and does not attribute the rise to the public service centres set up there [11][12]. An audit of the permits system found improved accessibility but only partial use and some reluctance by authorities, a mixed result [13]. The systems of the entrepreneurship and employment agencies are still being procured or built [14][15]. What remains is end-to-end online delivery and wider use of sector services, especially for citizens and in agencies such as the entrepreneurship and employment agencies. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is High.

**What remains.** The business services reform is in place, with the State Register of Legal Units launched in December 2025 and the life events methodology approved, and the Commission judges the payment step satisfactorily fulfilled. Still, only 133 of the 414 business services counted are fully digital end to end, citizen services lag at 61%, the Commission noted low demand in 2025, and the systems of the entrepreneurship agency and the employment agency are still being procured or built. What remains is end-to-end online delivery and wider use of sector services, especially for citizens and in agencies such as the entrepreneurship and employment agencies.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was used as context only, since this is part of the chapter. The Commission finds about 40% of mapped services available digitally in 2024, over 50% in 2025 with 64% for businesses and 54% for individuals in 2024, and 82% of business services and 61% of citizen services by June 2026, and its 2026 release decision confirms that the Reform Agenda objective of 75% of business services was met. The 2026 report also finds that user uptake increased significantly, including in rural areas.

**Priority.** Digital services for businesses are the subject of key recommendations in four reports: digitalising processes to cut administrative burden in 2023 and 2024, and digitalising the entrepreneurship agency's processes in 2025 and 2026. The related Reform Agenda payment step (75% of business services digitalised, with the State Register of Legal Units and the life events methodology) is met, so the rating rests on the repeated recommendations; online public services are central to the digital government theme.

**Tractability.** There is strong evidence of past progress, with the share of business services online rising each year from 2024 to 2026 and the payment step met. The work is led by the e-Governance Agency and the State Chancellery with shared platforms, although sector agencies and rural demand still need attention.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 2022 Opinion contains no item on the issue in the evidence. The 2023 and 2024 key recommendations framed it as reducing administrative burden by simplifying and digitalising processes [1][2]. Alongside, the Commission's measures of availability rose from about 40% of mapped services available digitally in 2024 to over 50% in 2025, with 64% for businesses and 54% for individuals in 2024, while noting each time that delivery conditions vary [16][17][18]. In 2025 the Commission also flagged low demand, especially in rural areas, and narrowed the key recommendation to the entrepreneurship agency's processes, which it repeats in 2026 [19][3][4]. The Reform Agenda business services step was met in March 2026, and the 2026 report records 82% of business and 61% of citizen services digitalised [10][11].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the State Register of Legal Units is used at scale is not shown, and registration data from the register or the Commission would settle it [6].
- How far use of e-services has grown in rural areas is not measured, and figures on services delivered through the public service centres, checked independently, would be needed [12].
- The Government gives two different counts of business service requests in 2023, 3.7 million with 2.48 million online in one report and 4,524,731 with 3,058,794 online in another, possibly on different measures, and a single defined series would settle the level of online use [23][22].
- Whether the entrepreneurship agency's integrated information system, procured in June 2026, and the employment agency's new system are in operation is not known, and evidence of their use would show whether the sector gap is closing [14][15].
