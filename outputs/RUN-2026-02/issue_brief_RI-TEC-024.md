# Issue brief: Security of 5G networks not ensured

RI-TEC-024 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, including supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers, is not fully transposed or applied when 5G networks are deployed. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission asks Moldova to implement the EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, including assessing the risk profile of suppliers and putting in place restrictions or exclusions for high-risk suppliers [1]. The Reform Agenda sets full alignment by December 2027 as a step: a legal framework, an assessment of assets and suppliers, and restrictions on high-risk suppliers for critical and highly sensitive assets [2]. Moldova has started preparatory work. A grant agreement with Swedfund, approved in March 2025, funds a study of the impact and risks of the Toolbox [3], and in October 2025 the ministry contracted KPMG AB for a supplier risk assessment, recommendations on restricting high-risk suppliers, cost estimates, an operator compensation mechanism and a draft law [4]. In April 2026 the Government reported the study still under way and a draft act on 5G network security still being finalised [5][6]. The Commission's 2026 report records secondary legislation on the security of public 5G networks among Moldova's steps to align with the NIS 2 Directive [7]. A law on 5G network cybersecurity is planned by December 2027 [8]. No source shows a supplier assessment completed or any restriction applied. The Government notes that compliance requires major investment in 5G networks [9]. What remains is the 5G security law, the completed supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers applied to critical assets. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel is persistent. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2026 report asks Moldova to start implementing the Toolbox measures, including restrictions or exclusions for high-risk suppliers, which the Reform Agenda also sets as a step. The Government reports a supplier risk assessment by a consultancy still in progress and a draft Government Decision on 5G network security still being finalised in 2026, with a 5G security law planned by December 2027. What remains is the 5G security law, the completed supplier risk assessment and restrictions on high-risk suppliers applied to critical assets.

**Direction of travel.** Sub-area wording on the 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, which matches this issue, recorded no progress in 2024. The Commission asked in 2025 for implementation of the Toolbox measures to continue and in 2026 for Moldova to start implementing them, including restrictions on high-risk suppliers, so the core requirement is unchanged across the three reports; the secondary legislation on security of public 5G networks noted in 2026 and the supplier risk study under way are preparatory steps.

**Priority.** Alignment with the 5G Toolbox is part of the key recommendation on the Digital Transformation Strategy priorities in the 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports and is a step of the Reform Agenda under the Reform and Growth Facility, after the sub-area recorded no progress in 2024. It is central to the cybersecurity theme.

**Tractability.** Funded preparatory work is under way, with a study funded by Swedfund and a consultancy contract covering supplier risk assessment, an operator compensation mechanism and a draft law. The Government notes that compliance requires major investment in 5G networks, so progress depends on operators bearing, or being compensated for, the cost of restricting high-risk suppliers.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The 5G Toolbox entered the Commission's key recommendations in 2024, as part of building capacity to implement the Digital Transformation Strategy, and the same report found no progress on alignment [10][11]. The 2025 report kept the Toolbox in the key recommendation and spelled out what implementation means: assessing the risk profile of suppliers and restricting or excluding high-risk suppliers [12][13]. The 2025 Reform Agenda turned this into a step with a December 2027 deadline and a national risk assessment of 5G suppliers carried out in consultation with the EU [2][14]. In 2026 the key recommendation again names the Toolbox, and the body of the report shifts from "continue" to asking Moldova to "start" implementing the measures, including restrictions or exclusions for high-risk suppliers [15][1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- It is not clear whether the secondary legislation on security of public 5G networks that the Commission reports in 2026 is the Government decision that was still being finalised in April 2026; the adopted act and its date would settle it [7][6].
- The results of the supplier risk assessment by KPMG AB are not in the evidence; the completed study would show which suppliers and assets are judged high risk [4].
- How the cost of replacing high-risk equipment will be met is not settled; the proposed operator compensation mechanism, and its funding, would show whether operators can comply [9][4].
- Whether the 5G security law will be adopted by December 2027 cannot yet be judged; a draft submitted to Parliament would show progress against the Reform Agenda deadline [8][2].
