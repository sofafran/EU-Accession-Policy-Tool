# Issue brief: Cybersecurity legal and strategic framework incomplete

RI-TEC-022 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Network and information security law is not fully aligned with the NIS2 Directive and the Cybersecurity Act, the national cybersecurity strategy is late or fragmented in implementation, and responsibilities are split without effective coordination. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02); Coordination failure (MEC-08).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The Commission's key recommendations of 2024, 2025 and 2026 ask Moldova to align its legislation with EU cybersecurity standards, and the Reform Agenda commits it to a law transposing the NIS 2 Directive, with cybersecurity responsibilities clarified [1][2][3][4][5]. Moldova adopted Law No. 48/2023 on cybersecurity, in force since January 2025, and the Commission records Government decisions implementing it with the aim of NIS 2 alignment, further secondary legislation on cyber incidents and 5G security in 2026, and the National Cybersecurity Programme 2026-2030, adopted in December 2025 [6][7][8][9][10]. The Government itself describes Law No. 48/2023 as only partially harmonising national law with NIS 2, and the amending law for NIS 2, the laws on the Cyber Resilience Act and on cybersecurity certification, and the designation of a market surveillance authority were still planned for 2026 and 2027 [11][12][13][14][15]. Most of the new acts are adopted but not yet shown in application, and no outcome has been assessed. The Cyber Security Agency reports that some of its 2025 work was only partly done because of too few staff and incomplete rules, and the ministry's cybersecurity policy unit has four posts, one vacant [16][17]. These Government documents are machine translated. What remains is full transposition of NIS 2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the certification framework, with the authorities designated to apply them. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, mainly legal and strategic. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The key recommendation of 2024, 2025 and 2026 asks Moldova to align legislation with EU cybersecurity standards. The Government describes Law No. 48/2023 as only partially harmonising with NIS 2, and the amending law for NIS 2, the laws on the Cyber Resilience Act and cybersecurity certification and the related authority designations were still planned in 2025 and 2026. What remains is full transposition of NIS 2 and of the Cyber Resilience Act and certification framework, with the authorities designated to apply them.

**Direction of travel.** The Chapter 10 scale is context only. The 2023 report recorded adoption of the Law on cybersecurity but said implementation had to start, the 2024 and 2025 reports recorded implementing Government Decisions aimed at NIS 2 alignment, and the 2026 report records further secondary legislation and the National Cybersecurity Programme 2026-2030 adopted in December 2025, replacing the strategy the Reform Agenda had described as lapsed. The improvement is mainly legal and strategic, as the Reform Agenda still found no holistic framework in 2025.

**Priority.** Alignment with EU cybersecurity standards is in the key recommendation of the 2024, 2025 and 2026 reports, and NIS 2 alignment with clarified institutional roles is part of the Reform Agenda. The legal framework is central to the cybersecurity theme.

**Tractability.** Moldova has adopted the main law and a series of implementing Government Decisions since 2023, which shows legislative capacity. The Cybersecurity Agency reports that missing staff and an incomplete framework limited supervision in 2025, the ministry's cybersecurity unit has four posts with one vacant, and the Reform Agenda expects compliance costs to meet resistance from operators.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* In 2021 the Commission's concern was operational: a national computer emergency response team foreseen by the 2019-2024 strategy was still pending approval of its mandate [18]. The February 2023 analytical report said Moldova would need to align with the NIS framework [19]. After the Law on cybersecurity was adopted, the November 2023 report said its implementation had to start [6][20]. From 2024 the requirement became a standing key recommendation to align legislation with EU cybersecurity standards, including the 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox, repeated in 2025 and, as "further aligning", in 2026 [1][2][3]. The 2025 Reform Agenda sharpened it: it found no holistic cybersecurity framework and no plans for large-scale incidents, noted that the 2019-2024 strategy had lapsed, and set out a NIS 2 law, clearer institutional roles and mandatory requirements for products in view of the Cyber Resilience Act [21][22][4][5][23]. It also warned that operators may resist compliance costs [24].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the law amending Law No. 48/2023 to transpose NIS 2 has been adopted, and how far it aligns, would be settled by the adopted text and a Commission assessment of it [12][11].
- How the supervision rules of 2025 are applied is not shown; numbers of audits received and inspections and sanctions by the Agency would settle it [29][30].
- The Agency's account that it designated 265 essential and important service providers is not corroborated by an independent source; a Commission or audit finding would confirm it [31].
- Whether staffing limits continue to hold back the Agency and the ministry would be shown by current staffing figures against approved posts [28][17].
