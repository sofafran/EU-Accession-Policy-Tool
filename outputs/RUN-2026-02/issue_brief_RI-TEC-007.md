# Issue brief: Online access for service providers and regulated professions incomplete

RI-TEC-007 · Moldova · **Status: Validated at G7 (GATE-0029), internal output.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The point of single contact does not give service providers full online information and procedures as the Services Directive requires, and there is no transparent electronic register of regulated professions and qualifications. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

In 2023 the Commission said that legal alignment on services and professional qualifications needs to be complemented by non-legislative measures, including electronic platforms and procedures and administrative capacity [1][2]. In this area that means a point of single contact giving service providers full online information and procedures, and a transparent electronic register of regulated professions and qualifications. Moldova's response so far consists of plans. Its 2025 accession programme lists reconfiguring the government public services portal to act as the point of single contact, and its qualifications framework roadmap for 2023 to 2027 includes making the National Register of Qualifications interoperable with the EU Europass database [3][4]. The public services portal already works as a general single point of access to public services, but the Government describes its successor, the EVO portal, as a one-stop shop for public services in general rather than as the point of single contact for service providers [5]. Nothing is shown to have happened yet on either measure, and the Government itself lists interoperability between national systems and EU digital platforms as a potential challenge [6]. The Government sources are machine translated. What remains is a working point of single contact for service providers and an electronic register of regulated professions and qualifications. The unresolved gap is open and the direction of travel cannot be determined (insufficient evidence). Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2023 Commission calls for electronic platforms and procedures are met so far only by plans: reconfiguring the public services portal as the point of single contact is planned, and making the National Register of Qualifications interoperable with Europass is under way. What remains is a point of single contact giving service providers full online information and procedures, and an electronic register of regulated professions and qualifications.

**Direction of travel.** A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found that the Commission's only items on these tools are two 2023 calls, in the analytical report and the 2023 report, for electronic platforms and procedures alongside legal alignment on professional qualifications, with no later EU assessment of them. The government's 2025 plans to reconfigure the public services portal as the point of single contact and to link the National Register of Qualifications with Europass are planned and under way respectively, so there is no sequence to judge.

**Priority.** The Commission's calls for electronic platforms in this area appear only in 2023, as part of wider recommendations on professional qualifications, and their link to the technology theme is uncertain; a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on them.

**Tractability.** The public services portal already works and could be reconfigured, and the work lies with national bodies. Against that, one measure is planned and one under way, the government expects possible problems of interoperability with EU platforms, and no item among the 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files reports past progress on either; the Government describes the EVO portal as a one-stop shop for public services in general, not as the point of single contact for service providers.

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission has raised this issue only in 2023, and only as part of wider recommendations on professional qualifications and services. The February 2023 Analytical Report called for alignment on professional qualifications to be complemented with non-legislative measures and structural reforms, such as the use of electronic platforms and procedures, administrative capacity and knowledge to assess foreign qualifications [1]. The November 2023 report repeated the call in shorter form, asking for electronic platforms and procedures to be set up and administrative capacity to be built [2]. No later Commission report in the evidence returns to these tools, and none of the 2025 or 2026 key recommendations, benchmarks or Reform Agenda payment conditions names them [1][2]. The requirement has therefore not moved since 2023, and there is no sequence of Commission wording to compare. Its link to the technology theme is uncertain, because the EU wording concerns electronic procedures as one means of applying the services and qualifications rules [2].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the public services portal is reconfigured to act as the point of single contact, with full online procedures for service providers, would be shown by the reconfigured portal and a Commission assessment of it [3].
- Whether the National Register of Qualifications becomes interoperable with Europass, and whether it covers regulated professions transparently, would be shown by evidence of the link working [4].
- Whether the expected interoperability problems with EU platforms arise, and how they are handled, could be settled by technical reports from the bodies responsible [6].
- How the Commission now views these tools could be settled by a later report or screening material that assesses them, since no EU item after 2023 does [2].
