# Issue brief: Work permits, quotas and residence conditions applied to EU workers

RI-FMW-002 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** EU workers and their family members are subject to work permits, annual quotas, residence permits or unspecified conditions (for example for seasonal work) that must be lifted or settled by accession. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Missing implementing rules (MEC-02).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

By accession, EU workers and their family members must no longer face work permits or similar conditions, yet in 2023 the Commission found that EU citizens needed an employer-requested temporary residence permit for work and numerous documents, and that their family members were treated as other foreign workers [1][2]. Moldova amended the law on the regime of foreigners, and from January 2024 EU citizens could work without a work permit [3][4]. In July 2025 it adopted Law No. 200/2025, under which EU citizens need no work authorisation and, for stays over 90 days, a residence card not tied to a purpose of stay; the Commission reports that the law entered into force in March 2026 [5][6][7]. These are adopted laws, not shown results. Practice lags: after the General Inspectorate for Migration approved an operating procedure in March 2026, 28 applications for the new residence documents had been submitted, while 422 EU citizens were still to be documented under the old stay-for-work regime in 2025 [8][9]. Full separation of the two regimes depends on a draft law on foreigners that would enter into force on 1 June 2027, and the Ministry of Internal Affairs still needs to revise its rules on registering EU citizens [10][11][12]. Most Government sources are machine translated. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is improving, mainly in law. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Under Law No. 200/2025 EU citizens need no work authorisation and, for stays over 90 days, a residence card not tied to a purpose, but the Commission reports that only 28 applications for the new residence documents had been submitted after the March 2026 procedure, while 422 EU citizens were still to be documented under the old stay-for-work regime in 2025. Full separation of the two regimes in the general law on foreigners depends on a draft law due to apply from 1 June 2027, and the Ministry of Internal Affairs' secondary legislation on registering EU citizens is still being revised. What remains is the practical move of EU citizens and their family members to the new residence cards and the completion of the implementing rules.

**Direction of travel.** In 2023 the Commission found that EU citizens needed a temporary residence permit for work and that their family members were treated as other foreigners, while the 2023 report recorded amendments easing temporary residence for EU citizens; the 2024 report recorded amendments in force from January 2024 letting them work without a work permit. The 2025 report recorded a separate regime for EU citizens, and the 2026 report the entry into force of the free movement law in March 2026 and a standard operating procedure for their residence documents; the improvement is mainly legal. The chapter rating covers the whole chapter and was used as context only.

**Priority.** Work permits and residence conditions for EU workers are central to the chapter, but they are not named separately in a key recommendation; the 2025 and 2026 key recommendations on access for EU citizens to the labour market can be read as covering them, which would still make only two reports. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on the issue.

**Tractability.** The legal change has been made and the General Inspectorate for Migration has an operating procedure, so what is left is mostly administrative and within national control. Few EU citizens had applied for the new documents by 2026, and full separation of the regimes depends on a new foreigners law that is still a draft, so progress depends on the Ministry of Internal Affairs and the Inspectorate following through.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The earliest Commission material on this issue in the evidence is the February 2023 Analytical Report, which described the permit and document requirements for EU citizens and the treatment of their family members as other foreigners [1][2]. The 2023 report found limited progress and recorded amendments easing temporary residence and a further amendment passed in first reading in July 2023 [13][14][3][15]. The 2024 report moved from describing the barrier to recording its removal in law, with amendments in force from January 2024 [4]. The 2025 report recorded the separate regime for EU citizens adopted in July 2025, and its key recommendations on access for EU citizens to the labour market, repeated in 2026, can be read as covering the issue, though they do not name it, and no benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition addresses it [16][17][18]. By 2026 the Commission's attention had turned to practice: the law's entry into force and the slow take-up of the new residence documents [7][8].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- How quickly EU citizens and their family members move from the old stay-for-work regime to the new residence cards could be settled by Migration Inspectorate figures on applications and cards issued after March 2026 [9].
- Whether the Ministry of Internal Affairs' revised rules on registering EU citizens, and the draft amendment to the identity documents decision, have been approved would be shown by the adopted acts [12][21].
- Whether the draft law on foreigners is adopted with the regimes separated and enters into force as planned in June 2027 would be shown by its adopted text [11][10].
- Whether the July 2023 first-reading amendment became the 2023 law on 90 days' work without a permit could be settled by the parliamentary record [15][22].
