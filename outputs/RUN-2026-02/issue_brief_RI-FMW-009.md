# Issue brief: National social security law not aligned with the coordination regulations

RI-FMW-009 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** National pension, health and other social security laws are only partly aligned with the EU coordination regulations, for example through citizenship conditions, and the administrative measures for applying them at accession are not identified or in place. **Problem mechanisms:** Legal or regulatory gap (MEC-01); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU requires Moldova's national social security law to be aligned with the coordination regulations, Regulations (EC) No 883/2004 and No 987/2009, and in 2026 the Commission asked Moldova to provide gap analyses for those regulations [1]. In 2023 the Commission found that national law covered most branches of social security but not pre-retirement benefits, and that non-nationals with temporary residence received no unemployment benefit [2][3]. Moldova has since adopted a law setting the legal basis for applying the EU coordination rules and connecting to the Electronic Exchange of Social Security Information [4]. A draft law on unemployment benefit would create the framework to apply Articles 64 and 65 of Regulation 883/2004 and introduce the notion of frontier worker, but its section on EU mobility would apply only from accession [5][6][7]. No source describes the adopted law being applied, and the Government states that current law does not regulate keeping unemployment benefit when moving to another Member State or registering EU jobseekers in Moldova [8]. Specialist capacity is thin: the health insurance fund's legal directorate has 3 of 8 planned staff working on harmonisation [9]. What remains is a gap analysis and full alignment of pension, health, unemployment and other social security law, with the administrative measures to apply it at accession. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving, legal only. Priority is Medium and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** A law establishing the legal basis for applying the EU coordination rules and EESSI interconnection has been adopted, which is a legal step only, while the draft law on keeping unemployment benefit when moving and on frontier workers remains a proposal, and the Government states that current law does not regulate these cases; only 3 of 8 planned staff of the legal directorate work on harmonisation with European rules. What remains is a gap analysis and full alignment of national pension, health, unemployment and other social security law with Regulations (EC) No 883/2004 and No 987/2009, together with the administrative measures to apply them at accession.

**Direction of travel.** The 2023 analytical report found that national law covers most branches of social security but not pre-retirement benefits, gives no unemployment benefit to non-nationals with temporary residence, and needs capacity building in coordination; a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no Commission item on this alignment in the 2024 or 2025 reports, so the wording is not repeated across three reports and the default of persistent does not apply. The 2026 report records the adoption of a law setting a legal basis for applying the coordination rules, which is improvement in law only: the Commission does not assess its alignment and asks for gap analyses of the coordination regulations, and the 2023 gap on unemployment benefit remains open. The Chapter 2 rating rose from an early stage in 2023 and 2024 to between some and a moderate level of preparation in 2026, but the progress it cites concerns bilateral agreements and EURES, so the scale was not decisive.

**Priority.** Alignment with the coordination regulations is core acquis for the chapter, and the 2026 key recommendation asks for gap analyses of them. A search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found the requirement only in the 2023 analytical report and the 2026 report, not in a key recommendation repeated across three reports, a benchmark or a Reform Agenda payment condition.

**Tractability.** The legal changes are within national control: a coordination law has been adopted and a detailed draft on unemployment benefits prepared. Against that, the work spans several institutions and benefit systems, specialist staff are few and the draft ties key provisions to the date of accession.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* No Commission item on this alignment from the 2022 Opinion appears in the evidence. The requirement was first set out in the February 2023 Analytical Report, which mapped which branches of social security national law covers, noted the exclusion of non-nationals with temporary residence from unemployment benefit, and called for capacity building in EU social security coordination and in distinguishing social security from social assistance [2][3][10]. The 2023, 2024 and 2025 annual reports contain no item on this alignment; their Chapter 2 findings concern bilateral agreements and EURES [11][12][13]. The 2026 report returns to it, recording the adoption of the coordination law and recommending gap analyses for the coordination regulations alongside further social security agreements [4][1]. The Commission does not assess how far the new law is aligned, and the requirement is not attached to a benchmark or a Reform Agenda payment condition.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- How far the adopted coordination law aligns national law with Regulations 883/2004 and 987/2009 is not assessed by any source; the gap analyses the Commission asks for, or its next assessment, would settle it [4][1].
- Whether the draft law on unemployment benefit will be adopted, and with which entry-into-force date and frontier worker definition, is open while ministries still propose changes; the adopted text in the Official Gazette would settle it [18][19].
- Whether the 2023 gap on unemployment benefit for non-nationals with temporary residence has been closed is not reported; a provision in the amended Law No. 105/2018 or a Commission finding would show it [3][8].
- Whether the institutions have the trained staff to apply the coordination rules is unclear; staffing and training data from the social insurance and health insurance bodies would show it [17][9].
