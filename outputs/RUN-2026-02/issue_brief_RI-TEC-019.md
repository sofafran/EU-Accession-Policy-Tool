# Issue brief: Data protection authority not consulted and its advice not followed

RI-TEC-019 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** The data protection authority is not systematically consulted on sector and implementing laws, its recommendations are often not implemented, and parliament does not follow up its reports. **Problem mechanisms:** Coordination failure (MEC-08); Weak enforcement or oversight (MEC-04).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

This issue concerns whether the data protection authority is consulted on sector and implementing laws and whether its advice is followed. It is not raised in EU material, so there is no EU requirement to compare against. The authority reports that 208 draft national normative acts and international treaties were submitted to it for review in 2025 [1], and that for most of them it found that the proposed provisions needed to be completed, adjusted or revised to comply with the legal framework on personal data protection [2]. Its opinions on individual drafts asked for clearer wording on personal data in a draft law [3] and found the draft data governance law unclear on notifying the authority of requests for personal data, stressing that its legal mandate must be respected [4]. These items show that drafts are sent to the authority and that it comments on them; no item shows whether its advice was then taken into account. What remains is to establish whether the authority's opinions are followed. The unresolved gap is insufficient evidence and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- It is not known whether the changes the authority asked for in 2025 were made; a comparison of its opinions with the texts finally adopted, or a follow-up report by the authority, would settle it [2].
- The share of relevant draft laws that are never sent to the authority is not known; a list of adopted acts touching on personal data, set against the drafts it reviewed, would show whether consultation is systematic [1].
- How the authority's comments on the draft data governance law were handled is not recorded; a later version of the draft or the table of comments from interinstitutional consultation would show whether the notification provision was clarified [4].
- Whether Parliament follows up the authority's reports is not addressed by any item; parliamentary records of hearings or decisions on the authority's annual report would settle it.
