# Issue brief: Low digital adoption by businesses

RI-TEC-033 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Digitalisation of the economy and adoption of digital technologies by small businesses remain low compared with the EU and undermine competitiveness, and EU programmes and digital innovation hubs are little used. **Problem mechanisms:** Insufficient resources (MEC-09).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

Adoption of digital technologies by businesses is not an acquis requirement, and the Commission raises it only in general findings on the economy, with no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on it. The Reform Agenda finds the share of Moldovan businesses using digital services low, which harms productivity and market reach, and the Government describes enterprise digitalisation, especially of small firms, as low, held back by lack of knowledge, high costs and slow returns [1][2][3]. In response, the Government approved an SME digital transformation programme, Parliament ratified association to the Digital Europe Programme in May 2024, nine investment projects were approved under the Programme for Digital Innovation and Tech Start-ups 2025-2030, and Moldova's first European Digital Innovation Hub was launched in April 2026 [4][5][6][7]. As the Digital Europe contact point, the e-Governance Agency reports supporting about 200 institutions and companies and recording 14 applications to calls in 2025 [8][9]. No source gives the number of enterprises helped by the support programmes or reports the innovation hub's activity. Two outcomes were assessed, the contact point's support and the instant payments system's effect on small entrepreneurs, and for both success is insufficient evidence [10][11]. The only repeated measure of business adoption, the share of enterprises making web sales, stands at 0.9% in both the Government's 2023 strategy and the baseline of its 2025 programme [12][13]. What remains is wider adoption of digital technologies by small businesses and greater use of EU programmes and the new hub. The unresolved gap is partly addressed and the direction of travel is insufficient evidence. Priority is Low and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** Responses are in place: SME digitalisation programmes, association to the Digital Europe Programme and the first European Digital Innovation Hub, launched in April 2026. Against that, the Reform Agenda finds the share of businesses using digital services low, and the government's 2025 programme still gives a baseline of 0.9% of enterprises making web sales. What remains is wider adoption of digital technologies by small businesses and greater use of EU programmes and the new innovation hub.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since the issue comes from a cross-cutting theme and its items sit in the economic sections. The Commission finds digitalisation lagging behind in 2023, slowly improving in 2024, steadily advancing in 2025 and continuing to advance in 2026, but these findings concern the economy as a whole. Across all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files, the only repeated measure of business adoption is the Government's share of enterprises making web sales, 0.9% both in its 2023 strategy and as the baseline of its 2025 programme, so no item shows a change in business adoption itself.

**Priority.** The Commission's findings on this issue are general statements in the economic sections, positive since 2024, and a search of all 3,631 items held in the Moldova evidence files found no key recommendation, benchmark or Reform Agenda payment condition on business adoption. Business adoption is not an acquis requirement.

**Tractability.** Support programmes, EU funding routes and the first innovation hub exist, and the Commission records steady progress in digitalisation. Against that, adoption depends on many small firms, and the government cites lack of knowledge, high costs and perceived long-term returns as barriers.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The Commission's wording on this issue consists of findings on digitalisation of the economy as a whole, not requirements. The 2022 Opinion noted that the IT sector had expanded noticeably from a low level, especially since the pandemic [14]. The 2023 report found Moldova lagging behind in digitalisation [15]. In 2024 the Commission found digitalisation slowly picking up thanks to more emphasis on e-government, digital skills and the ICT sector [16]. In 2025 it found digitalisation advancing with better internet access and more public e-services, though with regional disparities, and noted that e-commerce and digital payments were lowering transaction costs for small entrepreneurs [17][11]. The 2026 report says digitalisation continues to advance and describes Moldova as developing into a dynamic digital ecosystem [18][19]. The tone has thus turned from lagging to advancing, but none of these findings measures adoption by businesses. The Reform Agenda's finding on low business use of digital services is the most specific EU statement on the issue [1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether business adoption has changed since 2023 is not shown; an updated share of enterprises making web sales, or enterprise ICT survey data, would settle it [13].
- How many enterprises the SME digital transformation programme has supported, and with what results, is not reported; programme monitoring data from the ministry or the SME agency would show it [20][4].
- Whether Moldovan applicants have won Digital Europe funding is not known; the programme's results for Moldovan participants would settle it [9].
- What the European Digital Innovation Hub has done since its launch in April 2026 is not reported; its activity reports would show how many small firms it reaches [7].
