# Issue brief: Base registers incomplete, unreliable or not digitalised

RI-TEC-015 · Moldova · **Status: Draft, prepared at stage 12 for sign-off at G7.** It uses only judgements validated at G6 (GATE-0024) and, for comparator material, G6b (GATE-0028). Sections marked *Agent synthesis* are written by the tool from that material and add no new judgement. Numbers in square brackets refer to the evidence annex at the end.

**Issue definition.** Key registers, above all the population register, hold data of poor quality or are little used, and many institutional, property and intellectual property registers are not yet digitalised or kept up to date. **Problem mechanisms:** Poor transparency or unreliable data (MEC-07); Weak administrative capacity (MEC-03).

## In brief (agent synthesis)

The EU asks Moldova to complete the full digitalisation of the public property register, and the Reform Agenda requires a fully operational State Register of Legal Units by December 2025 and a digital cadastre covering 98 percent of the territory, with data regularly updated and online, by December 2027 [1][2][3]. Moldova digitalised the business register's paper archive between August 2023 and August 2024 and launched the State Register of Legal Units portal on 29 December 2025, with a World Bank loan funding its pilot phase [4][5][6]. The Commission judged the related Reform Agenda step satisfactorily fulfilled, and verified that 310 of 414 business services are digitalised, although only 133 are fully digital end to end [7][8]. The civil status records system went into production on 3 November 2025, but no source reports its use since [9]. Other registers have moved less: the public property register is still being prepared for digitalisation, the State Population Register concept was withdrawn in August 2025 and resumed in 2026, and the register of non-commercial organisations is described as outdated and not interoperable [10][11][12][13]. Sources disagree on whether all state information systems are registered in the register of state information systems. What remains is a fully digital public property register, the planned modernisation of the population register and the cadastre, and reliable data across base registers. The unresolved gap is partly_addressed and the direction of travel is improving. Priority is High and tractability is Medium.

**What remains.** The 2025 and 2026 key recommendations require completing the digitalisation of the public property register, which remains in progress, and the Reform Agenda requires a digital cadastre covering 98% of the territory by December 2027; the legal entities register is implemented. Sources conflict on the register of state information systems: SIGMA says all were registered, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed and 63% of registered systems not properly documented. What remains is a fully digital public property register, the planned modernisation of the population register and the cadastre, and reliable data across base registers.

**Direction of travel.** The chapter scale was not decisive, since this issue spans several chapters; the 2026 Chapter 6 rating of some progress cites the new register and is context only. The Commission recorded the business register's paper archive digitalised by August 2024 and the State Register of Legal Units launched on 29 December 2025, a Reform Agenda step it judged satisfactorily fulfilled, while the public property register was in progress in 2025 and still pending in 2026. The State Population Register concept was withdrawn in August 2025 and resumed in 2026 (machine translated), so not every register moved.

**Priority.** Base registers are named in two Reform Agenda payment conditions (the State Register of Legal Units and the digital cadastre), and the public property register is a key recommendation in 2025 and 2026; reliable base registers are central to the data and digital government themes. The legal entities register condition is already met, so the rating rests mainly on the cadastre condition and the repeated recommendation.

**Tractability.** The legal entities register was delivered with World Bank support, about six months after its planned July 2025 launch, and the civil status records system went into production in November 2025. Against that, the population register concept was suspended in 2025, the public property register has been in progress since 2025, and the Commission found the Public Services Agency short of administrative capacity in 2024.

*These reasons are shown as validated at G6. Where a reason says that implementation statuses were still proposals, it was written at stage 10; those statuses were validated at the same gate.*

## The EU requirement and how it has changed

*Agent synthesis:* The evidence holds no Commission item on base registers from the 2022 Opinion. In 2023 the Commission described the electronic cadastral system maintained by the Public Services Agency without raising a requirement [14]. In 2024 it recorded that the Agency, which registers companies, continued to suffer from limited administrative capacity and digitalisation resources [15]. The 2025 Reform Agenda made two registers payment conditions, the State Register of Legal Units and the digital cadastre, and the 2025 report asked Moldova to finalise digitalising the public property registry and to advance the Public Services Agency's roadmap [16][3][17][18]. In 2026 the Commission counted the new legal entities register as progress and repeated, in stronger terms, the call to complete the full digitalisation of the public property register [19][1].

## Open questions (agent synthesis)

- Whether the public property register is fully digitalised and operational is not yet shown; the Commission's next report would settle it [10][1].
- Progress towards a digital cadastre covering 98 percent of the territory by December 2027 is not reported; coverage figures from the cadastre agency or the Commission's assessment of the Reform Agenda step would settle it [3][27].
- Whether the State Register of Legal Units is used at scale since its launch is not shown; registration volumes would settle it [5][28].
- SIGMA states that all state information systems are registered, while the Court of Accounts finds registration delayed and 63 percent of registered systems not properly documented; a follow-up audit would settle it [22][21][23].
