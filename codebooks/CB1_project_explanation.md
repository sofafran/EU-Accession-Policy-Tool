# EU Accession Reform Intelligence Tool | Codebook 1

Version 1.1, 23 October 2026. Revised after the architecture review. The reasons for each change are recorded in the decision log (decisions A1, A2, B1 and E4).

We want to build an AI-enabled analytical tool that helps governments and TBI teams turn EU accession assessments into clearer reform priorities and practical policy options.

EU accession countries receive regular assessments from the European Commission setting out where progress has been made, where gaps remain and what further action is expected. These assessments cover many policy, regulatory and institutional areas and build up over several years. The challenge is that it is often difficult to see which issues matter most, what the government has already done, why progress has stalled, how comparable countries have dealt with similar problems, and what practical lessons follow.

The tool should create a structured analytical process for answering these questions. For each relevant issue, it should help to identify what the EU is asking for, translate that into a common reform problem, track how the issue has evolved over time, assess what the country has already done, identify what remains unresolved, find relevant comparator cases, understand what worked or failed elsewhere and why, test which lessons are transferable, prioritise the issue, and develop practical reform options.

The analytical chain has ten links. It brings together the original chain in this codebook and the reform journey in Codebook 5, so that adoption, implementation and outcome are kept apart:

- **EU requirement:** what the EU finds, recommends or sets as a benchmark.
- **Reform issue:** the common underlying problem behind the EU wording, taken from the reform-issue taxonomy.
- **Country response:** what the government has proposed, adopted or done.
- **Implementation:** whether the response is actually being applied in practice.
- **Outcome:** what happened as a result.
- **Unresolved gap:** what the EU asks for that has not yet been achieved.
- **Comparator evidence:** how comparable countries dealt with the same reform issue.
- **Lesson:** what worked, failed or mattered, and why.
- **Priority:** how important the issue is for accession and how realistic progress is.
- **Policy option:** practical reform options, always signed off by the reviewers named for the run.

Each link is either stated by a source or concluded by the analysis, and the tool keeps the two apart. A finding is something a source says. A judgement is something the tool or the analyst concludes. The same distinction applies to status, lessons and relevance: what a source states is recorded separately from what we assess.

The tool should allow users to explore this information in two main ways.

**Country view:** a user selects a country, such as Serbia, and sees the relevant accession assessments, reform issues, progress over time, government responses, unresolved gaps, comparator lessons and potential reform options.

**Thematic / cluster view:** a user selects a cluster, chapter, theme or reform issue, such as energy, rule of law or public procurement, and looks horizontally across accession countries to understand how the same issue appears in different contexts, what reforms have been attempted, what happened as a result and which experiences may be relevant to the target country.

The tool's purpose is to organise evidence around underlying reform problems and use that evidence to support diagnosis, comparison and decision-making. A reform should not be recommended simply because another country adopted it: the tool should first establish whether the problem is genuinely comparable, understand what was implemented and what happened afterwards, identify why it worked or failed, and only then assess whether the lesson is transferable.

The first version of the tool will focus on **Serbia** and on one negotiating chapter, Chapter 5 (Public procurement), allowing the methodology and information structure to be tested in a manageable setting. The first run has two parts. Run 1a is a Serbia diagnostic covering the chain from EU requirement to priority. Run 1b is a comparator pilot: for two or three Serbia reform issues chosen by the analyst, the tool gathers targeted evidence from comparator countries to test the remaining links, including lessons and draft policy options. The longer-term ambition is to expand the same structure incrementally across other EU accession countries, creating a common evidence base that supports both deep country analysis and cross-country comparison.

The tool supports the analyst; it does not replace them. AI can extract, organise and propose, but judgements about success, attribution, transferability, priority and policy options are always reviewed by a human analyst before they are used.

The project is described in seven codebooks: this explanation (1), sources (2), limits and log (3), data architecture (4), matching (5), assessment (6) and outputs (7). Agents working on the project follow the CLAUDE.md file and the procedures and rules that put these codebooks into practice.
